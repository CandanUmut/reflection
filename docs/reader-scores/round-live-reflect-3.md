## M8 — Wealth and charity (avg 6.7)

Fixed since round 2: context for 59:7 (fay', Banu al-Nadir) and 9:34 (religious leaders); 2.5% rate with Bukhari 1454; the eight groups of 9:60; riba positions named (IIFA 1985 Res. 10 vs Al-Azhar IRA 2002); "No shame in receiving" and "If you are in debt" (2:280, Muslim 1563, Bukhari 2397) — the hurting reader is now addressed.

Top 3 remaining gaps:
1. **The heading "The law: what the four schools hold" is not delivered.** No school is named and no position is given; it says only that they "differ in some details (for example on jewellery)". No nisab figure (gold/silver weight) is given. The FAQ points to "Zakat in brief" above, but no section has that name (broken reference).
2. **The practical doubt about interest is not met.** The doubter's real question is "I have a mortgage / student loan / pension — am I sinning, and is there any leeway (necessity, living as a minority)?" The page lists two positions and stops.
3. **Thin evidence for scientist and skeptic.** Research covers only the giver's wellbeing (Curry d ≈ 0.28, Dunn). Nothing on whether zakat or waqf actually reduces poverty. The gap box says inequality is "high" and zakat collection "weak" with no number or source, so the steelman is stated rather than argued.

## M12 — Care for the earth (avg 6.2)

Fixed since round 2: classical reading of 30:41 (Mujahid, Abu al-'Aliya via Ibn Kathir) and an honest note that applying it to pollution is "a modern extension"; animal hadith (Bukhari 2466, 3318); hima (Bukhari 2370); Lynn White named; "Plant it anyway" (Musnad Ahmad 12902) is a true, warm word for eco-anxiety.

Top 3 remaining gaps:
1. **Science is still the weakest layer.** "Religion's effect on environmental behaviour is mixed across studies" still names no study. The only study (White et al. 2019) is about nature and health, not excess or corruption. The WRI water data is listed in the sources, but the page gives no number from it.
2. **Some verses are shown but never discussed, and "balance" is used without flagging it.** 6:141 and 2:205 appear in the verse block, but the text never mentions them. 55:7–9 is read as ecological "balance", but in context it is about just weights and measures (verse 9). Unlike 30:41 and khalifa, the page does not say this reading is an extension. "Classical tafsir more often read khalifa as succession…" names no commentator.
3. **The dominion steelman is answered in two lines.** White's argument is stated, then answered with 22:65 ("subjected") — which is close to the very idea criticised — and "Use is allowed; abuse is not." The skeptic's follow-up is left open: if the teaching is this clear, why the record? Also, the gap box's example (MENA is the most water-stressed region) is mostly climate, not conduct, so it is a weak example of a gap in practice.

## S5 — Sun and moon, each in an orbit (avg 5.3)

Fixed since round 2: the lede now matches the verse; the reflection is a real paragraph (calendars, prayer, fasting; "a reliable sky is a gift people did not arrange"); honest line that the commentators "did not share our astronomy".

Top 3 remaining gaps:
1. **The science is still four disconnected bullets.** Axial tilt, galactic orbit and Moon recession are not tied to anything in 36:37–40. The 27.3 vs 29.5 day difference is stated, not explained, though it is exactly the "phases" (manazil) of 36:39. What the Sun's own motion is ("the sun runs") is never said in a sentence.
2. **Key words and claims are still unexplained.** *Falak* is never defined. "Later scholars read the prostration as obedience" still names no scholar, and no grade is shown for Bukhari 3199/4802. The popular modern claim is rejected in one line ("made no 'galactic orbit' prediction") without first being stated. The skeptic's strong form — the hadith says the sun goes beneath the Throne at sunset, while it is always rising somewhere — is not put at full strength.
3. **Confusing quotations and leftover notes.** The same phrase appears in three wordings: "toward its stopping point" (translation), "to a resting place" (summary line) and "its fixed course for a term" (tafsir). The outsider cannot tell these are the same words. The FAQ says "see the honest notes", but there is only one. It is still the shortest page (578 words) and reads like notes.

2. Claims attributed to unnamed groups ("later scholars", "classical tafsir", "the four schools", "studies").
