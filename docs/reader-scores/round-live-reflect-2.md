## M8 — Wealth and charity (avg 6.0)

Strengths to keep: every hadith carries collection, number and grade; the weak "honest merchant" hadith is named and set aside; the "gap between ideal and practice" box (El-Gamal, weak zakat collection) is a real steelman; Curry et al. effect size (d ≈ 0.28) is given and called small.

Top 3 gaps to reach 7.5:
1. **Verse context is missing.** 59:7 is about *fay'* (property taken without fighting) and 9:34 opens with "scholars and monks". The page uses both as general rules on wealth without saying so. Student and skeptic will catch this. Add one line on each verse's context and how scholars still drew a general principle from it.
2. **Zakat and riba are named, not explained.** "What is zakat?" gives no rate (2.5%), no threshold (nisab), and does not list the eight groups of 9:60. "Scholars differ on modern interest" names no positions or bodies. Say what the main views are and who holds them.
3. **Nothing for the poor or indebted reader.** The hurting reader in debt, or ashamed to receive zakat, finds no word. One paragraph on receiving help without shame (zakat as a right also means taking it is not humiliation), and on debt relief (2:280), would lift `hurting` from 5.

## M12 — Care for the earth (avg 5.7)

Strengths to keep: the honest statement that the stewardship reading of *khalifa* is modern and classical tafsir read it otherwise; the weak water hadith (Ibn Mājah 425) flagged and not used; "Does the Quran talk about climate change? No" — direct and trusted.

Top 3 gaps to reach 7.5:
1. **Classical material is thin.** No classical reading of 30:41 (what *fasad* meant to the commentators), and no classical teaching on animals or water. 6:141 and 2:205 are quoted in the verse block but never discussed. The sources list Musnad Aḥmad 12902 but the page text never shows that hadith.
2. **Science is weak for the scientist.** "Religion's effect on environmental behaviour is mixed across studies" has no study named. Only one research finding (White et al. 2019), and it is about nature and health, not about the topic of excess or corruption. Name the studies behind "mixed" and give one or two numbers on waste or water use.
3. **No steelman of the main critique.** The skeptic expects the "dominion" critique of Abrahamic religions (Lynn White, 1967) or the question "if Muslims are stewards, why the record?" The gap box states the record but does not engage the argument. The hurting reader (eco-anxiety, grief over loss of nature) gets no word either.

## S5 — Sun and moon, each in an orbit (avg 5.2)

Strengths to keep: Ibn Kathir's "dome on pillars, not a sphere" is quoted openly; the prostration hadith is not hidden; Ibn Mas'ud's reading "no resting place" is a real detail; the honest note rejects the "galactic orbit" claim.

Top 3 gaps to reach 7.5:
1. **The lede and the science do not connect to the verse.** The page opens with "The Moon recedes about 3.8 cm a year", which has nothing to do with 36:37–40. The facts (tilt, 27.3 / 29.5 days, galactic orbit) are bullets with no link to "phases" (36:39), "the sun runs" (36:38) or *falak* (36:40). Explain the 27.3 vs 29.5 day difference in a sentence (it is the "phases" the verse names) and say plainly what the Sun's motion is.
2. **Key words and claims go unexplained.** *Falak* is never defined (classically, a rotating sphere or spindle whorl). "Later scholars read the prostration as obedience" names no scholar. The popular modern claim is rejected in one line without saying what it is. Name the scholars and state the claim before answering it.
3. **The reflection is one sentence.** "Measured, reliable order… underpins calendars and prayer times" is all the doubter and the hurting reader get. Shortest page in the set (486 words); it reads like notes. Expand the reflection into a short paragraph and turn the bullets into prose.

## S20 — The ant (avg 6.0)

Strengths to keep: the clear four-layer split; the honest note on the feminine verb ("a nice coincidence at most"); the vivid alarm-pheromone facts; the reflection on fairness ("the ant excuses the army") is fresh and well taken from the tafsir.

Top 3 gaps to reach 7.5:
1. **Science citations are vague in the text.** "In one experiment…", "In a classic study, ants within roughly 5–19 cm…", "a 2025 Cell study" with no species, no authors and a news site as the source. Name the study, the species, and the lead authors inline.
2. **The skeptic's strongest point is not met.** A Solomon-and-the-ant story also appears in later Jewish tradition; the skeptic will ask about borrowing or a shared tradition. The doubter will ask whether a talking ant is meant literally. The page says the commentators "treat the ant's speech as real" but does not discuss the question.
3. **Tafsir quotes are one-sided toward Ibn Kathir.** Al-Qurtubi and al-Razi are named but not quoted. One concrete point from each (for example al-Razi on what animal "speech" means) would lift `student`. "Sometimes sound-based" (stridulation) is left unexplained.

## S26 — Darkness in the deep sea (avg 5.8)

Strengths to keep: the strongest honest note in the set ("people who sailed or dove knew that deep water grows dark"); the internal-waves claim is named and limited; the diagram is labelled "schematic, not a measured curve".

Top 3 gaps to reach 7.5:
1. **The last line raises a hard question the page ignores.** "Whoever God gives no light has no light" reads as fatalistic to the doubter and the hurting reader: is it God's fault if I am in darkness? The page calls light "a gift" and stops. Give the classical answer and the link to the Light Verse (24:35) just before it.
2. **The science lacks key numbers and one key fact.** "Most of the ocean, by volume, lies in total darkness" needs a figure. Light depth depends on water clarity (coastal water goes dark much sooner), and that is not said. Bioluminescence (light in the deep) is missing, though it bears directly on a page about light and darkness.
3. **Repetition and vague attribution.** The "text version" repeats the How-it-works bullets almost word for word. "Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi map the layers…" lumps three scholars together. Say who said which, and cut the repeated bullets.

## Overall verdict

**Live (M8 6.0, M12 5.7):** Honest, well graded on hadith, and the "gap between ideal and practice" boxes earn trust.
But each section is two or three sentences, so the student and skeptic leave with their main question open (verse context, riba positions, classical ecology).
The hurting reader is not addressed on either page; one pastoral paragraph each would be the cheapest gain.

**Reflect (S5 5.2, S20 6.0, S26 5.8):** The four layers are cleanly separated and the honest notes are good, with no overclaiming anywhere.
The science reads as bullets with vague or second-hand citations, and is often not tied back to the verse's own words.
Reflections are one or two lines; S5 especially needs a real reflection and a lede that matches its verse.
