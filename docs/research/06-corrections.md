## S1 — the seed and the date stone (6:95)

- **Research said:** "Taking up water triggers gibberellin (GA) signalling. Amylase enzymes turn stored starch into sugar." This is the textbook model for cereal grains, but 6:95 names both grain (*ḥabb*) and the date stone (*nawā*), and the page applied it to the date stone too.
- **Correct:** A date stone stores its food mainly as galactomannan in the thick walls of its endosperm cells, which is also what makes it so hard. Endo-β-mannanase, the enzyme that breaks these walls down, rises in the micropylar endosperm only after germination is complete (the root has emerged).
- **Source (read):** Gong X, Bassel GW, Wang A, Greenwood JS, Bewley JD (2005). "The Emergence of Embryos from Hard Seeds is Related to the Structure of the Cell Walls of the Micropylar Endosperm, and not to Endo-β-mannanase Activity." *Annals of Botany* 96(7):1165–1173. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247068 — "The endosperm of the date seed is extremely hard, due to the endosperm cell walls being composed of galactomannan"; "After the completion of germination (13 d), enzyme activity had increased in both the embryo and micropylar endosperm."
