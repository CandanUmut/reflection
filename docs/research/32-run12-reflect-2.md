## S9 — Fine-tuning

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via the site's verified Tanzil data (`src/data/quran.json`) — 25:2, 54:49, 15:19.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 54:49, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-49-sora-54.html; the current page's Ibn Kathir quotation on 25:2 (not re-read for this run).
- Simon Friederich, "Fine-Tuning", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (first published 22 August 2017; substantively revised 26 February 2026), https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fine-tuning/.
- Simon Friederich, "Reconsidering the inverse gambler's fallacy charge against the fine-tuning argument for the multiverse", *Journal for General Philosophy of Science* 50 (2019), pp. 29–41, abstract, https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/16785.

### In brief
25:2 says God "has created each thing and determined it with [precise] determination": the verse speaks of a world made to measure.

### Verse note
"He to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and who has not taken a son and has not had a partner in dominion and has created each thing and determined it with [precise] determination" (25:2), the second verse of Sura al-Furqān, Meccan. The verse is about God's sole lordship: no son, no partner, and everything made and measured by Him.

"Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (54:49), Meccan, near the end of Sura al-Qamar, after warnings to those who deny the Hour.

"And the earth - We have spread it and cast therein firmly set mountains and caused to grow therein [something] of every well-balanced thing" (15:19), Meccan.

- **Key words.** *Qaddarahu taqdīran* (25:2): "determined it with [precise] determination", from *qadr*, measure. *Bi-qadar* (54:49): "with predestination" — the same root, meaning both measure and decree. *Mawzūn* (15:19): "well-balanced", literally "weighed".
- **What the verses say.** That everything is made with a measure, and that this measure is God's. They do not mention physical constants.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 25:2.** "everything apart from Him is created and subject to Him" (as quoted on the current page).

**Ibn Kathir on 54:49.** He reads the verse as a statement about decree, not about physics: "They used this Ayah and similar Ayat and Hadiths to refute the Qadariyyah sect, who started their sect during the latter time of the Companions." He cites a report from Abu Hurayra (ra), attributed on the page to Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah: "The idolators of the Quraysh came to the Messenger of Allah… arguing with him and discounting the Qadar", after which the verses were revealed. (The *Qadariyya* were an early group who denied that God decrees human acts.)

**Al-Jalālayn on 54:49.** Its comment is grammatical: "everything read inna kulla shay'in is in the accusative". (This page's explanation, not al-Jalālayn's: the accusative supports reading "We created everything with a measure" rather than "everything that We created is with a measure".)

**Where they agree, and what they did not say.** Both read *qadar* as God's measuring and decree over all things. Neither speaks of constants of nature or of a life-permitting universe; that reading is modern.

### How it works
- **What "fine-tuning" means.** [Established, as a description] Physicists have found that some constants of nature and initial conditions of the universe sit in ranges that permit life, and that small changes would, on current models, make life impossible. The SEP lists fine-tuned constants, conditions of the early universe, and laws (Friederich).
- **The most famous case.** [Established] The *cosmological constant* (the energy of empty space, which drives cosmic expansion): a naive estimate from quantum field theory overshoots the observed value "by a huge margin", between 10^50 and 10^123 depending on assumptions, "about 120 orders of magnitude" (SEP). Steven Weinberg (1987) argued that galaxies could form only if the vacuum energy was no more than a few orders of magnitude above its observed value (SEP).
- **Three responses.** [Debated]
  1. *Design:* life-permitting constants are more probable if there is a designer (Bayesian versions in the SEP; Sober and others object that observation selection effects undercut this, Kotzen and Weisberg reply).
  2. *Multiverse:* if there are very many universes with varying constants, at least one would permit life, and observers would find themselves in it.
  3. *Brute fact or deeper law:* the constants may be fixed by a deeper theory not yet known.
- **Problems with the probabilities.** [Debated] McGrew, McGrew and Vestrup (2001) argue that if a constant can take any real value, a uniform probability gives zero probability to any finite range, so "how unlikely" the life-permitting range is cannot be properly defined (SEP).
- **Problems with the multiverse inference.** [Debated] Ian Hacking (1987) charged that inferring many universes from our fine-tuned one commits the "inverse gambler's fallacy" (inferring many past rolls from one lucky roll); Roger White (2000) and Manson and Thrush (2003) pressed related points; McGrath, Epstein and others dispute this (SEP). Friederich concludes that "established standards of rationality may not allow us to decide whether the inference from fine-tuning to a multiverse is fallacious or not".

### Points of awe
- The naive quantum estimate of the energy of empty space is wrong by about 120 orders of magnitude, and the observed value sits in a range compatible with galaxies (SEP).
- The constants of nature are measurable at all: the universe can be described in mathematics, and its numbers can be compared to what life requires.

### The reflection
The Quran says God "created each thing and determined it with [precise] determination" (25:2). The Arabic word is *qadr*, measure. Things are not just made; they are made to a measure.

Physicists today talk about measure too. Some of nature's numbers sit in narrow ranges that allow stars, chemistry and life. What that means is argued over: a designer, many universes, or a deeper law we do not yet know. This page does not claim that the science proves God; the honest notes show why the argument is contested.

The reflection is simpler than the debate. The world we live in has a measure, and that measure can be read. We can write down the strength of gravity and the energy of empty space, and compare them with what life needs. For the commentators, *qadar* meant that nothing escapes God's measuring and decree. For a reader today, it can also mean that the world is intelligible: built in a way a mind can follow.

A believer can hold both: the measure in the verse, and the honesty of the debate. Neither needs the other to be overstated. The fitting response to a measured world is to measure carefully, and to remember who set the measure.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** Fine-tuning is often presented as proof that God exists, and 25:2 or 54:49 as having foretold it. Neither is warranted. The verses speak of measure and decree; the commentators read them as about God's decree (Ibn Kathir uses 54:49 against the Qadariyya), not about physical constants.
- **The science is real; the inference is debated.** That some constants are life-permitting only in narrow ranges is widely accepted. Whether this points to design, a multiverse or a deeper law is a philosophical question, and the probability calculations behind each are disputed (SEP).
- **Weinberg's view.** The current page says Weinberg judged some cases "not so fine". That quotation was not found in the SEP text read for this run (see Corrections).

### Questions readers ask next
**Does fine-tuning prove God exists?** No. It is one of the stronger pointers in modern design arguments, but philosophers dispute the probabilities and the alternatives (multiverse, deeper law). The SEP sets out both sides.

**What does *qadar* mean in 54:49?** Measure and decree. Ibn Kathir reads the verse as affirming God's decree over all things, against the Qadariyya who denied it.

**What is the "inverse gambler's fallacy"?** Inferring many earlier trials from one surprising result — like assuming many dice were thrown before because the roll you saw was a double six. Hacking argued the multiverse inference makes this mistake; others dispute it.

**Is the multiverse a scientific theory?** Some versions follow from theories such as inflation, but there is no direct evidence for other universes; the SEP treats the inference to them as debated.

## S10 — Birds held in the sky

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 16:79, 67:19.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 16:79, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-79-sora-16.html, and on 67:19, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-19-sora-67.html.
- Guinness World Records, "The record-breaking bird that flew from Alaska to Australia without stopping" (January 2023), https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/the-record-breaking-bird-that-flew-from-alaska-to-australia-731576.

### In brief
16:79 and 67:19 ask us to look up: birds hang in the air, wings spread or folded, and "None holds them up except Allah."

### Verse note
"Do they not see the birds controlled in the atmosphere of the sky? None holds them up except Allah. Indeed in that are signs for a people who believe" (16:79), Meccan. It comes in a passage listing God's gifts: hearing, sight and hearts (16:78), then homes and tents (16:80).

"Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and [sometimes] folded in? None holds them [aloft] except the Most Merciful. Indeed He is, of all things, Seeing" (67:19), Meccan. It follows warnings that God could cause the earth to swallow people or send a storm of stones (67:16–17): the same God who could withhold safety holds the birds up.

- **Key words.** *Musakhkharāt* ("controlled", made subservient). *Jaww al-samāʾ* ("the atmosphere of the sky"). *Ṣāffāt* ("outspread") and *yaqbiḍna* ("folded in"). *Mā yumsikuhunna illā Allāh*: "none holds them up except Allah". In 67:19 the name used is *al-Raḥmān*, "the Most Merciful".

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 16:79.** "They are held up only by Him, it is He Who gave them the strength to do that, subjecting the air to carry them and support them."

**Ibn Kathir on 67:19.** "sometimes they spread their wings out in the wind ( gliding )", and at other times they flap.

**Al-Jalālayn on 16:79.** "Nothing holds them when they draw in their wings or open them lest they fall except God by His power."

**Al-Jalālayn on 67:19.** "Nothing sustains them from falling either when they are spreading them or closing them".

**Where they differ.** Ibn Kathir names the means: God gave the birds "strength" and subjected "the air to carry them". Al-Jalālayn names only God's power. Neither denies that the air holds the bird; Ibn Kathir says it in so many words. For him, the means is part of what God does.

### How it works
- **"Wings outspread" — gliding.** [Established] A spread wing deflects the air flowing past it downward; by Newton's third law, the air pushes the wing up. The pressure above the wing is also lower than below it. Together these produce *lift*. Soaring birds use rising columns of warm air (thermals) to gain height without flapping. (No named aerodynamics source was read for this run; this is textbook physics — see Gaps.)
- **"Folded in" — flapping and diving.** [Established] Flapping adds forward thrust; folding the wings reduces drag for diving or bounding flight.
- **How far the air can carry a bird.** [Established] On 13 October 2022, a bar-tailed godwit fitted with satellite tag 234684, "This five-month-old bird", left Alaska and landed at Ansons Bay in eastern Tasmania after flying "13,560 kilometres (8,435 miles)", in "11 days and one hour without the bird landing once", "without stopping for food or rest". Eric Woehler of BirdLife Tasmania estimated it lost "half or more of its body weight" on the way (Guinness World Records). It had never made the journey before.

### Points of awe
- A five-month-old godwit flew 13,560 km without landing, on a route it had never flown (Guinness World Records).
- It flew day and night for 11 days, losing perhaps half its body weight (Guinness World Records, quoting Eric Woehler).

### The reflection
Look up, the verses say: "Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and [sometimes] folded in?" (67:19). We see birds every day, and we stop seeing them. The Quran asks us to look again at something we take for granted: a living body, heavier than air, held up in the sky.

The commentators did not pretend the air does nothing. Ibn Kathir says God holds the birds up by "subjecting the air to carry them and support them". The physics of lift is part of the sign, not a rival to it. Al-Jalālayn simply says: nothing holds them, spread or folded, "except God by His power".

A young godwit that had never crossed an ocean flew from Alaska to Tasmania without landing. It was carried by air it did not make, steered by senses it did not design, and kept alive by fat its body had stored. In 67:19 the name used is *al-Raḥmān*, the Most Merciful. The verses before it warn that the ground could open and the sky could rain stones. Instead, the birds are held up.

The verse invites a double look: see the means clearly, and then see through them to the mercy that keeps them working.

### Honest notes
- **No aerodynamics in the verse.** The verses describe what anyone can see: birds gliding and flapping, held in the air. Claims that they "predicted" lift go beyond the words, and the commentators did not read them that way.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about these verses was read for this run.
- **Distance figures.** The current page mentions "Later models put the distance at about 13,391–13,560 km". The Guinness article read here gives 13,560 km and does not mention a recalculation (see Corrections).

### Questions readers ask next
**If air and wings keep birds up, why does the verse say God does?** The commentators did not see these as rivals. Ibn Kathir: God gave them strength and subjected "the air to carry them". The means are how the holding happens.

**What do "outspread" and "folded in" mean?** Gliding with wings spread, and flapping or folding them. Ibn Kathir describes both.

**How did the godwit know where to go?** The source read does not explain how it navigated, and no study of bird navigation was read for this run.

**Why is the name "the Most Merciful" used in 67:19?** The verse follows warnings of what God could send (67:16–17); holding the birds up is presented as mercy rather than punishment.

## S11 — The camel

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 88:17–20.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 88:17–20, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-17-sora-88.html; the current page's longer Ibn Kathir quotation (not re-read in full for this run).
- "Camel", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel.
- Ali, Abu Damir, Adem et al., "Effects of long-term dehydration on stress markers, blood parameters, and tissue morphology in the dromedary camel (*Camelus dromedarius*)", *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* 10:1236425 (2023), https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fvets.2023.1236425/full.

### In brief
"Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created?" (88:17): the Quran asks desert people to look closely at the animal they knew best.

### Verse note
"Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created? And at the sky - how it is raised? And at the mountains - how they are erected? And at the earth - how it is spread out?" (88:17–20), Meccan, in Sura al-Ghāshiya, after descriptions of the Hereafter (88:1–16).

- **Key words.** *Ibil*: camels. *Kayfa khuliqat*: "how they are created" — the question is about the *how*, the making.
- **The four signs.** Camel, sky, mountains, earth: everything a traveller in the desert sees, from near to far, from below to above.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 88:17.** "Indeed it is an amazing creation, and the way it has been fashioned is strange." The current page quotes his fuller description: it "is extremely powerful and strong, yet gentle, carrying heavy loads. It allows itself to be guided by a weak rider."

**Ibn Kathir on the four signs together.** The sky: "how Allah raised it in such magnificence above the ground". The mountains: "firmly affixed so that the earth does not sway with its dwellers". The earth: "how it has been spread out, extended and made smooth". His summary: "He directs the bedouin to consider what he himself witnesses." He also cites the report of Dimam ibn Thaʿlaba, a Bedouin who asked the Prophet (pbuh), among other questions, "Who erected these mountains and placed in them whatever is in them?"

**Al-Jalālayn on 88:17.** "Will they the Meccan disbelievers not consider by way of reflection the camels how they are created?" — naming the first audience and the kind of looking asked for: reflection.

**What they did not say.** Neither commentary describes camel physiology. The point for both is to look at the familiar, and from it to the Maker.

### How it works
Each point is tied to the verse's "how they are created".
- **The hump.** [Established] "The humps in fact are reservoirs of fatty tissue, which can be used as a reserve source of calories, not water" (*Wikipedia*). In dehydrated camels, hump size shrinks (Ali et al. 2023).
- **Surviving water loss.** [Established] "Camels can withstand losing 25% of their body weight in water", while most other mammals tolerate only about 12–14% before circulatory failure (*Wikipedia*). In a 2023 study, camels deprived of water for 20 days lost 31% of their initial body weight, from a mean of 363.6 kg to 251.8 kg (Ali et al.).
- **Drinking it back.** [Established] Dehydrated camels "can drink up to 110 liters of water within 10 min to restore one-third of their body weight loss", and store "the water for up to 24 h in the gut with slow absorption", so the blood is not suddenly diluted (Ali et al., Introduction).
- **Red blood cells.** [Established] "camels' red blood cells are oval rather than circular in shape" (*Wikipedia*), and "The RBCs of the dehydrated camels can swell up to 240% of the initial volume without rupture", against about 150% in other species (Ali et al.).
- **Saving water.** [Established] Body temperature ranges from "34 °C (93 °F) at dawn" to "40 °C (104 °F) by sunset" (*Wikipedia*), so the camel stores heat by day instead of sweating it away. "Camel urine comes out as a thick syrup, and camel faeces are so dry that they do not require drying when used to fuel fires" (*Wikipedia*).

### Points of awe
- A camel can lose a quarter of its body weight in water and survive (*Wikipedia*).
- It can drink up to 110 litres in ten minutes (Ali et al. 2023).
- Its red blood cells can swell to 240% of their volume without bursting (Ali et al. 2023).

### The reflection
The verse begins with the most ordinary thing in its listeners' world: "Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created?" (88:17). Not a rare creature, not a distant star, but the animal that carried their goods and gave them milk. Ibn Kathir notices the paradox: it is "extremely powerful and strong, yet gentle", and "allows itself to be guided by a weak rider".

Then the gaze widens: the sky raised above, the mountains set firm, the earth spread out. In Ibn Kathir's words, God "directs the bedouin to consider what he himself witnesses". Nothing in these verses needed a telescope.

Today we can add what the first listeners could not see: a body that stores heat instead of sweating, blood cells that swell without bursting, a gut that holds a hundred litres and releases it slowly. These details do not replace the verse's point; they deepen it. The ordinary is more extraordinary than we think.

The lesson reaches beyond camels. Each of us lives among things we stopped seeing long ago — the faces at home, the water in the tap, our own breathing. The verse asks for a habit: to look again, slowly, and to ask how it was made.

### Honest notes
- **No physiology in the verse.** The verse asks people to look; it does not describe the camel's kidneys or blood. The science on this page is modern and kept separate.
- **A figure to avoid.** Claims such as "200 litres in 3 minutes" circulate without solid sources (the current page's note). The sources read give "as much as 30 imperial gallons (140 litres) at a time" (*Wikipedia*) and "up to 110 liters of water within 10 min" (Ali et al.).
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about 88:17 was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does the hump store water?** No. It stores fat (*Wikipedia*).

**Why the camel, of all animals?** Because it was the animal the first listeners knew best. Ibn Kathir: God "directs the bedouin to consider what he himself witnesses".

**Why does the verse go on to the sky, mountains and earth?** It moves from the near to the far, through everything a desert traveller sees, asking the same question of each: how was it made?

**How much can a camel drink?** Up to about 110 litres in ten minutes after severe dehydration (Ali et al. 2023); *Wikipedia* gives up to 140 litres at a time.

## S12 — The spider's house

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 29:41.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 29:41, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-41-sora-29.html.
- Blackledge, Kuntner & Agnarsson, "Bioprospecting Finds the Toughest Biological Material: Extraordinary Silk from a Giant Riverine Orb Spider", *PLoS ONE* 5(9) (2010), abstract via University of Akron IdeaExchange, https://ideaexchange.uakron.edu/biology_ideas/119.
- "The Quran on Spider Webs (part 2 of 2)", IslamReligion.com (14 November 2016), https://tel.islamreligion.com/en/articles/11086/viewall — a popular modern claim.
- "Spider Web Miracle in the Quran", WikiIslam, https://www.wikiislam.net/wiki/Spider_Web_Miracle_in_the_Quran — a critic's reply.

### In brief
29:41 compares relying on anything other than God to "the home of the spider" — and calls it "the weakest of homes".

### Verse note
"The example of those who take allies other than Allah is like that of the spider who takes a home. And indeed, the weakest of homes is the home of the spider, if they only knew" (29:41), from Sura al-ʿAnkabūt ("The Spider"), usually counted Meccan. It comes after accounts of earlier peoples who were destroyed despite their power (29:38–40).

- **Key words.** *Awliyāʾ*: allies, protectors. *Ittakhadhat baytan*: "takes a home"; the verb is grammatically feminine. *Awhan al-buyūt*: "the weakest of homes".
- **What the verse is about.** It is a parable (*mathal*): not a statement about spiders, but about people who rely on false protectors.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** The idolaters "were like the house of a spider, which is so weak and frail". The current page also quotes him: like "a person who holds on to a spider's web, who does not gain any benefit from that".

**Al-Jalālayn.** The spider's home "neither protects it from heat nor cold".

**Where they agree.** Both read the weakness as practical: the web gives no shelter. Ibn Kathir stresses the uselessness of holding on to it; al-Jalālayn names what it fails to keep out. Neither discusses which sex of spider builds webs, or spider family life.

### How it works
- **The silk is strong.** [Established] The silk of Darwin's bark spider (*Caerostris darwini*, Madagascar) is the toughest biological material measured: "The toughness of forcibly silked fibers averages 350 MJ/m3, with some samples reaching 520 MJ/m3", "more than twice tougher than any previously described silk" and over ten times tougher than Kevlar (Blackledge, Kuntner & Agnarsson 2010). Its webs cover up to 2.8 m², anchored by threads up to 25 m long across rivers.
- **The home is weak.** [Established] A web is a thin net, built to catch prey. It does not keep out wind, rain, heat, cold or predators. (This is ordinary observation; no named source on web function was read for this run.)
- **So both are true.** The material is extraordinary; the shelter is not. That is exactly the gap the parable uses.

### Points of awe
- Darwin's bark spider makes silk with toughness up to 520 MJ/m³, more than twice any other known silk (Blackledge et al. 2010).
- Its web can be anchored across a river by threads 25 m long (Blackledge et al. 2010).

### The reflection
Spider silk is, strand for strand, one of the toughest materials known. And yet the verse says: "the weakest of homes is the home of the spider, if they only knew" (29:41). Both are true. The point is not that the silk is weak. It is that something cleverly made can still give no real shelter.

Al-Jalālayn names what the web cannot do: it "neither protects it from heat nor cold". Ibn Kathir names the people the parable is about: those who rely on protectors other than God are like someone holding on to a spider's web.

The parable is not only about idols of stone. Anything we lean on in God's place can become a spider's house: money, status, a person, our own cleverness. These things may be impressive, even beautiful. They may hold for a while. But when the storm comes — illness, loss, death — they do not hold us up.

The verse ends with a quiet regret: "if they only knew". It is not mockery. It is an invitation to check what we are leaning on before we need it to hold.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claims.** IslamReligion.com presents the verse as describing spider family life — "Females killing and eating males", spiderlings eating each other, mothers eaten by their young — and says God "mentioned that the worst household is that of a spider, long before naturalists and scientists started to study spider behavior in detail" (2016). Others argue that the feminine verb shows only female spiders build webs (as reported by WikiIslam).
- **Weighing them.** The verse's point, as both commentators read it, is the web's weakness as a shelter, not spider behaviour; the "household" reading goes beyond the text and the tafsir read here. On the feminine verb, the critic at WikiIslam points out that the grammatical gender follows the noun *ʿankabūt*, which Lane's lexicon describes as "generally fem., but sometimes masc.", and that male spiders also spin silk. The grammar does not settle biology. Sexual cannibalism occurs in some species, not all (the current page's note; no source read for this run).
- **"Takes", not "builds".** Saheeh renders *ittakhadhat* as "takes": the same verb as "those who take allies". The parallel is the point.

### Questions readers ask next
**If spider silk is so strong, why is the spider's house the weakest?** Because a web is a trap, not a shelter: it keeps out neither heat nor cold (al-Jalālayn). Strength of material is not strength of refuge.

**Is the verse about female spiders?** The verb is grammatically feminine, but that follows the noun's grammatical gender; the commentators read here do not draw a biological point from it.

**Who are "those who take allies other than Allah"?** In context, those who worshipped idols; Ibn Kathir reads the verse about them. The lesson extends to anything relied on in God's place.

**What does *awhan* mean?** "Weakest", the superlative of weak.

## S13 — Sleep as a sign, and souls "taken" in sleep

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 30:23, 78:9, 39:42.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 39:42, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-42-sora-39.html.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6320 (sunnah.com), https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6320; Sahih al-Bukhari 6312, https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6312.
- NIH/NINDS news release (via ScienceDaily, 18 October 2013) on Xie, Kang, Xu et al., "Sleep Drives Metabolite Clearance from the Adult Brain", *Science* 342(6156):373 (2013), https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/10/131017144636.htm.

### In brief
39:42 says God "takes the souls" in death and also in sleep, and "releases the others for a specified term": every night is a small rehearsal of death and return.

### Verse note
"Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die [He takes] during their sleep. Then He keeps those for which He has decreed death and releases the others for a specified term. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought" (39:42), Meccan, in Sura al-Zumar, in a passage on God's sole power and the futility of other intercessors (39:43–44).

"And of His signs is your sleep by night and day and your seeking of His bounty. Indeed in that are signs for a people who listen" (30:23), Meccan.

"And made your sleep [a means for] rest" (78:9), Meccan. *Subāt* comes from a root meaning to cut off or to pause.

- **Key word.** *Yatawaffā* ("takes"): to take in full; the same verb is used of death.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 39:42.** God causes people to "die a lesser death" when they sleep, as against the "greater death" when the angels take souls from bodies. He links it to 6:60–61: "Here Allah mentions the two deaths, the lesser death then the greater death after it."

**The bedtime prayer.** Ibn Kathir cites, from al-Bukhari and Muslim through Abu Hurayra (ra), the supplication: "if You take my soul, have mercy on it, and if You send it back, protect it." On sunnah.com, {hadith:bukhari:6320} (Abu Hurayra) gives the Prophet's (pbuh) instruction to shake out one's bed, "for he does not know what has come on to it after him", and the Arabic: "Bismika Rabbi Wada`tu Janbi wa bika arfa'uhu, In amsakta nafsi farhamha wa in arsaltaha fahfazha bima tahfazu bihi ibadakas-salihin." In {hadith:bukhari:6312} (Hudhayfa), the bedtime words are "Bismika amutu wa ahya" (literally, "In Your name I die and live"), and on waking, "Al-hamdu li l-lahil-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilaihin-nushur" — praise to God who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the rising.

**Al-Jalālayn on 39:42.** God takes those who have not died "in their sleep in other words He takes them during sleep."

**What they share.** Sleep is a "taking" of the soul and a return; each morning's waking is a small resurrection. Neither commentary describes what the brain does in sleep.

### How it works
Each point is tied to the verse's words "rest" (78:9) and "sign" (30:23).
- **The brain cleans itself during sleep — in mice.** [Moderate: animal evidence] In a study led by Maiken Nedergaard (University of Rochester), published in *Science* in 2013, "The space between brain cells increased by 60% when mice were asleep or anesthetized"; dye injected into the cerebrospinal fluid flowed quickly through the brains of unconscious mice but barely moved when they were awake; and labelled beta-amyloid (a protein linked to Alzheimer's disease) "cleared faster" from the brains of sleeping mice (NIH/NINDS release on Xie et al. 2013). The fluid system involved is called the *glymphatic system*.
- **What this does and does not show.** Every experiment in the 2013 study was done in mice. How large this effect is in humans was not addressed in the source read.

### Points of awe
- In sleeping mice, the spaces between brain cells widened by about 60%, letting fluid wash through (NIH/NINDS, 2013).
- Every night a person gives up control, awareness and self-defence, and most mornings all of it is given back.

### The reflection
Sleep is a daily, unchosen surrender. However strong or busy we are, each night we lie down, let go of control, and become unaware of everything — even of ourselves. And most mornings it is all given back.

The Quran calls this a "taking": "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die [He takes] during their sleep" (39:42). Ibn Kathir calls sleep "a lesser death". The Prophet (pbuh) taught words for both ends of the night: before sleep, "In Your name I die and live"; on waking, praise to the One who "gave us life after causing us to die".

That makes every bedtime a small act of trust, and every morning a small resurrection. It also makes waking a gift, not a right: "He keeps those for which He has decreed death and releases the others for a specified term."

Today we know that sleep is busy work for the body — in mice, the brain even makes room to rinse itself. That does not replace the verse's sign; it adds to it. We do not run our own rest. We are put to sleep, and we are woken. The verse ends: "Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought."

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** It is sometimes said that the Quran "predicted the glymphatic system". It did not: the verses speak of sleep as rest, as a sign and as a "taking" of the soul, not of brain fluid. No named source making the claim was read for this run.
- **Mouse evidence.** The 2013 glymphatic findings are from mice. The current page says "Human evidence is growing"; no human study was read for this run (see Gaps).
- **Sleep stages and the body clock.** The current page's points on ~90-minute sleep cycles, memory consolidation and light setting the body clock are not tied to a source on the page, and no source on them was read for this run (see Corrections).

### Questions readers ask next
**What does "the lesser death" mean?** Ibn Kathir's term for sleep: the soul is "taken" and then released, as against the "greater death" in which it is kept.

**What did the Prophet (pbuh) say before sleeping?** "Bismika amutu wa ahya" — "In Your name I die and live" ({hadith:bukhari:6312}); and, after shaking out his bed, a prayer asking mercy for the soul if God keeps it and protection if He sends it back ({hadith:bukhari:6320}).

**Does sleep clean the brain?** In mice, sleep widened the spaces between brain cells by about 60% and sped up waste clearance (Xie et al. 2013). How much this happens in humans is still being studied.

**Is sleep in Islam a kind of death?** The Quran uses the same verb, "take", for both. Ibn Kathir calls sleep a lesser death; it is not death, since the soul is released.

## S14 — Shadows extending and contracting

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 25:45–46.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 25:45–46, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-45-sora-25.html.
- "Asr prayer", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asr — the schools' definitions of the start of ʿAṣr.
- Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 149, via tohed.com, https://en.tohed.com/hadith/tirmidhi/149, with al-Albani's grade.

### In brief
25:45–46 asks us to watch a shadow: God "extends the shadow", could have made it still, made the sun its "indication", and then draws it in "for a brief grasp".

### Verse note
"Have you not considered your Lord - how He extends the shadow, and if He willed, He could have made it stationary? Then We made the sun for it an indication. Then We hold it in hand for a brief grasp" (25:45–46), Meccan, in Sura al-Furqān, followed by night as a covering and sleep as rest (25:47, not quoted from verified data).

- **Key words.** *Madda al-ẓill*: "extends the shadow". *Sākinan*: "stationary". *Dalīlan*: "an indication", a guide. *Qabḍan yasīran*: "a brief grasp", a gentle drawing-in.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** The shadow refers to "the period from the beginning of the dawn until the sun rises", a view he attributes to Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn ʿUmar, Mujahid, Qatada "and others". On "the sun its guide", he reports Qatada and al-Suddi: the sun is a guide that follows the shade until the shade disappears. On the "gradual withdrawal", he says it "refers to the shade".

**Al-Jalālayn.** The shadow lasts "from the point of daybreak to the point of sunrise."

**What they share.** For both, the extended shadow is the soft half-light before sunrise, which the rising sun then draws in. Neither reads the verse as describing the Earth's rotation.

### How it works
Each point is tied to the verse's words "extends", "stationary", "the sun… an indication" and "a brief grasp".
- **"The sun for it an indication".** [Established] A shadow's length is set by the sun's height in the sky: the length equals the object's height divided by the tangent of the sun's elevation angle. When the sun is low, shadows are long; when it is high, short. (Standard geometry; no named source was read for this run.)
- **"Extends… then… a brief grasp".** [Established] Shadows are longest at sunrise and sunset and shortest at local noon, so in the morning they shrink, steadily and gradually.
- **"If He willed, He could have made it stationary".** [Established] Shadows move because the Earth turns. A world that did not turn relative to the sun would have fixed shadows and, on one side, endless day.
- **Shadows as a clock in Islamic law.** [Established] The start of the afternoon prayer (ʿAṣr) is defined by shadows. For the Maliki, Shafiʿi and Hanbali schools it begins when "the length of any object's shadow equals the length of the object itself plus the length of that object's shadow at noon"; in "the dominant opinion in the Hanafi school", when the shadow "is twice the length of the object plus the length of that object's shadow at noon" (*Wikipedia*, "Asr prayer"). The basis is the hadith in which Ibn ʿAbbas (ra) reports that the Prophet (pbuh) said Jibril led him in prayer twice: once he "prayed Asr when everything was similar (to the length of) its shadow", and on the second day "when the shadow of everything was about twice as long as it" (Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 149; al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*). The schools differ on which end of this window marks the start.

### Points of awe
- A stick in the ground is a clock: its shadow tells the time of the afternoon prayer (al-Tirmidhi 149; *Wikipedia*).
- The shadow before sunrise, which the commentators saw in this verse, is drawn in gently, not snapped away: "a brief grasp".

### The reflection
"Have you not considered your Lord - how He extends the shadow" (25:45). The verse asks us to look at something we never look at. A shadow is the most ordinary thing in the world. It lies across the ground in the early morning, and then, without a sound, it shrinks.

The commentators thought of the soft shade before sunrise, when the whole world lies in shadow and the light is still gentle. Then the sun comes up, and the shadow is drawn in — "a brief grasp", slowly, not all at once.

The verse adds a phrase that changes everything: "if He willed, He could have made it stationary". A shadow that never moved would mean a sky that never changed: no morning, no evening, no way to tell time. Muslims still use shadows to know when to pray the afternoon prayer. The moving shadow is a clock given to everyone, free, everywhere.

There is also something for the heart here. Change in our lives often comes like this: not suddenly, but a little at a time, the way a shadow shortens. The verse invites us to notice the gentleness of that change, and the One who sets its measure.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** It is sometimes claimed that the verse "reveals Earth's rotation". The verse describes what anyone can see. Classical scholars read it within their own picture of the world, and that reading also fits the observation. No named source for the claim was read for this run.
- **The qibla and the overhead sun.** The current page says that twice a year in Mecca the noon sun is overhead and that Muslims historically used this to find the qibla. No source for this was read for this run (see Corrections).

### Questions readers ask next
**What shadow is the verse about?** Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn: the shade "from the beginning of the dawn until the sun rises".

**Why does it say God "could have made it stationary"?** Because the shadow's movement is not necessary; it depends on the sun and the Earth's motion. A still shadow would mean an unchanging sky.

**How are shadows used to time prayers?** ʿAṣr begins when a shadow equals the object's length plus its noon shadow (Maliki, Shafiʿi, Hanbali), or twice the length (the dominant Hanafi view), based on the hadith of Jibril (al-Tirmidhi 149).

**What does "a brief grasp" mean?** The gentle, gradual drawing-in of the shadow as the sun rises; Ibn Kathir says it "refers to the shade".

## S15 — Rain reviving dead earth

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 41:39.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 41:39, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-39-sora-41.html.
- Jessica Hill (Associated Press), "Wildflowers are blooming in the driest place in North America — but not for long", OPB (10 March 2026), https://www.opb.org/article/2026/03/10/wildflowers-are-blooming-in-the-driest-place-in-north-america-but-not-for-long.

### In brief
41:39 points to dry, still earth that "quivers and grows" when rain comes, and draws the conclusion: "He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead."

### Verse note
"And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and grows. Indeed, He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead. Indeed, He is over all things competent" (41:39), Meccan, in Sura Fuṣṣilat, in a run of signs (41:37–39) after the call not to prostrate to the sun or moon but to their Creator.

- **Key words.** *Khāshiʿa* ("stilled"): lowered, humbled, still; the same word describes a humble heart or lowered eyes. *Ihtazzat* ("quivers"): stirs, moves. *Rabat* ("grows"): swells, rises.
- **What the verse argues.** From something anyone sees — dry land turning green — to something no one sees: the raising of the dead.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "Stilled" "means, lifeless, with nothing growing in it; it is dead." When rain comes, "it brings forth all kinds of crops and fruits". And the last part "means, signs of His ability to bring the dead back to life."

**Al-Jalālayn.** "you see the earth desolate dried out without any vegetation"; when the rain falls, "it stirs it moves and swells swells and rises."

**What they share.** Both read the verse as an argument for resurrection from the yearly revival of the land. Al-Jalālayn's "stirs… swells… rises" follows the Arabic verbs closely.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "the earth stilled" and "it quivers and grows".
- **Waiting seeds.** [Established] In Death Valley, the driest place in North America, long-dormant seeds buried in the soil burst through after rain; desert plants "can go decades without water" while waiting for conditions to germinate (AP, 2026). The "stilled" earth is not empty; it is waiting.
- **The 2026 bloom.** [Established] After "nearly a year's worth of rain since October", including the wettest November on record (1.76 inches, 4.47 cm, according to the National Park Service), Death Valley had its best bloom since 2016; such blooms come about once a decade (AP, quoting ecologists Loralee Larios of UC Riverside and Tiffany Pereira of the Desert Research Institute).

### Points of awe
- Seeds in Death Valley's soil can wait for years — the AP report says plants can go "decades" — and then bloom after one wet season (AP, 2026).
- A single wet winter can turn the driest place in North America into fields of flowers (AP, 2026).

### The reflection
Most of us have seen it: a field brown and cracked in summer, and the same field green a few weeks after the rains. It is so ordinary that we forget to be amazed. The Quran does not forget. "And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and grows" (41:39).

The word for "stilled", *khāshiʿa*, is also used for a humble heart. The earth lies low, quiet, waiting. It has no power to green itself. Then the rain comes, and the verbs move: it stirs, it swells, it rises.

Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn see the conclusion the verse draws: "Indeed, He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead." The revival of the land is a picture of resurrection, repeated every year in every landscape, for anyone who looks.

Death Valley showed it this year. Ground that looked completely dead held seeds that had waited for years. One wet season, and the desert flowered. What seems finished may only be waiting for a command.

There is a lesson for the heart as well. A life that feels dry and still is not therefore dead. It too may be waiting for its rain.

### Honest notes
- **An analogy, not botany.** The verse uses the revival of the earth as an argument for resurrection; it makes no claim about seed banks or germination inhibitors.
- **Figures on the current page.** The page says seeds carry "germination inhibitors that only a soaking rain washes away" and that "Death Valley averages a little under 2 inches (about 50 mm)" of rain a year. Neither was found in the source read for this run (see Corrections).
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about this verse was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does *khāshiʿa* mean?** Still, lowered, humbled. Ibn Kathir: "lifeless, with nothing growing in it". The same word is used for a humble heart.

**How does rain "revive" earth?** It wakes seeds that have waited in the soil, sometimes for years, as in Death Valley in 2026 (AP).

**Is this verse about resurrection?** Yes: its last sentence says so, and Ibn Kathir reads it as "signs of His ability to bring the dead back to life."

**What is a superbloom?** A mass flowering of desert plants after unusually good rains; Death Valley's 2026 bloom was its best since 2016 (AP).

## S16 — Mountains as pegs

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 78:6–7, 16:15, 79:32, 15:19, 88:19.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 78:6–7, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-7-sora-78.html, and on 16:15, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-15-sora-16.html; Ibn Kathir on 88:19, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-17-sora-88.html.
- "Isostasy", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isostasy.
- USGS, *Seismicity of the Earth 1900–2010: Himalaya and vicinity*, Open-File Report 2010-1083-J (2013, revised 2014), https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20101083J.
- "Allah created the mountains stable to stabilise the earth and prevent it from moving and shaking", IslamQA, Question 223428, https://islamqa.info/en/answers/223428 — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
"Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7): the verse pictures mountains as the pegs of a tent, holding the earth steady as a home.

### Verse note
"Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7), Meccan, the start of a list of signs in Sura al-Nabaʾ (78:6–16) offered as evidence for the Day of Judgement.

"And He has cast into the earth firmly set mountains, lest it shift with you, and [made] rivers and roads, that you may be guided" (16:15), Meccan.

"And the mountains He set firmly" (79:32), Meccan.

- **Key words.** *Mihād* (78:6): a bed, a resting place. *Awtād* (78:7): tent pegs. *Rawāsiya* (16:15): firmly set, anchored. *An tamīda bikum*: "lest it shift with you".
- **The image.** A tent is pitched on open ground; its pegs hold it down so it does not move in the wind. The verses use that picture for the earth as a home for people.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 78:6–7.** The earth is "an established, firm and peaceful resting place that is subservient to them." The mountains: "He made them as pegs for the earth to hold it in place, make it stable and firm", "so that it may be suitable for dwelling and not quake with those who are in it."

**Al-Jalālayn on 78:7.** The mountains are pegs with which "the earth is tied down like tents are tied down with pegs."

**Ibn Kathir on 16:15.** The mountains "keep it from shaking in such a manner that the creatures dwelling on it would not be able to live."

**Al-Jalālayn on 16:15.** "lest it should shake move with you".

**Ibn Kathir on 88:19.** The mountains "are firmly affixed so that the earth does not sway with its dwellers."

**What they share, and what they did not say.** All read the mountains as giving the earth stability as a dwelling place, so that it does not move "with" its inhabitants. Ibn Kathir's phrase "would not be able to live" frames this as habitability. Neither commentary mentions mountain roots or plate tectonics.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "stakes" and "firmly set".
- **Mountains have roots.** [Established] *Isostasy* is "the state of gravitational equilibrium between Earth's crust (or lithosphere) and mantle": the crust "floats" at a height set by its thickness and density (*Wikipedia*). In the simple Airy model, mountain "roots will be about five times deeper than the height of the mountains", so an 8 km mountain would have a root about 32 km deep (*Wikipedia*). Crust in continental collision zones can reach about 80 km, against about 40 km for average continental crust (*Wikipedia*).
- **How the roots were found.** [Established] Plumb lines near the Andes and in India were pulled sideways by mountains "less than expected"; in 1855 George Airy and John Henry Pratt each proposed an explanation: lighter roots beneath the mountains (*Wikipedia*).
- **But mountain belts are earthquake zones.** [Established] The Himalaya formed, and is still forming, as the India and Eurasia plates converge "at a relative rate of 40–50 mm/yr"; "Northward underthrusting of India beneath Eurasia generates numerous earthquakes", making the region "one of the most seismically hazardous regions on Earth" (USGS). The same collisions that raise mountains cause earthquakes.

### Points of awe
- Beneath a high mountain, the crust may reach about 80 km deep, double the average (*Wikipedia*).
- In the Airy model, the hidden root is about five times deeper than the mountain is tall (*Wikipedia*).
- India is still pushing into Asia by 40–50 mm a year (USGS).

### The reflection
The verse speaks of something everyone in a desert camp knew: a tent stands because of its pegs. "Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7). The earth is pictured as a home pitched for us, held down so it does not blow away.

Ibn Kathir draws the point: the earth was made "suitable for dwelling". Al-Jalālayn keeps the picture: the earth "is tied down like tents are tied down with pegs". The verses are about a home made steady enough to live in.

Modern geology adds something the first listeners could not see. Mountains do have roots: most of a mountain's mass lies hidden below, balancing what rises above. A peg is mostly underground too.

But the verse is not a lesson in seismology, and it should not be made into one. Mountain belts are where many earthquakes happen. The verse's own promise is wider: that the ground under our feet, on the scale of a human life, is a place where we can build, plant and sleep. We rarely think of that as a gift until it shakes. The verse asks us to notice it while it is still.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** IslamQA writes: "Geological research proves that the mountains are fixed firmly in the earth and that they stabilise the earth", and "The roots of the mountains beneath the surface of the earth are several times deeper than the height of that which appears above the surface" (Question 223428).
- **Weighing it.**
  - *Roots:* true, and the Airy model's "about five times" supports "several times deeper" (*Wikipedia*).
  - *"Stabilise the earth":* not in the sense of preventing earthquakes. Mountain belts such as the Himalaya are among "the most seismically hazardous regions on Earth" (USGS), because the forces that build mountains also cause quakes. The verse does not say mountains stop earthquakes.
  - *"Proves":* this site does not use the word. The fair statement: the "peg" image is apt — mountains are anchored by deep roots — and the verse's point is the earth's stability as a home, which the commentators read as habitability.
- **The textbook claim.** The current page notes that the textbook *Earth* by Press and Siever is often said to "confirm the Quran", and that its authors made no such claim. Neither the textbook nor a source on this was read for this run.
- **Classical cosmology.** The current page says some classical reports describe the earth as at first moving until God fixed it with mountains. No such report appeared on the Ibn Kathir pages read for 16:15 and 78:7 (see Corrections).

### Questions readers ask next
**Do mountains really have roots?** Yes. Under isostasy, high mountains are balanced by thick roots of lighter crust; in the Airy model, about five times deeper than the mountain is tall (*Wikipedia*).

**Do mountains prevent earthquakes?** No. Mountain belts are earthquake zones, because the same plate collisions build mountains and cause quakes (USGS).

**Then what does "lest it shift with you" mean?** The commentators read it as the earth being made a stable home: so that it does "not quake with those who are in it" and its creatures can live (Ibn Kathir).

**What are *awtād*?** Tent pegs. Al-Jalālayn: the earth "is tied down like tents are tied down with pegs."
