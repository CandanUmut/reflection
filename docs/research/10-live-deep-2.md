## M6 — Forgiveness and anger

### Sources read
- Quran 3:134, 24:22, 42:40 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 3231, 4025, 6114; Muslim 2588.
- E. L. Worthington et al. / N. G. Wade et al. (2014), meta-analysis of forgiveness interventions (Δ = 0.56), and a 2022 *Child Development* meta-analysis (anger, g = 0.29).

### In brief
The Quran praises those "who restrain anger and who pardon the people" (3:134), and it keeps the right to fair redress (42:40). Forgiveness is a gift the wronged may give, never something forced out of them.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Restraining anger and pardoning.** The righteous are "who restrain anger and who pardon the people — and Allah loves the doers of good" (3:134). Two steps: first hold the anger, then, if you can, let the wrong go.

**Justice is kept, pardon is preferred.** "The retribution for an evil act is an evil one like it, but whoever pardons and makes reconciliation — his reward is [due] from Allah. Indeed, He does not like wrongdoers" (42:40). The verse allows a fair, equal response; it does not demand that a victim give up their right. It then offers something better than retribution, and its reward.

**Forgiveness that costs something.** When Abu Bakr (ra) swore to stop supporting his poor relative Mistah, who had spread slander about Aisha (ra), the verse came: "Let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you?" (24:22). Abu Bakr (ra) resumed his support {hadith:bukhari:4025}.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Real strength.** "The strong is not the one who overcomes the people by his strength, but the strong is the one who controls himself while in anger" {hadith:bukhari:6114}.

**Forgiving raises you.** "No one forgives another except that Allah increases his honor" {hadith:muslim:2588}.

**Ta'if.** The Prophet (pbuh) called the day at Ta'if, when he was rejected and stoned, harder than Uhud. The Angel of the Mountains offered to crush the town; he refused, hoping that their descendants would worship God {hadith:bukhari:3231}.

#### practice — In daily life
**A short path.** When anger rises: pause before speaking {hadith:bukhari:6114}; name the wrong honestly; decide whether to seek redress (42:40) or to pardon (3:134). Forgiveness does not mean trusting the person again at once, nor staying in harm.

#### research — What research shows
**Forgiveness can be learned.** A meta-analysis of forgiveness interventions (Wade et al., 2014) found that they increase forgiveness (Δ = 0.56) and reduce depression and anxiety. A 2022 meta-analysis in *Child Development* found reduced anger (g = 0.29). [Established, moderate]

#### heart — For the one who cannot forgive yet
**You are not failing.** 42:40 keeps your right to justice; forgiveness is offered, not demanded. Some wrongs take years. Pray for the strength to let go, and protect yourself in the meantime. Forgiving is between you and God; reconciling also needs the other person to change.

### Questions readers ask next
**Do I have to forgive someone who hasn't apologised?** The Quran praises pardon (3:134) and keeps the right to redress (42:40); it does not make forgiveness a duty imposed on the victim.

**Does forgiving mean forgetting or reconciling?** No. Forgiving lets go of the claim to revenge; reconciliation (42:40, "makes reconciliation") is a further step that needs both people.

**Is anger always wrong?** The hadith praises controlling anger {hadith:bukhari:6114}, not never feeling it.

### Honest limits
"Forgive" is sometimes demanded of victims, especially of abuse, to protect abusers. That contradicts 42:40, which preserves the right to justice. The research shows moderate effects in studies, not a guarantee for every person.

## M7 — Dignity and diversity

### Sources read
- Quran 5:32, 17:70, 49:13 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Muslim 2564.
- T. F. Pettigrew, L. R. Tropp, "A meta-analytic test of intergroup contact theory", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 90(5):751–783 (2006), abstract (PubMed 16737372).

### In brief
"We have certainly honored the children of Adam" (17:70): dignity belongs to every human being. Differences of people and tribe are "that you may know one another" (49:13), and rank before God is by righteousness alone.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Honour is given to all.** "We have certainly honored the children of Adam and carried them on the land and sea and provided for them of the good things" (17:70). The verse says "children of Adam", not believers only.

**Difference is for knowing each other.** "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you" (49:13). Race, nation and family give no rank; righteousness does, and only God sees it fully.

**One life, all of humanity.** "Whoever kills a soul unless for a soul or for corruption [done] in the land — it is as if he had slain mankind entirely. And whoever saves one — it is as if he had saved mankind entirely" (5:32).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**God looks at hearts.** "Allah does not look to your bodies nor to your faces but He looks to your hearts" {hadith:muslim:2564}.

#### research — What research shows
**Meeting people reduces prejudice.** A meta-analysis of 515 studies (713 samples) found that intergroup contact typically reduces prejudice; the effect generalises to the whole group and is not explained by publication bias (Pettigrew and Tropp, 2006). [Established] It supports the Quran's "that you may know one another" (49:13) as practical advice.

#### practice — In daily life
**Know one another.** Eat with, work beside, and learn from people of other backgrounds. Check your jokes and words for contempt (see the page on speech). In a mosque or community, ask who is missing or unwelcome.

#### heart — For the one who has been looked down on
**Your worth is not given by others.** 17:70 gives honour to every child of Adam, and 49:13 measures people only by what God sees in the heart {hadith:muslim:2564}. Racism or sectarian contempt among Muslims goes against both.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "most righteous" make Muslims superior?** The verse speaks of righteousness, which only God judges (49:13); it gives no group a right to look down on another.

**Does 5:32 apply to non-Muslims?** It speaks of "a soul" and "mankind entirely" (5:32).

**What about the "right hands possess" and other unequal rules?** Classical law did contain inequalities (for example slavery, see the question on slavery); this page states the principle of 17:70 and 49:13, and the hard questions are treated there.

### Honest limits
Racism and sectarianism exist in Muslim communities, against the teaching of 49:13. Classical law also had inequalities that modern readers find hard; they are discussed on the Ask pages, not hidden here.

## M8 — Wealth and charity

### Sources read
- Quran 2:261, 2:280, 9:34, 9:60, 59:7, 70:24–25, 83:1 (Saheeh International).
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English, Quran.com), on 59:7 and 9:34.
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 1417, 1427, 1454, 2397, 6436; Muslim 1563, 1598, 1605, 2588; Tirmidhi 1209, 1315.
- International Islamic Fiqh Academy (OIC), Resolution 10 (10/2), 1985, on bank interest; Al-Azhar Islamic Research Academy, statement of 2002 on fixed bank returns.
- maqasid (al-Ghazali, al-Shatibi, Ibn 'Ashur); al-Ghazali on miserliness; Curry et al. (2018); Dunn, Aknin and Norton (2008); waqf and hisba; El-Gamal (2006).

### In brief
Wealth is a trust: it must be earned fairly, shared — zakat is the poor's right, not a favour — and kept moving, "so that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich from among you" (59:7).

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Giving grows.** "The example of those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah is like a seed [of grain] which grows seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains" (2:261).

**Fair trade.** "Woe to those who give less [than due]" (83:1).

**Wealth must circulate.** Distribution is ordered "so that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich from among you" (59:7). In its context, this verse is about *fay'*: property that came to the Muslims without fighting, as with the Banu al-Nadir, which the Prophet (pbuh) spent on the poor and on public causes. Ibn Kathir explains the aim: "so that the wealth does not remain among the wealthy, who would spend it as they wish and desire and give none of it to the poor." Scholars draw a general principle from it: wealth should not circulate only among the rich.

**Hoarding.** "Those who hoard gold and silver and spend it not in the way of Allah — give them tidings of a painful punishment" (9:34). The verse begins by criticising religious leaders who take people's wealth unjustly; its warning then covers anyone who hoards and does not pay what is due on their wealth.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Giving does not shrink wealth.** "Charity does not decrease wealth" {hadith:muslim:2588}. "Save yourself from Hell-fire even by giving half a date-fruit in charity" {hadith:bukhari:1417}. "The upper hand is better than the lower hand", and giving starts with one's dependants {hadith:bukhari:1427}.

**Greed has no end.** "If the son of Adam had two valleys of money, he would wish for a third" {hadith:bukhari:6436}.

**Honest trade.** Finding wet grain hidden under dry in a market pile, the Prophet (pbuh) said: "Whoever cheats is not one of us" {hadith:tirmidhi:1315}. Hoarding goods to raise prices is a sin {hadith:muslim:1605}, and the one who consumes usury (*riba*), pays it, records it or witnesses it is cursed {hadith:muslim:1598}. The often-quoted "honest, trustworthy merchant is with the prophets…" ({hadith:tirmidhi:1209}) is graded weak by most graders, so it is not used here as evidence.

#### fiqh — Zakat in brief
**Who pays, how much, to whom.** Zakat is due once a year on savings above a minimum amount (*nisab*). On silver, the written instructions Abu Bakr (ra) sent with Anas (ra) say: "For silver the Zakat is one-fortieth of the lot (i.e. 2.5%), and if its value is less than two-hundred Dirhams, Zakat is not required" {hadith:bukhari:1454}. Today the *nisab* is usually worked out from the value of gold or silver, and the four schools differ in some details (for example on jewellery). The Quran names the eight groups who receive it: "the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler" (9:60).

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Wealth is one of the law's aims.** Protecting wealth (*hifz al-mal*) is one of the five classical objectives (*maqasid*) of the law in al-Ghazali and al-Shatibi; the modern scholar Ibn 'Ashur adds that wealth must circulate and be handled clearly and fairly. Al-Ghazali, in the *Ihya'*, treats greed as a disease of the heart and generosity as its cure. On *riba*, scholars agree on the ban but debate how it applies to modern bank interest. Most scholars and fiqh councils hold that bank interest is *riba*: the International Islamic Fiqh Academy of the OIC ruled in 1985 (Resolution 10) that any fixed increase on a loan is forbidden *riba*. A minority view, given by Al-Azhar's Islamic Research Academy in 2002, held fixed returns from bank investment to be permissible, treating the bank as an investment agent rather than a lender. [Debated in its details]

#### history — History
**Endowments built cities.** Across Islamic history, endowments (*waqf*) funded hospitals, schools, fountains and soup kitchens, and the market inspector (*muhtasib*) checked weights and fraud.

#### research — What research shows
**Giving helps the giver, a little.** A meta-analysis of 27 experiments found that acts of kindness raise the giver's wellbeing (d ≈ 0.28; Curry et al., 2018). Studies of "prosocial spending" (Dunn et al., 2008) mostly replicate, with smaller effects than first reported. [Established, small]

#### practice — In daily life
**Simple steps.** Give regularly, even a little {hadith:bukhari:1417}. Pay zakat as a right, not a favour. Never hide defects in what you sell {hadith:tirmidhi:1315}. Before buying more, remember the third valley {hadith:bukhari:6436}.

#### heart — If you are the one in need
**No shame in receiving.** The Quran calls what the poor receive "a known right" (70:24) "for the petitioner and the deprived" (70:25). Zakat is the poor's share by God's order, not a favour from the rich, and "those in debt" are one of its eight groups (9:60).

**If you are in debt.** The Quran tells lenders: "if someone is in hardship, then [let there be] postponement until [a time of] ease" (2:280). The Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever wants God to save him from the torments of the Day of Resurrection "should give respite to the insolvent or remit (his debt)" {hadith:muslim:1563}. He himself often prayed for protection "from being in debt", because debt pushes a person to break promises {hadith:bukhari:2397}. Need and debt are hardships to be eased, not a mark against you.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is all bank interest *riba*?** Most fiqh councils, including the International Islamic Fiqh Academy (1985), say yes; a minority, including Al-Azhar's Islamic Research Academy (2002), allowed fixed returns on bank investment. [Debated]

**What is zakat?** An obligatory yearly 2.5% of savings above the *nisab*, given to the eight groups the Quran names (9:60); see "Zakat in brief" above.

**Does charity really not decrease wealth?** The hadith says so {hadith:muslim:2588}; scholars understand it as blessing and reward, not as an accounting promise.

### Honest limits
Much modern "Islamic finance" has been criticised by Muslim economists (for example Mahmoud El-Gamal) for copying interest-based products under new names. Zakat collection is weak in many countries, and inequality in several Muslim-majority countries is high. Islamic economics has not been proven to work better at scale; what is shown here is its principles and its record.

## M9 — Trust and honesty

### Sources read
- Quran 4:58, 8:27, 17:34, 23:8, 33:72 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 33, 6094, 6496; Tirmidhi 1315.
- Knack and Keefer (1997); Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index.

### In brief
*Amana* — trust — covers what others leave with you, your word, your office, and the whole responsibility of being human (33:72). The Prophet (pbuh) was known as "the trustworthy" before his prophethood.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Return trusts, judge justly.** "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice" (4:58).

**Do not betray.** "Do not betray Allah and the Messenger or betray your trusts while you know [the consequence]" (8:27). Believers are those "who are to their trusts and their promises attentive" (23:8), and "fulfill [every] commitment. Indeed, the commitment is ever [that about which one will be] questioned" (17:34).

**The trust humans carried.** God offered the trust to the heavens, the earth and the mountains, and they declined; the human being took it on (33:72). Commentators read it broadly: property, secrets, office, one's own body and abilities.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Three signs of a hypocrite.** "Whenever he speaks, he tells a lie. Whenever he promises, he always breaks it. If you trust him, he proves to be dishonest" {hadith:bukhari:33}.

**When trust is lost.** "When honesty is lost, then wait for the Hour." Asked how it is lost, he said: "When authority is given to those who do not deserve it" {hadith:bukhari:6496}.

**Truth builds character** {hadith:bukhari:6094}; cheating breaks community {hadith:tirmidhi:1315}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Narrow exceptions only.** Al-Ghazali lists lying among the calamities of the tongue and allows only narrow exceptions — reconciling people, in war, and between spouses — which scholars themselves debate. [Debated]

#### research — What research shows
**Trust is wealth.** Countries and regions with higher general trust tend to have better economic growth and institutions (Knack and Keefer, 1997, and later work). [Established, correlational] Corruption harms growth, public services and trust. [Established]

#### practice — In daily life
**Keep small promises.** Arrive when you said you would; return what you borrowed; keep secrets; give positions to the qualified, not to friends {hadith:bukhari:6496}. At work, an honest report is part of *amana*.

#### heart — For the one who was betrayed
**Betrayal is serious.** The Quran and the Prophet (pbuh) treat it as a mark of hypocrisy {hadith:bukhari:33}. It is right to protect yourself and seek fair redress (see the page on forgiveness).

### Questions readers ask next
**Is any lie ever allowed?** Scholars mention narrow exceptions, debated among them; the rule is truthfulness {hadith:bukhari:6094}.

**Is a promise to a non-Muslim binding?** The verses on promises and trusts are general (4:58; 17:34).

**What counts as *amana* at work?** Honest reports, fair hiring, protecting what you are entrusted with (4:58; {hadith:bukhari:6496}).

### Honest limits
Many Muslim-majority countries score in the lower half of Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index. The distance between "the trustworthy" and much of public life today is one of the reasons young people give for doubt.

## M10 — Orphans, the poor, travellers and neighbours

### Sources read
- Quran 2:177, 4:36, 93:9, 107:1 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 5304, 5353, 6014, 6016.
- al-Qurtubi on 4:36; al-Ghazali on neighbours' rights; Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton (2010); the Bucharest Early Intervention Project.

### In brief
The Quran describes faith in social terms: denying the Judgment looks like pushing away the orphan (107:1–2). Care reaches out in rings — parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, near and far neighbours, the traveller (4:36).

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**The circle of care.** "Worship Allah and associate nothing with Him, and to parents do good, and to relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbor, the neighbor farther away, the companion at your side, the traveler" (4:36).

**Righteousness is giving what you love.** True righteousness includes giving wealth "in spite of love for it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask [for help]" (2:177).

**Faith shows in how you treat the weak.** "So as for the orphan, do not oppress [him]" (93:9). "Have you seen the one who denies the Recompense?" (107:1) — the sura answers: the one who pushes away the orphan and does not urge feeding the poor.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Next to him in Paradise.** "I and the one who looks after an orphan will be like this in Paradise", showing his middle and index fingers {hadith:bukhari:5304}.

**Caring for widows and the poor.** "The one who looks after a widow or a poor person is like a Mujahid who fights for Allah's Cause, or like him who performs prayers all the night and fasts all the day" {hadith:bukhari:5353}.

**Neighbours.** "Gabriel continued to recommend me about treating the neighbors kindly and politely so much so that I thought he would order me to make them as my heirs" {hadith:bukhari:6014}. And three times: "By Allah, he does not believe" — the one whose neighbour is not safe from his harm {hadith:bukhari:6016}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Neighbours, widely.** Al-Qurtubi, on 4:36, reports a wide definition of neighbour (some said forty houses in each direction) and includes non-Muslim neighbours in the right. Al-Ghazali lists neighbours' rights: greeting, visiting the sick, consoling, sharing food, covering faults.

#### research — What research shows
**Connection keeps people alive.** A meta-analysis of 148 studies (about 308,000 people) found that strong social relationships were linked to about 50% higher odds of survival over the study periods (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010). [Established, correlational] Children raised in large institutions show developmental delays compared with family-based care (Bucharest Early Intervention Project). [Established]

#### practice — In daily life
**Start next door.** Learn your neighbours' names; share food; check on the elderly; sponsor an orphan through a trusted programme; welcome travellers and newcomers (4:36).

#### heart — For the one who is alone
**You are named in these verses.** The orphan, the needy, the traveller and the stranger are the people the Quran asks others to care for (4:36). If you are alone, reaching out for help is not weakness; it is what the community owes you.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "neighbour" include non-Muslims?** Al-Qurtubi includes them, on 4:36.

**What is *kafala*?** Sponsoring an orphan who keeps their own name and lineage. It differs from adoption as practised in many countries; it is described here neutrally, not as simply "better".

**What does "those whom your right hands possess" in 4:36 mean?** It refers to slaves in the society of the time; slavery and the Quran are discussed honestly in the question on slavery.

### Honest limits
Neighbour relationships in modern cities are often thin. Orphan and refugee care in Muslim-majority countries ranges from strong programmes to serious neglect.
