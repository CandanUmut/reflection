## S1 — The seed and the date stone split open

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via the site's verified Tanzil data (`src/data/quran.json`) — 6:95, 56:63–64, 41:39.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 6:95, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-95-sora-6.html.
- UF/IFAS (University of Florida), "Germination", https://propg.ifas.ufl.edu/04-seeds/03-germination/04-seedsgermination-germination.html — the three phases of water uptake.
- Gong, Bassel, Wang, Greenwood & Bewley, "The Emergence of Embryos from Hard Seeds…", *Annals of Botany* 96:1165–1173 (2005), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247068 — as cited on the current page (not re-read for this run).
- National Geographic, "2,000 year old 'Phoenix' seed rises from the ashes", https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/2000-year-old-phoenix-seed-rises-from-the-ashes — the Masada seed (pointer to Sallon et al., *Science*, 2008).
- Phys.org, "Scientists grow date palm plants from 2,000-year-old seeds" (2020), https://phys.org/news/2020-02-scientists-date-palm-year-old-seeds.amp — the 2020 *Science Advances* study (pointer to Sallon et al. 2020; the paper itself could not be opened).

### In brief
"Indeed, Allah is the cleaver of grain and date seeds" (6:95): the verse asks us to look at the moment a hard, dry seed splits open and something alive comes out of it.

### Verse note
"Indeed, Allah is the cleaver of grain and date seeds. He brings the living out of the dead and brings the dead out of the living. That is Allah; so how are you deluded?" (6:95).

- **Key words.** *Fāliq* ("cleaver", the one who splits) comes from *falaqa*, to split. *Al-ḥabb* is grain; *al-nawā* is the stone of the date. The verse names two seeds: the small soft grain and the famously hard date stone.
- **Setting.** Sura al-Anʿām is Meccan. The verses that follow (6:96–99) continue the same argument from nature: the dawn, the sun and moon, the stars, rain and fruit. The passage addresses people who worshipped other gods: who, it asks, actually makes these things happen?
- **The second passage.** "And have you seen that [seed] which you sow? Is it you who makes it grow, or are We the grower?" (56:63–64), Meccan, from a series of questions in Sura al-Wāqiʿa about food, water and fire. The farmer sows; the verse asks who makes the seed grow.
- **A related verse.** The Quran uses the reviving of dead land as an image of resurrection: "Indeed, He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead" (41:39).

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir (the 14th-century Damascene scholar).** God "causes the seed grain and the fruit stone to split and sprout in the ground", producing many kinds of grain and fruit. He explains the next phrase by the plant itself: "He brings the living plant from the seed grain and the fruit stone, which is a lifless and inanimate object" (his translator's spelling). He then reports two other readings, without naming who held them: "He brings the egg from the chicken, and the opposite", and "Others said that it means, He brings the wicked offspring from the righteous parent and the opposite."

**Al-Jalālayn (a short classical commentary by Jalal al-Din al-Mahalli and Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti).** "God it is Who splits the grain from the plants and the date-stone from the palm-trees." On the living and the dead: "He brings forth the living from the dead such as the human being from the sperm and the bird from the egg", and the reverse, "the sperm and the egg from the living".

**Where they differ.** Ibn Kathir reads "the living out of the dead" first of all as the plant from the seed, because the sentence before it is about seeds. Al-Jalālayn's examples are animal and human: the bird from the egg, the human from the sperm. A third reading, reported by Ibn Kathir, is moral: a righteous child from a wicked parent. None of them treats the seed as literally dead in a biological sense; for them, "dead" means what shows no sign of life.

**What they did not say.** Neither commentary read here describes how a seed germinates. Their point is the argument the verse itself makes: "That is Allah; so how are you deluded?" (6:95).

### How it works
- **A seed is dormant, not dead.** [Established] A dry seed looks lifeless but is alive: as the next points show, it can wait for years and still grow when water reaches it.
- **Splitting begins with water.** [Established] UF/IFAS describes three phases. "Early seed germination begins with imbibition of water": the dry seed soaks up water and swells. Then comes "a period where there is little water uptake called the lag phase". Finally "water uptake drives the emergence of the radicle": the embryonic root breaks through the seed coat.
- **The hard date stone.** [Established] (2005): the date's stored food is a carbohydrate (galactomannan) laid down in very thick cell walls, which is why the stone is so hard; it is broken down mainly after the root has emerged. (This study has not yet been consulted again.)
- **How long a seed can wait.** [Established for the one seed; Moderate for the general point] A date seed excavated at Masada in the 1960s, then stored at room temperature for about four decades, was planted in 2005 by a team led by Sarah Sallon (Hadassah Medical Organisation), and cared for by Elaine Solowey. Fragments of its own shell were radiocarbon-dated to about 2,000 years old, "give or take about 50 years" (National Geographic, reporting Sallon et al., *Science*, 2008). Sallon put its survival down to Masada's hot, dry climate, which limited damage from free radicals, "a major force in seed ageing".
- **Not a single freak.** By 2020, seven ancient date seeds from three sites in the Judean desert, between about 1,800 and 2,400 years old, had sprouted. Thirty-five seeds had been planted in all (Sallon et al., *Science Advances*, 2020; Gros-Balthazard et al., *PNAS*, 2021). The paper itself was not opened.

### Points of awe
- A seed can wait at least two thousand years and still split when water reaches it (Sallon et al. 2008, via National Geographic).
- The first visible sign of life is the root, not the shoot: "water uptake drives the emergence of the radicle" (UF/IFAS).
- Seven palms have now grown from ancient Judean seeds (Phys.org, 2020).

### The reflection
Most of us have never watched a seed split. It happens underground, out of sight, in every field and garden. The verse stops us at that moment: "Indeed, Allah is the cleaver of grain and date seeds" (6:95). It chooses two seeds, the small grain and the hard date stone, as if to say: soft or hard, it is not the seed that opens itself.

Then it widens the view: "He brings the living out of the dead." The commentators heard many things in this line: the plant from the seed, the bird from the egg, a wicked child from a righteous parent and the reverse. All of them are about life appearing where we did not expect it.

Sura al-Wāqiʿa asks the farmer directly: "Is it you who makes it grow, or are We the grower?" (56:64). The farmer's work is real; it is not the whole story. Elsewhere the Quran draws the conclusion for us: the One who gives life to dead earth "is the Giver of Life to the dead" (41:39).

A seed that waited two thousand years and then answered water is a small, true picture of that hope. The question the verse leaves with us is its own: "so how are you deluded?"

### Honest notes
- **No prediction is claimed.** The verse describes something every farmer could see. It makes no claim about dormancy, hormones or seed longevity, and the commentators did not read it that way.
- **"Dead" is not a biological term here.** A dormant seed is alive. The verse speaks of how things look to us, and the commentators read "dead" in that sense, or morally.
- **Dates are approximate.** The Masada seed is about 2,000 years old, ± about 50 years. The 2020 seeds span roughly 1,800 to 2,400 years.
- **Popular claim.** No widespread "scientific miracle" claim about 6:95 was found; the page does not need one.

### Questions readers ask next
**Did classical scholars know how germination works?** Not in the modern sense. Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn read the verse as a sign of God's power to bring life from what looks lifeless. The biology on this page is modern and kept in its own layer.

**Why a date stone?** The verse names both grain and *nawā*, the date stone. The date stone is famously hard; a 2,000-year-old one has germinated (see "How it works").

**What does "He brings the living out of the dead" mean?** Ibn Kathir reads it first as the plant from the seed; al-Jalālayn as the human from the sperm and the bird from the egg; a reading reported by Ibn Kathir applies it to a righteous child born to a wicked parent.

**Is this verse about resurrection?** Not directly, but the Quran links new life in the earth to resurrection elsewhere: "He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead" (41:39).

## S2 — Milk "from between digested food and blood"

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 16:66.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 16:66, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-66-sora-16.html.
- "Anatomy of the Mammary Gland", Cornell University (adapted from DeLaval material) — blood volume per litre of milk; how milk components are made, as read for this run.
- "The Formation of Milk", by Truth Seeker Staff, "Taken with slight editorial modifications from Miracles of the Qur'an website", islam.com.kw, https://www.islam.com.kw/?p=30907 — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
16:66 points to an ordinary drink and calls it "a lesson": "pure milk, palatable to drinkers", which comes "between excretion and blood".

### Verse note
"And indeed, for you in grazing livestock is a lesson. We give you drink from what is in their bellies - between excretion and blood - pure milk, palatable to drinkers" (16:66).

- **Key words.** *ʿIbra* ("lesson") is something you cross over from: you look at one thing and pass to understanding another. *Farth* ("excretion") is the contents of the gut; *dam* is blood; *labanan khāliṣan* is "pure milk"; *sāʾighan* means easy to swallow.
- **"Their bellies".** The Arabic pronoun is singular (*buṭūnihi*, "its bellies"), although it refers to plural livestock; commentators discussed this (below).
- **Setting.** Sura al-Naḥl ("The Bee") is Meccan. This verse is one of a run of signs of God's provision: rain (16:65), milk (16:66), fruits of the palm and the vine (16:67), and the bee (16:68–69).

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "A lesson" means "a sign and an evidence of the wisdom, power, mercy and kindness of the Creator". On the singular pronoun: it "refers to one cattle, or it could refer to the whole species". On how the milk arises, he describes separation "after the food has been fully digested in its stomach", after which "the milk goes to the udder".

**Al-Jalālayn.** "between the refuse the intestinal waste and the blood pure milk uncontaminated by either the refuse or the blood".

**What they share.** Both read the verse as about purity: milk comes from among things no one would drink, yet nothing of either is in it. Ibn Kathir's sentence about digestion is the closest either comes to physiology, and it is a general description, not an anatomy.

### How it works
Each point below is tied to the verse's phrase "between excretion and blood".
- **From food to blood.** [Established] Digested food is absorbed from the gut into the blood. Milk's raw materials come out of the digestive tract and travel in the blood.
- **From blood to milk.** [Established] Cornell's guide: "Milk synthesis takes place in the alveoli", tiny sacs in the udder lined with milk-making cells. These cells take what they need from the blood: long-chain fatty acids are "absorbed from the blood" (short-chain ones are made in the gland), and "Milk protein is synthesized from amino acids also with origin from the blood". Milk is not filtered blood; the cells build new substances (milk sugar, milk proteins, milk fat) from what the blood brings.
- **How much blood.** [Established, as an average] "To produce 1 liter of milk 500 ltr. of blood have to pass through the udder" (Cornell). The figure is an average used in dairy science; it varies with the cow and her yield.

### Points of awe
- About 500 litres of blood pass through the udder for each litre of milk (Cornell).
- Milk sugar, milk protein and much of milk fat are made new inside the udder's alveoli, from materials in the blood (Cornell).

### The reflection
The verse could have said simply, "We give you milk." Instead it slows down and names where milk comes from: "between excretion and blood". Those are two things people turn away from. And out of them, the verse says, comes "pure milk, palatable to drinkers" (16:66).

The commentators heard a lesson about purity. Al-Jalālayn says the milk is "uncontaminated by either the refuse or the blood"; Ibn Kathir calls the whole process "a sign and an evidence" of the Creator's wisdom and kindness. The verse itself calls it *ʿibra*, a lesson you cross over: from the glass in your hand to the One who made it possible.

There is also a lesson a reader can take inward, as a reflection rather than a reading of the verse. What is clean and good does not always come from clean and easy places. It often comes from what was mixed and difficult, when it has passed through the right process. That is true of many good lives.

The verse sits in a passage about rain, fruit and honey. Each is ordinary. Each, the sura says, is provision. The fitting response is the one the Quran asks for again and again: to notice, and to be grateful.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says that "High-nutrition milk is thus produced from blood, which cannot be consumed directly, and semi-digested food", and that this "could not have been known at the time when the Qur'an was revealed" ("The Formation of Milk", Truth Seeker Staff, on islam.com.kw, from the *Miracles of the Qur'an* website).
- **Weighing it.** The verse's words fit what physiology says: milk's raw materials come from digested food through the blood. But the claim goes further than the text. The verse does not mention absorption, circulation or milk-making cells, and neither Ibn Kathir nor al-Jalālayn read it as physiology. They read it as purity coming from between impurities. Ibn Kathir himself, centuries before modern physiology, describes milk forming "after the food has been fully digested" and going "to the udder", without treating this as hidden knowledge. The fair statement is: the verse's image is accurate and striking; it is not a hidden description of cell biology.
- **Numbers are averages.** "500 litres of blood per litre of milk" is a rule of thumb, not a constant.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does the verse describe milk production scientifically?** No. It is an image of purity coming from between impurities. The physiology is described separately, with its limits, in "How it works".

**Why does it say "its bellies" and elsewhere "their bellies"?** Ibn Kathir says the singular pronoun can refer to one animal or to the whole species.

**Is milk really made from blood?** Its raw materials come from the blood, but milk is not blood. Cells in the udder build milk sugar, protein and fat from what the blood brings (Cornell).

**What is the "lesson" (*ʿibra*)?** For Ibn Kathir, a sign of the Creator's "wisdom, power, mercy and kindness". The verse invites the reader to pass from the drink to the Giver.

## S3 — The bee and honey

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 16:68–69.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), on 16:68, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-68-sora-16.html, and on 16:69, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-69-sora-16.html; *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English) on 16:69, same page.
- Susan Collins-Smith, "How honey is made", Mississippi State University Extension, https://extension.msstate.edu/blogs/extension-for-real-life/how-honey-made (no date on the page).
- Canadian Honey Council, "How to make a pound of honey", https://honeycouncil.ca/?p=633.
- Jull et al., "Honey as a topical treatment for wounds", *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews* (2015), as read for this run.
- Systematic review and meta-analysis of honey for upper respiratory tract infections, *BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine* (14 studies; PROSPERO CRD42017067582), as read for this run — the page read did not show the authors' names, so they are not given here.

### In brief
16:68–69 says the bee was "inspired" to build, to feed and to follow its paths, and that from it comes "a drink, varying in colors, in which there is healing for people".

### Verse note
"And your Lord inspired to the bee, 'Take for yourself among the mountains, houses, and among the trees and [in] that which they construct. Then eat from all the fruits and follow the ways of your Lord laid down [for you].' There emerges from their bellies a drink, varying in colors, in which there is healing for people. Indeed in that is a sign for a people who give thought" (16:68–69).

- **Key words.** *Awḥā* ("inspired") is from *waḥy*, the word also used for revelation to prophets; here it means a guiding prompt placed in a creature. *Shifāʾ* ("healing") is indefinite in Arabic: "healing", not "the cure".
- **Feminine verbs.** The commands to the bee (*ittakhidhī*, "take"; *kulī*, "eat"; *uslukī*, "follow") are grammatically feminine. Whether this says anything about the bees' sex is unclear: Arabic collective nouns like *naḥl* commonly take feminine agreement anyway (a grammatical point not checked in a named source). It is at least consistent with the biology (below).
- **Setting.** Sura al-Naḥl is Meccan and is named after this passage. It continues the run of signs of provision in 16:65–67 (rain, milk, fruit).

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on "inspired".** "What is meant by inspiration here is guidance." The bee "is guided to make its home in the mountains, in trees and in structures erected by man". Its comb has "hexagonal shapes and interlocking forms" with "no looseness in its combs". It goes out to feed and "comes back to its hive without swerving to the right or left".

**On "the ways of your Lord".** Ibn Kathir reports two readings. Mujahid: these are routes the bees follow, "as they are easy for you". Qatada and ʿAbd al-Rahman ibn Zayd: the bee follows them "in an obedient way". Ibn Jarir al-Tabari held that both are correct.

**On honey and "healing".** Ibn Kathir describes honey as "white, yellow, red, or of other good colors, depending on the different things that the bees eat", and reads the healing as honey's: "there is a cure in honey for diseases that people suffer from". He cites the hadith of a man whose brother had stomach trouble: the Prophet (pbuh) told him to give honey, and when the man came back saying it had not helped, he replied, in Ibn Kathir's translator's wording, "Allah speaks the truth and your brother's stomach is lying" (Ibn Kathir, citing al-Bukhari and Muslim; on sunnah.com, {hadith:bukhari:5684}: "Allah has said the truth, but your brother's `Abdomen has told a lie"). The brother recovered after more honey.

**Al-Jalālayn on "healing".** Its main reading is that honey is "a cure for mankind from all ailments". It then reports other views: "it is also said that it means that it is a cure only for some ailments", "as indicated by the indefinite noun *shifāʾun*", "or that it is a cure for all ailments when supplemented with some other remedy", "or without these other remedies but with the resolve niyya to get better".

**A second view.** *Tanwīr al-Miqbās*, a commentary attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas, reads the healing as honey's ("in honey there ( is healing for mankind ) from illness"), and then reports a second view: "it is also said that this means: there is in the Qur'an healing for mankind" (surahquran.com, 16:69). Ibn Kathir does not mention this second view.

### How it works
- **"From all the fruits".** [Established] Foragers collect nectar from flowers. "Nectar can contain up to 80 percent water" (Collins-Smith, MSU Extension).
- **"From their bellies".** [Established] Nectar is carried in "the bee's crop, or honey stomach", which can hold up to a third of the bee's weight. The forager adds an enzyme, invertase, "as they swallow it", which begins splitting sucrose into simpler sugars. Back at the hive, she "regurgitates her nectar load to special food-handling bees" (MSU Extension).
- **From nectar to honey.** [Established] Bees dry the nectar by "rapidly beating or fanning their wings in the hive", which "reduces that water content to around 18 percent" (MSU Extension).
- **"Varying in colors".** [Established] Honey's colour and flavour depend on the flowers the nectar came from, as Ibn Kathir also says.
- **The foragers are female.** [Established, but not shown by the source read] Foragers are female worker bees; the MSU Extension account only implies it ("her nectar load"). A named source is needed.
- **"Healing for people": what trials show.**
  - *Burns.* [Moderate to High for this one use] A Cochrane review (Jull et al. 2015; 26 trials, 3,011 participants) found that "honey dressings heal partial thickness burns more quickly than conventional dressings", by about 4.7 days (high-certainty evidence for this comparison). But for wounds overall it was "difficult to draw overall conclusions", and most of the evidence was of low or very low certainty.
  - *Coughs and colds.* [Moderate] A meta-analysis in *BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine* (14 studies) found honey reduced cough frequency (standardised mean difference −0.36; 95% CI −0.50 to −0.21) and cough severity (−0.44; −0.64 to −0.25) compared with usual care. The authors called for placebo-controlled trials.

### Points of awe
- Over 2.6 million flower visits for one pound of honey, and about 50,000 miles of flight, on the Canadian Honey Council's calculation (which assumes at least 100 flowers per load, after Ribbands 1949).
- A forager's honey stomach can hold up to a third of her body weight (MSU Extension).
- Bees turn nectar of up to 80% water into honey of about 18% water by fanning their wings (MSU Extension).

### The reflection
The bee's work is described in the verse as obedience. It is told where to live, what to eat and which paths to follow, and it does all three. Ibn Kathir sees the result in the comb itself: hexagons with "no looseness" in them, and a forager who "comes back to its hive without swerving to the right or left".

No single bee sees the jar. The work is shared among thousands of small lives, each doing its part faithfully, and the result is "a drink, varying in colors, in which there is healing for people" (16:69). Most good in the world is made this way: by many people doing small things well, who never see the whole.

The verse also uses a careful word. It says honey contains "healing" (*shifāʾ*), not that it cures everything. Al-Jalālayn reports a view that reads the grammar this way. Modern trials point the same way: real help for some things, not a cure-all. Gratitude for a gift does not require exaggerating it.

The passage ends with an instruction to the reader, not to the bee: "Indeed in that is a sign for a people who give thought" (16:69). The bee follows its Lord's ways without choosing to. A human being is asked to think, and then to choose.

### Honest notes
- **"Healing", not "cure for everything".** The verse's *shifāʾ* is indefinite. Al-Jalālayn's English text says both "from all ailments" and "only for some ailments"; the evidence today supports benefit for some uses (partial-thickness burns; cough symptoms), uncertain benefit for others, and no evidence for honey as a general cure.
- **The popular claims.** Two are common: that the feminine verbs "prove" the Quran knew worker bees are female, and that "from their bellies" anticipated the honey stomach. Both fit the biology, but neither is decisive: the feminine grammar may simply follow the collective noun, and the commentators read here did not draw either point. No named source making these claims was read.
- **The numbers are estimates.** The Council's 2.6 million flowers and 50,000 miles are its own calculations from assumed averages.
- **A figure to drop.** No source for this was read. The Canadian Honey Council page says "about 1 and ½ teaspoons", with no source either. The two disagree by a factor of 18; the figure should be removed until a primary source is found.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "inspiration" mean the bee receives revelation like a prophet?** No. Ibn Kathir explains it as guidance. The same Arabic root covers a range of meanings, from a prophet's revelation to an instinct placed in a creature.

**Is honey a cure for everything?** The verse says honey contains "healing" (16:69), with an indefinite noun. Al-Jalālayn notes that it can mean healing for some ailments. Trials show benefit for some uses, such as burns and coughs (see "How it works").

**What is the hadith about honey and the stomach?** A man's brother had stomach trouble; the Prophet (pbuh) told him to give honey, and when it seemed not to work he said, "Allah has said the truth, but your brother's `Abdomen has told a lie" ({hadith:bukhari:5684}, narrated by Abu Saʿid al-Khudri). The brother recovered after more honey.

**Does "from their bellies" mean honey is vomit?** Nectar is carried in the crop ("the bee's crop, or honey stomach") and is passed from bee to bee. It is changed by enzymes and drying, not digested (MSU Extension).

**Why does the verse say "varying in colors"?** Honey's colour depends on the flowers, as Ibn Kathir notes: "white, yellow, red, or of other good colors".

## S4 — Creation of the human being

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 23:12–14.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 23:12–14, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-14-sora-23.html; the current page's Ibn Kathir quotation on 23:12.
- The Endowment for Human Development, "Virtual Human Embryo: Carnegie stages 10–12", https://www.ehd.org/virtual-human-embryo/ages.php?stage=11 — ages, sizes, somites, heartbeat.
- UNSW Embryology, "Carnegie stage 12", https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_12.
- Lecturio, "Development of the Limbs", https://wp-assets.lecturio.com/concepts/development-of-limbs/ — limb buds, cartilage models, origin of limb muscle.
- LifeMap Discovery, *Review of Medical Embryology*, ch. 69, "Development of the Limbs", https://discovery.lifemapsc.com/library/review-of-medical-embryology/chapter-69-development-of-the-limbs (no author shown on the page).
- "Scientists' Comments On The Qur'an", Islamic Awareness, https://islamic-awareness.org/quran/science/scientists — Keith Moore (a site that promotes the claims), as read for run 9.

### In brief
23:12–14 traces a human being from clay to a drop, a clinging thing, a lump, bones and flesh, and then says something new appears: "then We developed him into another creation".

### Verse note
"And certainly did We create man from an extract of clay. Then We placed him as a sperm-drop in a firm lodging. Then We made the sperm-drop into a clinging clot, and We made the clot into a lump [of flesh], and We made [from] the lump, bones, and We covered the bones with flesh; then We developed him into another creation. So blessed is Allah, the best of creators" (23:12–14).

- **Key words.** *Sulāla min ṭīn*: an extract of clay. *Nuṭfa*: a small drop of fluid. *Qarār makīn*: a firm, secure lodging. *ʿAlaqa*: from a root meaning to cling or hang; it can mean a clot of blood or a leech. *Muḍgha*: a small piece of flesh, the size of a chewed mouthful. *ʿIẓām*: bones. *Khalqan ākhar*: "another creation".
- **The connecting words.** The Arabic links the stages with two different words: *thumma* ("then", with a gap) and *fa-* ("and so", following on). The verse does not give durations.
- **Setting.** Sura al-Muʾminūn is Meccan. The passage follows a description of the believers (23:1–11) and leads into death and resurrection: the next verses say that after this you will die and then be raised on the Day of Resurrection (23:15–16, paraphrased; not quoted from verified data here). The stages are part of an argument for the resurrection.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 23:12.** God "initially created man from an extract of *Tin*" — that is, Adam (pbuh) — and then his descendants from a drop.

**Ibn Kathir on the stages.** The *ʿalaqa*: "Then it becomes a red clot, like an elongated clot", and he cites ʿIkrima: "This is blood." The *muḍgha*: "like a piece of flesh with no shape or features". Then: "We gave it shape, with a head, two arms and two legs, with its bones, nerves and veins", and the flesh is "something to cover it and strengthen it". On "another creation": "then We breathed the soul into it, and it moved and became a new creature". He also reports Ibn ʿAbbas: "We change it from one stage to another until it emerges as an infant."

**The forty-day hadith.** Ibn Kathir cites the report of Ibn Masʿud (ra): "The creation of anyone of you is gathered for forty days in his mother's stomach as a Nutfah", followed by the clot and the lump, and "Then the angel is sent to him and he breathes the soul into it" (Ibn Kathir's wording, from the version in Musnad Ahmad, which he says was also "recorded by Al-Bukhari and Muslim"). The English of {hadith:bukhari:3208} reads differently: "(The matter of the Creation of) a human being is put together in the womb of the mother in forty days", and "Then the soul is breathed into him" comes after the angel writes four things.

**Al-Jalālayn.** "Then We transformed the drop of semen into a clot congealed blood… into a little lump of flesh… into bones… Then We clothed the bones with flesh", and "Then We produced him as yet another creature by breathing into him Our Spirit."

**What they share, and what they did not say.** Both read *ʿalaqa* as a clot of blood, and "another creation" as the breathing of the soul. Neither reads *ʿalaqa* as "leech", and neither discusses the order in which bone and muscle form. Their use of the stages, as in the verse's setting, is to show God's power to create, and so to raise the dead.

### How it works
Each point is tied to a word of the verse.
- **"A firm lodging".** [Established] The embryo implants in the wall of the womb and grows there, protected by the mother's body. (The timing of implantation has not yet been consulted in a source.)
- **"Clinging" (*ʿalaqa*).** [Established] By the fourth week the embryo is 2–5 mm long. EHD gives Carnegie stage 10 (about 22 days, by the 1987 convention it uses) as 2–3.5 mm with 4–12 somites, and stage 12 (about 26 days) as 3–5 mm with 21–29 somites; UNSW gives stage 12 as "Week 4, 26 - 30 days" and "3 - 5 mm". At this stage it is attached to the womb wall and depends on it.
- **The heart.** [Established, with debated dating] EHD says the heart's first contractions have "long been described as a Carnegie Stage 10 or possibly a very late Stage 9 event" — about 22 days after fertilisation by its convention — while noting other studies that report 23, 25, or even 28–30 days.
- **"Lump" (*muḍgha*).** [Established] Somites are paired blocks of tissue along the embryo's back; at stages 10–12 (about 22–26 days by EHD's convention) their number rises from 4 to 29 pairs (EHD). They give the embryo a segmented look.
- **"Bones… covered with flesh".** [Moderate, on the timing] Limb buds appear in the fourth week: "Day 24: appearance of upper limb bud" and "Day 26: appearance of lower limb bud" (Lecturio). The limb tissue condenses into "Models of hyaline cartilage" (Lecturio), which later turn to bone. Limb muscle comes from cells that migrate in from the somites: "Muscle comes from myotome: ventrolateral cells originating from somites" (Lecturio). On timing, LifeMap says that "As the bones form and limbs elongate, myoblasts aggregate and form the large muscle masses". In other words, the cartilage skeleton and the muscle develop in the same weeks, overlapping; bone proper (ossification) comes later. Exact week ranges for each were not found in the sources read.

### Points of awe
- At about four weeks, an embryo of 3–5 mm already has 21–29 pairs of somites (EHD; UNSW).
- The heart begins to beat at about 22 days by one standard convention (EHD).
- Limb buds appear on about day 24 (arms) and day 26 (legs) (Lecturio).

### The reflection
These verses tell a story everyone alive has lived but no one remembers. Clay, a drop, something clinging, a lump, bones, flesh. Then a break: "then We developed him into another creation" (23:14). The commentators understood that break as the breathing of the soul, the point at which a body becomes a person.

The order of the verses is not a lecture in anatomy. Its setting shows its purpose: the very next verses speak of death and of being raised again. The argument is simple. You did not make yourself. You were brought through stages you did not choose, in a place you cannot remember, into a life you now take for granted. The One who did that once can do it again.

There is something else here for the reader. Every person you meet passed through the same stages, the same weeks of being a few millimetres long, the same dependence. No one began as more than anyone else. That is a quiet reason for humility, and for respect.

The passage does not end with an explanation. It ends with praise: "So blessed is Allah, the best of creators" (23:14). The fitting response to one's own existence may be wonder before it is analysis.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** The best-known form is Keith Moore's: a Canadian anatomist who, by his own account, worked with an embryology committee at King Abdulaziz University and produced an edition of his textbook *The Developing Human* "with Islamic Additions" (1982/83). He is quoted as saying the Quran's descriptions "cannot be based on scientific knowledge in the seventh century" (Islamic Awareness). Photo captions there describe his "Investigations in to the *'alaqa* or leech-like stage".
- **Weighing it.**
  - The words *ʿalaqa* and *muḍgha* fit the appearance of the embryo strikingly well, and that is worth noticing.
  - But the commentators read here take *ʿalaqa* as a blood clot (Ibn Kathir; ʿIkrima: "This is blood"; al-Jalālayn), not as a leech. The leech reading was not found in the classical sources read.
  - "We made [from] the lump, bones, and We covered the bones with flesh" can be read as a strict sequence: bones first, then muscle. Modern embryology shows the cartilage skeleton and muscle developing in the same weeks, overlapping (Lecturio; LifeMap). A possible reply (this page's own, not from a named source) is that "covered" describes the muscles wrapping the skeleton, not that they start later. Both readings are possible; neither is proven by the text.
  - The fair position: the verses can be read in harmony with embryology at a general level; they are not a textbook, and the commentators read here did not read them as one.
- **The forty-day hadith.** {hadith:bukhari:3208} speaks of forty-day stages. How its timings relate to modern embryology is disputed among Muslim scholars; that debate has not yet been consulted in a named source.
- The historian of science Joseph Needham, quoting the Quran's embryological verses, described them as "A seventh-century echo of Aristotle" (*A History of Embryology*, 1959) — a judgement this page weighs in its notes on 23:14.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is "clay" about Adam or about every human?** Ibn Kathir reads the extract of clay as Adam's creation, and the later stages as every human's.

**Did the Quran describe embryology before science?** Classical scholars did not read it that way: they read the stages as signs of God's power to create and to resurrect. The modern claim and its problems are set out in the honest notes.

**What does *ʿalaqa* mean — a clot or a leech?** The root means to cling or hang. Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn read it as a clot of blood; the "leech-like" reading was not found in the classical sources read.

**What does "another creation" mean?** Ibn Kathir: "then We breathed the soul into it, and it moved and became a new creature". Al-Jalālayn says the same.

**Do bones really form before muscles?** In the limbs, the cartilage model and the muscle masses develop in the same weeks, overlapping; bone proper forms later. Whether the verse means a strict sequence is a matter of reading (see the honest notes).

## S5 — Sun and moon, each in an orbit

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 36:37–40.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), on 36:38, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-38-sora-36.html, and on 36:39–40, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-40-sora-36.html; *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English) on 36:38, same page.
- *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* (English, on Quran.com; the page does not name its author), on 36:38–40, https://quran.com/36:38/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran.
- Quranpedia, English renderings of 36:38, https://quranpedia.net/tafsir/ya-sin/38/en — how different translators render *mustaqarr*.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 3199 and 4802 (sunnah.com, https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4802; hadithunlocked.com, https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:4802).
- "The correct way to describe the sun is that it 'prostrates beneath the Throne' and not that it 'sets in a spring of warm water'", IslamQA answer 176375, https://islamqa.info/en/downloads/answers/176375 — quoting al-Khattabi, *Aʿlām al-Ḥadīth Sharḥ Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, p. 1893.
- Faraz Rabbani, "Does the Hadith of the Sun's Prostration Bear on the Earth's Rotation?", SeekersGuidance (6 August 2026), https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/does-the-hadith-of-the-suns-prostration-bear-on-the-earths-rotation/ — reporting Ibn Hajar's *Fatḥ al-Bārī* and others.
- NASA StarChild, on the sidereal and synodic month (as read for this run).
- "Galactic year", *Wikipedia* (citing NASA StarChild) — the Sun's speed and orbit around the galaxy.
- "Perfect orbits in the universe", Discovering Islam Team, AboutIslam (18 January 2017), https://aboutislam.net/reading-islam/understanding-islam/quran-perfect-orbits-universe/ — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
36:37–40 describes a sky that keeps time: night drawn off the day, a sun that "runs", a moon given "phases", and "each, in an orbit, is swimming".

### Verse note
"And a sign for them is the night. We remove from it [the light of] day, so they are [left] in darkness. And the sun runs [on course] toward its stopping point. That is the determination of the Exalted in Might, the Knowing. And the moon - We have determined for it phases, until it returns [appearing] like the old date stalk. It is not allowable for the sun to reach the moon, nor does the night overtake the day, but each, in an orbit, is swimming" (36:37–40).

- **Key words.**
  - *Tajrī* ("runs"): moves continuously, as water runs.
  - *Li-mustaqarrin lahā* ("toward its stopping point"): *mustaqarr* can mean a place of rest or a time of rest (*Maʿārif*). Translators differ: "its stopping point" (Saheeh), "a resting-place" (Pickthall), "a period determined for him" (Yusuf Ali), "its fixed term" (Khattab) (Quranpedia).
  - *Manāzil* ("phases", literally "stations"): the stages through which the moon passes.
  - *Al-ʿurjūn al-qadīm* ("the old date stalk"): the dried, curved stalk of a date cluster, an image of the thin crescent.
  - *Falak* ("orbit"): a rounded course. Ibn Kathir's sources compare it to "the arc of a spinning wheel" (below).
  - *Yasbaḥūn* ("swimming"): moving smoothly, as one swims or floats.
- **Setting.** Sura Yā Sīn is Meccan. These verses follow signs on earth (dead land revived, gardens and springs, 36:33–35) and precede signs at sea (ships, 36:41–42). The passage addresses people who deny the resurrection.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on "toward its stopping point".** He gives two views. The first, place: it "refers to its fixed course of location, which is beneath the Throne". The second, time: "this refers to when the sun's appointed time comes to an end, which will be on the Day of Resurrection", with Qatada: "It has an appointed time and it will not go beyond that." He also reports that Ibn Masʿud and Ibn ʿAbbas recited the verse as "with no fixed course" (*lā mustaqarra lahā*), which he explains as "never slowing down or stopping". The same page also has his description of the Throne: "a dome supported by legs or pillars, carried by the angels, and it is above the universe", and "the Throne is the roof of creation and it is not a sphere as many astronomers claim" (surahquran.com, 36:38). That is the cosmology of his time.

**Al-Jalālayn.** "to its resting-place in other words it does not overstep it", and "its running is the ordaining of the Mighty in His kingdom the Knower of His creation".

***Maʿārif al-Qurʾān*.** The word "is used to denote place of rest as well as time of rest"; on the time view, the sun reaches "the end of its movement already determined - and that time is the Day of Doom"; on the place view, it is "a place where one orbit of the movement of the sun stands completed".

**On the moon and the orbits.** Ibn Kathir: the moon wanes "until it appears like the old dried curved date stalk", which Ibn ʿAbbas explains as the stem joining the date cluster to the tree. "Each in an orbit" means "floating, i.e., revolving, in their orbits in the heaven". He cites Ibn ʿAbbas "and others among the Salaf" (the earliest generations): "In an orbit like the arc of a spinning wheel", and lists Ibn ʿAbbas, ʿIkrima, al-Dahhak, al-Hasan, Qatada and ʿAtaʾ al-Khurasani for the "floating, revolving" reading. On "nor does the night overtake the day", Mujahid says: "They seek one another rapidly"; Ibn Kathir explains that "there is no gap between night and day; each of them follows the other with no interval".

**The hadith of the sun's prostration.** Abu Dharr (ra) reports that at sunset the Prophet (pbuh) asked him, "O Abu Dharr! Do you know where the sun sets?" and said: "It goes and prostrates underneath (Allah's) Throne", then recited 36:38 ({hadith:bukhari:4802}). In the fuller report, it "takes the permission to rise again", and a time will come when "it will be ordered to return whence it has come and so it will rise in the west" ({hadith:bukhari:3199}). Both are in Sahih al-Bukhari.

**How scholars read the prostration.** They differ, and the differences are worth stating:
- **Al-Khattabi**, *Aʿlām al-Ḥadīth* (commentary on al-Bukhari): "It cannot be denied that it halts beneath the Throne, in a manner that we cannot see or comprehend" (IslamQA's translation). For him the manner belongs to the unseen.
- **Ibn Hajar**, *Fatḥ al-Bārī*, offers several readings without insisting on one: it may be "the prostration of the angel charged with it", or the sun "prostrates by the state it is in", that is, by its submission. He reports Ibn al-ʿArabi: "Some people denied its prostration, though it is sound and possible" (quoted in Ibn Hajar, *Fatḥ al-Bārī* 6/299), while others read it as the sun's permanent state of subjection (SeekersGuidance's renderings).
- ***Maʿārif al-Qurʾān*** calls the prostration "simply a similitude in consonance with the mores of effective prophetic teaching": the sun "is nowhere but under the ʿArsh, at all times and at every place", so it "at every moment while moving on its orbit keeps offering sajdah" and "needs no pause or break".
- **Ibn Kathir** (in *al-Bidāya wa al-Nihāya*, cited by SeekersGuidance through al-Qastallani) holds that "nothing in this hadith says the sun ascends above the heavens to prostrate": it sets from our sight while continuing in its orbit.

**What the commentators did not say.** None of the commentaries read here describes the sun moving through a galaxy. Their cosmology was that of their time; Ibn Kathir reads the orbits as "in the heaven".

### How it works
Each point is tied to a phrase in the verses.
- **"We have determined for it phases" (36:39).** The Moon circles the Earth relative to the stars in 27.32 days, but new moon to new moon takes 29.53 days, because the Earth has moved along its own orbit in the meantime (NASA Moon Fact Sheet). So the Moon needs just over two more days to line up with the Sun again. Phases depend on the Sun–Earth–Moon angle, so a full cycle of phases, new moon to new moon, is the 29.5-day month. That is the length of a lunar (Islamic) month: 29 or 30 days.
- **"Until it returns [appearing] like the old date stalk" (36:39).** [Established] Before and after new moon, only a thin curved edge of the lit half faces us: the crescent the verse compares to a dried date stalk.
- **"The sun runs" (36:38).** [Established] The Sun is not fixed. It carries the solar system around the centre of our galaxy at an average speed of about "230 km/s (828,000 km/h)" (*Wikipedia*, "Galactic year", citing NASA StarChild, February 2000). Radio measurements put the Sun about 8.3 kiloparsecs (some 27,000 light-years) from the centre of the galaxy, moving at about 240 km/s (Reid et al., *Astrophysical Journal*, 2014). At that speed one circuit takes a little over 200 million years.
- **"Nor does the night overtake the day" (36:40).** [Established] Day and night follow each other as the Earth turns; at every moment it is day on one half of the Earth and night on the other.
- **The skeptic's point about the hadith.** [Established] Because of the same rotation, the Sun is always setting somewhere and rising somewhere else. (What this means for the hadith of the sun's prostration is discussed in "Honest notes", not here.)

### Points of awe
- The lunar month (29.5 days) is two days longer than the Moon's trip around the sky (27.3 days), because the Earth itself is moving (NASA StarChild).
- The Sun moves at about 230 km/s around the galaxy, one circuit in about 230 million years (NASA StarChild).
- The crescent the verse compares to a dried date stalk is the same shape whose sighting begins each Islamic month.

### The reflection
These verses describe a sky that can be trusted. Night comes off the day like a garment. The sun "runs", and does not wander. The moon grows and shrinks through its phases and comes back, thin and curved, "like the old date stalk" (36:39). Nothing overtakes anything else: "each, in an orbit, is swimming" (36:40).

The verses give a name to that order: "the determination of the Exalted in Might, the Knowing" (36:38). Muslims live inside it every day. The five prayers follow the sun. The month begins with the crescent, and so does Ramadan.

The commentators did not share our astronomy, and this page does not pretend they did. They read "stopping point" as a place beneath the Throne, or as the end of time; they disagreed about what the sun's "prostration" means. What they agreed on, and what still holds, is that the sky is obedient, regular and given.

There is a lesson for a life, too, offered here as reflection. Each light in the verse keeps to its own course and does not try to take another's place: "It is not allowable for the sun to reach the moon." A person can find peace in doing the same.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says "the Quran stated 1400 years ago that the sun and the stars moved; this proves that the Quran is the word of Allah", and that the sun moves "faster than 720.000 km an hour… toward the star Vega on an orbit called the Solar Apex" (AboutIslam, Discovering Islam Team, 2017; no source is given for the figures).
- **Weighing it.**
  - "The sun runs" fits the fact that the Sun moves through the galaxy, and a reader may notice that.
  - But the verse does not mention a galaxy, and the commentators read here did not read it that way: for them, the sun's running was its daily and yearly course across the sky.
  - People in the seventh century already saw the sun move across the sky every day; a verse saying "the sun runs" was not, to them, a hidden claim.
  - The claim also uses "proves", which this site does not. The fair statement: the verse's words are compatible with what we now know; they are not a prediction of it.
- **The hadith, stated plainly.** Bukhari 3199 and 4802 are *ṣaḥīḥ* reports in which the sun, after setting, "goes and prostrates underneath (Allah's) Throne" and asks permission to rise. A skeptic's objection is real: the sun is always rising somewhere. The answers differ. Al-Khattabi leaves the manner to the unseen. Ibn Hajar allows a figurative reading (submission, or the prostration of its angel). *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān* calls it a similitude and says the sun is always beneath the Throne, so its prostration "needs no pause or break". *Limit:* a reader who takes the hadith's words as a physical sequence will find a conflict with astronomy; these answers resolve it only by reading the prostration as non-physical or unknowable. The page shows the hadith and the readings rather than choosing for the reader.
- **The Throne passage.** Ibn Kathir calls the Throne "a dome" and "not a sphere as many astronomers claim". The quotation is correct (surahquran.com, 36:38). That is his cosmology, and the page is right to show it.

### Questions readers ask next
**Why are there 27.3 days and 29.5 days?** The Moon goes around the sky in 27.3 days, measured against the stars. But phases depend on where the Sun is, and since the Earth has moved along its own orbit, the Moon needs just over two more days to line up with the Sun again. So new moon to new moon is 29.5 days (NASA StarChild). These are the "phases" of 36:39.

**What does "the sun runs" mean?** In the verse, that the sun moves continuously on its course. Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn read "toward its stopping point" as a place beneath the Throne or a fixed term at the end of time. Astronomy adds that the Sun moves around the galaxy at about 230 km/s.

**What is a *falak*?** A rounded course or orbit. Ibn Kathir's sources compare it to "the arc of a spinning wheel" and read "swimming" as "floating, i.e., revolving, in their orbits".

**What does the sun's "prostration" mean?** Scholars differ. Al-Khattabi: it happens "in a manner that we cannot see or comprehend". Ibn Hajar: possibly its submission, or the prostration of the angel charged with it. *Maʿārif al-Qurʾān*: a similitude; the sun is always beneath the Throne and always in prostration.

**The hadith says the sun prostrates after it sets — but the sun is always rising somewhere. How can that be?** This is a real objection. The answers above read the prostration as non-physical (submission), or continuous, or beyond our knowledge. A reader may or may not find them convincing; the page shows the hadith and the readings rather than hiding them.

**Did classical scholars think the sun goes around the earth?** Their cosmology was that of their time: Ibn Kathir reads the orbits as "in the heaven" and the Throne as "a dome" over the universe. (Whether he states an Earth-centred model in so many words has not yet been checked.) This page does not claim the verse described modern astronomy.

## S6 — Water and life

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 21:30, 56:68–70.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 21:30, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-30-sora-21.html.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), on 56:68–70, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-70-sora-56.html.
- USGS Water Science School, "The Water in You: Water and the Human Body", https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/water-you-water-and-human-body.
- USGS Water Science School, "Water Density", https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-density.

### In brief
"And have you seen the water that you drink?" (56:68). These verses turn the most ordinary act of the day into a question: who brought it down, and who keeps it sweet?

### Verse note
"Have those who disbelieved not considered that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them and made from water every living thing? Then will they not believe?" (21:30), Meccan (Sura al-Anbiyāʾ).

"And have you seen the water that you drink? Is it you who brought it down from the clouds, or is it We who bring it down? If We willed, We could make it bitter, so why are you not grateful?" (56:68–70), Meccan (Sura al-Wāqiʿa).

- **Key words.** *Jaʿalnā min al-māʾi kulla shayʾin ḥayy*: "made from water every living thing". *Al-muzn* (56:69): rain-clouds. *Ujāj* (56:70): bitter, salty water that cannot be drunk.
- **Setting.** 21:30 is part of an argument addressed to those who deny God: the heavens, the earth and life itself point to their Maker. 56:68–70 is the third of four questions in Sura al-Wāqiʿa about what sustains us: the seed (56:63–64), the water (56:68–70), the fire (56:71–72), after the creation of the human being (56:58–59). Each asks who really provides it.
- **This page and S24.** The first half of 21:30 ("joined entity… separated") is discussed on S24. This page is about its second half and about drinking water.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 21:30.** "meaning, the origin of every living thing is in water". He reads the verse as an argument from what anyone can see: rain, growth and life depend on water.

**Al-Jalālayn on 21:30.** "in other words water is the cause of such things having life". Where Ibn Kathir speaks of origin, al-Jalālayn speaks of cause: life depends on water.

**Ibn Kathir on 56:68–70.** *Al-muzn* means "clouds, according to Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid and others". "Allah is stating that indeed He is the One Who causes the rain to fall". "Bitter" (*ujāj*) means "salty, sour, undrinkable and unfit for growing plants". And the question "why are you not grateful?" asks: "why do you not appreciate the favor Allah does for you by sending down the rain fresh, ready to consume".

**What they did not say.** Neither commentary reads "made from water every living thing" as a statement about the origin of life in the modern biological sense, or about the chemistry of cells.

### How it works
- **"Every living thing" and water.** [Established] "Up to 60% of the human adult body is water" (USGS). By organ: "the brain and heart are composed of 73% water, and the lungs are about 83% water"; skin is about 64%, muscles and kidneys 79%, bones 31% (USGS, citing Mitchell and others, 1945). Babies are born at about 78% water, falling to about 65% by age one (USGS, citing Dr Jeffrey Utz).
- **"Is it you who brought it down from the clouds?" (56:69).** [Established] When seawater evaporates, the salt stays behind; the vapour that forms clouds is fresh. Rain is distilled water, which is why rain is drinkable and the sea is not.
- **"We could make it bitter" (56:70).** [Established] Salt water is heavier: "ocean water, which contains dissolved salt, is denser than pure water" (USGS, "Water Density").
- **Water's unusual behaviour.** [Established] Most substances shrink when they freeze. Water does the opposite: "Upon freezing, the density of ice decreases by about 9 percent" (USGS). Water is densest at about 4 °C, so near-freezing water is lighter and stays on top, where ice forms; otherwise, USGS notes, "the lake could freeze from the bottom up". Ice floating on top insulates the water below, where fish and plants survive the winter. (This is a fact about water that supports life; the verses do not mention it.)

### Points of awe
- An adult is up to about 60% water; the lungs about 83% (USGS).
- A newborn is about 78% water (USGS).
- Ice is about 9% less dense than liquid water, so lakes freeze from the top and life survives beneath (USGS).

### The reflection
"And have you seen the water that you drink?" (56:68). We drink without looking. The verse asks us to look.

Then it asks three questions. Who brought it down from the clouds? Who keeps it sweet? And why are we not grateful? Ibn Kathir hears in the last question a reproach and an invitation: to "appreciate the favor" of rain sent down "fresh, ready to consume".

Every glass of water has a long story. It was salt water once, or ice, or a cloud. It rose, it fell, it ran through soil and stone. Somewhere along the way the salt was left behind, and what reached your glass could keep you alive. You did none of that.

21:30 widens the view: "made from water every living thing". Ibn Kathir reads it as origin, al-Jalālayn as cause. Either way, life on earth depends on something we receive and cannot make.

The verses do not ask for a lecture on chemistry. They ask for a habit: to drink, and to know that it was given. A person who notices that every day is learning gratitude, and gratitude is where the Quran wants these questions to lead.

### Honest notes
- **Not a claim about where life began.** "Made from water every living thing" is read by Ibn Kathir as "the origin of every living thing is in water" and by al-Jalālayn as water being "the cause" of life. Neither is a theory of abiogenesis (life arising from non-living matter). Modern theories that life began in water exist, but the verse does not state one.
- **The popular claim.** Popular articles often present 21:30 as anticipating the discovery that cells are mostly water. No named source making that claim was read. The dependence of all life on water was visible to anyone in the seventh century, and that is how the commentators read it.
- **Numbers.** USGS gives "up to 60%" for adults; the figure varies with age, sex and body composition (USGS: about 60% for men, 55% for women).

### Questions readers ask next
**Does the verse mean all life is made of water?** Ibn Kathir reads it as "the origin of every living thing is in water"; al-Jalālayn as water being "the cause of such things having life". The biology of water in living things is in "How it works".

**Is this verse about the Big Bang?** Its first part ("joined entity… separated") is discussed on S24. This page is about water and life.

**Why is rain fresh when the sea is salty?** When water evaporates, the salt is left behind. Clouds are made of fresh water vapour, so rain is fresh.

**What does *ujāj* ("bitter") mean?** Ibn Kathir: "salty, sour, undrinkable and unfit for growing plants".

**Why does ice float, and why does it matter?** Ice is about 9% less dense than liquid water (USGS), so lakes freeze from the top, and the ice shelters the water and the life below.

## S7 — Two seas and a barrier

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 55:19–20, 25:53.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 55:19–20, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-19-sora-55.html, and on 25:53, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-53-sora-25.html.
- NOAA, *Estuaries Tutorial*, "Classifying Estuaries: By Water Circulation", https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_estuaries/est05_circulation.html.
- "Mediterranean outflow", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_outflow.
- Sarah Thompson, "Fact Check: This Is NOT A Picture Of Two Oceans Meeting But Not Mixing", Lead Stories (29 January 2021), https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2021/01/fact-check-this-is-not-a-picture-of-two-oceans-meeting-but-not-mixing.html.
- "The Quran on Seas and Rivers", islam-guide.com, on IslamReligion.com, https://www.islamreligion.com/articles/214/ — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
55:19–20 and 25:53 describe two bodies of water, "one fresh and sweet and one salty and bitter", released side by side with "a barrier" between them.

### Verse note
Sura al-Raḥmān is Meccan in the view of most commentators, though some (al-Zamakhshari, al-Suyuti) place it in Medina (as summarised by *Wikipedia*). The verses that follow say pearls and coral come from both (55:22, paraphrased here; not quoted from verified data), and each sign in the sura is followed by a refrain asking which of the Lord's favours the listeners would deny (55:13 and after, paraphrased).

"And it is He who has released [simultaneously] the two seas, one fresh and sweet and one salty and bitter, and He placed between them a barrier and prohibiting partition" (25:53), Sura al-Furqān, Meccan.

- **Key words.** *Maraja* ("released"): let loose, let flow freely. *Baḥr* ("sea"): a large body of water; Ibn Kathir's reading below shows it can cover rivers too. *Barzakh* ("barrier"): something that stands between two things. *Ḥijran maḥjūran* ("prohibiting partition"): a barrier that forbids passage.
- **What the verses say.** Two waters meet; something between them keeps each from overrunning the other. 25:53 says plainly that one is fresh and one is salt.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 55:19–20.** *Maraja* means "let them loose" (citing Ibn ʿAbbas). "The two seas are the fresh and salty waters, the former coming from running rivers." On the barrier: God placed "a barrier of land between these two types of waters", and he quotes Ibn Zayd: God "prevents them from meeting by the dividing barrier He placed between them".

**Ibn Kathir on 25:53.** The sweet water is "like that in rivers, springs and wells"; "nowhere in creation is there a sea which is fresh and sweet". The salt sea is "salty, bitter and not easy to swallow", like the Red Sea, the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean. The *barzakh* is "a partition, which is dry land", and the "prohibiting partition" is "a barrier, to prevent one of them from reaching the other". He cites Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Jarir al-Tabari for the sweet-water meaning, calling it "the meaning without a doubt".

**Al-Jalālayn on 55:19.** "He has loosed the two waters the sweet and the salty; and so they meet in the perception of the eye."

**Al-Jalālayn on 25:53.** "merged the two seas letting them flow one adjacent to the other"; "He set between the two an isthmus"; "a forbidding ban a shield that prevents the two from becoming mixed".

**Where they differ.** Ibn Kathir's barrier is physical land: rivers and seas are separated by the ground between them. Al-Jalālayn's is less specific: the two waters "meet in the perception of the eye", and a "shield… prevents the two from becoming mixed". Neither describes a boundary inside the sea, and neither mentions Gibraltar.

### How it works
Each point is tied to the verses' words "released… meeting", "fresh and sweet… salty and bitter" and "barrier".
- **Where fresh meets salt.** [Established] In an estuary, where a river meets the sea, fresh water "floats on top of the heavier seawater" because it is less dense (NOAA). This is the nearest physical picture of 25:53: "one fresh and sweet and one salty and bitter", side by side.
- **How sharp the boundary is depends on tides and river flow.** [Established] NOAA classifies estuaries by how much they mix:
  - "Salt-wedge estuaries occur when a rapidly flowing river discharges into the ocean where tidal currents are weak": a sharp boundary, fresh above, a wedge of salt below.
  - In partially mixed estuaries "saltwater and freshwater mix at all depths", but the lower layers stay saltier.
  - In well-mixed estuaries, "Strong tidal currents eliminate the vertical layering".
  So a "barrier" of density can be sharp, gradual or absent.
- **Two salt seas: Gibraltar.** [Established] Mediterranean water leaving through the Strait of Gibraltar has a salinity of "38 g/kg" and a temperature of "13 °C", "extremely high compared with any other waters in that depth range". It exits at about 120 m depth and sinks to an equilibrium depth of about 1,100 m, at a rate of about 1 Sverdrup (a million cubic metres per second) (*Wikipedia*, "Mediterranean outflow").
- **But it does mix.** At Gibraltar, tides flowing over the Camarinal Sill create "an internal bore", which breaks into internal waves that drive strong mixing (*Wikipedia*). As it leaves Gibraltar, the Mediterranean water (salinity about 38.6) mixes so strongly with Atlantic water that by the time it settles, at around 900–1,000 m, its salinity has fallen to about 36.5 (Price & Yang, WHOI; Sánchez-Román et al., 2018). Mediterranean water also leaves in spinning lenses called *meddies*, typically 40–150 km across and about 800 m thick. At least 17 form each year, and some last four or five years and travel well over a thousand kilometres (Richardson, Bower & Zenk, *Progress in Oceanography*, 2000).
- **The viral video.** [Established] A widely shared photo said to show "two oceans meeting but not mixing" in the Gulf of Alaska shows, according to Lead Stories, "another river plume taken during a research cruise in the Gulf of Alaska" (photo by Kent Smith, 2010). Ocean Networks Canada, quoted there, describes a similar plume as "a puddle of light water (formed by mixing fresh Fraser water with salt water)".

### Points of awe
- Where a river meets the sea, its fresh water can ride over the salt water in a distinct layer (NOAA).
- About a million cubic metres of Mediterranean water pour into the Atlantic every second, beneath the incoming Atlantic water (*Wikipedia*).
- Lenses of Mediterranean water up to about 100 km across "can survive for many years and can move through thousands of kilometers" (*Wikipedia*).

### The reflection
Two waters meet. One is sweet, one bitter. They are "released", free to flow, and still something keeps each from overrunning the other: "Between them is a barrier [so] neither of them transgresses" (55:20).

The commentators saw the plainest form of this. Rivers run sweet; seas are salt; the land stands between them. Al-Jalālayn adds that where they touch, they "meet in the perception of the eye" yet stay distinct. Today we can watch a river's fresh water ride over the sea before it slowly blends.

Sura al-Raḥmān does not offer this as a puzzle. It offers it as a favour, and after each sign asks which of the Lord's favours we would deny. Rivers that stay sweet long enough to water fields, and seas that stay salt, are both gifts.

There is a picture here for human life, offered as reflection. People of very different kinds live side by side. Each can keep its own nature without overrunning the other, within an order neither of them made. The verse calls that restraint a barrier; it might also be called justice.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says: "Modern Science has discovered that in the places where two different seas meet, there is a barrier between them", that "each sea has its own temperature, salinity, and density", and that "they do not mix or transgress this barrier", using the Mediterranean outflow at Gibraltar as its example (islam-guide.com, on IslamReligion.com, citing oceanography textbooks by Davis, Gross, Kuenen and Thurman).
- **Weighing it.**
  - **"They do not mix" is wrong.** The Mediterranean outflow is strongly mixed by tides at Gibraltar and blends as it moves west (*Wikipedia*). Estuaries range from sharply layered to fully mixed (NOAA). The viral Gulf of Alaska photo shows a river plume, which mixes (Lead Stories).
  - **The verse does not say "they never mix".** It says neither "transgresses" (*lā yabghiyān*): in this page's gloss, neither overruns or takes over the other. A boundary that holds two waters apart while they slowly blend fits that word; a claim of no mixing at all is the article's, not the verse's.
  - **The commentators did not read it as an invisible barrier in the sea.** Ibn Kathir's barrier is "dry land"; he says "nowhere in creation is there a sea which is fresh and sweet". Al-Jalālayn's is a "shield". Neither mentions a density boundary.
  - **The fair statement:** the verses describe fresh and salt water meeting and staying distinct; estuaries show that vividly; the "no mixing" claim is false and should not be repeated.
- **Figures not yet sourced.** Those sources have not yet been consulted; the page read gives 38 g/kg and about 1 Sv.

### Questions readers ask next
**Did classical scholars mean an invisible barrier in the sea?** No. Ibn Kathir explains the barrier as land between rivers and sea; al-Jalālayn as a "shield" keeping them from mixing. Modern estuary science is described separately.

**Is the barrier "proof" of modern oceanography?** No. The page treats it as a sign to notice, not a prediction, and the popular "never mix" claim is false (see the honest notes).

**What is an estuary?** The place where a river meets the sea, where fresh and salt water meet and, depending on tides and river flow, layer or mix (NOAA).

**What is happening at Gibraltar?** Salty, warm Mediterranean water flows out under the Atlantic water flowing in, then sinks to about 1,100 m; tides mix it strongly at the strait (*Wikipedia*).

**What does "neither of them transgresses" mean?** That neither overruns the other. For Ibn Kathir, land keeps rivers and seas apart; in estuaries, density keeps fresh water above salt water for a while.

## S8 — Pairs and the diversity of tongues and colours

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 51:49, 30:22, 49:13, 13:3.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 51:49, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-49-sora-51.html, and on 30:22, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-22-sora-30.html.
- Ethnologue, 27th edition (21 February 2024) — number of living languages, as read for this run.
- Nina G. Jablonski & George Chaplin, "The evolution of human skin coloration", *Journal of Human Evolution* 39(1):57–106 (2000), abstract via Penn State, https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/the-evolution-of-human-skin-coloration/.
- Sultan Bashir Mahmood (Robin Yasin Qusab is named in the end note), "The Holy Qur'an and Dirac's Theory of Pairs", *The Fountain*, issue 68 (March–April 2009), https://blog.fountainmagazine.com/2009/issue-68-march-april-2009/the-holy-quran-and-diracs-theory-of-pairs — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
51:49 says God created "two mates" of all things, and 30:22 counts "the diversity of your languages and your colors" among His signs: variety itself is something to notice.

### Verse note
"And of all things We created two mates; perhaps you will remember" (51:49), Sura al-Dhāriyāt, Meccan. It follows verses on the building of the sky and the spreading of the earth (51:47–48).

"And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth and the diversity of your languages and your colors. Indeed in that are signs for those of knowledge" (30:22), Sura al-Rūm, Meccan, in a run of "and of His signs" verses (30:20–25).

A related verse: "And from all of the fruits He made therein two mates" (13:3). And one that gives diversity a purpose: "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you" (49:13), Medinan.

- **Key words.** *Zawjayn* ("two mates"): a pair, two things that belong together; used of male and female, but also of any matching or complementary two. *Ikhtilāf alsinatikum wa-alwānikum*: the difference of your tongues and your colours. *Lil-ʿālimīn* (30:22): "for those of knowledge"; al-Jalālayn notes it can also be vowelled *lil-ʿālamīn*, which it glosses as "rational creatures".
- **Note on wording.** Ibn Kathir's translator renders 51:49 as "And of everything We have created pairs"; Saheeh has "And of all things We created two mates".

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 51:49.** "all the created are in pairs, the heaven and earth, night and day, sun and moon, land and sea, light and darkness, faith and disbelief, death and life, misery and happiness, Paradise and Fire", "in addition to the animals and plants". And "that you may remember": "know that the Creator, Allah, is One without partners". For him the pairs are complementary opposites across all creation, and their purpose is to point to the One who has no pair.

**Al-Jalālayn on 51:49.** The pairs are "two kinds such as male and female", with examples including "heaven and earth sun and moon" and "light and darkness".

**Ibn Kathir on 30:22.** He lists the languages he knew: "some speak Arabic, and the Tatars have their own language", and the Georgians, Romans, Franks, Berbers, Ethiopians, Indians, Persians, Slavs and others, and adds: "Only Allah knows the variety of languages spoken among the sons of Adam." On colours: "the difference of their colors mentioned here refers to their appearance": "from the time that Allah created Adam, and until the Hour begins", "Each face has its own characteristics and does not look like another".

**Al-Jalālayn on 30:22.** The final phrase "may be vocalised either as li'l-'ālamīna or li'l-'ālimīna", meaning "either rational creatures or people of knowledge respectively".

**What they did not say.** Neither reads "pairs" as a law of physics; neither treats the variety of colours as a ranking of peoples. Ibn Kathir reads "colours" as every face being different.

### How it works
- **"Of all things… two mates": pairs in living things.** [Established] Many living things reproduce through two sexes, but not all: many organisms reproduce without a partner (for example, by dividing). So "pairs" in the biological sense is very common, not universal. (No named source on asexual reproduction was read; this is textbook biology.)
- **"The diversity of your languages".** [Established] Ethnologue (27th edition, 2024) counts "a total of 7,164 living languages worldwide".
- **"And your colors".** [Established] Jablonski and Chaplin (2000) found that "skin reflectance is strongly correlated with absolute latitude and UV radiation levels". Their explanation: dark, "highly melanized skin protected against UV-induced photolysis of folate" (the breakdown by sunlight of a vitamin needed in pregnancy), while lighter skin away from the tropics allowed "UVB-induced synthesis of previtamin D3". The range of human skin colour "represents a compromise solution to the conflicting physiological requirements of photoprotection and vitamin D synthesis".

### Points of awe
- 7,164 living languages are spoken today (Ethnologue, 2024).
- Human skin colour tracks the strength of sunlight across the world, a balance between protecting folate and making vitamin D (Jablonski & Chaplin, 2000).
- Ibn Kathir, in the fourteenth century, already wrote that "Each face has its own characteristics and does not look like another".

### The reflection
Two verses, two kinds of variety. 51:49 sees the world built in pairs. Ibn Kathir's list moves from the sky to the soul: heaven and earth, night and day, land and sea, light and darkness, life and death, faith and disbelief. Each needs the other to be understood. And the verse says why we are shown this: "perhaps you will remember" (51:49). In a world of pairs, the Creator alone has no pair.

30:22 turns to us. Our languages and colours differ, and the verse calls that a sign. Ibn Kathir notices that no two faces, from Adam to the Hour, are the same. He knew a dozen languages by name; today we count thousands. "Only Allah knows the variety."

Then 49:13 gives that variety a purpose: "that you may know one another". Difference is for meeting, not for ranking. The same verse closes every door to pride of birth or colour: "the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you".

So the reader is asked two things. To look at the pairs, and remember that God is One. And to look at the faces and tongues of other people, and see in their difference not a threat, but a sign and an invitation to know them.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** Sultan Bashir Mahmood wrote that Paul Dirac showed "all particles in the universe must exist in pairs", and that the Quran's verse on pairs (36:36, not quoted from verified data here) "may be considered relevant to Dirac's discoveries", adding that it "clearly shows that the Qur'an is referring to a law of nature" (*The Fountain*, 2009). The article quotes 51:49 later but does not tie it to Dirac.
- **Weighing it.**
  - "Pairs" in the verses is a general word. Ibn Kathir's list is of complementary opposites (night and day, life and death, faith and disbelief), not of particles; al-Jalālayn's begins with "male and female".
  - Reading antimatter into the verse fits only if "pairs" is given a modern meaning the commentators read here did not give it. And not everything in physics or biology comes in pairs (see "How it works").
  - The fair statement: the verse invites reflection on pairing and complementarity in creation; it makes no antimatter "prediction".
- **Colour is not race.** The verse speaks of "your colors" as a sign; it does not divide people into races, and 49:13 rules out ranking by birth. The science of skin colour describes adaptation to sunlight; it does not support ranking either.
- **Language counts change.** "7,164" is Ethnologue's 2024 count; counts depend on how languages and dialects are distinguished.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "pairs" mean everything has male and female?** Al-Jalālayn begins with "male and female", but both commentators read it far more widely: Ibn Kathir lists night and day, land and sea, life and death, faith and disbelief.

**What does the diversity of languages show?** For Ibn Kathir, the range of human languages and faces is itself a sign that "only Allah knows" in full (30:22). Today about 7,164 living languages are counted (Ethnologue).

**Why do human skin colours differ?** Mainly because of sunlight. Jablonski and Chaplin (2000) found skin reflectance "strongly correlated" with UV levels: darker skin protects folate; lighter skin allows vitamin D to be made where sunlight is weaker.

**Is diversity a reason for pride or division?** 49:13 says the opposite: peoples and tribes exist "that you may know one another", and "the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you".

**Did the Quran predict antimatter?** No. That is a modern reading of "pairs"; the commentators read the word as complementary opposites and male and female (see the honest notes).
