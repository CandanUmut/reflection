## S12 — The spider's house

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 29:38–43.
- al-Tabari on 29:41 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura29-aya41.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 29:41, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-41-sora-29.html (as on the current page).
- Agnarsson, Kuntner & Blackledge, "Bioprospecting finds the toughest biological material: extraordinary silk from a giant riverine orb spider", *PLoS ONE* 5(9): e11234 (2010), abstract (DOAJ record).
- N. W. Burke, "Sexual cannibalism as a female resistance trait: a new hypothesis", *Evolution* 78(4) (2024) — what is and is not claimed about sexual cannibalism.

### In brief
"The example of those who take allies other than Allah is like that of the spider who takes a home. And indeed, the weakest of homes is the home of the spider, if they only knew" (29:41): a parable about false protection.

### Verse note
"The example of those who take allies other than Allah is like that of the spider who takes a home. And indeed, the weakest of homes is the home of the spider, if they only knew" (29:41). Sura al-ʿAnkabūt ("The Spider") is usually counted Meccan. It follows the fall of ʿAad, Thamud, Qarun, Pharaoh and Haman, peoples who were "endowed with perception" (29:38) and yet were seized for their sins (29:40). After it: "Indeed, Allah knows whatever thing they call upon other than Him" (29:42); "And these examples We present to the people, but none will understand them except those of knowledge" (29:43).

- *Awliyāʾ*: "allies", patrons, protectors.
- *Ittakhadhat*: "takes" — the same verb as "those who take allies". The parallel is the point: they take patrons as the spider takes a house.
- *Awhan*: "weakest", most frail.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari.** The verse describes those who take idols and false gods as patrons, hoping for «نَصْرها ونفعها عند حاجتهم إليها» ("their help and benefit when they need them"). The spider's house is weak because of «ضعفها، وقلة احتيالها لنفسها» — the spider's own weakness and lack of resource — and it «لم يغن عنها شيئا عند حاجتها إليه» ("did not avail her at all when she needed it"). He reports:
- Ibn ʿAbbas: «ذلك مثل ضربه الله لمن عبد غيره» ("a parable God struck for whoever worships other than Him");
- Qatada: the false god is «واهن ضعيف لا ينفعه» ("frail and weak, of no use to him");
- Ibn Zayd: «لا يغني أولياؤهم عنهم شيئا، كما لا يغني العنكبوت بيتها هذا» ("their patrons avail them nothing, as her house avails the spider nothing").
On "if they only knew": they «يجهلون ذلك، فيحسبون أنهم ينفعونهم ويقرّبونهم إلى الله زلفى» — they do not know this, and think their patrons help them and bring them near to God.

**Ibn Kathir.** The idolaters "were like the house of a spider, which is so weak and frail"; like "a person who holds on to a spider's web, who does not gain any benefit from that".

**What they share, and what they did not say.** All read the house's weakness as uselessness when needed. None speaks of spider family life, of which sex builds the web, or of the silk's strength.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "home" and "weakest". These are modern facts set beside the verse.

- **Strong thread, weak shelter.** [Established] Spider silk can be extraordinarily tough. The silk of Darwin's bark spider "averages 350 MJ/m(3), with some samples reaching 520 MJ/m(3)" — "more than twice tougher than any previously described silk, and over 10 times better than Kevlar"; its webs reach "up to 2.8 m(2)", hung across rivers by "anchor threads as long as 25 meters" (Agnarsson, Kuntner & Blackledge, 2010). Yet a web, however tough its thread, gives no shelter from wind, rain, heat or a hand that brushes it away. The verse speaks of the *home*, not the thread.
- **Spider family life.** [Established that it exists; not universal] "Female spiders and praying mantises are renowned for their cannibalism of male partners before, during, or after mating" (Burke, *Evolution*, 2024). The source read gives no figure for how many species do this; it is known in some species, not all.

### Points of awe
- Darwin's bark spider makes the toughest biological material yet measured, over ten times tougher than Kevlar (Agnarsson et al., 2010).
- It hangs webs over rivers with anchor threads up to 25 m long (Agnarsson et al., 2010).
- A thread that strong still makes a house that cannot shelter anyone.

### The reflection
The verse paints a picture anyone has seen: a web in a corner, fine and intricate, gone at a touch. "And indeed, the weakest of homes is the home of the spider" (29:41).

The weakness is not in the thread. Modern science has found spider silk tougher than Kevlar. The weakness is in what the web is asked to be: a home, a shelter, a refuge. It cannot keep out heat or cold or a hand. Al-Tabari puts it in one line: it "did not avail her at all when she needed it".

That is the parable. People take "allies other than Allah" — idols, then; money, power, status, people, now — as the spider takes a house. They hope, al-Tabari says, for "their help and benefit when they need them". But in the hour of need, such allies "avail them nothing" (Ibn Zayd). The verses before name peoples who had strength and perception and were seized all the same (29:38–40).

"If they only knew." The verse does not mock; it laments. And it ends with an invitation: "none will understand them except those of knowledge" (29:43). The question for the reader is quiet and personal: what am I leaning on, and will it hold?

### Honest notes
- **The popular claims.** IslamReligion.com presents the verse as describing spider family life — "both female and male spiders commonly kill and eat the other after mating", "aggressive females kill their mates indiscriminately", and a mother who "sacrifices her own body as food for her young" (2016). Others argue that the feminine verb shows only female spiders build webs (as reported by WikiIslam).
- **Weighing them.** The verse's point, in al-Tabari's reports (Ibn ʿAbbas, Qatada, Ibn Zayd) and in Ibn Kathir, is the web's uselessness as a shelter; the "household" reading goes beyond the text. Sexual cannibalism is real but occurs in some species, not all (Burke, 2024, gives no count). On the feminine verb, the grammatical gender follows the noun *ʿankabūt*, which Lane's lexicon describes as "generally fem., but sometimes masc." (as cited by WikiIslam), and male spiders also spin silk; the grammar does not settle biology.
- **"Takes", not "builds".** Saheeh renders *ittakhadhat* as "takes" — the same verb as "those who take allies".

### Questions readers ask next
**Is a spider's web really weak?** The thread is strong — some silk is tougher than Kevlar (Agnarsson et al., 2010). But as a *home* it gives no shelter. The verse speaks of the home, and al-Tabari explains its weakness as uselessness in need.

**Who are "those who take allies other than Allah"?** In the first setting, those who worshipped idols (Ibn ʿAbbas and Qatada, in al-Tabari). The parable applies to anything a person relies on in place of God.

**Does the verse say only female spiders build webs?** No. The feminine verb follows the grammar of *ʿankabūt*; male spiders spin silk too. No classical commentator read here drew a biological lesson from the grammar.

**Is the verse about spiders eating their mates?** No. That happens in some species, but the verse and its commentators speak only of the web's weakness as a house.

**Why does the verse end "if they only knew"?** Al-Tabari: because they do not realise how little their patrons can do, and think those patrons will help them and bring them near to God.

## S14 — Shadows extending and contracting

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 25:45–47.
- al-Qurtubi on 25:45 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura25-aya45.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 25:45–46, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-45-sora-25.html (as on the current page).
- US Naval Observatory, "Rise, Set, and Twilight Definitions" (aa.usno.navy.mil).
- Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 149 (hadithunlocked.com) — the hadith of Gabriel and the prayer times (read for run 19).
- Gibril F. Haddad, "Time of ʿAsr in the Hanafi Madhab" (islamqa.org) (read for run 19).

### In brief
"Have you not considered your Lord - how He extends the shadow, and if He willed, He could have made it stationary?" (25:45): the verse asks us to watch the shadow and see in its movement the hand of God.

### Verse note
"Have you not considered your Lord - how He extends the shadow, and if He willed, He could have made it stationary? Then We made the sun for it an indication. Then We hold it in hand for a brief grasp" (25:45–46). Sura al-Furqān is Meccan. Next: "And it is He who has made the night for you as clothing and sleep [a means for] rest and has made the day a resurrection" (25:47).

- *Madda al-ẓill*: "extends the shadow".
- *Sākinan*: "stationary", still.
- *Dalīl*: "an indication", a guide.
- *Qabḍan yasīran*: "a brief grasp" — drawn in gently, little by little.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Qurtubi.** "Extends the shadow" is the time «من طلوع الفجر إلى طلوع الشمس» ("from the break of dawn to sunrise"), the view of al-Hasan, Qatada and others; a second, unattributed view takes it as from sunset to sunrise; he judges «والأول أصح» ("the first is more correct"). Ibn ʿAbbas on "He could have made it stationary": «إلى يوم القيامة» ("until the Day of Resurrection"); another view: God could have kept the sun from rising. On "the sun for it an indication": the sun, by removing the shade, shows that shade is a real thing, since things are known by their opposites. Abu al-ʿAliya said the day of Paradise is like this time — the time of the dawn prayer.

**Ibn Kathir.** The shadow is "the period from the beginning of the dawn until the sun rises", from Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn ʿUmar, Mujahid, Qatada "and others". On "the sun its guide", Qatada and al-Suddi: the sun is a guide that follows the shade until the shade disappears. The "gradual withdrawal" "refers to the shade".

**Where they differ.** Most read the extended shadow as the light before sunrise; a minority as the whole night. "Stationary" is read as "lasting until the Resurrection" (Ibn ʿAbbas) or as a sun that never rose. **What they did not say:** none discusses the earth's rotation; they read the verse within their own picture of the sky, and that reading also fits what anyone sees.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "extends", "stationary", "the sun… an indication" and "a brief grasp".

- **The light before sunrise.** [Established] Astronomers define civil twilight as the time when the Sun's centre is up to 6° below the horizon, nautical twilight 12°, and astronomical twilight 18°; before astronomical dawn, "scattered light from the Sun is less than that from starlight" (US Naval Observatory). The soft, shadowless light that the commentators saw in "extends the shadow" is this twilight.
- **The sun as the guide.** [Established] A shadow's direction and length follow the Sun's position; as the Sun climbs, shadows shorten, and they lengthen again in the afternoon. This is what lets a stick in the ground serve as a clock.
- **A shadow that tells the prayer time.** [Established] In the hadith of Gabriel teaching the prayer times, the afternoon prayer began "when everything was similar (to the length of) its shadow", and on the next day was prayed when "the shadow of everything was about twice as long as it" ({hadith:tirmidhi:149}; al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*). The one-length view is that of the Maliki, Shafiʿi and Hanbali schools and of Abu Hanifa's two leading students; Abu Hanifa held two lengths (Haddad).

### Points of awe
- Before sunrise there are three named stages of twilight, set by the Sun 18°, 12° and 6° below the horizon (US Naval Observatory).
- A stick's shadow measured the time of the afternoon prayer in the hadith of Gabriel (Tirmidhi 149).
- The withdrawal of the shadow is so gradual that no one sees it move, only that it has moved.

### The reflection
"Have you not considered your Lord - how He extends the shadow" (25:45). The verse does not ask us to consider the shadow; it asks us to consider our Lord *through* the shadow.

The commentators placed the scene in the quiet before sunrise (al-Qurtubi, with al-Hasan and Qatada), when light is everywhere and shadow nowhere, "extended" over the world. Abu al-ʿAliya said the day of Paradise is like that hour. Then the sun rises and becomes the shadow's "indication": we only know shade is real because the sun shows us its edge. Things are known by their opposites (al-Qurtubi).

"And if He willed, He could have made it stationary." Ibn ʿAbbas: until the Day of Resurrection. A world without moving shadows would be a world without time — no hours, no prayer times, no rhythm of work and rest. The next verse names the gift: night as clothing, sleep as rest, day as a rising (25:47).

"Then We hold it in hand for a brief grasp." The shadow is drawn in gently, so gently that no one ever sees it move. A believer may take from this, as reflection, a picture of how God changes things — not always in a flash, but by a steady hand we notice only afterwards.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** It is sometimes claimed that the verse "reveals Earth's rotation". The verse describes what anyone can see; the classical commentators read it as the light before sunrise and as God's power to stop the sun. No named source for the claim was read.
- **Two readings of "extends the shadow".** Dawn to sunrise (al-Hasan, Qatada; al-Qurtubi: "more correct") or sunset to sunrise (unattributed). The page should give both.
- **The Hanafi Asr time.** Abu Hanifa held two shadow-lengths; his two students and the other schools held one; later Hanafis differ on which view the school follows (Haddad; see run 19).

### Questions readers ask next
**What is the "extended shadow"?** Most commentators say the time between dawn and sunrise (al-Hasan and Qatada, in al-Qurtubi; Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn ʿUmar, Mujahid and Qatada, in Ibn Kathir), when the light is spread out and there are no sharp shadows.

**What does "He could have made it stationary" mean?** Ibn ʿAbbas: it could have stayed so "until the Day of Resurrection"; another view: God could have kept the sun from rising (al-Qurtubi).

**How is the sun an "indication" of the shadow?** Shade is known by its opposite: the sun shows that shade is real by removing it and by setting its edge (al-Qurtubi).

**How do shadows tell the prayer times?** In the hadith of Gabriel, the afternoon prayer began when an object's shadow equalled its length ({hadith:tirmidhi:149}); the schools differ on one length or two.

**Does the verse describe the earth's rotation?** No. It describes the shadow's movement as anyone sees it; the commentators did not read it as cosmology.

## S10 — Birds held in the sky

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 16:78–80, 67:19–20, 24:41.
- al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi on 67:19 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-tabary-qortobi/sura67-aya19.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 16:79 and 67:19, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-79-sora-16.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-19-sora-67.html (as on the current page).
- NASA Glenn Research Center, "What is Lift?" (www1.grc.nasa.gov).
- Weimerskirch et al., "Frigate birds track atmospheric conditions over months-long transoceanic flights", *Science* (2016), as reported by Phys.org (July 2016).
- Guinness World Records (January 2023) on the bar-tailed godwit's 13,560 km flight (read for run 17).

### In brief
"Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and [sometimes] folded in? None holds them [aloft] except the Most Merciful" (67:19): the verse asks us to look up at something ordinary and see who holds it.

### Verse note
"Do they not see the birds controlled in the atmosphere of the sky? None holds them up except Allah. Indeed in that are signs for a people who believe" (16:79), Meccan, between the gifts of hearing, sight and hearts (16:78) and of homes and tents (16:80).

"Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and [sometimes] folded in? None holds them [aloft] except the Most Merciful. Indeed He is, of all things, Seeing" (67:19), Meccan; next: "Or who is it that could be an army for you to aid you other than the Most Merciful?" (67:20). Compare "the birds with wings spread [in flight]" exalting God (24:41).

- *Musakhkharāt*: "controlled", made subject.
- *Ṣāffāt*: "with wings outspread"; *yaqbiḍna*: "[sometimes] folded in".
- *Mā yumsikuhunna*: "none holds them".

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari on 67:19.** The birds «تصف أجنحتها أحيانا، وتقبض أحيانا» ("sometimes spread their wings and sometimes fold them"). Qatada: «الطير يصف جناحه كما رأيت، ثم يقبضه» ("the bird spreads its wing, as you have seen, then folds it"); Mujahid: «بسطهن أجنحتهن وقبضهن» ("their spreading and folding of their wings"). None holds them but the Merciful — a reminder that their Lord is one, with no partner.

**al-Qurtubi on 67:19.** *Ṣāffāt*: «باسطات أجنحتهن في الجو عند طيرانها» ("spreading their wings in the air as they fly"); *yaqbiḍna*: «يضربن بها جنوبهن» ("they strike their sides with them" — flapping). What holds them in the air is «إلا الله عز وجل».

**Ibn Kathir on 16:79.** "They are held up only by Him, it is He Who gave them the strength to do that, subjecting the air to carry them and support them."

**What they share.** All describe gliding and flapping as anyone sees them, and all see God's holding behind the air that carries the birds. Ibn Kathir's "subjecting the air to carry them" is the closest to a physical explanation. **What they did not say:** none speaks of lift or air pressure.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "wings outspread", "folded in" and "None holds them".

- **How a wing holds a bird up.** [Established] "Lift occurs when a moving flow of gas is turned by a solid object"; the force arises "in the opposite direction, according to Newton's Third Law"; and "no fluid, no lift" (NASA Glenn Research Center). A spread wing moving through air turns the air downward, and the air holds the bird up — Ibn Kathir's "subjecting the air to carry them".
- **Wings outspread: soaring.** [Established] Great frigate birds can "stay aloft for up to two months at a time", covering up to about 400 km (250 miles) in a day; they "slip under a cumulus cloud" to be carried up as high as 4,000 m, without flapping (Weimerskirch et al., *Science*, 2016, as reported by Phys.org).
- **Wings folded and beating: powered flight.** [Established] A juvenile bar-tailed godwit flew 13,560 km from Alaska to Tasmania without stopping, in 11 days and 1 hour (Guinness World Records, 2023; not yet peer reviewed at the time).

### Points of awe
- Frigate birds can stay in the air for up to two months without landing (Weimerskirch et al., 2016).
- They rise to 4,000 m under clouds without a single wingbeat (as reported by Phys.org).
- A young godwit flew 13,560 km non-stop across the Pacific (Guinness, 2023).

### The reflection
"Do they not see the birds above them" (67:19). The verse uses the plainest word: *see*. Not study, not measure — just look up.

What we see is a bird with wings spread, then folded, then spread again. Qatada, in al-Tabari, says exactly that: "the bird spreads its wing, as you have seen, then folds it." Al-Qurtubi adds the beat of the wings against the body. Nothing hidden; nothing exotic. And yet: "None holds them [aloft] except the Most Merciful."

Ibn Kathir saw the means: God "subjecting the air to carry them". The air is the means; God is the One who made the means to work. Modern physics describes the same means in detail — a wing turning the air, the air pushing back — and still cannot say why there is air, or wings, or a law at all.

The verse chooses the name "the Most Merciful". Holding birds in the sky is an act of mercy, renewed every second for every bird. And the next verse asks the obvious question: if He holds them, who else could be "an army for you" (67:20)? A believer who watches a bird held up by invisible air may learn to trust the invisible hand that holds them too.

### Honest notes
- **No aerodynamics in the verse.** The verses describe what anyone can see: birds gliding and flapping, held in the air. Claims that they "predicted" lift go beyond the words; the commentators did not read them that way, though Ibn Kathir names the air as the means.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about these verses was read.
- **Distance figures.** The godwit record (13,560 km) was "not yet published or peer reviewed" when reported (run 17).

### Questions readers ask next
**What do "outspread" and "folded in" mean?** Gliding with wings spread, and flapping or folding them; Qatada and Mujahid (in al-Tabari) and al-Qurtubi describe exactly this.

**If birds fly by lift, why does the verse say only God holds them?** Ibn Kathir: God holds them by "subjecting the air to carry them". The physics describes the means; the verse names who gave the means.

**How long can a bird stay in the air?** Great frigate birds can stay aloft for up to two months (Weimerskirch et al., 2016).

**What is the longest non-stop bird flight recorded?** A juvenile bar-tailed godwit flew 13,560 km from Alaska to Tasmania in 2022 (Guinness World Records).

**Why "the Most Merciful" in 67:19?** Because holding every bird in the air is a constant mercy; the next verse asks who else could protect us (67:20).

## S13 — Sleep as a sign, and souls "taken" in sleep

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 30:23, 78:9, 39:42–43, 6:60, 25:47.
- al-Tabari on 39:42 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura39-aya42.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) on 39:42, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-42-sora-39.html (as on the current page).
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6314 (hadithunlocked.com), from Hudhayfa — the words at sleeping and waking.
- NIH/NINDS release (via ScienceDaily, 18 Oct. 2013) on Xie et al., "Sleep Drives Metabolite Clearance from the Adult Brain", *Science* 342:373 (2013) (as on the current page).
- Ringstad et al., *JCI Insight* 3(13): e121537 (2018); NINDS, "Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep" (read for run 17).

### In brief
"Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die [He takes] during their sleep" (39:42): every night, the Quran says, we pass through a small likeness of death and are given back.

### Verse note
"Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die [He takes] during their sleep. Then He keeps those for which He has decreed death and releases the others for a specified term. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought" (39:42), Meccan, in Sura al-Zumar; next: "Or have they taken other than Allah as intercessors?" (39:43).

"And it is He who takes your souls by night and knows what you have committed by day. Then He revives you therein that a specified term may be fulfilled" (6:60). "And of His signs is your sleep by night and day and your seeking of His bounty" (30:23). "And made your sleep [a means for] rest" (78:9).

- *Yatawaffā*: "takes" in full — the same verb used of death.
- *Subāt* (78:9): "rest", a cutting-off, a pause.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari on 39:42.** God takes souls at death and the souls of sleepers; he reports that «أرواح الأحياء والأموات تلتقي في المنام» ("the spirits of the living and the dead meet in sleep"). Saʿid ibn Jubayr: God gathers the spirits of the living and the dead, keeps those whose death is decreed and returns the rest to their bodies. al-Suddi: the souls of sleepers and the dead meet and question each other; the living return to their bodies. Ibn Zayd: «فالنوم وفاة» ("sleep is a death").

**Ibn Kathir on 39:42.** God causes people to "die a lesser death" when they sleep, as against the "greater death"; linking it to 6:60–61: "Here Allah mentions the two deaths, the lesser death then the greater death after it."

**The Prophet's (pbuh) words at sleeping and waking.** Hudhayfa reports that at night he would say "Allahumma bismika amutu wa ahya" (O Allah, in Your name I die and live), and on waking: "Al-Hamdu lil-lahi al-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana, wa ilaihi an-nushur" — praise to Allah who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the rising ({hadith:bukhari:6314}).

**What they share, and what they did not say.** All read sleep as a lesser death and waking as a small resurrection. The reports that souls of the living and dead meet in sleep (Saʿid ibn Jubayr, al-Suddi) are the commentators' reports, not the verse's words. None describes the body's physiology of sleep.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "rest" (78:9) and "sign" (30:23). These are modern findings set beside the verses.

- **Cycles in the night.** [Established] "REM sleep first occurs about 90 minutes after falling asleep"; "Memory consolidation most likely requires both non-REM and REM sleep"; the brain's master clock "receive[s] information about light exposure directly from the eyes" (NINDS, "Brain Basics").
- **Cleaning while asleep — in mice.** [Established in mice] In 2013 Xie and colleagues reported that in sleeping mice the space between brain cells widened and waste such as beta-amyloid was cleared faster than in waking mice (Xie et al., *Science*, 2013, via NIH/NINDS).
- **In humans.** [Moderate] An MRI study of 17 people found "in vivo evidence of access to all human brain subregions" for a tracer carried by brain fluid, with clearance "delayed in the dementia cohort" (Ringstad et al., *JCI Insight*, 2018). Human evidence on sleep and clearance is still growing.

### Points of awe
- The first dream-sleep (REM) usually begins about 90 minutes after falling asleep (NINDS).
- In mice, the brain clears waste faster during sleep than when awake (Xie et al., 2013).
- The body's clock is set by light reaching the eyes (NINDS).

### The reflection
Every night we lie down, our awareness fades, and we let go of everything we were holding. The Quran says this is not nothing: "Allah takes the souls… [He takes] during their sleep" (39:42). The word is the same as for death.

Ibn Kathir calls sleep "the lesser death", and death "the greater". Ibn Zayd, in al-Tabari, says simply: "sleep is a death." And every morning is a small resurrection. The Prophet (pbuh) used to wake with praise to Allah "who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the rising" ({hadith:bukhari:6314}).

"Then He keeps those for which He has decreed death and releases the others for a specified term." Each waking is a release, a new term granted. That is a sign "for a people who give thought".

Science now adds, as reflection, that sleep is not idle: it orders memory, sets our clocks by the light, and — in mice at least — clears the brain. The verses say none of this; they call sleep "rest" (78:9) and a sign (30:23). But a believer who learns how busy the sleeping body is may find the word "rest" larger than it seemed. We are kept, all night, by a care we do not see.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** It is sometimes said that the Quran "predicted the glymphatic system". It did not: the verses speak of sleep as rest, as a sign and as a "taking" of the soul, not of brain fluid.
- **Mice, then humans.** The 2013 clearance finding is from mice; the human evidence (Ringstad et al., 2018) shows access of a tracer to the brain and slower clearance in dementia, but its link to sleep is still being studied.
- **Reports in the tafsir.** That souls of the living and the dead meet in sleep is reported by Saʿid ibn Jubayr and al-Suddi (al-Tabari); it is not in the verse itself.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does it mean that God "takes" souls in sleep?** The Quran uses the same verb for death and sleep. Ibn Kathir calls sleep "the lesser death"; Ibn Zayd: "sleep is a death" (al-Tabari).

**Do the souls of the living and the dead meet in sleep?** Saʿid ibn Jubayr and al-Suddi said so, as reported by al-Tabari. It is their explanation, not the words of the verse.

**What did the Prophet (pbuh) say on waking?** "Al-Hamdu lil-lahi al-ladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana, wa ilaihi an-nushur" — praise to Allah who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the rising ({hadith:bukhari:6314}).

**Does science show what the brain does in sleep?** Partly: REM and non-REM cycles, memory consolidation, the light-set body clock (NINDS), and in mice faster clearance of brain waste (Xie et al., 2013).

**Did the Quran predict the glymphatic system?** No. It speaks of rest and of the soul, not of brain fluid.

## S2 — Milk "from between digested food and blood"

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 16:65–67.
- Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari and al-Saʿdi on 16:66 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-katheer-tabary-saadi/sura16-aya66.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 16:66, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-66-sora-16.html (as on the current page).
- Renaudeau, Lebreton, Noblet & Dourmad, "Measurement of blood flow through the mammary gland in lactating sows: methodological aspects", *Journal of Animal Science* 80(1):196–201 (2002), abstract.
- "Anatomy of the Mammary Gland", Cornell University (adapted from DeLaval material) (as on the current page).

### In brief
"We give you drink from what is in their bellies - between excretion and blood - pure milk, palatable to drinkers" (16:66): purity drawn out from between two impurities, as a lesson.

### Verse note
"And indeed, for you in grazing livestock is a lesson. We give you drink from what is in their bellies - between excretion and blood - pure milk, palatable to drinkers" (16:66), Meccan, in Sura al-Naḥl ("The Bee"), in a run of provisions: rain reviving the earth (16:65), milk (16:66), the fruits of palm and vine (16:67), and then honey (16:68–69).

- *ʿIbra*: "a lesson" — literally a crossing-over, from what is seen to what it points to.
- *Farth*: the contents of the stomach and gut ("excretion" in Saheeh).
- *Khāliṣ*: "pure"; *sāʾigh*: "palatable", going down easily.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** «إذا نضج الغذاء في معدته تصرف منه دم إلى العروق، ولبن إلى الضرع» ("when the food is digested in its stomach, blood from it goes to the veins, and milk to the udder"); the milk, in its whiteness, taste and sweetness, is drawn out from between *farth* and blood, and each «لا يشوب الآخر ولا يمازجه» ("does not mix with or blend into the other"). "A lesson" is "a sign and an evidence of the wisdom, power, mercy and kindness of the Creator".

**al-Tabari.** "Pure": «خلص من مخالطة الدم والفرث، فلم يختلطا به» ("free from mixing with blood and *farth*; they did not mix into it"). "Palatable": «فلا يَغَصّ به» ("no one chokes on it").

**al-Saʿdi (d. 1956).** «لبنا خالصا من الكدر» ("milk free of any murkiness"), and: «فهل هذه إلا قدرة إلهية لا أمور طبيعية» ("is this anything but divine power, not mere natural processes?").

**What they share, and what they did not say.** All read the verse as purity coming out from between impurities. Ibn Kathir, centuries before modern physiology, already describes food digested in the stomach, blood going to the veins and milk to the udder — without treating it as hidden knowledge. None describes milk-making cells.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "between excretion and blood" and "pure milk".

- **From food, through blood, to milk.** [Established] Nutrients from digested food pass into the blood; the cells of the mammary gland take their raw materials from the blood and make milk (Cornell University, "Anatomy of the Mammary Gland").
- **How much blood.** [Moderate; measured in sows] In lactating sows, "blood flow through the entire mammary gland averages 3.6 L/min" and "about 470 L of blood are required to produce 1 kg of milk" — an estimate that assumes the measured artery drains a quarter of the gland (Renaudeau et al., *Journal of Animal Science*, 2002). For dairy cows the current page gives about 500 litres of blood per litre of milk (Cornell University); such figures are rules of thumb, not constants.

### Points of awe
- Around 470 litres of blood pass through a sow's mammary glands for each kilogram of milk (Renaudeau et al., 2002).
- Milk is made in the udder from what the blood brings, yet carries neither the colour of blood nor the smell of the gut (Ibn Kathir's observation; Cornell on the physiology).
- Ibn Kathir described digested food sending "blood to the veins, and milk to the udder" in the 14th century.

### The reflection
Every glass of milk passes through a place we would rather not think about. The verse names it without embarrassment: "from what is in their bellies - between excretion and blood - pure milk, palatable to drinkers" (16:66).

That is the lesson, the *ʿibra*, the crossing-over. From the gut and from the blood, both of which we would turn away from, comes something white, clean and sweet. Al-Tabari: free of any mixing. Al-Saʿdi asks: is this anything but divine power?

Ibn Kathir saw the path long ago: food digested, blood to the veins, milk to the udder, each kept apart. Modern physiology fills in the scale — hundreds of litres of blood for each litre of milk — but the wonder the verse points to is the same: purity drawn out of what is not pure.

The surrounding verses build the same picture: rain from the sky reviving dead earth (16:65); fruit from palm and vine (16:67); then honey from the bee. Each is a gift brought out of something that does not look like a gift. A believer may take the lesson further, as reflection: God can bring what is pure out of the most unlikely places — including a life that seems mixed and stained.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says that "High-nutrition milk is thus produced from blood, which cannot be consumed directly, and semi-digested food", and that this "could not have been known at the time when the Qur'an was revealed" ("The Formation of Milk", islam.com.kw).
- **Weighing it.** The verse's image is accurate and striking, but the claim goes beyond the text: the verse does not mention absorption, circulation or milk-making cells. And it *was* known: Ibn Kathir describes digested food sending blood to the veins and milk to the udder, without treating it as hidden knowledge.
- **Numbers are averages.** The ~470 L figure is from sows and rests on a stated assumption (Renaudeau et al., 2002); "500 litres per litre" for cows is a rule of thumb.

### Questions readers ask next
**What is *farth*?** The contents of the stomach and gut — digested food. Saheeh renders it "excretion".

**What does "pure milk" mean here?** Free of any mixing with blood or gut contents (al-Tabari), "free of murkiness" (al-Saʿdi); in whiteness, taste and sweetness (Ibn Kathir).

**Did the Quran reveal how milk is made?** It describes milk coming from between food and blood. Ibn Kathir already explained it as digested food sending blood to the veins and milk to the udder. The details of milk-making cells are modern physiology, not in the verse.

**How much blood does it take to make milk?** In sows, about 470 litres of blood per kilogram of milk (Renaudeau et al., 2002); for cows the page's Cornell source gives about 500 litres per litre.

**Why is it called a "lesson"?** Because it points beyond itself: to "the wisdom, power, mercy and kindness of the Creator" (Ibn Kathir).

## S17 — Clouds and winds

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 24:41–44, 30:48, 15:22.
- al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi on 24:43 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-tabary-qortobi/sura24-aya43.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) on 24:43 and 15:22, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-43-sora-24.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-22-sora-15.html (as on the current page).
- NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory, "Hail Basics" (archived page).
- USGS Water Science School, "How much does a cloud weigh?" (as on the current page).

### In brief
"Do you not see that Allah drives clouds? Then He brings them together, then He makes them into a mass, and you see the rain emerge from within it" (24:43): the verse walks us through a storm, step by step, and asks us to see who drives it.

### Verse note
"Do you not see that Allah drives clouds? Then He brings them together, then He makes them into a mass, and you see the rain emerge from within it. And He sends down from the sky, mountains [of clouds] within which is hail, and He strikes with it whom He wills and averts it from whom He wills. The flash of its lightening almost takes away the eyesight" (24:43), Medinan, in Sura al-Nūr, between birds exalting God (24:41) and the alternation of night and day (24:44).

"It is Allah who sends the winds, and they stir the clouds and spread them in the sky however He wills, and He makes them fragments so you see the rain emerge from within them" (30:48). "And We have sent the fertilizing winds and sent down water from the sky and given you drink from it. And you are not its retainers" (15:22).

- *Yuzjī*: "drives", pushes along.
- *Yuʾallifu baynahu*: "brings them together".
- *Rukām*: "a mass", heaped up.
- *Wadq*: "rain". *Barad*: "hail". *Sanā barqihi*: "the flash of its lightning".

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari.** *Yuzjī*: «يعني: يسوق» ("drives"); bringing together: «جمعه بين متفرّقها» ("gathering its scattered parts"); *rukām*: «متراكما بعضه على بعض» ("heaped one on another"); *wadq*: rain coming out from between the clouds. On "mountains… within which is hail", two readings: «من جبال في السماء من برد مخلوقة هنالك» ("mountains of hail created there in the sky"), or hail falling «قدر جبال، وأمثال جبال» ("in the amount of mountains, like mountains"). *Sanā*: «ضوء البرق» ("the light of the lightning").

**al-Qurtubi.** The cloud is gathered «ليقوى ويتصل ويكثف» ("so that it grows strong, joined and dense"); *rukām*: «يركب بعضه بعضا» ("one part rides on another"). *Wadq*: rain (the majority) or lightning. On the "mountains", he repeats both readings and adds al-Farraʾ — «فالجبال عنده هي البرد» ("for him the mountains are the hail itself") — and al-Akhfash.

**Ibn Kathir.** God "drives the clouds from the beginning, when they are formed and are still weak"; "He piles them up on top of one another"; rain comes "from the gaps between them". He gives both readings of the mountains; on 15:22 he gives the reading of winds that pollinate.

**Where they differ.** The "mountains" are real mountains of hail in the sky, or hail as great as mountains (al-Tabari), or the hail itself (al-Farraʾ). Saheeh's bracket "[of clouds]" chooses a further, modern-sounding reading not stated by these commentators.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "drives", "brings together", "a mass", "hail" and "lightning".

- **Weight in the sky.** [Established, as an example] A cumulus cloud of 1 km³ with 0.5 g of water per m³ holds about 500 tonnes of water (USGS Water Science School).
- **Heaped into a storm.** [Established] Hail "occurs when updrafts in thunderstorms carry raindrops upward into extremely cold areas of the atmosphere where they freeze into ice"; strong updrafts keep lifting the stones, which grow until the updraft "can no longer" hold them (NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory).
- **How big.** [Established] The largest US hailstone recovered fell in Aurora, Nebraska, on 22 June 2003, 7 inches (about 18 cm) across; the 1970 Coffeyville, Kansas, stone (5.7 inches; 1.67 pounds) remains the heaviest on record (NOAA NSSL).

### Points of awe
- An ordinary cumulus cloud can hold hundreds of tonnes of water (USGS).
- Hailstones grow by being thrown up and down inside a storm until they are too heavy to hold (NOAA NSSL).
- A hailstone 18 cm across fell in Nebraska in 2003 (NOAA NSSL).

### The reflection
The verse moves like a film: "Do you not see that Allah drives clouds? Then He brings them together, then He makes them into a mass, and you see the rain emerge from within it" (24:43). Driven, gathered, heaped, and then rain. Al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi follow the verbs closely: scattered pieces gathered, pressed together until they grow strong and dense.

Then the scene darkens: hail, sent down from mountain-like masses, striking "whom He wills" and turned away "from whom He wills"; lightning so bright it "almost takes away the eyesight". The same sky that gives rain can destroy a harvest in minutes. The verse does not hide that.

We now know clouds carry hundreds of tonnes of water and that hail is built by updrafts tossing ice up and down until it is too heavy to hold. None of that is in the verse. But it sharpens the question the verse asks: who drives the clouds? We can describe the updraft; we did not make it, and we cannot stop it. "And you are not its retainers" (15:22).

The next verse turns from storm to time: "Allah alternates the night and the day" (24:44). Both are offered as "a lesson for those who have vision". The rain, the hail, the light — all are in another's hands.

### Honest notes
- **No meteorology claimed.** The verses describe what anyone can watch. They do not predict the stages of a thunderstorm, and the commentators did not read them that way.
- **"Mountains [of clouds]".** Saheeh's bracket is a translator's choice. al-Tabari read the "mountains" as real mountains of hail in the sky or as hail in mountain-sized amounts; al-Farraʾ as the hail itself. The page should give these readings.
- **Estimates.** The 500-tonne figure is USGS's example for a 1 km³ cloud at 0.5 g/m³; real clouds vary.
- **"Fertilizing winds."** Ibn Kathir already gives the pollination reading of 15:22, so it is not a modern addition.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about these verses was read.

### Questions readers ask next
**What are the "mountains… within which is hail"?** al-Tabari gives two readings — mountains of hail created in the sky, or hail falling in mountain-like amounts — and al-Qurtubi adds al-Farraʾ's view that the mountains are the hail itself. "[Of clouds]" is the translator's addition.

**How does hail form?** Updrafts carry raindrops into very cold air where they freeze; they grow as they are lifted again and again, until they fall (NOAA NSSL).

**How much water is in a cloud?** About 500 tonnes in a 1 km³ cumulus cloud, by USGS's example.

**What are "fertilizing winds" (15:22)?** Ibn Kathir explains them as winds that pollinate as well as winds that bring rain clouds.

**Why does the verse say hail strikes "whom He wills"?** Because the same sky that brings rain can bring ruin; the verse places both in God's hand.

## S11 — The camel

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 88:17–21.
- al-Qurtubi, al-Tabari and al-Saʿdi on 88:17 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-qortobi-tabary-saadi/sura88-aya17.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) on 88:17–20, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-17-sora-88.html (as on the current page).
- Ali et al., "Effects of long-term dehydration on stress markers, blood parameters, and tissue morphology in the dromedary camel", *Frontiers in Veterinary Science* (2023), doi 10.3389/fvets.2023.1236425 (read for run 19).
- Schmidt-Nielsen, Schroter & Shkolnik, "Desaturation of exhaled air in camels", *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. B* 211 (1981) 305–319 (read for run 19).

### In brief
"Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created?" (88:17): the verse sends its first listeners to the animal they knew best, and asks them to look again.

### Verse note
"Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created? And at the sky - how it is raised? And at the mountains - how they are erected? And at the earth - how it is spread out?" (88:17–20). Sura al-Ghāshiya is Meccan. Before: the Hereafter (88:1–16); after: "So remind, [O Muhammad]; you are only a reminder" (88:21).

- *Al-ibil*: camels.
- *Kayfa khuliqat*: "how they are created".
- **The four sights.** Camel, sky, mountains, earth — what a traveller in Arabia saw all day.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari.** Qatada: when God described Paradise, the disbelievers wondered, and this verse came — pointing them to camels, part of their daily life. The judge Shurayh used to say «اخرجوا بنا ننظر إلى الإبل كيف خُلقت» ("come out with us, let us look at the camels, how they were created"). al-Tabari's own gloss: the camel kneels to be loaded and then rises with its load.

**al-Qurtubi.** al-Hasan: the camel was singled out because it eats date-stones and fodder and gives milk; asked why not the elephant, he said the Arabs were far from it. Qatada and Muqatil: «تبرك حتى يحمل عليها ثم تقوم» ("it kneels until it is loaded, then rises"). Some sages: «صبرها على احتمال العطش» ("its patience in bearing thirst"). A minority reading took *ibil* as rain-bearing clouds (al-Mubarrad; reported by Abu ʿAmr for a variant pronunciation); al-Mawardi gave both and preferred the camel, and al-Thaʿlabi found no basis for the clouds reading.

**Ibn Kathir.** "Indeed it is an amazing creation, and the way it has been fashioned is strange"; it "is extremely powerful and strong, yet gentle, carrying heavy loads. It allows itself to be guided by a weak rider."

**al-Saʿdi.** God «ذللها لمنافعهم الكثيرة» ("made it subservient for their many needs").

**What they share.** Strength with gentleness, kneeling to be loaded, patience with thirst. **What they did not say:** nothing about blood cells or kidneys; the "patience with thirst" is the closest they come to physiology.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "how they are created". These are modern findings set beside the verse.

- **Surviving without water.** [Established] In a 2023 study, camels given no water for 20 days lost 31% of their body weight, from a mean of 363.6 kg to 251.8 kg, and survived; their humps shrank (Ali et al., 2023).
- **Drinking it back.** [Established] Dehydrated camels "can drink up to 110 liters of water within 10 min", and store "the water for up to 24 h in the gut with slow absorption" (Ali et al., Introduction, citing earlier work).
- **Red blood cells.** [Established, background] "The RBCs of the dehydrated camels can swell up to 240% of the initial volume without rupture", against about 150% in other species (Ali et al., Introduction; the 240% is stated without a reference).
- **Saving water by storing heat.** [Established] A well-watered camel's temperature varies by less than 2 °C over a day; a dehydrated camel lets it swing from about 36 °C at night to about 41 °C by day, storing heat instead of sweating it away (Schmidt-Nielsen, Schroter & Shkolnik, 1981).
- **Saving water in the breath.** [Established] Camels cool and dry their exhaled air in the nose, recovering water that would otherwise be lost; the nasal surface exposed to breath exceeds 1,000 cm², against about 12 cm² in humans (Schmidt-Nielsen et al., 1981).

### Points of awe
- Camels survived losing 31% of their body weight in 20 days without water (Ali et al., 2023).
- They can drink up to 110 litres in ten minutes (Ali et al., Introduction).
- A camel's nose recovers water from its own breath (Schmidt-Nielsen et al., 1981).

### The reflection
"Then do they not look at the camels - how they are created?" (88:17). The first listeners had heard about Paradise and wondered; Qatada says the verse answered by pointing at their own camels. Look at what you already have.

The commentators saw strength that kneels. Qatada and Muqatil: it kneels to be loaded, then rises. Ibn Kathir: "extremely powerful and strong, yet gentle", guided "by a weak rider". Some sages noticed "its patience in bearing thirst".

Modern physiology has measured that patience: twenty days without water, nearly a third of the body's weight gone, and still alive; a hundred litres drunk in minutes; heat stored instead of sweated away; water saved from its own breath. None of this is in the verse. But it gives the old wonder new detail. "How they are created" turns out to have more answers than anyone guessed.

The verse then lifts our eyes: the sky raised, the mountains set, the earth spread — everything a traveller saw on a long road (88:18–20). And it ends with a task for the Prophet (pbuh): "So remind" (88:21). The camel is not proof by force. It is a reminder, offered to anyone who will look.

### Honest notes
- **No physiology in the verse.** The verse asks people to look; it does not describe the camel's kidneys or blood. The science on this page is modern and kept separate.
- **A figure to avoid.** Claims such as "200 litres in 3 minutes" circulate without solid sources. The sources read give "up to 110 liters of water within 10 min" (Ali et al.) and, on *Wikipedia*, "as much as 30 imperial gallons (140 litres) at a time".
- **"Camels" or "clouds"?** A minority classical reading took *ibil* as clouds (al-Mubarrad); al-Mawardi and the majority preferred the camel (al-Qurtubi).
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about 88:17 was read.

### Questions readers ask next
**Why does the verse mention camels, not another animal?** Because the Arabs knew them best and lived by them (Qatada, in al-Tabari). al-Hasan, asked about the elephant, said the Arabs were far from it (al-Qurtubi).

**What did the commentators find amazing in the camel?** Its strength with gentleness (Ibn Kathir), kneeling to be loaded and rising (Qatada, Muqatil), and its patience with thirst (al-Qurtubi).

**How long can a camel go without water?** In a 2023 study, 20 days, losing 31% of body weight (Ali et al.).

**How much can a camel drink at once?** Up to 110 litres in ten minutes after severe dehydration (Ali et al., Introduction).

**Could *ibil* mean clouds?** A minority said so (al-Mubarrad), but most commentators, including al-Mawardi, read it as camels (al-Qurtubi).

## S16 — Mountains as pegs

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 78:6–7, 16:15, 79:32–33, 13:3, 88:19.
- al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and *al-Muyassar* on 78:7 (Arabic), https://quranislam.github.io/tafsir/sura78-aya7.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) on 78:6–7 and 16:15, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-7-sora-78.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-15-sora-16.html (as on the current page).
- Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 3369 (hadithunlocked.com) — the report that the earth shook until the mountains were made (read for run 17).
- UC San Diego, Scripps IGPP, Lecture 2 "Gravity, Isostasy and Flexure" (after Kearey, Klepeis & Vine 2009) (read for run 19).
- USGS, *Seismicity of the Earth 1900–2010: Himalaya and vicinity*, Open-File Report 2010-1083-J (as on the current page).
- IslamQA, Question 223428 (as on the current page) — the popular claim.

### In brief
"Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7): mountains are offered as pegs that keep the earth a fit place to live.

### Verse note
"Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7), Meccan, the start of a list of signs in Sura al-Nabaʾ (78:6–16) offered as evidence for the Day of Judgement.

"And He has cast into the earth firmly set mountains, lest it shift with you, and [made] rivers and roads, that you may be guided" (16:15). "And the mountains He set firmly, As provision for you and your grazing livestock" (79:32–33). "And at the mountains - how they are erected?" (88:19).

- *Awtād*: "stakes", tent-pegs.
- *Rawāsī*: "firmly set" (mountains).
- *An tamīda bikum*: "lest it shift with you" — sway, tilt.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari on 78:7.** «والجبال للأرض أوتادا أن تميد بكم» ("the mountains as pegs for the earth, lest it sway with you").

**Ibn Kathir on 78:7.** «جعلها لها أوتادا أرساها بها وثبتها» ("He made them pegs for it, by which He anchored and fixed it"); in the abridged English, "so that it may be suitable for dwelling and not quake with those who are in it". On 16:15: mountains "make it stable and keep it from shaking".

***al-Muyassar*.** The mountains are firm, so that the earth does not shake with you.

**A weak report.** al-Tirmidhi 3369, from Anas: "When Allah created the earth, it started shaking. So He created the mountains…". al-Tirmidhi calls it *gharīb*; al-Albani grades it *ḍaʿīf* (weak). Nothing on this page rests on it.

**What they share, and what they did not say.** All read the mountains as fixing the earth so that it does not sway with its people — the earth as a stable home (78:6, "a resting place"). None speaks of roots beneath mountains or of plates; and none claims that mountains stop earthquakes, since the commentators lived with earthquakes.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "stakes" and "firmly set". These are modern findings set beside the verses.

- **Mountains have roots.** [Established] The crust "floats" on the denser mantle (isostasy). In the simple Airy model, with typical densities for water, crust and mantle (1.03, 2.8 and 3.3 g/cm³), the hidden root is about 5.6 times the height of the mountain: "r = 5.6 h" (Scripps lecture, after Kearey, Klepeis & Vine). So an 8 km mountain would need a root of about 45 km. Real roots vary.
- **How the roots were found.** [Established] Plumb lines near great mountains "did not deflect as much as expected", and surveys in India in the 1800s showed that the Himalaya is compensated by lighter material below (Scripps lecture).
- **But mountain belts are earthquake zones.** [Established] The Himalaya formed, and is still forming, as India and Eurasia converge "at a relative rate of 40–50 mm/yr"; "Northward underthrusting of India beneath Eurasia generates numerous earthquakes", making it "one of the most seismically hazardous regions on Earth" (USGS).

### Points of awe
- A mountain's hidden root, in the simple model, is more than five times as deep as the mountain is high (Scripps lecture).
- India is still pushing into Asia by 40–50 mm a year (USGS).
- Surveyors in 19th-century India found the Himalaya's pull on their plumb lines weaker than expected — the first clue to roots (Scripps lecture).

### The reflection
"Have We not made the earth a resting place? And the mountains as stakes?" (78:6–7). The image is a tent. A tent without pegs flaps and lifts in the wind; a pegged tent is a home. The earth, the verse says, has been pegged for us.

The commentators read it that way: the mountains anchor the earth "so that it may be suitable for dwelling" (Ibn Kathir), "lest it sway with you" (al-Tabari). Their point is home and safety, a world steady enough to live on, with "rivers and roads, that you may be guided" (16:15) and pasture "for you and your grazing livestock" (79:33).

Modern geology has found that mountains do have deep roots of lighter rock, more than five times their height in the simple model — "stakes" in a striking sense. It has also found that mountain belts are where the earth moves most: the Himalaya rises because India is still pushing north, and the region shakes often. A reader should hold both facts honestly.

What the verse asks is not a lecture in tectonics. It is a question — "Have We not…?" — and it is the first of a list of signs leading to the Day of Judgement. The steadiness we live on every day is given, not earned, and the One who gave it can also bring it to an end.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** IslamQA writes: "Geological research proves that the mountains are fixed firmly in the earth and that they stabilise the earth", and "The roots of the mountains beneath the surface of the earth are several times deeper than the height of that which appears above the surface" (Question 223428).
- **Weighing it.** *Roots:* true, and the Airy model (about 5.6 times the height) supports "several times deeper". *"Stabilise the earth":* not in the sense of preventing earthquakes; mountain belts are among the most earthquake-prone places on Earth (USGS). The classical commentators read the verses as the earth made a steady home, not as a claim about seismology.
- **The current page's numbers.** "About five times… so an 8 km mountain would have a root about 32 km deep" is inconsistent (32 is four times 8); with the source's 5.6, it is about 45 km (see run 19).
- **The weak report.** The report that the earth shook until the mountains were made (Tirmidhi 3369) is graded weak by al-Albani; it should not be presented as established.

### Questions readers ask next
**Do mountains really have roots?** Yes. Under isostasy, high mountains are balanced by thick roots of lighter crust; in the simple Airy model, about 5.6 times their height (Scripps lecture).

**Do mountains stop earthquakes?** No. Mountain belts such as the Himalaya are among the most earthquake-prone regions on Earth (USGS). The classical commentators read "stakes" as making the earth a steady home, not as a promise of no earthquakes.

**What does "lest it shift with you" mean?** That the earth does not sway with its people (al-Tabari; Ibn Kathir: so that it is "suitable for dwelling").

**Is there a hadith that the earth shook until God made the mountains?** There is a report in al-Tirmidhi (3369), but al-Tirmidhi calls it *gharīb* and al-Albani grades it weak.

**Why are mountains mentioned among the signs of Judgement Day?** Because 78:6–16 lists the gifts of a stable world — earth, mountains, pairs, sleep, night, day, sky, sun, rain — as signs that the One who made them will also judge (78:17).
