**Summary for Umut.**
- **M5:** "Faith is half patience and half gratitude" was not found as a hadith. What exists is "Patience is half of faith", a saying of the Companion Ibn Masʿud in al-Hakim (al-Dhahabi: sound; *mawqūf*, his own words, not the Prophet's). It should be credited that way.
- **M14:** "Seeking knowledge is a duty upon every Muslim" (Ibn Majah 224) is graded *ṣaḥīḥ* by al-Albani, but only through its other routes and only for that first sentence. Al-Bayhaqi said its "text is well known and its chains are weak", and Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi grades it weak. So it is a disputed grade, not a settled one.
- **M9:** "Do not betray those who betrayed you" (Abu Dawud 3535) is graded *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ* by al-Albani, while al-Bayhaqi criticised its chain. The title *al-Amīn* is in Musnad Ahmad 15504 ("The trustworthy one has come to you", at the rebuilding of the Kaʿba; Darussalam: chain *ṣaḥīḥ*).
- **M15:** Ibn Kathir says the speaker of 12:53 ("And I do not acquit myself") is the minister's wife, not Joseph. He calls this "the most viable" reading; al-Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim give the other view.

## M5 — Gratitude and patience
- **Limits:** "It is widely quoted; its authenticity has not yet been checked"; "The passage in al-Ghazali on 'two halves' and the hadith behind it have not yet been checked."
  **Verdict:** Checked — the saying is reworded and the limit can come off
  **Source:** al-Hakim, *al-Mustadrak* 3666 (hadithunlocked.com, under *al-Maṭālib al-ʿĀliya* 3469): from Ibn Masʿud, "Patience is half of faith" — graded sound by al-Dhahabi, classed as *mawqūf* (the Companion's own words). *Al-Maṭālib al-ʿĀliya* 3469 (Ibn Hajar), from al-Shaʿbi, with a suspended chain (*muʿallaq*): "Patience is half of faith, gratitude is two-thirds of faith, and certainty is all of faith" — no grade given. The wording "faith is two halves: half patience and half gratitude" was not found in the sources read.
  **New text:** The Companion ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿud said: "Patience is half of faith" (al-Hakim, *al-Mustadrak*; al-Dhahabi: sound). These are his words, not the Prophet's (pbuh). The popular form "faith is half patience and half gratitude" was not found with a sound chain, and al-Ghazali builds on the idea rather than on a graded hadith (al-Ghazali's own passage still to be read).

- **Limit:** Cregg and Cheavens (2021) not checked again.
  **Verdict:** Not consulted in this pass — stays. (The gratitude meta-analysis by Kirca et al., 2023, is already on the page.)

## M9 — Amāna: trust, honesty, keeping promises
- **Limit:** the grade of "Return the trust to those who entrusted you, and do not betray those who betrayed you".
  **Verdict:** Checked — limit can come off, with the dispute stated
  **Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 3535 (hadithunlocked.com), Abu Hurayra: "Pay the deposit to him who deposited it with you, and do not betray him who betrayed you." Al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*; al-Tirmidhi (as cited by al-Mundhiri): *ḥasan gharīb*; Abu Hatim al-Razi: only Sharik narrated it; al-Bayhaqi criticised the chain (Qays weak; Sharik not relied on by most).
  **New text:** "Pay the deposit to him who deposited it with you, and do not betray him who betrayed you" ({hadith:abudawud:3535}; al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*; al-Tirmidhi: *ḥasan gharīb*; al-Bayhaqi judged its chain weak).

- **Limit:** "the specific report of the title *al-Amīn*… has not yet been checked."
  **Verdict:** Checked — limit can come off
  **Source:** Musnad Ahmad 15504 (en.tohed.com): at the rebuilding of the Kaʿba, when the Quraysh disputed who would set the Black Stone, the Prophet (pbuh) arrived and «فَقَالُوا: أَتَاكُمْ الْأَمِينُ» — "The trustworthy one has come to you." Darussalam: «إسناده صحيح». (The page notes that its placement under al-Saʾib ibn Abi al-Saʾib is probably an editorial error.)
  **New text:** When the Quraysh were rebuilding the Kaʿba, before his prophethood, and disputed who would set the Black Stone, they said as he arrived: "The trustworthy one (*al-Amīn*) has come to you" (Musnad Ahmad 15504; Darussalam: chain *ṣaḥīḥ*).

## M14 — Knowledge
- **Limit:** "Its chain is graded *ḍaʿīf*… by the Darussalam editors; other scholars have judged it acceptable through its many chains, but that has not yet been checked."
  **Verdict:** Checked — limit can come off, with the dispute stated
  **Source:** Sunan Ibn Majah 224 (en.tohed.com), Anas: "Seeking knowledge is a duty upon every Muslim, and he who imparts knowledge to those who do not deserve it…". Al-Albani: «صحيح دون قوله وواضع العلم… فإنه ضعيف جدا» (*ṣaḥīḥ* except the second part, which is very weak); Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi: «ضعيف، إسناده ضعيف جدًا», and "all its chains are weak"; al-Bayhaqi: «هذا حديث متنه مشهور وأسانيده ضعيفة» ("its text is well known and its chains are weak").
  **New text:** "Seeking knowledge is a duty upon every Muslim" (Ibn Majah 224). Its grade is disputed: al-Albani accepted this first sentence as *ṣaḥīḥ* through its many routes, while al-Bayhaqi said its "text is well known and its chains are weak", and Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi grades it weak. The second half of the same report is very weak by all accounts.

## M15 — Self-discipline and character
- **Limit:** "Ibn Kathir and others differ on whether the speaker is Joseph or the woman; that discussion has not yet been consulted."
  **Verdict:** Checked (Ibn Kathir) — limit can come off
  **Source:** Ibn Kathir on 12:52–53 (abridged English, surahquran.com): the wife of al-ʿAziz speaks; this is "the most viable and suitable understanding for the continuity of the story and the meanings of Arabic speech"; "the first view is stronger and more obvious"; the view that Joseph speaks is given only by Ibn Jarir al-Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim, and Joseph "was not present at all during this time".
  **New text:** "And I do not acquit myself. Indeed, the soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil" (12:53). Ibn Kathir holds that the speaker is the minister's wife, admitting her fault before the king — "the most viable" reading of the story — while al-Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim report it as Joseph's words. Either way, the sentence is a confession that the self pulls towards wrong.
