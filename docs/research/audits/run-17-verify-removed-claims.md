## M10 — Orphans, the poor, travelers and neighbors
- **Claim:** Al-Qurtubi on 4:36: who counts as a neighbour; the "forty houses in each direction" report; whether non-Muslim neighbours are included.
  **Section:** scholars — How scholars have understood it
  **Verdict:** Confirmed
  **Source:** al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, on 4:36 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-tabary-qortobi-katheer/sura4-aya36.html: «الجار الجنب أي الغريب ؛ قاله ابن عباس»; Nawf al-Shami: «الجار ذي القربى المسلم والجار الجنب اليهودي والنصراني»; al-Qurtubi's own judgement: «مأمور بها مندوب إليها مسلما كان أو كافرا ، وهو الصحيح»; the three kinds of neighbour: «فأما الجار الذي له حق واحد هو الكافر له حق الجوار»; the report «ألا إن أربعين دارا جار» introduced only with «وروي» ("it is reported"), and al-Awzaʿi's and Ibn Shihab's view «أربعون دارا من كل ناحية». Ibn Kathir on the same page cites the three-neighbours hadith from al-Bazzar via Jabir, with «فجار مشرك لا رحم له».
  **New text:** Al-Qurtubi (d. 1273, Córdoba and Egypt), commenting on 4:36, explains "the neighbor farther away" (*al-jār al-junub*) as "the stranger", following Ibn ʿAbbas, and reports the early view of Nawf al-Shami that it means a Jewish or Christian neighbour. His own judgement: care for the neighbour is "commanded and recommended, whether the neighbour is a Muslim or a non-believer — and that is the correct view." He cites a hadith of three kinds of neighbour, in which the neighbour with one right "is the unbeliever: he has the right of neighbourhood". He also reports, without giving its chain, the saying "Forty houses are neighbours", and that al-Awzaʿi and Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri counted "forty houses in every direction". Ibn Kathir gives the three-neighbours hadith from al-Bazzar, through Jabir; its grade was not checked here, so nothing rests on it.

## S1 — The seed and the date stone split open
- **Claim:** Gong et al. (2005): the date's stored food is galactomannan in very thick cell walls; it is broken down mainly after the root has emerged.
  **Section:** How it works
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Gong, Bassel, Wang, Greenway & Bewley, "The emergence of embryos from hard seeds is related to the structure of the cell walls of the micropylar endosperm, and not to endo-β-mannanase activity", *Annals of Botany* 96(7):1165–1173 (2005), abstract, https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/124021: "Seeds of carob, Chinese senna, date and fenugreek are hard due to thickened endosperm cell walls containing mannan polymers"; "little to no thickening in the micropylar endosperm except in date seeds"; the wall-weakening enzyme "increased in activity only after emergence of the radicle from the seed."
  **New text:** The date stone is hard because its endosperm cell walls are thickened with mannan polymers, a stored carbohydrate; the main enzyme that breaks these walls down becomes active "only after emergence of the radicle from the seed" (Gong et al., *Annals of Botany*, 2005, a study of four hard-seeded species including the date).

## S7 — Two seas and a barrier
- **Claim:** Salinities "about 38.4–38.5" and "about 36.1", an interface "defined at salinity 37", an outflow of "about 0.7–1 Sverdrup" (Bryden et al.; *Science Advances*, 2018).
  **Section:** Honest notes

## S13 — Sleep as a sign
- **Claim:** "Human evidence is growing" for glymphatic clearance.
  **Section:** Honest notes
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Ringstad, Valnes, Dale, Pripp, Vatnehol, Emblem, Mardal & Eide, "Brain-wide glymphatic enhancement and clearance in humans assessed with MRI", *JCI Insight* 3(13):e121537 (2018): 17 people (8 reference, 9 with idiopathic normal pressure hydrocephalus); "in vivo evidence of access to all human brain subregions of a substance administered intrathecally"; clearance "delayed in the dementia cohort". ## S32 — The human being taught to speak
- **Claim:** How language evolved is "still debated"; the role of a lowered larynx.
  **Section:** Honest notes
  **Verdict:** Confirmed
  **Source:** CNRS press release on Boë et al., *Science Advances* (2019): the "laryngeal descent theory" held that "the larynx must be in a low position to produce differentiated vowels"; Boë and colleagues argue that "monkeys produce well differentiated proto-vowels" and that speech is "not therefore a question of anatomical variants but of control of articulators", so it "could have emerged" much earlier, "at least 20 million years" ago — a hedged conclusion against a long-standing view.
  **New text:** How speech evolved is still debated. An older view held that only a lowered larynx makes clear vowels possible; a 2019 study argued instead that monkeys can produce distinct vowel-like sounds and that the key is control of the mouth, not anatomy (Boë et al., *Science Advances*, 2019).
