# Run 18 — Primary sources for Ask (first pass: Q22, Q21, Q4, Q6, Q24)

Checked by Claude directly, 9 November 2026. **Partial run, by Umut's choice to work on the highest-impact items first.** This pass covers the most sensitive pages in the run — Banu Qurayza (Q22), slavery (Q21), and single claims on the Quran's preservation (Q4), women (Q6) and hijab (Q24) — and replaces *Wikipedia* with the primary text wherever one could be read. The other pages are listed at the end.

**Summary for Umut.**
- **The most important correction (Q22):** the page says one Qurayza woman was executed "reportedly for killing a Muslim by dropping a millstone", citing Abu Dawud. The hadith (Abu Dawud 2671, from ʿAʾisha) does not say that. It says only that she was killed "for an act I committed" / "I did a new act". The millstone story is a later comment by Ibn Hisham. An early commentator (al-Khattabi) gives a different reason. The page should say what each source says.
- **Q22 numbers:** now from the primary texts. Ibn Ishaq (in Ibn Hisham) says "six hundred or seven hundred", with the highest estimate "between eight hundred and nine hundred". Ibn Kathir on 33:26 says "between seven hundred and eight hundred". The 25-day siege is in Ibn Kathir.
- **Q4:** the Birmingham Quran's radiocarbon figure is "1456 ± 21 BP" on the University's own page, not "1465".
- **Q21:** Mohsen Kadivar explicitly says his view is *not* "technical abrogation", so the page's "abrogation by reason" misdescribes him. The Kecia Ali quotation was not found where it is said to be. Mauritania's 1981 abolition is confirmed, and its 2007 criminalisation added.

## Q22 — The Prophet's wars and Banu Qurayza
- **Claim:** "One woman was also executed, reportedly for killing a Muslim by dropping a millstone (Wikipedia, citing Abu Dawud and al-Mubarakpuri)."
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 2671 (ʿĀʾisha), hadithunlocked.com: "No woman of Banu Qurayzah was killed except one"; she said "I did a new act"; graded "Good" by al-Albani and «إسناده حسن» by Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾut. The commentary *ʿAwn al-Maʿbūd*, citing al-Khattabi, says her act was insulting the Prophet (pbuh). Ibn Hisham, *al-Sīra* (hadithunlocked.com, ibnhisham:1168): ʿĀʾisha's report «لَمْ يُقْتَلْ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ إلَّا امْرَأَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ»… «لِحَدَثِ أَحْدَثْتُهُ»; then Ibn Hisham's own note: «طَرَحَتْ الرَّحَا عَلَى خَلَّادِ بْنِ سُوَيْدٍ، فَقَتَلَتْهُ» ("she threw the millstone on Khallad ibn Suwayd and killed him").
  **New text:** One woman was also executed. ʿAʾisha (ra), who saw her before her death, says only that she was killed for "a new act" she had committed ({hadith:abudawud:2671}; al-Albani: *ḥasan*). Ibn Hisham (d. 833) adds that she was the one who threw a millstone on Khallad ibn Suwayd and killed him; the commentator al-Khattabi, as cited in *ʿAwn al-Maʿbūd*, says instead that she had insulted the Prophet (pbuh).

- **Claim:** "The later sources give: Ibn Ishaq, 600 or 700, with the highest estimate 800–900; al-Tabari, 600 or 700, up to 800–900; Ibn Kathir, 700–800 (Wikipedia, 'Siege', citing these sources)."
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (now from the primary texts, for Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Kathir)
  **Source:** Ibn Hisham, *al-Sīra*, ch. 67 (hadithunlocked.com, ibnhisham:1166), from Ibn Ishaq: «وَهُمْ سِتُّ مِئَةٍ أَوْ سَبْعُ مِئَةٍ، وَالْمُكْثِرُ لَهُمْ يَقُولُ : كَانُوا بَيْنَ الثَّمَانِ مِئَةٍ وَالتِّسْعِ مِئَةٍ», given with no chain of transmitters. Ibn Kathir on 33:26 (abridged English, surahquran.com): "There were between seven hundred and eight hundred of them." Al-Tabari's text was not read; hadithanswers.com (Mawlana Suhail Motala, 2015) cites al-Tabari, *Tārīkh* 1/515, for "six hundred or seven hundred".
  **New text:** Ibn Ishaq, in Ibn Hisham's edition, says: "They were six hundred or seven hundred; the one who gives the highest number says between eight hundred and nine hundred" — with no chain of transmitters for the figure. Ibn Kathir, on 33:26, gives "between seven hundred and eight hundred". The hadith collections give no number.

- **Claim:** "After the coalition withdrew, the Muslims besieged Qurayza for 25 days (Wikipedia, 'Siege')."
  **Section:** history — The siege and the verdict
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (primary source)
  **Source:** Ibn Kathir on 33:26: "laying seige to them for twenty-five days"; Saʿd ibn Muʿadh's verdict: "My judgement is that their fighters should be killed and their children and wealth should be seized."
  **New text:** After the coalition withdrew, the Muslims besieged Qurayza for twenty-five days (Ibn Kathir on 33:26).

- **Claim:** "Ibn Ishaq reports that they readmitted Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the leader of al-Nadir…"
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Corrected (narrowed)
  **Source:** Ibn Kathir on 33:26: Huyayy persuaded Kaʿb ibn Asad to break the covenant and "laid down the condition that if the Confederates went away without doing anything, he [ Huyay ] would join them in their stronghold and would share their fate." Ibn Ishaq's own text on this point was not read.
  **New text:** Ibn Kathir reports that Huyayy ibn Akhtab, leader of al-Nadir, persuaded Qurayza's leader Kaʿb ibn Asad to break their covenant, promising to join them in their fortress if the coalition left.

- **Claim:** Malik ibn Anas rejected Ibn Ishaq's stories about the Jews of Medina, "they were taken solely based on accounts by sons of Jewish converts" (as reported by *Wikipedia*, citing Arafat).
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Not found (not read)
  **Source:** Arafat (*JRAS*, 1976) could not be opened in this pass; Malik's remark was not found in a primary text.
  **New text:** none beyond the present attribution to Arafat.

## Q21 — Slavery
- **Claim:** "The Iranian scholar Mohsen Kadivar argues that slavery is 'no longer permissible in Islam' by 'abrogation by reason' (Wikipedia)."
  **Section:** (views)
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Mohsen Kadivar, "The Issue of Slavery in Contemporary Islam", trans. Sadiq Meghjee, Syed Ali & Syed Hadi (iqraonline.net, September 2018): "in our times where the reasonable people consider it to be detested and oppressive, it is forbidden and illegitimate"; "This does not imply technical abrogation of rulings (as understood in the jurisprudential context)"; such rulings are "temporary", laws "whose era has passed".
  **New text:** The Iranian scholar Mohsen Kadivar argues that slavery is now "forbidden and illegitimate", because "the reasonable people consider it to be detested and oppressive". He treats its earlier permission as a temporary ruling "whose era has passed", and says explicitly that this is not "technical abrogation" ("The Issue of Slavery in Contemporary Islam", 2018).

- **Claim:** "Mauritania abolished slavery in 1981, and slavery has been documented there since (Wikipedia)."
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (with an addition)
  **Source:** Anti-Slavery International, "Mauritania makes slavery a criminal offence" (9 August 2007): "Even though Mauritania abolished slavery in 1981"; criminalisation on 8 August 2007; "Thousands of people in Mauritania are enslaved as a result of being born into an established slave class."
  **New text:** Mauritania abolished slavery in 1981 but made it a crime only in 2007; at that time Anti-Slavery International reported that "Thousands of people in Mauritania are enslaved as a result of being born into an established slave class."

- **Claim:** Kecia Ali (cited by *Wikipedia*): "there has not been a strong internally developed critique of past or present slaveholding practices".
  **Section:** (Did Islam produce an abolitionist movement?)
  **Verdict:** Not found
  **Source:** Not on the Brandeis Feminist Sexual Ethics Project page for Kecia Ali; her published work containing the sentence was not identified or read.
  **New text:** none, until the passage is found in her own work.

- **Other Q21 claims** (owners' duties; Ottoman 1847; the Prophet's household; Saudi Arabia 1962; Hijaz 1854; Morrow on Mariya; Tunisian reformers and Ameer Ali; captives; Gordon and Clarence-Smith; *dhimmī* protection): not checked in this pass.

## Q4 — Was the Quran preserved?
- **Claim:** "The Birmingham leaves… were radiocarbon-dated at Oxford to '1465±21 years BP (before 1950), which corresponds with 95.4% confidence to the calendar years CE 568–645…'"
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** University of Birmingham, Cadbury Research Library, "The Birmingham Qur'an Manuscript", https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/TheBirminghamQuranManuscript: "1456 ± 21 BP"; "568 (95.4%) 645 calAD"; "Parts of Surahs 18 - 20"; the handwriting "was Hijazi"; dated by the "University of Oxford's Radiocarbon Accelerator Unit". The page's "1465" is not what the University's own page gives (the 2015 press release was not read).
  **New text:** The University of Oxford's Radiocarbon Accelerator Unit dated the parchment to 1456 ± 21 years before 1950, which corresponds, with 95.4% probability, to the years 568–645 CE (University of Birmingham, Cadbury Research Library). The date is the parchment's, not the writing's.

## Q6 — Does Islam treat women unequally?
- **Claim:** "Ibn ʿAbbas (ra), asked what 'without severity' means, said: 'the siwak (tooth-stick) and the like' (via al-Tabari, as reported by *Wikipedia*); Ibn Kathir reports him and al-Hasan al-Basri as requiring 'a beating that is not violent' or 'not severe', and cites a hadith from Muʿawiya ibn Hayda forbidding 'striking her face or cursing her' ({hadith:abudawud:2142})"
  **Section:** (fiqh / the hard verse 4:34)
  **Verdict:** Confirmed for Ibn Kathir and Abu Dawud 2142; Not found for the siwak report (al-Tabari not reached)
  **Source:** Ibn Kathir on 4:34 (abridged English, surahquran.com): "Ibn 'Abbas and several others said that the Ayah refers to a beating that is not violent"; "Al-Hasan Al-Basri said that it means, a beating that is not severe"; Muʿawiya ibn Hayda: "refrain from striking her face or cursing her" (Ibn Kathir gives no number). Sunan Abi Dawud 2142 (hadithunlocked.com): "do not strike her on the face, do not revile her or separate yourself from her except in the house"; al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*; Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾut: «إسناده حسن»; al-Daraqutni notes that one line of transmission is *mursal*.
  **New text:** Ibn Kathir reports that "Ibn 'Abbas and several others said that the Ayah refers to a beating that is not violent", and al-Hasan al-Basri "a beating that is not severe". The Prophet (pbuh), asked about a wife's rights, said: "do not strike her on the face, do not revile her or separate yourself from her except in the house" ({hadith:abudawud:2142}; al-Albani: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*). (The siwak report from Ibn ʿAbbas stays credited to al-Tabari via *Wikipedia* until al-Tabari's text is read.)

## Q24 — Hijab and coercion
- **Claim:** 2:256 was revealed, according to Ibn ʿAbbas (via Abu Dawud and al-Nasaʾi), when some Medinan families whose children had been raised Jewish wanted to keep them from leaving with the Banu Nadir; the verse forbade forcing them into Islam (Wikipedia, "Al-Baqarah 256").
  **Section:** (Who may enforce it, and how?)
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (primary source)
  **Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 2682 (sunnah.com; al-Albani: *ṣaḥīḥ*): Ibn ʿAbbas: "When the children of a woman (in pre-Islamic days) did not survive"… "When Banu an-Nadir were expelled (from Arabia), there were some children of the Ansar (Helpers) among them"… "So Allah the Exalted revealed; 'Let there be no compulsion in religion. Truth stands out clear from error.'" (The verse wording is the hadith translator's, not Saheeh International.)
  **New text:** Ibn ʿAbbas reports that before Islam some women of Medina whose children did not survive vowed to raise a child as a Jew; when the Banu Nadir were expelled, some children of the Ansar were among them, and their families wanted to keep them back — and 2:256 was revealed: "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion" ({hadith:abudawud:2682}; al-Albani: *ṣaḥīḥ*).
