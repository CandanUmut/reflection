## Q22 — The Prophet's wars and Banu Qurayza
- **Claim:** "One woman was also executed, reportedly for killing a Muslim by dropping a millstone (Wikipedia, citing Abu Dawud and al-Mubarakpuri)."
  **Section:** history
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Sunan Abi Dawud 2671 (ʿĀʾisha), hadithunlocked.com: "No woman of Banu Qurayzah was killed except one"; she said "I did a new act"; graded "Good" by al-Albani and «إسناده حسن» by Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾut. The commentary *ʿAwn al-Maʿbūd*, citing al-Khattabi, says her act was insulting the Prophet (pbuh). Ibn Hisham, *al-Sīra* (hadithunlocked.com, ibnhisham:1168): ʿĀʾisha's report «لَمْ يُقْتَلْ مِنْ نِسَائِهِمْ إلَّا امْرَأَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ»… «لِحَدَثِ أَحْدَثْتُهُ»; then Ibn Hisham's own note: «طَرَحَتْ الرَّحَا عَلَى خَلَّادِ بْنِ سُوَيْدٍ، فَقَتَلَتْهُ» ("she threw the millstone on Khallad ibn Suwayd and killed him").
  **New text:** One woman was also executed. ʿAʾisha (ra), who saw her before her death, says only that she was killed for "a new act" she had committed ({hadith:abudawud:2671}; al-Albani: *ḥasan*). Ibn Hisham (d. 833) adds that she was the one who threw a millstone on Khallad ibn Suwayd and killed him; the commentator al-Khattabi, as cited in *ʿAwn al-Maʿbūd*, says instead that she had insulted the Prophet (pbuh).

  **New text:** Ibn Ishaq, in Ibn Hisham's edition, says: "They were six hundred or seven hundred; the one who gives the highest number says between eight hundred and nine hundred" — with no chain of transmitters for the figure. ## Q2 — Free will
- **Claim:** The readiness potential began "approximately five hundred milliseconds before" the movement, the reported urge came about 200 ms before it, "so the brain signal led conscious awareness by roughly 300 ms (*Wikipedia*)."
  **Section:** (What Libet found)
  **Verdict:** Corrected
  **Source:** Libet, Gleason, Wright & Pearl, *Brain* 106(3):623–642 (1983), abstract: brain activity "clearly preceded by at least several hundred milliseconds the reported time of conscious intention to act"; when people did not plan ahead, the readiness potential "preceded the corresponding mean W value by an average of about 350 ms, and by a minimum of about 150 ms"; when some planning was reported, by "about 800 ms". (The 200 ms figure is not in the abstract.)
  **New text:** In Libet's 1983 study, when people moved on a sudden urge, the brain signal began on average about 350 milliseconds before the moment they reported first feeling the urge (Libet et al., *Brain*, 1983).

- **Claim:** (new) later work on deliberate choices.
  **Section:** (What Libet found / limits)
  **Verdict:** Addition (from a source read)
  **Source:** Maoz, Yaffe, Koch & Mudrik, *eLife* 8:e39787 (2019), 18 participants: "While we found the expected RPs for arbitrary decisions, they were strikingly absent for deliberate ones", "challenging the generalizability of studies that argue for no causal role for consciousness".
  **New text:** A 2019 study found the readiness potential for arbitrary choices but found it "strikingly absent" for deliberate, meaningful choices (Maoz et al., *eLife*, 2019, with 18 participants). Libet-style results may not apply to the choices that matter morally.

- **Claim:** Britannica calls *kasb* "one of the most vague theories in Islāmic theology".
  **Section:** summary / limits
  **Verdict:** Confirmed
  **Source:** *Britannica*, "Kasb": "as a mean between predestination and free will"; "indistinguishable from pure predetermination"; "one of the most vague theories in Islāmic theology".
  **New text:** no change.

## Q17 — Who created God?
- **Claim:** BGV shows that an expanding universe "cannot be infinite in the past but must have a past spacetime boundary" (*Wikipedia*).
  **Section:** (The Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem)
  **Verdict:** Confirmed in substance (primary source)
  **Source:** Borde, Guth & Vilenkin, *Physical Review Letters* 90:151301 (2003) (arXiv gr-qc/0110012): a spacetime that is "inflating -- or just expanding sufficiently fast" "must be incomplete in null and timelike past directions"; "a simple kinematical argument, requiring no energy condition". A 2023 paper (*Physical Review D* 107:044024, arXiv 2207.00955) calls the claim a "well-known conjecture" that "relies on specific bounds on the integral of the Hubble parameter" (abstract only).
  **New text:** Borde, Guth and Vilenkin (2003) showed that a universe that has on average been expanding "must be incomplete in null and timelike past directions" — in plain words, its history cannot be followed back forever. Their argument needs no assumption about the kind of energy in the universe. Some later physicists dispute how far the result reaches (e.g. *Physical Review D* 107:044024, 2023).

## Q37 — Murjiʾa

  **Verdict:** Confirmed (better source)
  **Source:** *Britannica*, "Murjiʾah": "one of the earliest Islamic sects to believe in the postponement (irjāʾ) of judgment on committers of serious sins"; "no one who once professed Islam could be declared kāfir (infidel), mortal sins notwithstanding"; the Kharijites "held the extreme view that serious sinners should be ousted from the community and that jihād ('holy war') should be declared on them".

## Q27 — The Byzantine prediction
- **Claim:** the Persians took Jerusalem "following a three weeks siege in 614"; Heraclius counter-attacked from 622; Nineveh "on 12 December 627" (*Wikipedia*).
  **Section:** (What is complicated about it)
  **Verdict:** Confirmed for 12 December (primary source); 614 confirmed; the three-week siege not found
  **Source:** Theophanes, *Chronicle*, trans.
