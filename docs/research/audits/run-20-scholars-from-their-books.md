# Run 20 — Scholars quoted from their own books

Checked by Claude directly, 9 November 2026. **Partial run, done in order of impact.** For each sentence, the scholar's own work was looked for. Where the "scholar" is a modern institution or author publishing on its own site (Dar al-Iftaʾ, SeekersGuidance, Yaqeen, the Mufti of the Federal Territory), that page *is* the primary source; it was opened and the quotation checked word for word. For hadith gradings, al-Tirmidhi's own remark was checked in a second presentation of the collection (en.tohed.com or quranx.com), beside hadithunlocked. Classical books on al-Maktaba al-Shamila could not be opened from here, so classical quotations reached only through a modern page are marked as such. Nothing in `src/` is changed.

**Summary for Umut.**
- **Confirmed word for word, from the author's own publication:**
  - Alalwani on apostasy (IIIT 2011, p. 41; his three early names on p. 7).
  - Dar al-Iftaʾ's hijab fatwa (no. 7272, 2014) and its apostasy fatwa (no. 8002).
  - Awad on *waswasa* (Yaqeen, 2017).
  - Sedick on *wilāyat al-ijbār* (SeekersGuidance, 2024).
  - The Yaqeen Aisha paper (2018).
  - The Malaysian Mufti on jewellery and the 85 g *niṣāb*.
  - The grades for Tirmidhi 2466, 3522 and 2517 and Nasaʾi 3104.
- **Corrections:**
  - **Q9:** the hadith's last phrase is "and alleviate your poverty" (*wa-asudda faqrak*), not "dissipate your meekness".
  - **S27:** Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi calls Tirmidhi 3118 weak because al-Aʿmash narrates it with "from" (*ʿanʿana*). That is not a charge of lying against Sayf ibn Muhammad, and al-Tirmidhi says only that Sayf's brother ʿAmmar is the more reliable of the two. The "accused of lying" line was not found.
  - **Q15:** the grade "its chain is good" for Ahmad 11133 is shown under the Darussalam edition. The attribution to al-Arnaʾut was not confirmed.
  - **Q8/Q13:** Awad says there is "little research", not that there are no controlled studies. The page's "*Limit:*" sentence is the page's own and should not be credited to her.
  - **Q20:** the Yaqeen Aisha paper is by an "Anonymous Guest Contributor". The page should not present it as an institutional position without saying so.
- **Still from a modern page only:** al-Nawawi's "whoever was not reached by the call of Islam is excused" (*Sharh Muslim* 2/188) — the Arabic and the page number are confirmed through IslamQA's Arabic answer, but not in the book itself. Al-Albani's "pure clear message" quotation has no traceable source (*Wikipedia* tags it "citation needed").

## Q7 — Is leaving Islam punishable by death?
- **Sentence:** **Taha Jabir Alalwani** (1935–2016): apostasy without "anything else deemed a criminal act… remains strictly between God and the individual".
  **Section:** Who holds the "not for belief alone" view today?
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (his own book)
  **Source:** Taha Jabir al-Alwani, *Apostasy in Islam: A Historical and Scriptural Analysis*, trans. Nancy Roberts (IIIT, 2011), ch. 2, p. 41: "As long as one's apostasy has not been accompanied by anything else that would be deemed a criminal act… it remains strictly between God and the individual, and is not the province of earthly rulers or anyone else." Ch. 1, p. 7: "'Umar ibn al-Khattab, Ibrahim al-Nakh'i, Sufyan al-Thawri and others did not support such a penalty." (The death date printed for al-Nakhaʿi on p. 9, "196 AH/811 CE", looks like a misprint and should not be copied without checking.)
  **New text:** Taha Jabir al-Alwani (1935–2016) wrote that as long as apostasy "has not been accompanied by anything else that would be deemed a criminal act… it remains strictly between God and the individual, and is not the province of earthly rulers" (*Apostasy in Islam*, 2011, p. 41).

- **Sentence:** **Ali Gomaa** (Grand Mufti of Egypt 2003–2013), writing in 2007: "can a person who is Muslim choose a religion other than Islam?"
  **Section:** Who holds the "not for belief alone" view today?
  **Verdict:** Confirmed (the institution's own text); the 2007 date is not on that page
  **Source:** Dar al-Iftaʾ al-Misriyya, fatwa no. 8002, "Can a person who is a Muslim choose a religion other than Islam?" (dar-alifta.org, dated on the site 3 March 2015): "If the case in Question is one of merely rejecting faith, then there is no worldly punishment"; society intervenes when "the crime of undermining the foundations of the society is added to the sin of apostasy"; it cites 2:256, 109:6 and 18:29.
  **New text:** Egypt's Dar al-Iftaʾ, under Ali Gomaa, answered the question "Can a person who is a Muslim choose a religion other than Islam?": "If the case in question is one of merely rejecting faith, then there is no worldly punishment"; the state steps in only when "the crime of undermining the foundations of the society is added to the sin of apostasy" (fatwa 8002). (Keep the 2007 date only with a source for it.)

## S27 — Gardens watered by one water
- **Sentence:** Tirmidhi 3118: al-Tirmidhi *ḥasan gharīb*; al-Albani *ḥasan*; Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi *ḍaʿīf*, "and one narrator in the chain, Sayf ibn Muḥammad, was accused of lying (hadithunlocked)".
  **Section:** A hadith Ibn Kathir cites
  **Verdict:** Corrected (the reason for the weak grade)
  **Source:** Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 3118 (en.tohed.com): al-Tirmidhi: «هَذَا حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ غَرِيبٌ», adding «وَسَيْفُ بْنُ مُحَمَّدٍ هُوَ أَخُو عَمَّارِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدٍ» and that ʿAmmar is «أَثْبَتُ» (more reliable) than Sayf; al-Albani: *ḥasan*; Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi: «ضعيف, الأعمش عنعن» (weak, because al-Aʿmash narrated it with "from").
  **New text:** Al-Tirmidhi graded it *ḥasan gharīb*, and al-Albani *ḥasan*. Zubayr ʿAli Zaʾi graded it weak, because one narrator, al-Aʿmash, reports it with the vague "from" (*ʿan*) ({hadith:tirmidhi:3118}). (Remove "accused of lying" unless a rijāl source is cited.)
