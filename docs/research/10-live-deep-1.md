## M1 — Justice

### Sources read
- Quran 4:135, 5:8, 16:90 (Saheeh International).
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English, Quran.com), on 4:135.
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 2444, 2447, 3475.
- Earlier research, `01-batch-1.md` (M1): al-Shatibi's *maqasid*; Ibn al-Qayyim; procedural-justice research; Arab Barometer.

### In brief
Justice in the Quran is not only for others: it is "even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives" (4:135), and even toward people you dislike (5:8). The Prophet (pbuh) refused to spare even the powerful from the law.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Justice starts with yourself.** "O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice, witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives. Whether one is rich or poor, Allah is more worthy of both. So follow not [personal] inclination, lest you not be just" (4:135). Ibn Kathir explains: say the truth when you are asked, "even if harm will effect you as a consequence", and do not let the blame of anyone stop you.

**Justice even toward those you dislike.** "Do not let the hatred of a people prevent you from being just. Be just; that is nearer to righteousness" (5:8). The verse expects that we will have enemies, and still asks for fairness toward them.

**Justice with goodness.** "Indeed, Allah orders justice and good conduct and giving to relatives and forbids immorality and bad conduct and oppression" (16:90). Justice (*'adl*) gives each their due; good conduct (*ihsan*) gives more than is due. The Quran asks for both.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Help the oppressor too — by stopping him.** The Prophet (pbuh) said: "Help your brother, whether he is an oppressor or he is an oppressed one." People asked how one helps an oppressor. He said: "By preventing him from oppressing others" {hadith:bukhari:2444}.

**Oppression has a cost beyond this life.** "Oppression will be a darkness on the Day of Resurrection" {hadith:bukhari:2447}.

**No exemption for the powerful.** When a woman of a noble tribe stole and people sought to have her spared, the Prophet (pbuh) refused, warning that earlier nations were ruined because they punished the weak and let the noble go, and said he would apply the law even to his own daughter Fatima {hadith:bukhari:3475}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Justice is the aim of the law.** In the earlier research for this page, two classical voices stand out. Al-Shatibi (d. 1388) described the law as serving its objectives (*maqasid*), such as protecting life, mind, faith, family and property. Ibn al-Qayyim (d. 1350) went further: wherever justice is found, God's law is there. Both mean that a ruling which produces injustice has missed its own purpose.

#### practice — In daily life
**Be fair where it costs you.** Tell the truth in a dispute even when it hurts your side (4:135). Give a fair review to someone you dislike (5:8). When a friend wrongs someone, "help" him by stopping him {hadith:bukhari:2444}. At work and at home, apply the same rule to the people you like and the people you don't.

#### research — What research shows
**Fair processes build trust.** Research on procedural justice links fair processes — being heard, consistent rules, respectful treatment — to trust in institutions, even when people do not get the outcome they wanted. [Established]

#### heart — For the one who was treated unjustly
**Your claim is not lost.** The Quran commands justice and forbids oppression (16:90), and the Prophet (pbuh) called oppression "a darkness" for the one who does it {hadith:bukhari:2447}. Seeking justice through fair means is not a lack of patience; it is what these verses ask for.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does justice mean treating everyone the same?** Justice gives each person their due; good conduct (*ihsan*) can give more. The verse joins the two (16:90).

**What if being just harms my family?** 4:135 names that exact case — "even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives" — and asks for the truth anyway.

**Is justice only for Muslims?** No: 5:8 asks for justice even toward a people you hate.

### Honest limits
Muslim societies do not always live this teaching: surveys such as the Arab Barometer show falling trust in religious leaders. The page states the ideal and its sources; it does not claim the ideal is lived everywhere.

## M2 — Mercy

### Sources read
- Quran 3:159, 7:156, 21:107 (Saheeh International).
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English, Quran.com), on 3:159.
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 3467, 5997, 6000, 6013; Muslim 1955, 2593, 2752; Tirmidhi 1924.
- Earlier research, `01-batch-1.md` (M2).

### In brief
God's "mercy encompasses all things" (7:156), and the Prophet (pbuh) was sent "as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107). Mercy in Islam is not softness alone: it is care that reaches people, animals, and even the way a task is done.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Mercy encompasses everything.** "My mercy encompasses all things" (7:156). The Prophet (pbuh) is described as sent "as a mercy to the worlds" (21:107).

**Gentleness is how hearts are won.** "So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. So pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter" (3:159). Ibn Kathir explains: "With Allah's mercy you became this kind" (quoting Qatada), and al-Hasan al-Basri said this is "the description of the behavior that Allah sent Muhammad with". Note what the verse pairs with mercy: pardon, prayer for the other, and consultation.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Mercy comes in a hundred parts.** God made mercy in a hundred parts and sent one into the world; from it creatures are merciful to one another {hadith:bukhari:6000}, and ninety-nine are kept for the Day of Resurrection {hadith:muslim:2752}.

**Mercy is received by those who give it.** "He who is not merciful to others, will not be treated mercifully" {hadith:bukhari:6013}. "The merciful are shown mercy by Ar-Rahman. Be merciful on the earth, and you will be shown mercy from Who is above the heavens" {hadith:tirmidhi:1924}.

**Tenderness with children.** When the Prophet (pbuh) kissed his grandson al-Hasan, a man said he had ten children and had never kissed one. The Prophet (pbuh) looked at him and said that whoever is not merciful will not be shown mercy {hadith:bukhari:5997}.

**Even to an animal.** A woman was forgiven because she gave water to a dog dying of thirst {hadith:bukhari:3467}.

**Gentleness in everything.** "Allah is kind and He loves kindness and confers upon kindness which he does not confer upon severity" {hadith:muslim:2593}. "Allah has enjoined goodness to everything" — even an animal being slaughtered must be spared needless suffering {hadith:muslim:1955}.

#### practice — In daily life
**Make mercy concrete.** Speak gently when correcting someone (3:159). Hold and kiss your children {hadith:bukhari:5997}. Give water and shade to animals {hadith:bukhari:3467}. Before a hard conversation, ask: will this make the other person come closer or "disband" (3:159)?

#### heart — For the one who finds mercy hard
**Mercy is learned.** The verse itself says the Prophet's (pbuh) gentleness was "by mercy from Allah" (3:159). If you were raised with harshness, you can still ask for, and practise, a softer heart — mercy is promised to those who show it {hadith:bukhari:6013}.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does mercy mean never being firm?** No. The same verse that praises gentleness ends with decision and trust in God (3:159). Mercy shapes how firmness is used.

**Is mercy only for Muslims?** The verse says "the worlds" (21:107), and the hadith speak of "others", children and animals.

**What is *ihsan*?** Doing things beautifully and well; the hadith applies it "to everything" {hadith:muslim:1955}.

### Honest limits
Harsh preaching and cold religious authority contradict 3:159, which says harshness drives people away. The page describes the teaching; it does not claim every Muslim community lives it.

## M3 — Parents and family

### Sources read
- Quran 17:23–24, 31:14–15 (Saheeh International).
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English, Quran.com), on 17:23.
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 2620, 5971, 5985; Nasai 3104; Tirmidhi 3895.
- Earlier research, `01-batch-1.md` (M3): maqasid and lineage; family cohesion research.

### In brief
Kindness to parents comes right after worshipping God in the Quran (17:23). It is owed even to parents of another faith, but obedience stops where they command wrong (31:15). And the best person is "the best to his wives" {hadith:tirmidhi:3895}.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Not even "uff".** "Your Lord has decreed that you not worship except Him, and to parents, good treatment. Whether one or both of them reach old age [while] with you, say not to them [so much as], 'uff,' and do not repel them but speak to them a noble word. And lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy and say, 'My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up [when I was] small'" (17:23–24). Ibn Kathir: do not let them hear anything offensive from you, "not even say 'Uff!' which is the mildest word of disrespect".

**The mother's burden is named.** "His mother carried him, [increasing her] in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years. Be grateful to Me and to your parents" (31:14).

**Kindness without obedience in wrong.** "But if they endeavor to make you associate with Me that of which you have no knowledge, do not obey them but accompany them in [this] world with appropriate kindness" (31:15). Two duties stay separate: you do not obey a command to do wrong, and you do not stop being kind.

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Your mother, three times.** Asked who most deserves good companionship, the Prophet (pbuh) said "Your mother" three times, then "your father" {hadith:bukhari:5971}.

**Paradise is beneath her feet.** When a man wanted to go to battle, the Prophet (pbuh) asked whether he had a mother, and said: "Then stay with her, for Paradise is beneath her feet" {hadith:nasai:3104}.

**A parent of another faith.** Asma' bint Abu Bakr (ra) asked whether she should receive her mother, who was not Muslim; the Prophet (pbuh) told her to keep good relations with her {hadith:bukhari:2620}.

**Family ties widen life.** "Whoever is pleased that he be granted more wealth and that his lease of life be prolonged, then he should keep good relations with his Kith and kin" {hadith:bukhari:5985}.

**The measure at home.** "The best of you is the best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives" {hadith:tirmidhi:3895}.

#### practice — In daily life
**Small, regular care.** Call, visit, listen. Speak to an ageing parent with a "noble word" (17:23), especially when they repeat themselves. Keep ties with relatives even when it takes effort {hadith:bukhari:5985}. At home, let your family be the first to see your best character {hadith:tirmidhi:3895}.

#### research — What research shows
**Close families help young people.** Family cohesion is associated with adolescent wellbeing. [Established, correlational] The studies show a link, not that cohesion causes wellbeing on its own.

#### heart — For the one with a hard family
**Kindness is not submission to harm.** 31:15 separates kindness from obedience in wrong. If a parent is abusive, protecting yourself is not disobedience to God; seek safe help. Kindness can sometimes only be a prayer: "My Lord, have mercy upon them" (17:24).

### Questions readers ask next
**Must I obey my parents in everything?** No: not in wrongdoing (31:15). The Quran asks for kindness always, and obedience in what is right.

**What about a parent who is not Muslim?** The Prophet (pbuh) told Asma' (ra) to keep good relations with her non-Muslim mother {hadith:bukhari:2620}.

**Is family loyalty more important than fairness?** No: justice is owed "even if it be against … parents and relatives" (4:135; see the page on justice).

### Honest limits
"Family honour" is sometimes used to control, especially women and girls. That is not what these verses command: they ask for kindness and set limits on obedience (31:15). The page does not claim families always live up to them.

## M4 — Ethics of speech

### Sources read
- Quran 49:11–12 (Saheeh International).
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English, Quran.com), on 49:12.
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 10, 6094, 6475, 6478; Muslim 2589.
- Earlier research, `01-batch-1.md` (M4): al-Ghazali on the "calamities of the tongue".

### In brief
The Quran forbids mocking, insulting nicknames, suspicion, spying and backbiting (49:11–12). The Prophet (pbuh) gave a simple rule: "talk what is good or keep quiet" {hadith:bukhari:6475}.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**No mockery, no insulting names.** "Let not a people ridicule [another] people; perhaps they may be better than them … And do not insult one another and do not call each other by [offensive] nicknames" (49:11).

**Suspicion, spying, backbiting.** "Avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin. And do not spy or backbite each other. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother when dead? You would detest it" (49:12). Ibn Kathir explains that this forbids suspicion without foundation, and quotes 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (ra): "Never think ill of the word that comes out of your believing brother's mouth, as long as you can find a good excuse for it."

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Good words or silence.** "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should talk what is good or keep quiet" {hadith:bukhari:6475}.

**What backbiting is.** "Do you know what is backbiting?" … it is talking about your brother in a way he would dislike; and if what you say is false, it is slander {hadith:muslim:2589}.

**One word can lift or sink.** A person may say a word that pleases God without thinking much of it and be raised by it, and a careless word that displeases God and fall by it {hadith:bukhari:6478}.

**Truth builds character.** "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. And a man keeps on telling the truth until he becomes a truthful person" {hadith:bukhari:6094}.

**Safety from your tongue.** "A Muslim is the one who avoids harming Muslims with his tongue and hands" {hadith:bukhari:10}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**A whole book on the tongue.** Al-Ghazali (d. 1111) gave a book of his *Ihya'* to the "calamities of the tongue", listing about twenty, from lying and backbiting to excessive joking and needless argument (earlier research for this page).

#### practice — In daily life
**Before you speak or post.** Three questions: Is it true {hadith:bukhari:6094}? Would the person dislike it if it were said in their absence {hadith:muslim:2589}? Is it good, or is silence better {hadith:bukhari:6475}? Online speech counts as speech: a post can be backbiting, mockery (49:11) or spying (49:12).

#### heart — For the one hurt by words
**Your hurt is real.** The Quran compares backbiting to eating a dead brother's flesh (49:12): harm by words is taken seriously. And for the one who spoke: the verse ends "Allah is Accepting of repentance and Merciful" (49:12); apologise, correct the record, and speak well of the person.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is it backbiting if it's true?** Yes, if the person would dislike it; if it is false, it is worse — slander {hadith:muslim:2589}.

**Can I warn someone about a person's behaviour?** The hadith defines backbiting; scholars discuss exceptions such as warning of real harm or seeking justice. (The sources on this page do not set out those exceptions in detail.)

**Does this apply to online comments?** The verses speak of ridicule, nicknames and backbiting without limiting the medium (49:11–12).

### Honest limits
Online, doubters and minorities are often abused in religious language. That contradicts 49:11–12. This page states the teaching; it does not claim believers always follow it.

## M5 — Gratitude and patience

### Sources read
- Quran 2:153, 14:7, 39:10 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 1302, 4837; Muslim 2963, 2999; Abu Dawud 4811.
- Earlier research, `01-batch-1.md` (M5): al-Ghazali; Cregg & Cheavens (2021); the 2023 meta-analysis of gratitude interventions.

### In brief
"If you are grateful, I will surely increase you" (14:7); "Allah is with the patient" (2:153). The Prophet (pbuh) described the believer as grateful in ease and patient in hardship, and good in both {hadith:muslim:2999}.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Gratitude grows.** "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe" (14:7).

**Patience has company.** "Seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient" (2:153). "Indeed, the patient will be given their reward without account" (39:10).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Two halves of one life.** "Strange are the ways of a believer for there is good in every affair of his": in delight he thanks God, in trouble he endures patiently, and there is good for him in both {hadith:muslim:2999}.

**Gratitude in action.** The Prophet (pbuh) prayed at night until his feet cracked. Asked why, since he was forgiven, he said: "Shouldn't I love to be a thankful slave?" {hadith:bukhari:4837}.

**Thank people too.** "He who does not thank the people is not thankful to Allah" {hadith:abudawud:4811}.

**Look in the right direction.** When you look at someone above you in wealth or body, also look at someone below you {hadith:muslim:2963}.

**Patience at the first blow.** "The real patience is at the first stroke of a calamity" {hadith:bukhari:1302}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Faith in two halves.** Al-Ghazali described faith as half patience and half gratitude (earlier research for this page).

#### practice — In daily life
**A short daily habit.** Name three specific gifts each day; thank one person by name {hadith:abudawud:4811}. When a loss comes, give yourself the first moment to turn to God (2:156; {hadith:bukhari:1302}), and then seek help — patience is not doing nothing.

#### research — What research shows
**Gratitude exercises help a little.** Cregg and Cheavens (2021) found small effects of gratitude interventions on depression (g = −0.29 after the intervention, −0.23 later), smaller against active controls. A 2023 meta-analysis (23 studies, 6,745 people) found g = 0.22 against neutral controls and 0.12, not significant, against active ones. [Established, small] Gratitude is good practice, not a cure.

#### heart — For the one told to "just be patient"
**Patience is not silence about harm.** The Quran praises patience, and it also commands justice and forbids oppression (16:90). "Be patient" must never be used to keep someone in abuse. Patience with what cannot be changed, and action against what can.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is it wrong to feel sad if I am patient?** No. The Prophet (pbuh) wept at his son's death (see the question on suffering); patience is about what you hold on to, not about not feeling.

**Does gratitude mean pretending everything is fine?** No: the hadith asks for gratitude in delight and patience in trouble {hadith:muslim:2999} — two different responses to two different things.

**Do gratitude exercises work?** A little, on average (see "What research shows").

### Honest limits
"Be patient" is sometimes used to silence victims of abuse or injustice. That misuses the teaching. The research on gratitude shows small effects, not large ones.
