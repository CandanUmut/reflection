## A5. Verification — Nişancı's "11%"

**Finding [Established]:** both reports are correct; they describe **two different survey items**.

1. **Non-belief in God: 11% of 18–24-year-olds.** From the "Allah inancı" question. National distribution: 85.7% "I know God exists, no doubt"; 8.6% "I believe despite some doubts"; 2.5% agnostic; 1.7% "I don't believe in God but there is a supernatural power"; ~1.5% "no belief in God". The report's 5.7% "non-belief" is the last three combined. VOA Türkçe reports 11% of 18–24s "say they do not believe in God" versus 2% at 65+; Euronews Türkçe reports the same 11%, with 6% at 25–34. So the 11% is **not atheists alone**.
2. **Closeness to a "deist" identity: 11% of 18–24-year-olds.** From a separate identity-closeness battery: "deist" 4% at 65+ and 11% at 18–24; "atheist" 2% and 5%. Nationally, "atheist" (4%), "deist" (8%) and "Alevi" (9%) were the least-embraced identities.

**Why it matters:** feeling close to a label is much weaker than holding deist beliefs. The report's belief question puts deism-like belief under 2%. Nişancı told VOA the debate about deism spreading is "in one respect artificial", reflecting sociological rejection of *observed religiosity* rather than philosophical deism.

**Method caveats:** fieldwork December 2021–May 2022; 1,942 adults; 12 NUTS-1 regions (35 provinces). The report claims random sampling; VOA says respondents used an "electronic link". The methods section reconciles this: respondents were approached randomly face-to-face, then sent a link or QR card (face-to-face interviews for those without devices); 2,453 links, 1,942 completed (79%). A 2024 critique by Mustafa Sarmış in *Din Sosyolojisi Araştırmaları* (4/7: 63–79) faults merged categories, reliance on 1–3-point differences, leading subheadings, and undefined terms.

**Site wording:** "In a 2021–22 Turkish survey, about 1 in 9 adults aged 18–24 gave a non-belief answer about God, and a similar share felt close to the label 'deist'. Most young people still described themselves as believers."

## Q9 — Why does God need our worship?

**1. Steelmanned.** If God is perfect and self-sufficient, He needs nothing, so commanding worship seems pointless or a need for praise. A human demanding constant praise would seem insecure.

**2. Why people ask.** Intellectual: divine self-sufficiency (ghinā) versus commands. Emotional: worship felt as fear-driven burden. Social: authoritarian images of God and of religious figures.

**3. Short answer.** The Quran agrees God does not need worship: people are the needy ones, God the self-sufficient (35:15). Worship serves the worshipper, as medicine serves health. Prayer, remembrance and gratitude reorient a person from ego toward truth. A God inviting worship for our sake differs from a human demanding praise for his own.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Verses:* 51:56 ("I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me") is followed by "I want no provision from them" (51:57); 39:7; 31:12.
- *Hadith:* ḥadīth qudsī, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2577: all creation's piety adds nothing to God's kingdom; all its wickedness takes nothing away.
- *Classical:* Ibn ʿAbbās (via al-Ṭabarī) glosses "to worship Me" as "to know Me". Al-Rāzī: creation's purpose is the creature's perfection. Al-Ghazālī (Iḥyāʾ, Kitāb al-Maḥabba): worship is the path to love and knowledge of God, the soul's highest happiness.
- *Kalam:* Ashʿarīs deny God acts for a purpose completing Him; Māturīdīs affirm wisdom (ḥikma) for creatures' benefit. Both agree He gains nothing.
- *Modern:* Said Nursi (Sözler) on worship as humanity's true duty and mirror of the divine Names; Tim Winter on worship as healing the fractured self.

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Gratitude fits goodness whether or not the recipient benefits. Objection: why obligatory with consequences? Response: consequences follow the soul's state (79:40–41); neglect is self-harm. Residue: wrath language can feel personal; read through "My mercy outweighs My wrath" (Bukhārī 7404).

**6. Scientific perspective.** [Debated, modest] Gratitude and contemplative practices show small-to-moderate wellbeing associations: evidence that practices can help, not proof of theology.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Fear-based God images make worship feel like appeasement; reframe as relationship. Intrusive fear of invalid worship may be vesvese; where it impairs life, professional help is appropriate.

**8. Honest note.** The Quran says worship benefits humans but does not explain every form; some acts are taʿabbudī. "Science proves prayer heals" overstates correlational data.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) "Two arrows" card: worship goes up, benefit returns, with 35:15. (b) Nursi's mirrors: mirrors gain light, the sun loses nothing. (c) Slider: "needy king" vs "doctor prescribing medicine".

**10. Key sources.** Quran 51:56–57, 35:15; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2577; al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī on 51:56; al-Ghazālī, Iḥyāʾ; Said Nursi, Sözler; Elmalılı Hamdi Yazır, Hak Dini Kur'an Dili; S.H. Nasr, The Heart of Islam.

## Q10 — Why five daily prayers, and why do I feel nothing?

**1. Steelmanned.** Five fixed prayers in a foreign language can feel mechanical. If I feel nothing, isn't it hollow ritual, even hypocrisy?

**2. Why people ask.** Ritual seen as anti-rational; spiritual dryness and guilt; prayer taught as duty without meaning. TİDA finds only about one in five under-25s in Türkiye pray.

**3. Short answer.** Feeling nothing is common and has been discussed for a thousand years; it doesn't prove prayer empty. Prayer is first a rhythm of returning to God (20:14), with or without emotion. Presence of heart (khushūʿ) grows with understanding the words, slowing down and patience. Keeping the rhythm while dry is fidelity, not hypocrisy.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Verses:* 20:14; 29:45; 23:1–2; 107:4–5.
- *Five times:* the number comes from the Miʿrāj hadith (Bukhārī 349; Muslim 162); the Quran indicates times (11:114; 17:78; 30:17–18). Some Turkish Quranists argue for three [Debated].
- *Classical:* al-Ghazālī (Asrār al-Ṣalāt) lists six inner meanings: presence, understanding, reverence, awe, hope, shame; a prayer without presence fulfils the legal duty but is spiritually thin. Ibn al-Qayyim describes five grades of praying people.
- *Sufi:* al-Qushayrī on qabḍ and basṭ normalizes dry phases.

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Habit theory (Aristotle; Miskawayh): virtues form by repeated acts before they are felt. Objection: ritual without feeling is inauthentic. Response: authenticity is long commitment, as in marriage.

**6. Scientific perspective.** [Debated] Structured ritual can lower anxiety under uncertainty; studies of Muslim prayer show physiological changes in small samples.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** If "feeling nothing" covers everything (food, friends), suggest a mental-health check. Repeating ablution or fearing invalid prayer is classic vesvese; the classical rule ignores excessive doubt after finishing.

**8. Honest note.** The five-count rests on hadith and practice, not an explicit Quranic number. No technique guarantees feeling.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Day ring of five prayers tracking real solar angles for the user's city. (b) Word-by-word al-Fātiḥa. (c) "Dry seasons" tree keeping roots in winter.

**10. Key sources.** Quran 20:14, 23:1–2; Bukhārī 349; Muslim 162; al-Ghazālī, Iḥyāʾ; Ibn al-Qayyim, al-Wābil al-Ṣayyib; al-Qushayrī, al-Risāla; TİDA (2023); Diyanet İlmihal.

## Q11 — Are hadith reliable? (including "Kuran İslamı")

**1. Steelmanned.** Canonical collections came two centuries after the Prophet; fabrication happened, as classical scholars admit. If a ṣaḥīḥ report like Aisha's marital age can be argued by an Oxford thesis to be an 8th-century formulation, why trust any? Isn't "Quran alone" more faithful?

**2. Why people ask.** Western source-criticism (Goldziher, Schacht, Juynboll) and Quranist media; morally troubling hadith; in Türkiye, "Kuran İslamı" offers a rational-seeming Islam and refuge from traditional authorities.

**3. Short answer.** Hadith are neither all reliable nor all fabricated. Muslim scholars built an elaborate testing system and rejected thousands of reports. Modern methods have dated many hadith to the 1st Islamic century and challenged particular ones. The Quran commands obeying the Messenger (4:59, 59:7), and how to pray depends on practice, so "Quran alone" has serious problems. Honest position: a graded, critically examined body of evidence.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Quran:* 4:59; 59:7; 16:44.
- *Classical method:* isnād criticism plus matn criticism; Ibn al-Qayyim's al-Manār al-Munīf lists content signs of fabrication.
- *Schools:* Ḥanafīs weighed solitary (āḥād) reports against principles; Māturīdīs do not treat āḥād as certain in creed; Atharīs give hadith wider scope. Shiʿis have a parallel corpus (al-Kāfī) with its own grading.
- *Turkish academy:* DergiPark studies trace Quran-alone views to some Muʿtazila positions and modern "Ahl al-Qurʾān", arguing that reducing the Prophet to a "postman" contradicts the Quran, while Turkish faculties also develop matn criticism.

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Skeptic: long oral transmission plus politics means low reliability. Response: early written collections (Hammām's ṣaḥīfa, the Muwaṭṭaʾ), multiple chains, and Motzki's isnād-cum-matn analysis (ICMA) dating material to the late 1st century AH. Counter: Joshua Little's Oxford DPhil (2022/23) used ICMA to argue Hishām b. ʿUrwa formulated the Aisha marital-age wording in Iraq in the mid-8th century; Muslim respondents (ICRAA) contest his steps, and even sympathetic critics say he missed a corroborating al-Zuhrī transmission. The debate concerns *specific reports*.

**6. Scientific perspective.** Memory research: formal recitation cultures transmit with high fidelity; casual anecdote drifts.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Quranism is often relief from troubling texts. Acknowledge that, then show classical tools (grading, context, evidence hierarchy) that keep the Sunna.

**8. Honest note.** Some ṣaḥīḥ hadith are contested by serious academics; classical grading is strong, not infallible. Avoid "every Bukhārī report is certain" and "all hadith are fabricated".

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Interactive isnād tree converging on a "common link", toggling classical vs ICMA views. (b) Grading spectrum card, mutawātir to mawḍūʿ. (c) Timeline from the Prophet to Bukhārī with early ṣaḥīfas.

**10. Key sources.** J.J. Little, The Hadith of ʿĀʾišah's Marital Age (Oxford DPhil); ICRAA, "The Age of Aisha"; "Teori ile Güncel Arasında Hadis/Sünnet İnkârı" (DergiPark); "Tarihten Günümüze Kur'an ile Yetinme Söyleminin Kritiği"; Kâmil Çakın, Hadis İnkârcıları; J.A.C. Brown, Hadith; H. Motzki, Die Anfänge der islamischen Jurisprudenz; TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, "Hadis".

## Q12 — Are "scientific miracles" in the Quran real?

**1. Steelmanned.** Popular media claim the Quran predicted embryology, the Big Bang, even the speed of light. If true, it proves divine origin; if forced, it makes Islam look dishonest.

**2. Why people ask.** Viral videos (Zaghloul El-Naggar, Harun Yahya), debunking videos, and doubters who feel misled when a claim collapses.

**3. Short answer.** The Quran invites reflection on nature, and many verses harmonize with science. But the genre claiming verses *predicted* specific discoveries (i'jāz ʿilmī) often stretches word meanings, ignores classical tafsir, and ties the Quran to changeable theories. Many Muslim scientists and scholars reject it while fully affirming the Quran. This site treats verses as signs (āyāt), not hidden science lectures.

**4. Religious explanation.** The Quran calls itself guidance (2:2) and nature signs for reflectors (3:190). Al-Shāṭibī (al-Muwāfaqāt) warned against reading in sciences unknown to the first audience; al-Ghazālī (Jawāhir al-Qurʾān) and al-Rāzī were more open. Modern supporters: Ṭanṭāwī Jawharī, El-Naggar; critics: Amīn al-Khūlī, Maḥmūd Shaltūt, Nidhal Guessoum. Classical i'jāz was mainly *literary* (al-Bāqillānī, al-Jurjānī).

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Guessoum's 2008 Zygon article assesses and dismisses scientific miraculousness as resting on misunderstandings of science and the Quran, upholding Ibn Rushd's "no possible conflict": show compatibility, not prediction. Objections: post-hoc fitting, Arabic polysemy, falsifiability risk. Supporters' reply: cumulative convergence. Response: note striking harmonies as reflections, labelled as such.

**6. Scientific perspective.** Batch 1's embryo, orbit and two-seas entries model the approach: describe accurately, flag overclaims.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Miracle-based faith is brittle; one collapse generalizes to "it's all lies". Yaqeen's finding that science ranks *low* among triggers means dropping overclaims costs little and builds trust.

**8. Honest note.** Some Muslim institutions still promote i'jāz ʿilmī; this site's stance is a scholarly position, not consensus.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Claim checker: verse wording, classical tafsir, science. (b) Timeline of claims appearing after discoveries. (c) "Sign vs proof" toggle.

**10. Key sources.** Guessoum, Zygon 43 (2008); Guessoum, Islam's Quantum Question (2011); al-Shāṭibī, al-Muwāfaqāt; Ibn Rushd, Faṣl al-Maqāl; Indonesian studies of Guessoum vs El-Naggar (Akademika); El-Naggar, Scientific Facts Revealed in the Qur'an; TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, "İ'câzü'l-Kur'ân".

## Q13 — Is eternal Hell just for finite sins?

**1. Steelmanned.** Even the worst life involves finite wrongs. Infinite punishment seems disproportionate. How can the Most Merciful torment anyone forever?

**2. Why people ask.** Proportionality intuitions, love for non-Muslim friends, fear-based childhood teaching.

**3. Short answer.** The majority hold Hell is eternal for those who knowingly and persistently reject God, explained as the outcome of a fixed inner state rather than an act-count. A minority of major classical scholars, including Ibn Taymiyya in his last treatise and Ibn al-Qayyim, argued the punishment of unbelievers will end. The Quran stresses no one is wronged even slightly (4:40), and God alone knows who is truly culpable.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Verses:* "abiding therein forever" (4:169; 72:23); "as long as heavens and earth endure, except as your Lord wills" (11:106–107); "for ages" (78:23).
- *Majority (Ashʿarī, Māturīdī, most Atharīs):* eternal for disbelievers; sinning believers leave. Al-Subkī refuted Ibn Taymiyya sharply.
- *Minority:* per Jon Hoover, Ibn Taymiyya's Fanāʾ al-nār argues there is no consensus on eternity and that chastisement is purposive, though he never states categorically that it ends; Ibn al-Qayyim develops Hell as therapeutic and reformative. Ibn al-Wazīr withheld judgment; Rashīd Riḍā leaned toward cautious universalism.
- *Sufi:* Ibn ʿArabī: for its people, the Fire's torment (ʿadhāb) becomes "sweetness" (ʿadhb) [Debated].

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Responses: (a) fixed character: punishment tracks enduring rejection (6:28); (b) offence scales with the dignity of the one wronged (weakest for modern readers); (c) the universalist minority.

**6. Scientific perspective.** Not applicable.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Hell-anxiety is common in scrupulosity. Avoid graphic imagery; pair justice with the hadith that God's mercy has 100 parts, 99 reserved for the Hereafter (Muslim 2752).

**8. Honest note.** The minority view is classical but remains a minority and was widely condemned; it is not the hidden "real" Islam.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Positions slider with names and dates. (b) Card on 11:107's exception with three classical readings. (c) 99:1 mercy graphic.

**10. Key sources.** J. Hoover, "Islamic Universalism," The Muslim World (2009); Hoover, Ibn Taymiyya's Theodicy of Perpetual Optimism (Brill, 2007); Hoover in Locating Hell in Islamic Traditions (Brill, 2016); Ibn Taymiyya, al-Radd ʿalā man qāla bi-Fanāʾ al-Janna wa-l-Nār; Ibn al-Qayyim, Ḥādī al-Arwāḥ; M.H. Khalil, Islam and the Fate of Others (OUP, 2012).

## Q14 — Are good people of other faiths saved? What about those who never heard?

**1. Steelmanned.** Billions are born into other religions by no choice. Many are kind and sincere. Condemning them for an accident of birth seems unjust.

**2. Why people ask.** Friendships, mixed families, migration. Pew data that most US ex-Muslims become non-religious suggests pluralism pressures are real.

**3. Short answer.** God does not punish until He sends a messenger (17:15). Mainstream scholars hold that those who never received the message, or received a distorted version, are not accountable as rejecters. Al-Ghazālī wrote that most Christians of Byzantium and Turks of his day would likely be covered by God's mercy. Who has "truly heard" is known only to God; Muslims are not asked to pronounce on individuals.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Verses:* 17:15; 2:62 and 5:69; 3:85; 4:165.
- *Hadith:* Muslim 153 (whoever "hears of me" and dies rejecting is of the Fire), read by scholars as hearing accurately; hadith on people of fatra tested on Judgment Day (Aḥmad).
- *Al-Ghazālī, Fayṣal al-Tafriqa:* (1) never heard the Prophet's name: excused; (2) heard his name with the opposite of his true character: excused like the first; (3) heard accurately and investigated: in a good state if dying while searching. Only accurate-hearing deliberate rejecters are culpable.
- *Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim:* people of fatra are tested in the Hereafter.
- *Shiʿi (Persian):* Murtaḍā Muṭahharī (ʿAdl-e Elāhī) distinguishes jāhil qāṣir (inculpably ignorant) from jāhil muqaṣṣir (culpable); non-culpable non-Muslims are the majority and will not be punished, and sincere believers in God are rewarded. Khomeini's writings are similar, per a Berkeley Institute study.
- *Modern Sunni:* Rashīd Riḍā: distorted presentation means not truly "heard". Yaqeen's paper lays out exclusivism with exceptions, moral theism (2:62) and Perennialism (Nasr, Schuon).

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Islamic soteriology rests on *access and response*, not birth (2:286). Tension remains: 3:85 and Muslim 153 carry exclusivist weight; inclusive readings depend on "true hearing". Perennialism [Debated] is rejected by most jurists.

**6. Scientific perspective.** Not applicable.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Grief for non-Muslim family is real; allow hope without false certainty.

**8. Honest note.** No major Sunni school teaches all religions are equally valid paths. Inclusive positions rely on God's knowledge of each person's access and sincerity.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Al-Ghazālī "three doors" diagram. (b) "Who has truly heard?" map with a distortion-fog overlay. (c) Slider: Muṭahharī, al-Ghazālī, Riḍā, Nasr.

**10. Key sources.** al-Ghazālī, Fayṣal al-Tafriqa, trans. S. Jackson (OUP, 2002); Khalil (2012); Muṭahharī, ʿAdl-e Elāhī (Persian; English "Divine Justice", al-Islam.org); T. Tabrizi, "Khumaynī and the Salvation of Non-Muslims" (BIIS); Yaqeen, "The Fate of Non-Muslims"; H.K. Nakata, "The Border of Salvation" (JISMOR); M. Demichelis, The Marginalia Review.

## Q15 — Why are prayers (du'a) unanswered?

**1. Steelmanned.** "Call on Me, I will answer you" (40:60). I prayed for my mother's recovery and she died. Either the promise is false, God doesn't care, or no one is listening.

**2. Why people ask.** Personal trauma; Yaqeen lists a loved one's death among triggers and trauma as possibly the most common pathway.

**3. Short answer.** The Prophet taught that no sincere du'a is wasted: God grants it soon, stores its reward for the Hereafter, or turns away an equal harm. When companions said they would pray more, he replied God gives even more. "Answer" means "respond", not "always grant as asked". This reframes du'a as relationship, not vending machine, without erasing grief.

**4. Religious explanation.** Verses: 2:186; 40:60; 2:216. Hadith: Musnad Aḥmad 11133 (Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī), graded jayyid by al-Arnaʾūṭ, related in Tirmidhī 3573 and al-Adab al-Mufrad 710; Bukhārī and Muslim: answered "as long as one is not hasty". Classical: Ibn al-Qayyim (al-Dāʾ wa-l-Dawāʾ) on du'a as a cause within the decree; Ibn ʿAṭāʾillāh (Ḥikam): delay is no reason for despair, God responds as He chooses.

**5. Philosophical analysis.** Objection: "three outcomes" is unfalsifiable. Response: it is a theological claim about divine wisdom, coherent given belief in a wise God. Petitionary prayer still expresses dependence and relationship.

**6. Scientific perspective.** [Established] The STEP trial (Benson et al., American Heart Journal 2006; 1,802 bypass patients at 6 US hospitals) found complications in 52% (315/604) of patients who received intercessory prayer versus 51% (304/597) of those who did not, and 59% among patients told they were being prayed for, so no benefit was measured. Islamic theology does not predict du'a works like a drug.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Anger at God in grief is common in religious-coping research. The Quran preserves Jacob's lament: "I complain of my grief to God" (12:86). Validate lament as faith.

**8. Honest note.** The three-outcomes hadith comforts but cannot be tested; present it as a promise to trust.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Three-branch du'a path: granted, stored, harm averted. (b) Jacob's lament card. (c) Journal prompt: "What has God turned away that you never saw?"

**10. Key sources.** Musnad Aḥmad 11133; Bukhārī 6340; Muslim 2735; Ibn al-Qayyim, al-Dāʾ wa-l-Dawāʾ; Ibn ʿAṭāʾillāh, al-Ḥikam; Yaqeen, "Modern Pathways"; Benson et al., American Heart Journal (2006); K. Pargament, The Psychology of Religion and Coping.

## Q16 — Earthquakes and disasters: are they punishment? (Türkiye, February 2023)

**1. Steelmanned.** On 6 February 2023 earthquakes killed 53,537 people in Türkiye and injured 107,213 (Interior Minister Ali Yerlikaya, AFAD press conference, February 2024), including countless children. If punishment, what was the children's crime? If "kader", why blame contractors? If God could stop it, how is He just?

**2. Why people ask.** The scale of loss and public framing of deaths as "kader planı" (President Erdoğan), which provoked theological pushback; the earthquake dominated 2023 searches, and "İstanbul Depremi" was 2025's second rising search.

**3. Short answer.** Mainstream scholarship does not teach every disaster punishes its victims. The Prophet called believers dying under collapsed buildings martyrs, the opposite of punishment. Earthquakes follow the natural laws (sunnatullāh) God placed in creation, and humans answer for how they build. Diyanet's scholarly body and a former Diyanet president rejected fatalistic and punishment-only readings after 2023.

**4. Religious explanation.**
- *Verses:* 30:41; 2:155–156; 42:30; 64:11.
- *Hadith:* martyrs include one killed under a collapsed building (Bukhārī 2829; Muslim 1914); plague as mercy and martyrdom for believers (Bukhārī 5734); "tie your camel and trust" (Tirmidhī 2517).
- *Turkey 2023:* former Diyanet president Mehmet Görmez said seeing every disaster as only divine punishment is as "shallow, wrong and incomplete" as purely mechanical explanation, called earthquakes "âyet" with many wisdoms, and warned that punishment framing questions divine justice "because there are innocent children". Media reported Diyanet's ruling as saying a fatalistic framing was "not consistent with our faith".
- *Classical:* older literature sometimes read earthquakes as warnings (al-Suyūṭī, Kashf al-Ṣalṣala); Görmez attributes this to limited natural knowledge. Nursi's theodicy treats musibet as purification and martyrdom.

**5. Philosophical analysis.** See Batch 1's suffering entry. Here: a lawful, stable world in which plate tectonics recycles carbon and builds continents entails hazards; building codes are human responsibility. Objection: God could have made a world without lethal quakes. Response: Batch 1's soul-making and stable-law defences, with honest limits.

**6. Scientific perspective.** [Established] The sequence was Mw 7.8 (Pazarcık) and Mw 7.5 (Elbistan) on the East Anatolian Fault. Damage correlated strongly with construction quality and code enforcement: the human dimension 30:41 points to.

**7. Psychological and social perspective.** Survivors face guilt, trauma and anger at God; punishment narratives deepen shame. Link to AFAD psychosocial support and professional trauma care.

**8. Honest note.** Some verses describe punishments of specific peoples (ʿĀd, Thamūd); scholars restrict these to prophetic warning and rejection. Don't deny them; explain why they cannot apply to 2023's victims.

**9. Visualization ideas.** (a) Animated East Anatolian Fault map with rupture lengths. (b) "Two responsibilities" card: sunnatullāh and building codes, with 30:41. (c) Memorial card with the martyrdom hadith.

**10. Key sources.** Mehmet Görmez's 2023 statements (Habertürk); Diyanet DİYK ruling as reported (Cumhuriyet, 2023); Bukhārī 2829, 5734; al-Suyūṭī, Kashf al-Ṣalṣala; Said Nursi, Lem'alar; AFAD and Kandilli reports.

# TASK C — Signs for reflection (pilots)

## S10 — Birds held in the sky (16:79; 67:19)

- **Verses:** "None holds them up except God" (16:79); birds "spreading and folding their wings… none holds them except the Most Merciful" (67:19), within passages on God's care.
- **Classical tafsir:** al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr: a heavy body held in air points to a Sustainer, who creates the means (wings, air); aerodynamics is not denied.
- **How it works:** wings deflect air downward and create pressure differences (lift); flapping gives thrust; soaring birds use thermals; migrants navigate by sun, stars and magnetic cues.
- **Points of awe [Established]:** in October 2022 a satellite-tagged juvenile bar-tailed godwit, about five months old, flew roughly 13,560 km nonstop from Alaska to Tasmania in 11 days and 1 hour, without food, water or rest, on a route it had never flown. Before departure godwits shrink digestive organs and carry fat over half their body weight (BirdLife International). Later models put the distance at about 13,391–13,560 km.
- **Reflection:** dependence and care: held up by laws it did not make, guided across an unseen ocean.
- **Honest note:** the verses don't describe aerodynamics; don't claim they "predicted" lift.
- **Visualization:** globe route with a day counter and draining fuel gauge.

## S11 — The camel (88:17)

- **Verse:** "Do they not look at the camel, how it was created?" (88:17), then sky, mountains, earth: what a desert traveller sees.
- **Classical tafsir:** al-Zamakhsharī and al-Qurṭubī stress endurance, usefulness, and how even a child can lead it.
- **How it works [Established]:** the hump stores fat, not water. Camels tolerate about 25% body-weight water loss, versus about 12–14% before circulatory failure in most mammals. Plasma volume is protected; oval red blood cells swell without bursting on rehydration; kidneys concentrate urine; body temperature rises by day to save sweat.
- **Points of awe:** a dehydrated camel can drink about one-third of its body weight in about 10 minutes (Iraqi camel colloquium review); a study cited secondhand reports she-camels drinking about 83 litres after six days without water.
- **Reflection:** a creature fitted to the first hearers' environment; look closely at the ordinary.
- **Honest note:** "200 litres in 3 minutes" circulates without solid sources; use conservative ranges.
- **Visualization:** cutaway camel with a dehydration slider showing blood cells, kidneys and temperature.

## S12 — The spider's house (29:41)

- **Verse:** those taking protectors other than God are like the spider that takes a house; "the frailest of houses is the spider's house, if they only knew" (29:41).
- **Classical tafsir:** al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Qurṭubī read frailty as failure to shelter from heat, cold, wind or predators. Some note the feminine verb (ittakhadhat), matching that the female builds the web.
- **How it works [Established]:** dragline silk of Darwin's bark spider (Caerostris darwini, Madagascar) averages about 350 MJ/m³ toughness, some fibres about 520: twice any other known silk and about ten times Kevlar, stretching up to about 91% (Agnarsson et al., PLoS ONE 2010; Garb et al., Communications Biology 2019). Bridge lines span up to 25 m.
- **Reflection:** not "silk is weak", but that something impressively engineered can still offer no real refuge: the point about false reliance.
- **Honest note:** claims that the verse "refers to" females eating males or family breakdown go beyond the text; sexual cannibalism occurs only in some species.
- **Visualization:** split screen: silk toughness bar against steel and Kevlar; a web failing to shelter in rain and wind.
