# Run 1 — Ask: God and religion (Q3, Q32, Q28, Q36)

Output file: `docs/research/08-enrich-ask-1.md`

---

You are researching deep, honest answers for a bilingual website on Islamic faith, aimed at six kinds of reader: doubters, curious outsiders, students of Islamic knowledge, scientists, people who are hurting, and informed skeptics. The tone is calm, respectful and honest: it states every doubt at full strength and admits the limits of every answer. Simple, clear English; explain every technical term the first time (hadith, tafsir, kalam, fiqh, Companion, the schools, etc.) and introduce every scholar in a few words.

This is enrichment run 1 of a whole-site upgrade. The site already has short answers to these questions, and readers scored them very low (2.8–3.5 out of 10): too short, one-line sections, internal cross-references instead of real content, no real arguments. The new answers must be much deeper, at the level of the site's model answer on suffering, which readers scored 7.7/10. What made that model strong: the objection at full strength in the reader's own terms before any answer; a 5–7 sentence short answer a hurried reader can take away; for personal topics a "heart" section first; each section opening with a bold plain sentence stating its point; hard material faced, not skipped; every reply to an objection followed by its stated Limit; hadith quoted with grades and graders; scholars quoted in their own words where possible; named classical and modern scholars with their actual arguments step by step, not just names; real numbers with sources for any science or survey claim.

**Questions for this run** (all four are about God, revelation and other religions; each should stand alone but focus on its own question):
1. Q3 — Why believe the Creator is involved (deism)? (Belief in a Creator but not in revelation: the case for a God who speaks.)
2. Q32 — Is deism a stable position?
3. Q28 — Why Islam and not another religion?
4. Q36 — Is claiming one religion is true arrogant?

Note: a separate deep answer already covers the salvation of non-Muslims (al-Ghazali's Fayṣal al-Tafriqa, Muslim 153, 2:62 vs 3:85, people of fatra, Khalil 2012). For Q28 and Q36, summarise that briefly where needed and focus on the question asked.

**What readers found missing — the new research must answer these where sources support it:**

Q3 (deism / is God involved):
- A clear definition of deism and how it differs from atheism and from Islamic theism.
- The actual case for prophethood, laid out step by step and weighed: how the kalam tradition argued for the need for prophethood; what would count as evidence that Muhammad (pbuh) received revelation.
- What al-Ghazali's al-Munqidh min al-Dalal actually argues, and how certainty returned to him; what Said Nursi's Tabiat Risalesi (Treatise on Nature) argues, step by step; what "inayet" (providence) means.
- Why a Creator would be "unlikely to be indifferent" — the actual argument from God's goodness and wisdom.
- A real reply to rival revelations, and to J.L. Schellenberg's divine hiddenness argument from "non-resistant non-believers" (state it at full strength, then the best Muslim and philosophical replies, then the limit).
- Fine-tuning with real numbers and the multiverse/anthropic replies, briefly, on this page.
- A section addressing the moral protest: people who leave practice because of religious hypocrisy and institutions — can one trust God while not trusting religious institutions?

Q32 (is deism stable):
- A real steelman of deism: why a thinking person concludes a Creator exists but revelation does not (contradictions between religions, moral problems in scripture, hiddenness).
- The argument from God's grace (luṭf) and wisdom (ḥikma) to the sending of prophets: which kalam scholars made it and in which works; how Ashʿarī and Muʿtazilī theologians differed on whether sending prophets is required of God (and the Maturidi view).
- How classical mufassirūn read 6:91 ("they did not appraise Allah with true appraisal").
- A short history of English and French deism (Herbert of Cherbury, Toland, Tindal, Voltaire, etc.) and what historians say about why it faded — and the skeptic's question whether its fading shows instability or just social fashion.
- The Turkish 2018 deism debate briefly as one example among others, and the survey method behind figures (TİDA/Nişancı: actual deist belief under 2% vs 11% of 18–24-year-olds feeling close to the label; KONDA 2025 "believer but not religious" 34%), plus any comparable data from other countries.
- Gentle guidance for family members of deists; and for the deist reader: am I still in some relationship with God?

Q28 (why Islam):
- An actual comparison: what Islam claims versus Christianity, Judaism, and non-Abrahamic faiths (concretely, e.g. what Islam says about Jesus, Moses and the earlier scriptures), and why.
- What "continuation of all the earlier prophets" means in practice.
- The classical arguments for Islam over other religions (e.g. Ibn Taymiyya's al-Jawab al-Sahih, Ibn Hazm's al-Fisal, al-Qarafi), and what Seyyed Hossein Nasr, Tim Winter (Abdal Hakim Murad), Abdulaziz Sachedina and Mohammad Hassan Khalil actually argue, in which works.
- Evidence and caveats for the Quran's preservation: early manuscripts and radiocarbon dates (Birmingham, Tübingen, Sana'a palimpsest and its lower text), the qirāʾāt (variant readings), the Uthmanic standardisation — stated honestly, as a skeptic would raise them.
- "Apply the same criteria": state explicitly what criteria could count as evidence for one religion over another, then apply them to Islam's weak points as well as its strong ones.
- For the reader whose closest friend is Christian: conviction and respect in practice.

Q36 (is exclusivism arrogant):
- A real steelman: "religious luck" (birthplace decides belief) and exclusivism combined with eternal punishment.
- Plantinga's defence of exclusivism ("A Defense of Religious Exclusivism", 1995) and John Hick's pluralism, explained clearly, with the main critiques of each (e.g. whether pluralism is itself exclusive; the religious-luck objection); what the strongest replies to Plantinga are.
- How Muslims reconcile 109:6 ("For you is your religion") and 2:256 with calling others to Islam; what 10:99 and 49:13 mean in context according to tafsir.
- The actual findings of intellectual-humility research (Leary et al. 2017; Porter & Schumann; others) with effect sizes, and evidence on whether humble confidence improves relations between groups.
- What research shows about how religious belief is passed on within families and regions.
- For readers with loved ones of other faiths: how to love them without feeling one betrays one's faith (brief, link to the salvation answer).

**Rules (strict):**
- Use only sources you actually read, and cite each precisely (work, author, edition/page or URL). Never cite from memory. If something cannot be sourced, put it in a "Gaps" list instead of writing it.
- Quran: give the reference for every verse (e.g. 2:256). When quoting, quote the Saheeh International translation exactly.
- Hadith: give collection and number in sunnah.com numbering (Sahih Muslim by Abd al-Baqi number), written as `{hadith:collection:number}` with collection one of bukhari, muslim, abudawud, tirmidhi, nasai, ibnmajah. Give the grade and grader you found. Other collections (Musnad Ahmad, Muwatta, al-Hakim…): cite in words with the grader. No claim may rest on a weak (da'if) hadith; mention a weak one only to say it is weak.
- Tafsir: quote or summarise named commentators (al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Razi, al-Sa'di…) on the specific verse.
- Fiqh, where law applies: the four Sunni schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali), each with a named scholar or work. Shi'i and other views may appear in "The range of views", clearly labelled.
- Philosophy and science: named scholars and their actual arguments; peer-reviewed or reputable academic sources (e.g. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, university presses, journals); give numbers with their source.
- Honorifics outside quotations: "the Prophet (pbuh)", "Jesus (pbuh)"; a Companion named as narrator "(ra)". Never change a quotation.
- No "miracle", "proves" or "predicted" for science claims about verses. Not centred on any one country (Türkiye may be one example among others).
- Face the hard material; never skip it. After each reply to an objection, state its limit.
- Search in English, Arabic, Turkish, Persian, Urdu, French and German where useful; prefer primary texts, university presses, peer-reviewed work and official bodies.
