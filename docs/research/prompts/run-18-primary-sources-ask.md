## Who this is for

You are researching content for **Tefekkür**, a bilingual (Turkish/English) website that invites reflection on the signs the Quran points to in nature, its moral vision, and honest answers to hard questions of faith. Six kinds of reader use it, and every page must satisfy all six:

1. **the doubter**, who half-believes and needs real reasons;
2. **the curious outsider**, who knows little and needs every term explained;
3. **the student** of Islamic knowledge, who checks every source and attribution;
4. **the scientist**, who wants named studies, numbers and honest uncertainty;
5. **the person who is hurting**, who needs care as well as answers;
6. **the informed skeptic**, who knows the strongest objections and will spot evasion.

Tone: calm, respectful and honest. Plain words and short sentences. Explain every technical term the first time (hadith, tafsir, fiqh, kalam, Companion, the four schools…), and introduce every scholar in a few words (who, when, why they matter). Write to the reader, never notes to a writer.

## The rules (they come from real mistakes on this site — please follow every one)

**1. Only what you actually read.** Use only sources you opened and read during this task. Cite each one precisely: work, author, edition and page, or URL. Never cite from memory: not a page number, a date, a grade, a study's result or a quotation. If you cannot read a source, do not use it; list it under "Gaps". (Once, a remembered paper ID pointed to a study on knee pain.)

**2. Say whose words they are.** Keep four voices apart and label each one:
- the **Quran**;
- the **Prophet (pbuh)** in a hadith;
- a **narrator or later transmitter** inside a hadith report. *Example of the mistake:* in Muslim 2605, the list of "three cases" in which lying is allowed is the narrator al-Zuhri's addition, not the Prophet's words;
- a **commentator or scholar**, and a **modern reading**.

**3. Quote the Quran exactly, in Saheeh International.** Use https://quran.com (Saheeh International). Give the reference for every verse (e.g. 4:135). Mark omissions with "…". A tafsir translation's own wording of a verse is **not** the verse.
- *Example of the mistake:* the English Ibn Kathir renders 25:2 as "measured it exactly according to its due measurements"; Saheeh has "determined it with [precise] determination".
- When the commentator's wording matters, say so: "Ibn Kathir's translator renders it as …".

**4. Context before claims (the most important rule).** For every verse you use, state:
- what it literally says;
- its setting: the verses before and after, Meccan or Medinan, and the occasion of revelation if a sound report exists;
- whom it addresses.

Never write "the verse asks / commands / means X" unless X is in the verse's words.
- If X is a commentator's reading, write "Ibn Kathir reads it as …".
- If X is a modern extension, label it as one.
- *Example of the mistake:* the site said 4:135 "asks for the truth anyway". The verse commands **justice**; honest **testimony** is the means it names ("if you distort [your testimony] or refuse [to give it]"). Justice is not the same as telling the truth in every situation. Al-Ghazali, quoted by al-Nawawi, even requires a lie to protect an innocent person from an oppressor.
- *Another example:* 59:7 ("so that it will not be a perpetual distribution among the rich") is about *fay'*, property gained without fighting. Using it as a general economic rule is a scholarly extension and must be labelled as one.

**5. Name names; no anonymous authorities.** Never write "scholars say", "later scholars", "classical tafsir holds", "studies show" or "the schools differ" without saying **who**, **where** (work, page) and **what exactly**. For a disagreement, give each side with its holder.

**6. Hadith.**
- Give collection and number in sunnah.com numbering (Sahih Muslim by the Abd al-Baqi number). Write each as `{hadith:collection:number}`, where collection is one of bukhari, muslim, abudawud, tirmidhi, nasai, ibnmajah.
- Give the grade and who graded it (e.g. al-Albani, Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut).
- Other collections (Musnad Ahmad, Muwatta Malik, al-Adab al-Mufrad, al-Hakim…): cite in words, with the grader.
- **No claim may rest on a weak (da'if) hadith.** Mention a weak one only to say that it is weak.
- Quote only words that are in the hadith's text.

**7. Fiqh: the four Sunni schools only** (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali). Give each school's position with a named scholar and work. Where they differ, give each position and the reason. Shi'i, Ibadi and modern views may appear in "The range of views", clearly labelled.

**8. Science and research: specific, measured, and tied to the verse.**
- For every study give: authors, year, journal, what was measured, the sample size, and the result with numbers (effect size, percentage, units).
- Label the strength of the evidence: [Established], [Moderate], [Mixed] or [Debated].
- Prefer primary papers, systematic reviews and official data. A news article is only a pointer to the paper; read the paper.
- Tie each fact to a specific word or claim on the page. Leave out facts that have nothing to do with the verse.
  - *Example of the mistake:* a page on 36:37–40 opened with the Moon receding 3.8 cm a year, which the verses never mention.
- Never use "miracle", "proves", "predicted" or "scientific foreshadowing" for a verse. If a popular modern claim exists (e.g. "the Quran described X before science"), state it fairly, then weigh it, and say whether classical commentators read the verse that way.

**9. Objections: full strength, a real reply, then the limit.** State every objection as its most serious holder would. Reply with an actual argument, not a line. Then say what the reply does not settle ("*Limit:* …").
- *Example of the mistake:* Lynn White's "dominion" critique was answered in two lines by quoting a verse about subjection, which is close to the very thing criticised.

**10. Face the hard material.** Never skip a difficult verse, hadith, ruling or historical event. Readers find it anyway, and a page that hides it loses their trust.

**11. Care for the hurting reader.** Wherever a topic touches people's lives (grief, guilt, debt, abuse, doubt, illness, family conflict), include a section for the person on the receiving end or the one who finds it hard. Base it on sources, and be practical (what to do next, where to find help).

**12. Honorifics, outside quotations only.**
- "the Prophet (pbuh)"; other prophets "Moses (pbuh)", "Jesus (pbuh)";
- a Companion named as a narrator "(ra)";
- never change a quotation, and do not use the ﷺ glyph.

**13. Not centred on any one country.** Türkiye may be one example among others. Search in English, Arabic, Turkish, Persian, Urdu, French and German where useful.

**14. Errors on the current page.** You will be given each page's live URL. Read it first. Anything on it that is wrong, stretched beyond its source, wrongly attributed or unsourced goes in "## Corrections to the current pages" at the end, with the sentence quoted and the evidence.

**15. Length follows the question.** Hard Ask questions take about 2,500–4,000 words. Live and Reflect pages take about 1,500–2,500. Depth comes from arguments and evidence, not from padding.

### Q17 — Who created God?
Page: https://candanumut.github.io/reflection/en/ask/who-created-god/
- "**Al-Ghazali against the philosophers — and Ibn Rushd against al-Ghazali.** Al-Ghazali accepted a creator but held that the philosophers (above all Ibn Sina) could not *demonstrate* their metaphysics with the logic they used elsewhere; three of their theses he judged unbelief, the first being "The theory of a pre-eternal world" (*Wikipedia*)."
- "**The Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem (2003).** It shows that a universe that has, on average, been expanding "cannot be infinite in the past but must have a past spacetime boundary" (*Wikipedia*)."
- "*Limit:* this debate is unresolved; and Vilenkin himself, replying to Craig in 2015, said of the universe's origin, "No cause is needed" (*Wikipedia*, "Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem")."
- "*Wikipedia* traces the kalām argument to John Philoponus (6th century, a Christian) and to medieval Islamic kalām, including al-Ghazali; its best-known modern defender is the Christian philosopher William Lane Craig, and the modern name "kalām cosmological argument" keeps its Islamic source."
- "Al-Ghazali's version, in *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*: "Every being which begins has a cause for its beginning; now the world is a being which begins" (*Wikipedia*'s translation)."
- "Apply this to the whole collection (*jumla*) of contingent things — even if it were infinite: the collection too needs a cause outside itself, and since that cause cannot be contingent, it must be necessary (*Wikipedia*, "Seddiqin argument")."
- "He also argued that a thing's essence (*what* it is) does not explain its existence (*that* it is), so existence must be given to it by a cause (*Wikipedia*, "Cosmological argument")."
- "He treats existence as one reality that differs among its instances "only in perfection and imperfection and in intensity and weakness", and calls the Necessary Existent "Pure Existence than which nothing is more complete" (*Wikipedia*, "Seddiqin argument")."
- "Ibn Rushd's *Incoherence of the Incoherence* "defends the doctrines of the philosophers and criticizes al-Ghazali's own arguments" (*Wikipedia*)."
- "Michael Martin argues the kalām argument shows at most that some agent or agents caused the universe (*Wikipedia*)."
- "On this view the universe is a *brute fact*: it just is. - *The parts-and-whole point.* The "Hume–Edwards principle" says: "If the existence of every member of a set is explained, the existence of that set is thereby explained" (*Wikipedia*)."
- "This is the origin of what is now called the "kalām cosmological argument", whose modern form is: "Everything that begins to exist has a cause"; "The universe began to exist"; so the universe has a cause (*Wikipedia*)."
- "William Lane Craig replies that such events still have "many physically necessary conditions" (*Wikipedia*, "Kalam cosmological argument")."
- "[Established, within its assumptions] **Its assumptions and limits.** [Debated] - It applies to classical spacetime; it "does not allow to tell when the singularity takes place", and "does not tell if it is associated to any other event in the past" (*Wikipedia*). - Sean Carroll argues it "may not hold under consideration of a complete theory of quantum gravity"; Lesnefsky, Easson and Davies have built classical models that satisfy its condition yet are past-complete (*Wikipedia*). - Its authors disagree on what it means: Vilenkin thinks the universe had a beginning; Guth, according to Carroll, does not (*Wikipedia*)."

### Q21 — Slavery
