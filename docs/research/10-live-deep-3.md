## M11 — Humility

### Sources read
- Quran 17:37, 25:63, 31:18–19, 49:13 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 676; Muslim 91.
- al-Ghazali on arrogance; Porter et al., *Nature Reviews Psychology* (2022).

### In brief
The Prophet (pbuh) defined arrogance precisely: "disdaining the truth … and contempt for the people" {hadith:muslim:91}. Humility is therefore not looking shabby or hating yourself; it is being open to truth and never looking down on others.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Walk gently.** "The servants of the Most Merciful are those who walk upon the earth easily, and when the ignorant address them [harshly], they say [words of] peace" (25:63).

**Luqman's advice.** "Do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful. And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice" (31:18–19).

**Know your size.** "Do not walk upon the earth exultantly. Indeed, you will never tear the earth [apart], and you will never reach the mountains in height" (17:37).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**What arrogance is.** "He who has in his heart the weight of a mustard seed of pride shall not enter Paradise." A man asked about loving fine clothes and shoes. The Prophet (pbuh) said: "Allah is Graceful and He loves Grace. Pride is disdaining the truth (out of self-conceit) and contempt for the people" {hadith:muslim:91}.

**At home.** Asked what he did at home, Aisha (ra) said: "He used to keep himself busy serving his family" {hadith:bukhari:676}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**The most dangerous pride.** Al-Ghazali, in the *Ihya'*, lists the roots of arrogance — knowledge, worship, lineage, beauty, wealth, strength, followers — and warns that pride in knowledge and piety is the most dangerous, because it hides inside good things. The definition in {hadith:muslim:91} ties humility directly to intellectual honesty: accepting the truth even from someone you dislike.

#### research — What research shows
**Intellectual humility.** Recognising that your beliefs might be wrong is linked to more openness to opposing views, less polarisation and better learning (review: Porter et al., 2022). [Moderate, mostly correlational]

#### practice — In daily life
**Small tests.** Can you say "I was wrong"? Do you help at home {hadith:bukhari:676}? Do you speak to a cleaner the way you speak to a manager? In a disagreement, look for the truth in the other side first.

#### heart — For the one who feels small
**Humility is not self-hatred.** The same hadith affirms that "Allah is Graceful and He loves Grace" {hadith:muslim:91}. You may care for yourself and your appearance; what is forbidden is contempt for others and for the truth.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is wanting nice clothes arrogant?** No: the Prophet (pbuh) answered exactly this {hadith:muslim:91}.

**Is claiming my religion is true arrogant?** That is its own question (see "Is claiming one religion is true arrogant?"). This page's definition helps: arrogance is rejecting truth and despising people, not holding a belief.

### Honest limits
Religious arrogance — "only my group is right", looking down on the less observant — is one of the reasons people give for leaving faith. This page names the teaching; it does not claim communities always live it.

## M12 — Care for the earth

### Sources read
- Quran 7:31, 30:41, 55:8 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 2320, 2370; Ibn Majah 425.
- stewardship readings; S. H. Nasr (1968); the Islamic Declaration on Global Climate Change (2015); White et al. (2019); World Resources Institute water-stress data.

### In brief
"Eat and drink, but be not excessive" (7:31). "Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned" (30:41). Planting is charity even when an animal eats the fruit {hadith:bukhari:2320}.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**No excess.** "O children of Adam, take your adornment at every masjid, and eat and drink, but be not excessive. Indeed, He likes not those who commit excess" (7:31).

**Corruption has consequences.** "Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned so He may let them taste part of [the consequence of] what they have done that perhaps they will return" (30:41).

**Keep the balance.** "That you not transgress within the balance" (55:8).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**Planting is charity.** "There is none amongst the Muslims who plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person or an animal eats from it, but is regarded as a charitable gift for him" {hadith:bukhari:2320}.

**Protected land.** "No Hima except for Allah and His Apostle": the Prophet (pbuh) made the place called al-Naqi' a protected pasture (*hima*), ending private reserves kept by the powerful {hadith:bukhari:2370}.

**Water.** A hadith about not wasting water even at a flowing river is often quoted ({hadith:ibnmajah:425}); its chain is weak, so it is not used here as evidence. 7:31 makes the same point.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Stewardship, old and new.** Many modern scholars read the human as *khalifa* (2:30) in the sense of steward: the earth is a trust, not property to exhaust. Classical tafsir more often read *khalifa* as succession of generations or as ruling with justice; the ecological reading is a modern extension, consistent with 7:31 and 55:8. Thinkers such as S. H. Nasr (*Man and Nature*, 1968) and the Islamic Declaration on Global Climate Change (Istanbul, 2015) built on it.

#### research — What research shows
**Nature is good for people.** Spending about 120 minutes a week in nature is associated with better health and wellbeing (White et al., 2019). [Moderate, correlational] Religion's effect on environmental behaviour is mixed across studies: values need to be taught explicitly to turn into action. [Mixed]

#### practice — In daily life
**Concrete steps.** Waste less food, especially in Ramadan; use water carefully, even at the mosque; plant something {hadith:bukhari:2320}; support protected green spaces, the modern *hima*.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does the Quran talk about climate change?** No; it speaks of excess (7:31), corruption on land and sea (30:41) and balance (55:8), which believers apply to today's problems.

**Is "khalifa" really about the environment?** That is a modern reading; classical commentators mostly read it otherwise (see above).

### Honest limits
The Middle East and North Africa is the most water-stressed region on earth, and waste and pollution are serious in many Muslim-majority countries. There is no basis for claiming that Muslims are naturally better environmentalists; what is shown here is the teaching, and the gap between it and practice.

## M13 — Marriage

### Sources read
- Quran 2:187, 2:229, 4:19, 30:21 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Abu Dawud 2578; Bukhari 676; Muslim 2328; Tirmidhi 3895.
- al-Ghazali's *Adab al-Nikah*; Gottman's research and its critique (Heyman and Slep, 2001); Robles et al. (2014).

### In brief
Marriage is called a sign of God: "He placed between you affection and mercy" (30:21). Spouses are "clothing" for each other (2:187), and if a marriage ends it must end "with good treatment" (2:229).

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**A sign, with affection and mercy.** "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy" (30:21). Commentators distinguish affection (*mawadda*), strong early, from mercy (*rahma*), which carries a marriage through age, illness and hardship.

**Clothing for each other.** "They are clothing for you and you are clothing for them" (2:187): covering, warmth, protection — both ways.

**Kindness even in dislike.** "Live with them in kindness. For if you dislike them — perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good" (4:19).

**Ending well.** "Either keep [her] in an acceptable manner or release [her] with good treatment" (2:229).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**The measure of a person.** "The best of you is the best to his wives, and I am the best of you to my wives" {hadith:tirmidhi:3895}.

**At home and at play.** He "used to keep himself busy serving his family" {hadith:bukhari:676}. Aisha (ra) raced him on a journey and won; years later he won, and said "This is for that" {hadith:abudawud:2578}.

**He never struck a woman.** Aisha (ra) reported that he "never beat anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant" {hadith:muslim:2328}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Gentle character and patience.** Al-Ghazali, in his book on marriage in the *Ihya'*, stresses gentle character, patience with a spouse's faults, and play. In the *maqasid* view, marriage protects family and dignity, and is a contract with rights on both sides.

#### research — What research shows
**Kindness predicts lasting love.** John Gottman's research found that stable couples have roughly five positive interactions for every negative one during conflict, and that contempt is a strong warning sign; his divorce-prediction claims have been criticised as overfitted (Heyman and Slep, 2001). [Moderate] Marital quality is linked to health in a meta-analysis of 126 studies (Robles et al., 2014). [Established, small]

#### practice — In daily life
**Daily mercy.** Help at home {hadith:bukhari:676}; play together {hadith:abudawud:2578}; thank each other; in conflict, avoid contempt; if it ends, end it with good treatment (2:229).

#### heart — If you are not safe
**Violence contradicts these verses.** Abuse goes against "affection and mercy" (30:21) and "live with them in kindness" (4:19), and the Prophet (pbuh) never struck a woman {hadith:muslim:2328}. Your safety comes first; the support on this page can help. Verse 4:34, which many ask about, is discussed honestly in the question "Does Islam treat women unequally?".

### Questions readers ask next
**What about 4:34?** It is discussed on the page "Does Islam treat women unequally?", with the classical and modern readings.

**Is divorce allowed?** Yes, with rules and good treatment (2:229); reconciliation is preferred where possible.

**What does "clothing for each other" mean?** Mutual covering, warmth and protection (2:187).

### Honest limits
Domestic violence is a serious problem in many societies, Muslim-majority ones included. Divorce rates are rising in many countries. Gottman's numbers are widely cited but his predictions have been criticised.

## M14 — Knowledge

### Sources read
- Quran 20:114, 39:9, 96:1 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Muslim 2699; Ibn Majah 224; Tirmidhi 2647.
- al-Ghazali's Book of Knowledge; Ibn Rushd's *Fasl al-Maqal*; Gruber et al., *Neuron* (2014); Akram Nadwi, *al-Muhaddithat*.

### In brief
The first word revealed was "Recite" (96:1), and the Prophet (pbuh) was told to ask: "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (20:114) — the one thing he is told to ask more of.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**It began with reading.** "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" (96:1).

**Ask for more.** "Say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge'" (20:114).

**Not equal.** "Say, 'Are those who know equal to those who do not know?'" (39:9).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**A path to Paradise.** "He who treads the path in search of knowledge, Allah would make that path easy, leading to Paradise for him" {hadith:muslim:2699}.

**Two famous weak reports.** "Seeking knowledge is an obligation on every Muslim" ({hadith:ibnmajah:224}) and "Whoever goes out seeking knowledge is in Allah's cause until he returns" ({hadith:tirmidhi:2647}) are widely quoted, but their chains are graded weak, so they are not used here as evidence. The Quran and Muslim 2699 make the point.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Two kinds of duty.** Al-Ghazali opens the *Ihya'* with the Book of Knowledge. He distinguishes knowledge every person must have for faith and daily life (*fard 'ayn*) from knowledge the community must have through some of its members (*fard kifaya*), such as medicine and mathematics. Ibn Rushd (*Fasl al-Maqal*) argued that studying nature with reason is commanded by the Quran's invitations to reflect.

#### history — History
**Learning, by men and women.** Baghdad's House of Wisdom translated and researched in the 9th century; al-Qarawiyyin in Fez was founded by a woman, Fatima al-Fihri (859). Akram Nadwi documented thousands of women hadith transmitters (*al-Muhaddithat*).

#### research — What research shows
**Curiosity helps memory.** When people are curious, they remember the answer — and even unrelated information — better (Gruber et al., 2014). [Moderate] Education is strongly linked to health, income and longevity. [Established, partly causal]

#### practice — In daily life
**A daily habit.** Learn one thing a day, in faith and in the world; ask "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (20:114); read the sources, not only summaries.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is secular knowledge religiously valuable?** Al-Ghazali counted fields like medicine as communal duties (*fard kifaya*).

**Why does the page reject a famous hadith?** Because its chain is graded weak by the hadith scholars named in its badge; the site does not rest claims on weak hadith.

### Honest limits
Research output, literacy and education quality vary widely across Muslim-majority countries, several far below what 96:1 would lead one to expect. "Golden age" nostalgia can wrongly imply that nothing needs to change today. Current UNESCO figures are not yet included here.

## M15 — Self-discipline

### Sources read
- Quran 13:11, 29:69, 79:40, 91:9–10 (Saheeh International).
- Hadith (text and grades from the collections' data): Bukhari 6464; Tirmidhi 1621.
- al-Ghazali, Miskawayh, al-Tusi; Moffitt et al., *PNAS* (2011); Lally et al. (2010); Hagger et al. (2016); Vohs et al. (2021).

### In brief
"He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]" (91:9–10). Character is trained, little by little: "the most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant even if it were little" {hadith:bukhari:6464}.

### Go deeper

#### quran — What the Quran says
**Purify the self.** "He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]" (91:9–10).

**Hold back, and strive.** "As for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination" (79:40); "those who strive for Us — We will surely guide them to Our ways" (29:69).

**Change starts inside.** "Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves" (13:11).

#### sunnah — What the Prophet taught
**The real struggle.** "The Mujahid is one who strives against his own soul" {hadith:tirmidhi:1621}.

**Small and steady.** "The most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant even if it were little" {hadith:bukhari:6464}.

#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
**Training, not crushing.** Al-Ghazali (*Ihya'*) compares training the soul to training a horse: neither crushing nor indulging, with gradual habits. Miskawayh and al-Tusi describe virtue as a balanced middle formed by repeated action until it becomes second nature. The stages of the soul — commanding evil (12:53), blaming (75:2), at peace (89:27) — give a map of growth.

#### research — What research shows
**Self-control matters, and it grows slowly.** In the Dunedin study of about 1,000 children, childhood self-control predicted adult health, wealth and fewer convictions, even after controlling for intelligence and social class (Moffitt et al., 2011). [Established] New habits took a median of 66 days to become automatic, ranging from 18 to 254 (Lally et al., 2010). [Small study] The popular idea that willpower is a fuel tank that runs out ("ego depletion") failed large replications (Hagger et al., 2016; Vohs et al., 2021). [Established that the original effect was overstated]

#### practice — In daily life
**One small habit.** Choose one small, regular practice {hadith:bukhari:6464}; attach it to an existing routine; expect weeks, not days; when you slip, return without despair (29:69).

#### heart — For the one who keeps failing
**Return is part of the path.** The verse promises guidance to "those who strive" (29:69), not to those who never fall. Habits take time; a broken streak is not a broken soul.

### Questions readers ask next
**Is willpower limited?** The evidence does not support picturing willpower as a fuel tank that runs out (see above).

**What are the stages of the soul?** Commanding evil (12:53), blaming (75:2) and at peace (89:27).

### Honest limits
No one can promise that discipline will feel easy or quick; the research and the tradition both describe a long, uneven path. The ego-depletion myth still appears in popular self-help material, including Islamic.
