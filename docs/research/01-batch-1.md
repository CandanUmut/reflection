# Questions of the Age & Signs for Reflection — Research Foundation, Batch 1

The doubts most likely to move young Turkish and Muslim audiences are mostly not about whether a Creator exists. They are about how God acts: suffering, destiny, and whether God is involved at all (deism). Next come gender, apostasy and how religion is taught. The site should therefore open with accurate wonder and moral substance, then take on hard questions with steelmanned, limit-aware answers. It should never rely on overstated "scientific miracle" claims, which cost trust when they collapse.

## TL;DR
- **What people doubt:** Turkish workshops, Diyanet-linked research and KONDA surveys all rank evil, kader, deism and women's position as the main friction points. Belief in God stays high (94.3% in Zübeyir Nişancı and Hüseyin Sağlam's Marmara University study "Sayılarla Türkiye'de İnanç ve Dindarlık") while religious identification falls: KONDA's "atheist/non-believer" share rose from 2% (2008) to 8% (October 2024 fieldwork).
- **What the signs section should do:** Present phenomena accurately and keep verse, tafsir, reflection and science separate. Examples: a Masada date seed, dated by the University of Zurich to 1,990 ± 50 years, that sprouted (*Science*, 2008); about 500 litres of blood passing through a cow's udder per litre of milk (CSIRO); over 2.6 million flower visits per pound of honey (Canadian Honey Council); the Moon receding about 3.8 cm a year (NASA JPL lunar laser ranging). Correct overstated claims such as "the two seas never mix".
- **What the moral section can claim:** Meta-analyses find gratitude interventions have small effects (g = −0.29 on depression/anxiety) and forgiveness interventions moderate ones (Δ = 0.56). Present Quranic ethics as a coherent vision, use research as support rather than proof, and name the gaps between ideal and practice.

## Key Findings
1. **Turkish doubt is mostly deism plus theodicy, not atheism.** A 2018 Konya workshop of 50 religious-education teachers made three points:
   - Troubled students lean toward deism rather than atheism.
   - The most-asked question is why God stays silent before evil.
   - Destiny and how to conceive of God follow.

   A 2019 Diyanet publishing-congress paper found the same list among young people: destiny, evil, creation and women.
2. **Belief remains broad, but identification and practice are eroding.**
   - Zübeyir Nişancı and Hüseyin Sağlam's Marmara University study ("Sayılarla Türkiye'de İnanç ve Dindarlık") found 94.3% belief in God. VOA Türkçe reports that among 18–24-year-olds, 11% feel close to deism and 5% to atheism, versus 4% and 2% among those over 65. Euronews Türkçe instead reports the 11% as people who do not believe in God, so the exact category needs checking against the report itself. *(Resolved in Batch 2, A5: both figures exist and refer to different survey items.)*
   - KONDA (n = 6,137, October 2024, published May 2025), compared with 2008: "sofu" (very devout) unchanged at 12%; "dindar" (religious) down from 55% to 46%; "believer, not religious" up from 31% to 34%; atheist/non-believer up from 2% to 8%.
   - KONDA's 2008–2018 youth report (ages 15–29): "religious-conservative" lifestyle fell from 28% to 15%, regular fasting from 74% to 58%, and regular prayer from 27% to 24%.
3. **Secularization is not one-way.**
   - Arab Barometer: "not religious" rose from 8% to 13% between 2013 and 2018–19 (18% among under-30s). The 2021–22 wave partly reversed this; young Tunisians became 15 points less likely to say they are not religious.
   - Pew: 77% of Americans raised Muslim still identify as Muslim, and 85% of all religious switching happens before age 30.
   - So doubt peaks between about 16 and 30, and it is often reversible.
4. **How religion is taught drives doubt.** Konya teachers cited three causes: preaching that contradicts conduct, worship-centred teaching without grounding in belief, and religion framed against science. [Scholarly]
5. **"Scientific miracle" framing is a liability.** Keith Moore's 1983 "Islamic Additions" embryology edition is widely criticized. The classical "signs" framing is more faithful to the tradition and holds up better. [Scholarly]
6. **The moral evidence base is modest but real.**
   - Cregg & Cheavens (2021): 27 trials, N = 3,675, g = −0.29.
   - Wade et al. (2014): 54 reports, Δ = 0.56.
   - Neither intervention replaces therapy. [Established]

## Phase 2 — Deep answers (top 8)

### Q1. Why is there suffering if God is all-good and all-powerful?
1. **Steelmanned.** An omnipotent God could prevent suffering and a good God would want to. Yet suffering is vast and often seems pointless: children's cancer, animal pain across deep time, earthquakes.
2. **Why asked.**
   - It is the strongest atheist argument.
   - It usually arrives through grief.
   - Turkish teachers rank it first, framed as God being "silent".
3. **Short answer.** Islam calls life a real test (67:2; 21:35) in which grief is legitimate and final justice comes after death. Philosophers widely regard the "logical" problem as answered. The "evidential" problem remains serious. An honest answer gives partial reasons, invites trust about the rest, and calls for solidarity now.
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - Quran: hardship with ease (94:5–6); Khidr's hidden wisdom (18:60–82); Job's praised patience (38:41–44); mercy encompassing all things (7:156).
   - Hadith: the believer's affair is all good, gratitude or patience (Muslim 2999). The Prophet wept for his son Ibrahim (Bukhari 1303).
   - Al-Ghazali held there is "nothing more wonderful than what is". [Debated]
   - Ash'aris ground God's justice in His sovereignty; Maturidis stress wisdom (ḥikma) that reason can partly grasp. [Scholarly]
   - Ibn Taymiyya: evil is never purely evil from God's side.
   - Said Nursi: creating evil is not evil, choosing it is.
5. **Philosophy.**
   - Defences: free will, soul-making, natural regularity and sceptical theism.
   - Strongest objection: the amount and distribution of suffering, including animals.
   - Responses: animal recompense (Muslim 2582), eschatology and epistemic limits. These reduce the problem but do not dissolve it.
6. **Science.** Plate tectonics causes earthquakes but also recycles carbon and helps stabilize long-term climate. [Established geophysics; the theodicy use is Debated]
7. **Psychology.** Positive religious coping (God as partner) predicts better outcomes than negative coping (feeling punished). Grief needs presence before argument.
8. **Limits.** No theodicy explains individual cases.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A "two lenses" zoom from one event to a whole life.
   - A three-panel Khidr story.
   - A tectonics globe showing hazard and life together.
10. **Sources.**
   - Al-Ghazali, *Iḥyā'*
   - Nursi, *Sözler*
   - Ormsby, *Theodicy in Islamic Thought* (Princeton)
   - Sherman Jackson, *Islam and the Problem of Black Suffering* (OUP)
   - Konya workshop declaration
   - Stanford Encyclopedia entries on evil

### Q2. If everything is written, how am I free?
1. **Steelmanned.** If God decreed every act from eternity, I could not have done otherwise. Punishment then looks unjust and effort pointless.
2. **Why asked.** Fear of being "written" for Hell, paralysis, guilt. Kader is a top Turkish fracture point.
3. **Short answer.** Knowing something is not causing it. Islam affirms both decree and real choice (kasb, cüz'î irade), and holds people accountable only within their capacity (2:286). Asked "why act?", the Prophet answered: act, each is eased toward what they were created for (Bukhari 4949).
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - Quran: 18:29 ("whoever wills, let him believe"); 81:29 ("you do not will unless God wills").
   - Ash'aris: God creates acts and humans "acquire" them. Maturidis give choice a stronger role, the view favoured by Turkish theologians such as Elmalılı. [Scholarly]
   - Nursi: partial will is small but decisive.
5. **Philosophy.**
   - Responses: atemporal knowledge, compatibilism and Frankfurt cases.
   - Strongest objection: God creating acts, not merely knowing them. Muslim theologians themselves have called kasb obscure. [Debated]
6. **Science.** The Libet experiments do not settle free will. [Scholarly]
7. **Psychology.** Fatalistic readings can feed depression and scrupulosity.
8. **Limits.** The tradition calls qadar a mystery (sirr).
9. **Visuals.**
   - A "map vs. driving" animation.
   - A three-schools comparison card.
   - A circle of control.
10. **Sources.**
   - Al-Maturidi, *Kitāb al-Tawḥīd*
   - Al-Ghazali, *al-Iqtiṣād*
   - Nursi, 26th Word
   - TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, "Kader" and "Kesb"
   - Temel Yeşilyurt, *Çağdaş İnanç Problemleri* (Diyanet, 2018)

### Q3. Why believe the Creator is involved (deism)?
1. **Steelmanned.** Reason may reach a First Cause, but not a God who reveals laws or answers prayer. Religions look culturally shaped, so a distant Creator seems the rational minimum.
2. **Why asked.** It is the dominant Turkish youth pattern. Its roots are disappointment with religious representatives, religion taught against science, and evil.
3. **Short answer.** A Creator who makes meaning-seeking minds is unlikely to be indifferent to them. The case for involvement rests on God's character (mercy and wisdom, which deism quietly drops), the historical evidence for prophethood, and revelation's fit with human nature (fiṭra, 30:30).
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - The Quran argues from provision to guidance (20:50; 87:2–3).
   - Al-Ghazali's *al-Munqidh* narrates his own crisis of doubt and recovery.
   - Nursi reads nature's beneficence (inayet, providence) as personal intent.
5. **Philosophy.**
   - Design and fine-tuning suggest intention.
   - Objections: divine hiddenness (Schellenberg) and rival revelations.
   - Reply: hiddenness preserves the test, since compelling signs would end it (6:158).
6. **Science.** See S9.
7. **Sociology.** Konya teachers note that hypocrisy and outdated religious discourse push young people away. Deism is often a moral protest, so answers must address integrity.
8. **Limits.** Arguments give plausibility, not compulsion.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A "reach of reason" staircase.
   - Al-Ghazali's journey as a scroll story.
   - A gallery of care-signs.
10. **Sources.**
   - Al-Ghazali, *al-Munqidh*
   - Nursi, *Tabiat Risalesi*
   - Diyanet, *Güncel İnanç Problemleri* (2018)
   - Turkish Academic Research Review on youth deism
   - Pamukkale İlahiyat Dergisi
   - Stanford Encyclopedia, "Fine-Tuning"

### Q4. Was the Quran preserved?
1. **Steelmanned.** Ancient texts change. The Quran was collected after Muhammad's death, standardized by Uthman, and rival codices were burned.
2. **Why asked.** Online polemics, and Turkish Quranist debates that blur the reliability of Quran and hadith.
3. **Short answer.** Transmission was oral and written from the start. The parchment of the Birmingham folios is radiocarbon-dated to 568–645 CE with 95.4% probability, close to the Prophet's lifetime. Scholarship finds a stable consonantal text with documented variant readings. "Not one letter differs in any manuscript" overstates the case.
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - 15:9 promises preservation.
   - The collections under Abu Bakr and Uthman are reported in Bukhari 4986 and 4987.
   - The canonical qirā'āt are regarded as revealed modes. [Scholarly]
5. **History.**
   - The Birmingham leaves (Mingana 1572a) contain parts of suras 18–20 in Hijazi script.
   - The date applies to the skin, not the ink; the library catalogues the manuscript as mid-7th century.
   - Small argues the original text cannot be fully reconstructed, while Donner calls this tentative. [Debated]
6. **Science.** 1461±21 BP (OxA-29418). [Established]
7. **Psychology.** Separating three questions (preservation, authorship, interpretation) lowers anxiety.
8. **Limits.** Early codices such as Ibn Mas'ud's differ slightly.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A zoomable folio.
   - A 610–656 CE timeline.
   - A radiocarbon explainer.
10. **Sources.**
   - University of Birmingham, Cadbury Research Library
   - Déroche, *Qur'ans of the Umayyads* (Brill)
   - Sadeghi on the Ṣan'ā' palimpsest
   - Small, *Textual Criticism and Qur'an Manuscripts*
   - Corpus Coranicum (German)

### Q5. Is evolution compatible with Islam?
1. **Steelmanned.** Biology shows common ancestry by natural selection, while the Quran says Adam came from clay.
2. **Why asked.** Biology classes, and religion taught as opposed to science.
3. **Short answer.** Shoaib Ahmed Malik (*Islam and Evolution*, Routledge 2021) maps four positions: creationism, human exceptionalism, Adamic exceptionalism and no exceptions. Using al-Ghazali's hermeneutics, he finds the first three scripturally compatible and favours Adamic exceptionalism, developed by David Solomon Jalajel. The real dispute is narrow: whether Adam himself had ancestors.
4. **Religious explanation.** 3:59 likens Adam to Jesus as a sign. Malik cautions against reading Darwin into Ibn Khaldun or Ibn Miskawayh. [Scholarly]
5. **Philosophy.**
   - "Unguided" is metaphysics, not biology. In Ash'ari occasionalism, every natural process is God's act.
   - Objection: population genetics argues against a single founding pair. Adamic exceptionalism answers that other hominins could have coexisted with Adam. Critics call this ad hoc. [Debated]
6. **Science.** Common descent is [Established].
7. **Psychology.** Learning that these options exist often brings relief.
8. **Limits.** Institutional positions remain cautious.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A spectrum slider of the four positions.
   - A "science vs. metaphysics" sorting game.
   - A tree of life.
10. **Sources.**
   - Malik (open access)
   - Jalajel, *Islam and Biological Evolution*
   - *Philosophy in Review* (2024)
   - Guessoum, *Islam's Quantum Question*
   - Malik's letter in a Turkish DergiPark journal

### Q6. Does Islam treat women unequally?
1. **Steelmanned.** Inheritance (4:11), testimony (2:282), 4:34 and male leadership look structurally unequal.
2. **Why asked.** It is among the top four Turkish youth concerns, and tied to lived experience of patriarchy.
3. **Short answer.** The Quran affirms equal spiritual worth (33:35; 16:97) and creation from one soul (4:1). Several rulings are tied to the husband's full duty of maintenance. Many scholars restrict 4:34 sharply. Some injustices are custom, not text, and some questions remain contested.
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim limited 2:282 to the context of financial contracts.
   - On 4:34, many jurists required "ḍarb" to be symbolic. The Prophet never struck a woman (Muslim 2328). [Debated]
5. **Philosophy.** Equality vs. sameness. Objection: the rulings may encode their patriarchal context.
6. **Science.** Not central.
7. **Sociology.** Avoid a lecturing tone.
8. **Limits.** Reform proposals on inheritance are contested.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A verse-in-context explorer.
   - A timeline of women hadith scholars.
   - A diagram of household rights flows.
10. **Sources.**
   - Akram Nadwi, *al-Muhaddithat*
   - Kecia Ali, *Sexual Ethics and Islam*
   - Asma Barlas, *"Believing Women" in Islam*
   - Hidayet Şefkatli Tuksal, *Kadın Karşıtı Söylemin İslam Geleneğindeki İzdüşümleri*

### Q7. Is leaving Islam punishable by death?
1. **Steelmanned.** Classical law prescribed death for the male apostate, citing "whoever changes his religion, kill him" (Bukhari 3017). If so, faith is not free.
2. **Why asked.** Doubters fear for their safety, and the rule seems to undercut 2:256 ("no compulsion in religion").
3. **Short answer.** The Quran mentions apostasy (2:217; 5:54) with no worldly penalty, and declares no compulsion in religion (2:256). Classical jurists did prescribe execution. Many modern scholars, including Turkish scholars, argue the penalty targeted treason, not belief. [Debated, but widely held today]
4. **Religious explanation.**
   - Hanafis imprisoned rather than executed female apostates, which suggests the penalty was linked to being a combatant.
   - Sufyan al-Thawri is reported to have opposed executing apostate women.
   - Nihat Dalgın (İSAM, 1998–99) concludes apostasy alone cannot warrant death.
   - Diyanet Haber links historical executions to joining enemy ranks.
5. **Philosophy.** Coerced belief is nifāq (hypocrisy).
6. **Science.** Not applicable.
7. **Sociology.** In Pew Research Center's "The World's Muslims" (2013), 88% of Egyptian Muslims said leaving Islam should be punishable by death; the median across the 37 countries where the question was asked was 28%. The gap between ideal and attitudes must be acknowledged.
8. **Limits.** The reformist reading is an argument, not the uniform historical view.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A three-column card: Quran, fiqh, modern scholarship.
   - A legal-practice map.
   - A "safe to ask" card.
10. **Sources.**
   - Dalgın, "İrtidat ve Cezası"
   - Diyanet Haber, "Dinde Zorlama Yoktur"
   - Abdullah Saeed & Hassan Saeed, *Freedom of Religion, Apostasy and Islam*
   - Pew (2013)

### Q8. Is doubting a sin? I keep having blasphemous thoughts.
1. **Steelmanned.** Unchosen thoughts about God keep coming, and I recheck my prayer and ablution. Would a real believer have these thoughts?
2. **Why asked.** Scrupulosity (vesvese) is common in religious people and hides behind piety.
3. **Short answer.** Unwanted thoughts are not beliefs. God pardons inner whispers that are not acted on or spoken (Bukhari 5269; Muslim 127). The Prophet called the Companions' distress at such thoughts "clear faith" (Muslim 132). Frequent loops of checking may be scrupulosity OCD, which is treatable, and seeking help is consistent with faith.
4. **Religious explanation.** Jurists advised people with vesvese to ignore their doubts and not repeat acts, which parallels exposure and response prevention.
5. **Philosophy.** Doubt as inquiry (al-Ghazali) differs from doubt as compulsion.
6. **Clinical.**
   - Scrupulosity appears in about 5–33% of OCD patients.
   - About 70% of scrupulosity patients report interference with their religious practice.
   - Religious-themed OCD averages around 52% in Middle Eastern samples and about 27% in Turkey. [Scholarly; samples vary]
   - A Traditional Islamically Integrated Psychotherapy (TIIP) trial at Ibn Haldun University (NCT07872462) was completed in March 2026, with results not yet available.
7. **Psychology.** Yaqeen Institute: waswasa is a religious presentation of OCD, not a "Muslim disease". It needs clinician and imam to work together.
8. **Limits.** A website cannot diagnose.
9. **Visuals.**
   - A "thought vs. belief" flip card.
   - A "question or loop?" self-check linked to help resources.
   - An animation of the hadith in Muslim 132.
10. **Sources.**
   - Sahih Muslim 127 and 132
   - Yaqeen Institute, "Clinicians, Imams, and the Whisperings of Satan"
   - "Scrupulosity and Islam: a perspective"
   - NCT07872462
   - PMC, "From Doubt to Direction"

## Phase 3b — Signs for reflection (9 topics)

### S1. The seed and date stone split open (6:95; 56:63–64)
- **Verse:** "God is the splitter of grain and date-stone" (6:95). "Is it you who make it grow, or We?" (56:64).
- **Tafsir:** Al-Razi and al-Qurtubi stress the living emerging from the seemingly dead. Ibn Kathir stresses human dependence.
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Abscisic acid (ABA) maintains dormancy.
  - Water uptake triggers gibberellin signalling, and amylases convert stored starch to sugar.
  - The radicle (embryonic root) emerges and grows down by gravitropism; the shoot then rises.
- **Awe:**
  - A Masada date seed sprouted in 2005 (Sallon, Solowey et al., "Germination, Genetics, and Growth of an Ancient Date Seed," *Science* 320:1464, 2008). The University of Zurich dated it to 1,990 ± 50 years; the 2020 follow-up paper calls it a 1,900-year-old seed. The palm is named "Methuselah".
  - Six more ancient seeds were germinated in 2020 (*Science Advances*).
  - Dead Sea dryness limited free-radical damage.
  - Secondary sources report a female palm, "Hannah", later bore dates.
- **Reflection:** A stone that waits two millennia and still answers to water pictures 6:95, and the Quran's resurrection analogy (41:39).
- **Accuracy:** The verse makes no dormancy "prediction", and the dating is approximate.
- **Visual:** A germination time-lapse with an ABA/GA "switch", and a 2,000-year timeline.

### S2. Milk "from between digested food and blood" (16:66)
- **Tafsir:** Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi stress pure milk emerging from among impurities.
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Nutrients pass from gut to blood to the udder's alveoli, where cells synthesize lactose, casein and fat.
  - CSIRO Publishing (2005) puts it at about 500 L of blood through the udder per litre of milk. Cornell notes that a cow producing 60 litres a day circulates 30,000 litres of blood through the mammary gland.
- **Awe:** The range is 400–600 L of blood per litre of milk.
- **Reflection:** The verse's "between" fits the actual pathway from gut to blood to gland.
- **Accuracy:** It is not a cell-biology text. Present it as a fitting image.
- **Visual:** A nutrient-journey animation with a counter of litres of blood.

### S3. The bee and honey (16:68–69)
- **Verse:** God "inspired" the bee and from it comes a drink "of varied colours, with healing", a sign "for people who reflect".
- **Tafsir:** Al-Razi reads the bee's wahy (inspiration) as instinct. The verbs are feminine; worker bees are female. The Prophet recommended honey (Bukhari 5684).
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Female foragers carry nectar in the crop.
  - The enzyme invertase splits sucrose.
  - Fanning drives water below about 20%.
- **Awe:**
  - 50–100 flowers per trip.
  - Canadian Honey Council: over 2.6 million flower visits per pound (2,670,588, assuming at least 100 flowers per load, after Ribbands 1949) and about 50,000 miles of flight. The Council's own "Bee Facts" page rounds this to two million.
  - One bee makes about 1/12 teaspoon in its lifetime.
- **Accuracy:** These figures are assumption-based estimates. Honey is antibacterial and helps coughs, but it is not a cure-all.
- **Visual:** A hive cross-section and a jar-filling flower counter.

### S4. Creation of the human being (23:12–14; 22:5)
- **Verse:** The stages run nuṭfa (drop), ʿalaqa (clinging thing or clot), muḍgha (chewed lump), then bones clothed with flesh, then "another creation".
- **Tafsir:** Classical commentators read ʿalaqa as a blood clot and linked the stages to forty-day phases (Bukhari 3208), emphasizing power and resurrection.
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Implantation occurs around days 6–10.
  - The heartbeat begins around day 22.
  - Somites appear in weeks 3–4.
  - Bone and muscle both arise from mesoderm; cartilage models form around weeks 6–7, alongside muscle.
- **Reflection:** Capacity to create implies capacity to restore (22:5), and "another creation" marks personhood.
- **Accuracy:** Moore's "textbook embryology" claim is contested. Bone and muscle develop concurrently, and Needham noted Galenic parallels. Present the verses as a sign, not as science. [Debated]
- **Visual:** An embryo scroll across weeks 1–10 with a "verse / embryology" separator.

### S5. Sun and moon, each in an orbit (36:37–40; 21:33)
- **Verse:** The sun runs "to a resting place" (36:38); "each swims in an orbit" (36:40).
- **Tafsir:**
  - Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of the sun prostrating beneath the Throne (Bukhari 3199, 4802).
  - He also cites the reading of Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn 'Abbas: the sun has "no resting place".
  - Later scholars read the prostration as obedience. [Scholarly]
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - A 23.4° axial tilt gives seasons.
  - The Moon orbits in 27.3 days; its phases repeat every 29.5 days.
  - The Sun circles the galaxy in about 230 million years.
  - NASA JPL: laser ranging off the Apollo 11, 14 and 15 retroreflectors shows the Moon receding about 3.8 cm (1.5 inches) a year, with days lengthening about 1.7 ms per century.
- **Reflection:** The verses stress measured, reliable order (taqdīr), which underpins calendars and prayer times.
- **Accuracy:** The verse made no "galactic orbit" prediction; classical cosmology was geocentric. Do not hide the hadith; explain how it has been interpreted.
- **Visual:** An orbit and seasons simulator, and a laser-ranging animation.

### S6. Water and life (21:30; 56:68–70)
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Water is densest at about 4 °C. Ice is about 9% lighter than liquid water, so lakes freeze from the top.
  - Specific heat is about 4.18 J/g·K, which buffers climate.
  - Evaporation leaves salt behind, so rain is fresh, the contrast with "bitter" water in 56:70.
- **Reflection:** Every glass is the product of planetary distillation that humans did not make.
- **Accuracy:** The verse is not a claim about where life originated.
- **Visual:** A water cycle where "the salt stays behind", and a cross-section of a frozen lake.

### S7. Two seas and a barrier (55:19–20; 25:53)
- **Tafsir:** Al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir understood fresh and salt waters kept distinct by God's power.
- **How it works [Established]:**
  - Haloclines (salinity boundaries) and pycnoclines (density boundaries) separate water masses.
  - At Gibraltar, Mediterranean water (salinity about 38.4–38.5, about 13 °C) flows beneath Atlantic water (about 36.1). The interface is defined at salinity 37, and the outflow is about 0.7–1 Sverdrup.
- **Awe:** Rotating lenses of Mediterranean water ("meddies") travel far into the Atlantic.
- **Accuracy:** "Never mix" is wrong. Tides mix the water strongly, and the outflow blends downstream. Viral Gulf of Alaska videos show sediment fronts.
- **Visual:** A Gibraltar salinity cross-section and a halocline tank experiment.

### S8. Pairs and the diversity of tongues and colours (51:49; 30:22)
- **Tafsir:** Pairs are read broadly as complementary opposites.
- **Science [Established]:**
  - Not all life reproduces sexually.
  - Physics has charge pairs and particle–antiparticle pairs.
  - There are about 7,000 living languages.
  - Skin colour tracks UV exposure.
- **Reflection:** 49:13 gives diversity its purpose: "that you may know one another".
- **Accuracy:** The verse made no "antimatter prediction".
- **Visual:** A language map and a pairs flip-deck.

### S9. Fine-tuning (25:2; 54:49)
- **Background:** The kalam argument from particularization (takhṣīṣ), used by al-Ghazali and al-Razi.
- **Physics [Established; inferences Debated]:** Some constants sit in narrow ranges that permit life. One estimate puts the cosmological constant's fine-tuning at 1 part in 10^120 (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy).
- **Responses:**
  - The multiverse: inflation plus the string landscape.
  - Critics of that inference cite the "inverse gambler's fallacy" and the "this-universe objection" (Hacking, White); defenders dispute it.
  - Weinberg judged some cases "not so fine".
- **Accuracy:** Fine-tuning is an invitation, not proof.
- **Visual:** "Cosmic dials", alongside a three-way panel: design, multiverse, future physics.

## Phase 4 — Moral vision (7 themes)

**M1. Justice even against oneself (4:135; 5:8; 16:90).**
- Sources: the Prophet would have applied the law even to his daughter Fatima (Bukhari 3475). Al-Shatibi's maqāṣid (objectives of the law) and Ibn al-Qayyim: wherever justice is, there is God's law.
- Evidence: procedural-justice research links fair processes to trust. [Established]
- Gap: Arab Barometer shows falling trust in religious leaders.
- Visual: a "scales" game that detects bias.

**M2. Mercy and ihsān (21:107; 7:156; 3:159).**
- Sources: mercy in 100 parts, one sent to earth (Bukhari 6000); ihsān prescribed in all things (Muslim 1955); the woman who gave water to a thirsty dog (Bukhari 3467).
- Gap: harsh preaching, which contradicts 3:159.
- Visual: "100 parts of mercy".

**M3. Parents and family (17:23–24; 31:14–15).**
- Sources: "your mother" said three times (Bukhari 5971); there is no obedience in sin (31:15). Maqāṣid connects family to the preservation of lineage.
- Evidence: family cohesion correlates with adolescent wellbeing. [Established, correlational]
- Gap: honour-based control.
- Visual: a family-rights circle.

**M4. Ethics of speech (49:11–12).**
- Sources: speak good or stay silent (Bukhari 6018); al-Ghazali's twenty "calamities of the tongue".
- Gap: online abuse of doubters.
- Visual: a "before you post" filter.

**M5. Gratitude and patience (14:7; 2:153; 39:10).**
- Sources: Muslim 2999. Al-Ghazali: faith is half patience, half gratitude. The Prophet: "Should I not be a grateful servant?" (Bukhari 4837).
- Evidence: Cregg & Cheavens (2021) found g = −0.29 post-test and −0.23 at follow-up, smaller against active controls. A 2023 meta-analysis (23 studies, 6,745 people) found g = 0.22 vs neutral controls and 0.12 (not significant) vs active ones. [Established, small]
- Gap: "be patient" used to silence victims.
- Visual: a daily "three gifts" card.

**M6. Forgiveness and anger (3:134; 42:40; 24:22).**
- Sources: the strong one controls anger (Bukhari 6114). 24:22 was revealed when Abu Bakr cut off a relative who had slandered Aisha. 42:40 preserves the right to justice.
- Evidence: Wade et al. (2014) found Δ = 0.56 on forgiveness, plus lower depression and anxiety. A 2022 *Child Development* meta-analysis found reduced anger (g = 0.29). [Established, moderate]
- Story: Ta'if; Hind.
- Gap: forgiveness demanded of victims.
- Visual: a REACH forgiveness path.

**M7. Dignity and diversity (17:70; 49:13; 5:32).**
- Sources: the Farewell Sermon, "no superiority except by piety" (Musnad Ahmad 23489); the Hanafi view of inviolability through being human; Ibn 'Ashur and Auda.
- Evidence: contact research shows positive contact reduces prejudice. [Established]
- Gap: racism and sectarianism.
- Visual: a "circle of rights" running from self to all creation.

## Phase 5 — Presentation guidance

**Learning path.** Three doors: "Reflect", "Live", "Ask". The default flow runs signs → morals → questions, with cross-links between them.
- Doubt pages open with the steelmanned question.
- They close with a sign or practice.
- Many visitors arrive in crisis, so direct entry points are needed.

**Formats:**

| Content | Format |
|---|---|
| Signs | Scroll stories and simulations |
| Fine-tuning and orbits | Sliders |
| Questions | Layered cards: short answer → deep dive → sources |
| Disagreements | Side-by-side panels |
| Morals | Daily cards and scenario games |
| Manuscripts | Zoomable timelines |
| Accuracy | A distinct "Honest note" component |

**Languages.**
- Turkish: use established vocabulary (tefekkür, âyet, kader, cüz'î irade, vesvese) and lean on TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, Elmalılı, Nursi and DergiPark journals. The context includes KONDA trends, Diyanet's role and the 2023 earthquakes.
- English: gloss Arabic terms, and expect secular and New Atheist frames.

**Sensitive topics.** Apostasy, homosexuality, 4:34, slavery, Aisha, the Banu Qurayza, hijab, and theodicy after disasters. The approach:
- Steelman first.
- Show the range of views and name the gaps between ideal and practice.
- Avoid politics.
- Signpost mental-health support.
- Have a theologian and a clinician review content.

## Recommendations
1. Build the "Honest note" component into the design system. It is the credibility engine.
2. Launch with the eight question pages and nine sign pages above.
3. In Batch 2, mine Google Trends and "People also ask" data in Turkish, English and Arabic, and code Ekşi Sözlük and Reddit posts.

   - a Maturidi/Hanafi theologian
   - an Ash'ari or Athari scholar
   - domain scientists
   - a clinician experienced in scrupulosity

## Caveats
- Question frequencies are tiered, not measured.
- KONDA's "2025" data come from October 2024 fieldwork and combine atheists with non-believers.
- Bee and milk figures are approximations.
- Hadith references cite Sunni collections. Shi'i, Persian, Urdu, Malay, French and German sources are under-represented.
- The TIIP trial has no published results yet.

## Türkçe Özet

**Ana sonuç.** Gençlerin şüpheleri çoğunlukla "Allah var mı?" sorusundan değil, "Allah nasıl davranıyor?" sorusundan doğuyor. Öne çıkan konular kötülük, kader, deizm, kadının konumu ve dinin öğretilme biçimi.

**Veriler:**
- 2018 Konya çalıştayındaki 50 öğretmen, gençlerin ateizmden çok deizme yöneldiğini ve en sık sorulan sorunun "Allah kötülüğe neden müdahale etmiyor?" olduğunu bildirdi.
- Zübeyir Nişancı ve Hüseyin Sağlam'ın Marmara Üniversitesi çalışmasında Allah'a inanç %94,3 çıktı.
- KONDA (Ekim 2024, 6.137 kişi): "dindar" oranı %55'ten %46'ya indi, "ateist/inançsız" oranı %2'den %8'e çıktı, "sofu" oranı %12'de sabit kaldı.
- 15–29 yaş grubunda "dindar muhafazakâr" yaşam tarzı 2008–2018 arasında %28'den %15'e düştü.
- Arap dünyasında 2021–22'de dinî kimlikte kısmi bir geri dönüş görüldü. Şüphe çoğu zaman geçici bir dönemdir.

**Öncelikli sorular:** kötülük, kader, deizm, kadın, vesvese, evrim ve "bilimsel mucize" iddiaları, Kur'an'ın korunmuşluğu, dinden dönme.

**Güçlü âyetler:** 1.990 ± 50 yıllık hurma çekirdeğinin filizlenmesi (6:95); bir litre süt için memeden yaklaşık 500 litre kan geçmesi (16:66); bir libre bal için 2,6 milyondan fazla çiçek ziyareti (16:68–69); Ay'ın her yıl yaklaşık 3,8 cm uzaklaşması (36:37–40); buzun suyun üstünde yüzmesi (21:30); Cebelitarık'ta Akdeniz suyunun Atlantik suyunun altından akması (55:19–20); ince ayar (25:2).

**Doğruluk notları:** "İki deniz asla karışmaz" ifadesi yanlıştır. Embriyoloji âyetleri bir "ders kitabı" değil, kudretin âyetleri olarak sunulmalıdır. 36:38'deki secde hadisi gizlenmemeli; yorum tarihiyle birlikte anlatılmalıdır.

**Ahlâk temaları:** adalet, rahmet, anne-baba, dil âdâbı, şükür ve sabır, affetme ve öfke kontrolü, insan onuru. Araştırmalar şükür egzersizlerinin küçük, affetme müdahalelerinin orta düzeyde etkisi olduğunu gösteriyor. Bunlar destekleyici kanıttır, ispat değildir.

**Ruh sağlığı.** İstemsiz düşünceler inanç değildir. Hz. Peygamber bu düşüncelerden duyulan rahatsızlığı "sarih iman" diye nitelemiştir (Müslim 132). Sık ve yoğun kontrol döngülerinde profesyonel destek önerilmelidir.
