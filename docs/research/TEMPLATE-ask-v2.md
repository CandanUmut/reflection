# Research template v2 — deep answers for Ask (Phase 6 onward)

One file per phase (e.g. `07-ask-deep-I.md`), one section per question. Every claim has a source that was **actually read**; every Quran, hadith and tafsir citation is checked by the source checker before it reaches the site (CONTENT_PLAN.md §5). Write for the reader in plain words: these sections become the page.

## Rules (from Umut, 2026-10-31)
- Nothing invented. If a source cannot be read and checked, the claim is not used and the gap goes in `docs/CONTENT_GAPS.md`.
- **No claim rests on a weak hadith.** A weak (da'if) hadith appears only when it is discussed as weak.
- Fiqh: the **four Sunni schools** (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali), each with a named scholar or work.
- Hadith are cited as tokens, `{hadith:collection:number}` (sunnah.com numbering; Muslim by Abd al-Baqi). The page adds the grade from the data; never type a grade by hand.
- Not centred on Türkiye: Türkiye can be one example among others.
- Honest notes and honest limits never go inside the full answer, which may be folded behind "Read the full answer"; they go under "Honest limits", which is always visible.
- Honorifics (Umut, 2026-10-31), outside quotations: English "the Prophet (pbuh)" and "(pbuh)" after other prophets (Moses (pbuh)); Turkish "Hz. Peygamber (s.a.v.)" and "(a.s.)" after other prophets (Mûsâ (a.s.)). A Companion named as a narrator gets "(ra)" / "(r.a.)". Never change a verbatim Quran or hadith quotation. The ﷺ glyph is not in the site's fonts, so it is not used.

## Template

```
## Q<n> — <title>

### Sources read
- <full reference, with URL or edition/page> — what it was used for

### The question, at its strongest
<the doubt as its most serious holder would put it; 3–6 sentences>

### Short answer
<4–6 plain sentences>

### The full answer
#### quran — What the Quran says
<the verses (refs), what comes before and after, and asbab al-nuzul where a sound report exists>
#### sunnah — What the Prophet ﷺ taught
<hadith as {hadith:…} tokens, quoted or paraphrased from the checked text>
#### scholars — How scholars have understood it
<named classical and modern scholars, their works, and their actual arguments>
#### fiqh — What the four schools hold   (only where law applies)
<Hanafi / Maliki / Shafi'i / Hanbali, with named works; where they agree and differ, and why>
#### history | science | reasoning   (as the question needs)
<facts with sources and numbers; arguments set out step by step>
#### objection — The strongest objection, and the reply
<the hardest remaining objection, stated fairly, and the best reply; say if the reply is incomplete>
#### heart — For the heart and mind   (where readers are personally affected)
<honest, compassionate words and practical next steps>

#### views — The range of views
<each view: who holds it, the work, and the reason, in a sentence or two>

### Questions readers ask next
<the reader critic's follow-up questions, each with a short answer from the sources above>

### Honest limits
<what remains uncertain, disputed or unanswered>
```

## Length
About 1,200–2,500 words for the hardest questions, less for simpler ones. The short answer stays short.
