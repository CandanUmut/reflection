## S25 — The protected roof

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 21:30–33, 2:22, 35:41, 22:65, 15:16–18, 37:6–7.
- al-Tabari, *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān*, and al-Qurtubi, *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, on 21:32 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-tabary-qortobi-saadi/sura21-aya32.html — the meanings of *maḥfūẓ* and of "its signs".
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English), *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* and *Tanwīr al-Miqbās* on 21:32, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-32-sora-21.html — as on the current page.
- US Environmental Protection Agency, ozone glossary (epa.gov) — where the ozone layer is, and which ultraviolet it absorbs.
- NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, "Solar Wind Strips the Martian Atmosphere" (svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4370) — Mars, without a global magnetic field, losing atmosphere to the solar wind.
- NASA Science, "Meteors & Meteorites: Facts" (read for run 17) — "shooting stars", about 48.5 tons of material a day, "Less than 5% of the original object usually makes it down".

### In brief
"And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32): the verse calls the sky a roof that is kept safe, and its real complaint is that people do not look at it.

### Verse note
"And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32). Sura al-Anbiyāʾ is Meccan. The verse sits in a run of signs: the heavens and earth once "a joined entity" and separated, every living thing made from water (21:30); mountains "lest it should shift with them" (21:31); then this verse; then night and day, sun and moon, "all [heavenly bodies] in an orbit are swimming" (21:33). The run is addressed to "those who disbelieved" (21:30) — people who saw the sky every day.

A second verse uses the same image: "[He] who made for you the earth a bed [spread out] and the sky a ceiling and sent down from the sky, rain…" (2:22), Medinan, among the first commands of Sura al-Baqara, and it ends: "So do not attribute to Allah equals while you know."

- *Saqf*: a ceiling or roof.
- *Maḥfūẓ*: guarded, kept, preserved — from the root *ḥ-f-ẓ*, to keep safe.
- *Muʿriḍūn*: "turning away", turning the side of the face from something.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari (d. 923).** He reads "protected" as protected from devils: «حفظناها من كلّ شيطان رجيم» ("We guarded it from every accursed devil"). He reports Mujahid's gloss «مرفوعا» ("raised up") and Qatada's «سقفا مرفوعا، وموجا مكفوفا» ("a raised roof, and a wave held back"). The "signs" from which they turn away are «شمسها وقمرها ونجومها» — its sun, its moon and its stars — on which the disbelievers do not reflect.

**al-Qurtubi (d. 1273).** He gives a first meaning: «محفوظا من أن يقع ويسقط على الأرض» ("protected from falling down onto the earth"), and supports it with "Indeed, Allah holds the heavens and the earth, lest they cease" (35:41). A second meaning, from the grammarian al-Farraʾ: «محفوظا بالنجوم من الشياطين» ("guarded by the stars from the devils"). He also reports Mujahid's "raised up", and lists other views (kept from ruin; standing without pillars). On "its signs" he cites Mujahid: the sun and the moon.

**Ibn Kathir (d. 1373).** "High and protected from anything reaching it"; with Mujahid, "Raised up". On the end of the verse, people do not think about how it was made, "so vast and high".

**al-Jalālayn.** "a roof for the earth functioning like the roof of a house preserved from collapsing".

**Where they differ.** Three kinds of protection are named: from falling (al-Qurtubi, al-Jalālayn; compare 22:65, "He restrains the sky from falling upon the earth"), from being reached (Ibn Kathir), and from devils (al-Tabari, al-Farraʾ; compare 15:17 and 37:7). **What they did not say:** none of them speaks of protection from radiation or of layers of air; the protection they describe is the sky's own, not the earth's.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "protected ceiling" — what keeps the world under the sky safe. These are modern facts, set beside the verse; they are not what the commentators meant.

- **Ultraviolet light.** [Established] The ozone layer "lies approximately 15-40 kilometers (10-25 miles) above the Earth's surface, in the stratosphere". It "protects the Earth against most UVB coming from the sun"; the harshest band, UV-C, "is completely absorbed by ozone and normal oxygen"; UV-A "is not absorbed by ozone" (US EPA).
- **Falling rock.** [Established] Material from space falls into the atmosphere constantly — about 48.5 tons a day — and most of it burns up as "shooting stars"; of a larger meteoroid, "Less than 5% of the original object usually makes it down" (NASA Science).
- **The solar wind.** [Established] The Sun sends out a stream of charged particles. Earth's global magnetic field turns most of it aside. Mars "lacks a global magnetic field to deflect the incoming solar wind", and NASA's MAVEN orbiter has watched the solar wind strip ions from its upper atmosphere; the solar wind is "a major driver" of Mars's atmospheric loss (NASA Scientific Visualization Studio). [Moderate, for how much the magnetic field matters for keeping an atmosphere: Venus, also without a global field, has kept a thick one, and this is debated.]

### Points of awe
- The ozone layer sits 15–40 km up and absorbs most of the Sun's UV-B and all of its UV-C (US EPA).
- About 48.5 tons of space material enter the atmosphere every day, and most of it burns up before reaching the ground (NASA Science).
- Mars, without a global magnetic field, is still losing its atmosphere to the solar wind (NASA, MAVEN).

### The reflection
A roof is something we only notice when it leaks. The verse asks us to look at the sky that way: "And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32).

The commentators each saw a different protection. For al-Qurtubi and al-Jalālayn, the sky is held up and does not fall — the same thought as "He restrains the sky from falling upon the earth" (22:65). For Ibn Kathir, it is high, beyond reach. For al-Tabari, it is guarded from devils. In every reading the sky is kept, and kept for us.

But the verse's weight falls on its last words: "they… are turning away". Al-Tabari names what they ignore: its sun, its moon and its stars. The signs are not hidden. They are overhead every day, and their very constancy makes them easy to stop seeing.

We now know of protections the first listeners could not see: a thin band of ozone, a magnetic field, an atmosphere that burns up falling rock. They do not prove the verse; the verse never mentioned them. But they can do what the verse asks: make us look up again, and ask who keeps the roof in place. The sura answers in the next verse: "it is He who created the night and the day and the sun and the moon" (21:33).

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** That 21:32 "describes the ozone layer" or "the magnetosphere" is widely repeated. No classical commentator read it that way: they spoke of the sky being held up, out of reach, or guarded from devils. The modern facts are a reflection that fits the image, not the verse's meaning.
- **"Protected" from what?** The verse does not say. The commentators disagreed, and their answers (falling, being reached, devils) are all about the sky itself, not about shielding the earth.
- **The magnetic field.** It turns aside most of the solar wind, but how much a magnetic field matters for keeping an atmosphere is still debated (Venus has a thick atmosphere without one). The page should not say the magnetic field alone "keeps our air".
- **Meteors.** Most small meteoroids burn up, but large ones do reach the ground; the "roof" is not a perfect shield, and the verse does not say it is.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does "protected ceiling" mean?** The classical commentators gave three answers: protected from falling (al-Qurtubi, citing 35:41; al-Jalālayn), raised out of reach (Ibn Kathir, with Mujahid), and guarded from devils (al-Tabari; al-Farraʾ). The verse itself does not specify.

**Does the verse describe the ozone layer?** No. The ozone layer and the magnetic field are modern facts that fit the image of a protective roof, but no classical commentator read the verse that way, and the verse names neither.

**Why does the verse say people "turn away" from its signs?** Al-Tabari says the signs are the sun, the moon and the stars, and that the disbelievers do not reflect on them. The complaint is not that the signs are hidden, but that people stop looking.

**Is the sky a solid roof?** The Quran uses "roof" and "ceiling" as images (2:22; 21:32). Al-Jalālayn compares it to "the roof of a house". The commentators did not need a solid dome to make their point: the sky is held, kept and ordered.

**What do the verses around it say?** 21:30–33 form one passage: the heavens and earth separated, life from water, the mountains, the protected sky, and the sun and moon each in an orbit. Each is a sign, and the passage asks: "Then will they not believe?" (21:30).

## S23 — Expansion of the heavens

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 51:47–49, 21:30.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English), with *Tanwīr al-Miqbās*, *al-Jalālayn* and Mawdudi, on 51:47, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-47-sora-51.html.
- al-Qurtubi on 51:47 (Arabic), https://ssr.quran.com/51:47/tafsirs/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi — the meanings of *bi-ayd* and *mūsiʿūn* and who held each.
- Hubble, "A Relation between Distance and Radial Velocity among Extra-Galactic Nebulae", *PNAS* 15(3):168 (1929) (NASA ADS record).
- Kragh, "Hubble Law or Hubble–Lemaître Law? The IAU Resolution", arXiv 1809.02557 (2018) (read for run 17) — Lemaître 1927; the IAU's 2018 renaming.
- Nobel Prize in Physics 2011, press release (nobelprize.org) — the accelerating expansion.
- Planck Collaboration, "Planck 2018 results. VI. Cosmological parameters", arXiv 1807.06209 (*A&A* 641, A6, 2020) — H₀ = 67.4 ± 0.5.
- Riess et al., "A Comprehensive Measurement of the Local Value of the Hubble Constant…", arXiv 2112.04510 (*ApJL* 934, L7, 2022) — H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04; "a 5-sigma difference".

### In brief
"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander" (51:47): the verse speaks of a sky built with power and of a God who is *mūsiʿ* — one who makes wide, or who has wide power.

### Verse note
"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander" (51:47). Sura al-Dhāriyāt is Meccan. The verse opens a short passage on creation: "And the earth We have spread out, and excellent is the preparer" (51:48); "And of all things We created two mates; perhaps you will remember" (51:49). The passage follows the stories of earlier peoples and leads to the call "So flee to Allah" (51:50).

- *Bi-ayd*: "with strength" — *ayd* is power, not "hands" (from *ʾ-y-d*, to strengthen).
- *Mūsiʿūn*: from *wasiʿa*, to be wide. The form can mean "We make [it] wide", "We are making [it] wide", or "We have vast capacity / are able". Saheeh's bracket "[its]" chooses the first.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "With strength" is the explanation of "'Abdullah bin 'Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ath-Thawri and several others". *La-mūsiʿūn*: "We made it vast and We brought its roof higher without pillars to support it."

**al-Qurtubi.** *Bi-ayd*: «بقوة وقدرة» ("with strength and power"), from Ibn ʿAbbas and others. For *mūsiʿūn* he lists several meanings:
- Ibn ʿAbbas: «لقادرون» ("We are able"); and also «موسعون الرزق على خلقنا» ("We widen provision for Our creatures").
- al-Hasan al-Basri: «وإنا لمطيقون» ("We are capable of it"), and «موسعون الرزق بالمطر» ("widening provision by rain").
- al-Dahhak: «أغنيناكم» ("We have made you rich").
- al-Qutabi: «ذو سعة على خلقنا» ("possessing abundance over Our creatures").
- Others: God widened the space between heaven and earth.
- al-Jawhari, from the verb *awsaʿa*: «أغنياء قادرون» ("rich and able").
He notes that the verse covers all these readings.

**Tanwīr al-Miqbās** (attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas): "We it is who make the vast extent (thereof) as We will", or "we expand the provision thereof". **Mawdudi** gives two readings: one who "possesses power and means", or one who "can extend and expand something".

**Where they differ.** Most early readings take *mūsiʿūn* as power or wealth — God is able, God widens provision — and a smaller group as making the sky wide. **What they did not say:** none of them describes the sky as growing larger over time; Ibn Kathir's "We made it vast" is about how it was made.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "expander". These are modern findings set beside the word, not its classical meaning.

- **The universe is expanding.** [Established] In 1927 Georges Lemaître was the first "to propose and justify that the universe is in a state of expansion", in a Belgian journal; in 1929 Edwin Hubble published the relation between galaxies' distances and their speeds of recession (*PNAS* 15:168). In 2018 the International Astronomical Union recommended calling it the "Hubble–Lemaître law" (Kragh, 2018).
- **The expansion is speeding up.** [Established] In 1998 two teams studying "over 50 distant supernovae whose light was weaker than expected" found that the expansion is accelerating; the cause "is thought to be driven by dark energy", whose nature is unknown. They received the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics (nobelprize.org).
- **How fast — and a puzzle.** [Established that the measurements disagree; Debated why.] The Planck satellite's map of the early universe gives a Hubble constant of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s per megaparsec (Planck 2018). Measurements of nearby Cepheid stars and supernovae give 73.04 ± 1.04, "a 5-sigma difference" (Riess et al., 2022). A megaparsec is about 3.26 million light-years. This disagreement is the "Hubble tension"; no agreed explanation exists.

### Points of awe
- Every distant galaxy is moving away from us, and the farther it is, the faster (Hubble, 1929; Lemaître, 1927).
- The expansion is accelerating, driven by something called dark energy that no one yet understands (Nobel Prize 2011).
- Two of the most careful measurements in science disagree on the expansion rate by about 8% (67.4 against 73.0), and the gap is statistically significant at five sigma (Planck 2018; Riess et al. 2022).

### The reflection
"And the heaven We constructed with strength" (51:47). The verse begins with power. The sky, so vast and high, was built — and built with strength. Then: "and indeed, We are [its] expander".

The early commentators heard in *mūsiʿūn* the breadth of God's power and generosity: He is able; He widens provision, by rain and by wealth (al-Qurtubi, reporting Ibn ʿAbbas, al-Hasan and al-Dahhak). Ibn Kathir heard the breadth of the sky itself, raised "without pillars". All of these are true to the word.

A modern reader brings another picture: a universe whose distances are growing, and growing faster. The word allows that reading too, and a reader may notice the fit. But the verse is not a measurement, and the commentators show how much more the word holds.

The passage itself moves quickly from the sky to the earth "spread out", to the pairs in all things, and then to the point of it all: "perhaps you will remember" (51:49), and "So flee to Allah" (51:50). The wide sky is meant to send us somewhere — not only outward to the edge of the universe, but back to the One whose power and generosity are wider still.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** "The Quran predicted the expanding universe" overstates. The word *mūsiʿūn* allows an "expanding" reading, but most early commentators read it as power or as widening provision, and Ibn Kathir as having made the sky vast. None read it as continuing expansion.
- **Harmony, not prediction.** The modern reading is one the word allows; it stands beside the classical readings and is not the verse's only meaning (al-Qurtubi: the verse covers all of them).
- **Numbers are unsettled.** The expansion rate is still disputed (the Hubble tension), so claims that "the Quran matches the number" cannot hold.
- **Who discovered it.** Lemaître proposed the expansion in 1927, before Hubble's 1929 paper; the IAU now recommends "Hubble–Lemaître law".

### Questions readers ask next
**Does 51:47 say the universe is expanding?** The word *mūsiʿūn* can be read that way, but the early commentators mostly read it as God's power or His widening of provision (al-Qurtubi, reporting Ibn ʿAbbas, al-Hasan and al-Dahhak), and Ibn Kathir as having made the sky vast.

**What does *bi-ayd* mean?** "With strength" — the reading of Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Thawri and others (Ibn Kathir; al-Qurtubi). It is not "hands".

**Who discovered the expansion?** Georges Lemaître proposed and justified it in 1927, and Edwin Hubble published the distance–velocity relation in 1929. The IAU now recommends the name "Hubble–Lemaître law" (Kragh, 2018).

**What is the Hubble tension?** The early universe (Planck) gives an expansion rate of 67.4 km/s per megaparsec; nearby stars and supernovae give 73.0. The difference is too large to be chance, and no one yet knows why.

**What is dark energy?** It is the name for whatever is making the expansion speed up. It makes up about three quarters of the universe, but its nature is unknown (Nobel Prize press release, 2011).

## S19 — Iron "sent down"

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 57:25–26.
- al-Tabari on 57:25 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura57-aya25.html.
- al-Qurtubi on 57:25 (Arabic), https://ssr.quran.com/57:25/tafsirs/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* on 57:25, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-25-sora-57.html (as on the current page).
- IUPAC Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW), "Iron" (ciaaw.org) — isotopic composition of natural iron.
- "Earth's accretion inferred from iron isotopic anomalies of supernova nuclear statistical equilibrium origin", arXiv 2110.06045 (2021; abstract) — iron isotopes made in supernovae, and where Earth's iron came from.
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, "Iron: Fact Sheet for Health Professionals" — iron in haemoglobin and myoglobin; 3–4 g in an adult body.

### In brief
"And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people" (57:25): the verse places iron beside the messengers, the Book and the balance — power given to serve justice.

### Verse note
"We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people, and so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen. Indeed, Allah is Powerful and Exalted in Might" (57:25). Sura al-Ḥadīd ("Iron") is Medinan. The next verse turns to Noah and Abraham and the prophethood in their descendants (57:26).

- *Anzalnā* ("We sent down"): the same verb is used in this verse for the Scripture and the balance.
- *Baʾs shadīd*: "great military might"; *baʾs* is force, harm, the hardness of war.
- *Manāfiʿ*: "benefits", uses.
- **The structure.** Messengers, Book, balance, iron: guidance, the standard of justice, and the power to defend it — and a test: "so that Allah may make evident those who support Him… unseen".

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari.** He glosses the verb as "We sent down iron *for them*" («وأنزلنا لهم الحديد»). He records a report through ʿIkrima from Ibn ʿAbbas: «ثلاثة أشياء نزلت مع آدم: السندان والكلبتان والميقعة والمطرقة» ("Three things came down with Adam: the anvil, the tongs, the… and the hammer"). On *baʾs shadīd*: Ibn Zayd, «السيوف والسلاح الذي يقاتل الناس بها» ("swords and the weapons with which people fight"); Mujahid, «جُنّة وسلاح» ("shields and weapons"); al-Tabari's own gloss, «فيه قوّة شديدة» ("in it is great strength"). On *manāfiʿ*: Ibn Zayd, people dig the earth and the mountains with it.

**al-Qurtubi.** He gives several meanings of "sent down":
- al-Hasan: «أنزلنا الحديد أي: أنشأناه وخلقناه» ("We brought it into being and created it");
- "the people of meanings" (*ahl al-maʿānī*): God brought it out of the mines and taught people how to work it;
- reports that iron came down with Adam (Ibn ʿAbbas via ʿIkrima; al-Thaʿlabi, from Ibn ʿAbbas, of five iron tools);
- a report attributed to ʿUmar that God sent down "four blessings" from the sky, iron among them.
On *baʾs shadīd*: weapons and armour, or the fear of being killed; on *manāfiʿ*: tools such as knives, axes and needles.

**Ibn Kathir.** The balance is "justice, according to Mujahid, Qatadah and others"; iron is "a deterrent for those who refuse the truth and oppose it after the proof has been established against them"; its benefits include "coins, hammers, axes, saws chisels, shovels and various tools". **Al-Jalālayn:** "We caused it to be extracted from mineral ores."

**Where they differ.** "Sent down" is read as *created* (al-Hasan), as *brought out of the earth and taught* (*ahl al-maʿānī*, al-Jalālayn), or literally, with reports of tools descending with Adam. The reports about Adam and ʿUmar's "four blessings" are given without grades in the pages read, and nothing on this page rests on them. **What they did not say:** no commentator connects iron to the stars or to meteorites.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "iron" and "benefits for the people". These are modern facts set beside the verse.

- **What natural iron is.** [Established] Natural iron is a mixture of four stable isotopes; iron-56 is 91.754% of it (amount fraction "0.917 54"), with iron-54 at 5.845%, iron-57 at 2.119% and iron-58 at 0.282% (IUPAC, CIAAW).
- **Made in stars.** [Established] Iron is built in stars, and much of it in exploding stars. A 2021 study of iron-isotope anomalies traces some of the isotopes in meteorites and Earth's mantle to "nuclear statistical equilibrium either in type Ia supernovae or in the Si/S shell of core-collapse supernovae" (arXiv 2110.06045). That iron reached Earth as the planet formed, in the dust and rock from which it grew; it did not later "fall" as a separate gift. [Iron's place as the end-point of energy-releasing fusion is standard physics; its nickel-56 → cobalt-56 → iron-56 route is reported here from *Wikipedia*.]
- **Iron in us.** [Established] "Iron is an essential component of hemoglobin", the red-blood-cell protein that carries oxygen, and of myoglobin in muscle; "Most of the 3 to 4 grams (g) of elemental iron that is present in adults is found in hemoglobin" (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements).

### Points of awe
- An adult body holds only 3–4 grams of iron — less than a small nail — and most of it carries oxygen in the blood (NIH ODS).
- More than nine-tenths of all natural iron is a single isotope, iron-56 (IUPAC, CIAAW).
- The iron in Earth's rocks carries isotopic traces of the exploding stars that made it (arXiv 2110.06045).

### The reflection
The verse sets iron in surprising company. First the messengers; then the Book; then "the balance", which Ibn Kathir, with Mujahid and Qatada, explains as justice. And then: "And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people" (57:25).

Iron is power. It makes ploughs and swords, needles and chains. The verse does not pretend power is harmless; it calls it "great military might". But it places that power after the balance. Strength is meant to serve justice, not to replace it. Al-Tabari's reporters name swords and shields; al-Qurtubi's name knives, axes and needles. The same metal defends and serves.

"Sent down" is the word used for the Book. The commentators heard it in different ways — created, brought out of the earth and taught, or given from above. Whatever the path, the verse treats iron as a trust, given with a test: "so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen".

A modern reader may add, as reflection, that the iron in our blood was made in stars long before the earth existed — and that each of us carries a few grams of it. The question the verse leaves with us is about our own strength, in whatever form we hold it: is it serving the balance, or have we put it in the balance's place?

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** "The Quran revealed that iron came from space." The word *anzalnā* is used in the same verse for the Book and the balance; the classical readings are *created*, *brought out of the mines and taught*, or reports about tools descending with Adam. None connects it to stars or meteorites.
- **Iron is not special in this.** Every element heavier than hydrogen and helium — the oxygen we breathe, the carbon in our bodies — was made in stars. A stellar origin for iron enriches the reflection; it is not a fulfilled prediction.
- **Iron did not "arrive later".** Earth's iron came with the material the planet formed from; the 2021 study traces isotope traces in that material to supernovae. It is not the case that Earth formed without iron and iron was then delivered.
- **The reports in the tafsir.** The report that tools came down with Adam (al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi) and ʿUmar's "four blessings" (al-Qurtubi) were not found with grades in the sources read; they are reported here as what the commentators record, not as established hadith.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "sent down" mean iron came from the sky?** Not in the classical commentaries. Al-Hasan read it as "created", al-Jalālayn and "the people of meanings" as "brought out of the mines", and some reports speak of tools descending with Adam. The same verb is used for the Book in the same verse.

**Why is iron mentioned next to the Book and the balance?** Ibn Kathir explains the balance as justice and iron as "a deterrent" for those who oppose the truth after it is made clear. Guidance, the standard of justice and the power to uphold it belong together.

**Is it true that Earth's iron was made in stars?** Yes. Iron, like other heavier elements, was made in stars and supernovae; a 2021 study traces isotope anomalies in Earth's mantle to supernova processes (arXiv 2110.06045). That is true of oxygen and carbon too.

**What are the "benefits for the people"?** The commentators name tools: hammers, axes, saws, shovels (Ibn Kathir), knives and needles (al-Qurtubi), and digging the earth (Ibn Zayd, in al-Tabari). Today one would add iron's role in our blood (NIH ODS).

**What is the test at the end of the verse?** "So that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen" (57:25) — whether people use strength to support justice when no one is watching.

## S15 — Rain reviving dead earth

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 41:37–39, 22:5, 35:9, 50:11, 30:19.
- al-Tabari on 41:39 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/kortoby-tabary/sura41-aya39.html — *khāshiʿa*, *ihtazzat*, *rabat*, and the resurrection link (the page did not show al-Qurtubi's text).
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* on 41:39, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-39-sora-41.html (as on the current page).
- USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service, "Vertisols" (nrcs.usda.gov) — clay soils that swell when wet and crack when dry.
- Jessica Hill (Associated Press), "Wildflowers are blooming in the driest place in North America — but not for long", OPB (10 March 2026) (as on the current page).

### In brief
"And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and grows" (41:39): the verse points from dry ground turning green to the God who will raise the dead.

### Verse note
"And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and grows. Indeed, He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead. Indeed, He is over all things competent" (41:39). Sura Fuṣṣilat is Meccan. The verse closes a run of signs: night and day, sun and moon, with the command "Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostate to Allah, who created them" (41:37), and the angels who "exalt Him by night and by day" (41:38).

- *Khāshiʿa* ("stilled"): low, humbled, still; the same word describes a humble heart or lowered eyes.
- *Ihtazzat* ("quivers"): stirs, moves.
- *Rabat* ("grows"): swells, rises.
- **The same picture elsewhere.** "And you see the earth barren, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and swells and grows [something] of every beautiful kind" (22:5); "We drive them to a dead land and give life thereby to the earth after its lifelessness. Thus is the resurrection" (35:9; compare 50:11, 30:19).

### Classical tafsir
**al-Tabari.** On *khāshiʿa*: Qatada, «غبراء متهشمة» ("dusty and crumbling"); al-Suddi, «يابسة متهمشة» ("dry and crumbling"). On *ihtazzat*: Mujahid, «بالنبات» ("with plants"); al-Tabari: it «تحرّكت به» — moved with the rain. On *rabat*: al-Suddi, «انتفخت» ("swelled"); Mujahid, the earth rises before the plants come out. On the conclusion, al-Suddi draws the parallel directly: «كما يحيي الأرض بالمطر، كذلك يحيي الموتى بالماء يوم القيامة بين النفختين» ("as He revives the earth with rain, so He will revive the dead with water on the Day of Resurrection, between the two blasts").

**Ibn Kathir.** "Stilled" "means, lifeless, with nothing growing in it; it is dead"; when rain comes, "it brings forth all kinds of crops and fruits"; "And among His signs" "means, signs of His ability to bring the dead back to life."

**al-Jalālayn.** "you see the earth desolate dried out without any vegetation"; with the rain "it stirs it moves and swells swells and rises."

**What they share.** All read the verse as an argument from the yearly revival of the land to the resurrection. Al-Suddi, in al-Tabari, makes the parallel physical: water revives the earth, and water will revive the dead. **What they did not say:** none treats the verse as a lesson in soil science; Mujahid and al-Suddi notice that the earth *swells* before plants appear, but as observation, not theory.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "stilled", "quivers" and "grows". These are modern facts set beside the verse.

- **Ground that swells.** [Established] Some soils visibly change with water. Vertisols "have a high content of expanding clay minerals" and "undergo pronounced changes in volume with changes in moisture": they shrink and form "deep, wide cracks" when dry and swell when wet (USDA NRCS). The early observation in al-Tabari — the earth "swells" (al-Suddi) and rises before the plants (Mujahid) — matches what anyone can see in such ground.
- **Seeds that wait.** [Established] In Death Valley, the driest place in North America, long-dormant seeds in the soil germinate after rare heavy rain; ecologist Tiffany Pereira (Desert Research Institute): "desert plants have adapted to go decades without water" (AP, 2026).
- **The 2026 bloom.** [Established] After "nearly a year's worth of rain since October", including the wettest November on record (1.76 inches, 4.47 cm, per the National Park Service), park ranger Matthew Lamar called it "the best the park has seen since 2016"; such blooms are "once-in-a-decade" (AP, 2026).

### Points of awe
- Clay soils can swell and shrink with every wet and dry season, opening cracks wide and deep (USDA NRCS).
- Seeds in desert soil can wait for decades for enough rain (AP, 2026).
- One wet winter turned Death Valley into fields of flowers in 2026 (AP).

### The reflection
Most of us have seen it: a field brown and cracked in summer, and the same field green a few weeks after the rains. It is so ordinary that we forget to be amazed. The Quran does not forget. "And of His signs is that you see the earth stilled, but when We send down upon it rain, it quivers and grows" (41:39).

The word for "stilled", *khāshiʿa*, is used elsewhere for a humble heart. Qatada saw the ground "dusty and crumbling"; al-Suddi, "dry". It lies low, with no power to green itself. Then the rain comes and the verbs move: it stirs, it swells, it rises.

The verse draws its own conclusion: "Indeed, He who has given it life is the Giver of Life to the dead." Al-Suddi, in al-Tabari, made the comparison exact: as He revives the earth with rain, so He will revive the dead. The Quran repeats the picture — "Thus is the resurrection" (35:9) — because it is repeated every year in every landscape, for anyone who looks.

There is a lesson for the heart as well, offered as reflection. A life that feels dry and still is not therefore dead. The verse that began by forbidding prostration to the sun and moon (41:37) ends by pointing to the One who sends the rain. Hope is not in the weather; it is in Him.

### Honest notes
- **An analogy, not botany.** The verse uses the revival of the earth as an argument for resurrection. It makes no claim about seed banks, clay chemistry or germination.
- **Figures not sourced.** The current page says seeds carry "germination inhibitors that only a soaking rain washes away" and that "Death Valley averages a little under 2 inches (about 50 mm)" of rain a year. Neither was found in the sources read; they should stay off unless sourced.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about this verse was read.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does "stilled" (*khāshiʿa*) mean?** Low, humbled, lifeless. Qatada (in al-Tabari) explains it as "dusty and crumbling", al-Suddi as "dry", Ibn Kathir as "lifeless, with nothing growing in it".

**Why compare rain to resurrection?** Because both are life after death that only God can give. The Quran makes the comparison several times (22:5; 35:9; 50:11; 30:19), and al-Suddi says that as God revives the earth with rain, He will revive the dead with water.

**Does the earth really "swell" when it rains?** Some soils do: clay-rich Vertisols swell when wet and crack when dry (USDA NRCS). Mujahid and al-Suddi noticed the earth swelling before the plants appear. The verse's point, though, is the coming of life, not soil physics.

**How long can seeds wait for rain?** Desert plants "have adapted to go decades without water", according to an ecologist quoted by the AP (2026).

**Does the verse speak to the heart too?** The Quran uses *khāshiʿ* of humble hearts and eyes. Reading the verse as hope for a dry life is a reflection, not its literal meaning — but it is the same God who sends both rains.

## S22 — Fingertips and resurrection

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 75:1–5, 75:40.
- Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and al-Tabari on 75:4 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-katheer-qortobi-tabary/sura75-aya4.html.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English), *al-Jalālayn* and *Tanwīr al-Miqbās* on 75:3–4, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-4-sora-75.html (as on the current page).
- Glover et al., "The developmental basis of fingerprint pattern formation and variation", *Cell* 186 (2023), doi 10.1016/j.cell.2023.01.015 (abstract, Cardiff University repository).
- Glenn Langenburg, *Scientific American*, "Are one's fingerprints similar to those of his or her parents…?" (as on the current page).

### In brief
"Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:3–4): the verse answers doubt about resurrection with the smallest bones of the hand.

### Verse note
"Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:3–4). Sura al-Qiyāma ("The Resurrection") is Meccan. It opens with two oaths: "I swear by the Day of Resurrection" (75:1) and "I swear by the reproaching soul" (75:2). After the answer comes the real reason for doubt: "But man desires to continue in sin" (75:5). The sura ends: "Is not that [Creator] Able to give life to the dead?" (75:40).

- *Banān*: the ends of the fingers (and toes).
- *Nusawwiya*: "proportion", make even, shape right.

### Classical tafsir
The commentators read 75:4 in two ways.

**1. God can restore even the fingertips.** al-Qurtubi gives this reading from Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjaj: «بلى قادرين على أن نعيد السلاميات على صغرها، ونؤلف بينها حتى تستوي» ("We are able to restore the small finger-bones, small as they are, and join them until they are made even"), and he judges it «أشبه بمساق الآية» — closer to the flow of the verse. Ibn Kathir also takes this as «الظاهر من الآية» (the apparent sense): God can rebuild him, "and if We wished We could surely resurrect him with more than what he originally had" (abridged English).

**2. God could have made the hand a hoof.** Ibn ʿAbbas: «لو شاء لجعله خفا أو حافرا» ("Had He willed, He would have made it a camel's foot or a hoof"); Mujahid: «كخفّ البعير فلا يعمل بهما شيئا» ("like a camel's foot, so that he could do nothing with them"); ʿIkrima and Qatada likewise (al-Tabari). Ibn Kathir names Mujahid, ʿIkrima, al-Hasan, Qatada, al-Dahhak and Ibn Jarir for this view, and reports that Ibn Jarir took it to mean God could have done this in this world. al-Qurtubi calls it the view of Ibn ʿAbbas and «عامة المفسرين» ("most commentators"). *Tanwīr al-Miqbās*: "such that his palm becomes like the hoof of a camel or beasts."

**al-Hasan al-Basri** adds a third angle (al-Tabari; al-Qurtubi): «جعلها يدا، وجعلها أصابع يقبضهنّ ويبسطهنّ» ("He made it a hand, and made it fingers that he closes and opens") — the gift of a working hand.

**Where they differ.** One reading stresses restoration (Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjaj; preferred by al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir); the other stresses God's power over the hand's very form (Ibn ʿAbbas and most early commentators). **What they did not say:** none mentions fingerprints, ridges or lines on the skin.

### How it works
Each point is tied to *banān*, the fingertips. These are modern facts set beside the verse.

- **Ridges formed in waves.** [Established] Fingerprint ridges form before birth. A 2023 study found that ridges form "as a set of waves spreading from variable initiation sites", and that "the propagation and meeting of these waves" determine the pattern type; the spacing is set by a "Turing reaction-diffusion system" built on signalling among EDAR, WNT and BMP (Glover et al., *Cell*, 2023).
- **When.** [Established] Small pads on the fetal fingertips grow and then shrink back from about the 10th week; the events that set the ridges "collide during weeks 10 through 15"; a high pad tends to give a whorl, an intermediate one a loop, a low one an arch (Langenburg, *Scientific American*).
- **Inherited, but not copied.** [Established] Pattern types run in families, but the exact ridge details are "random and not genetically linked". Identical twins often share pattern types; "The identifying characteristics are different, however" (Langenburg).

### Points of awe
- Fingertip ridges are laid down in the womb, roughly between weeks 10 and 15 (Langenburg).
- They spread as waves from several starting points, and where the waves meet decides whether a loop, whorl or arch forms (Glover et al., 2023).
- Identical twins, with the same DNA, still have different fingerprint details (Langenburg).

### The reflection
The doubter's question is old: when bones have scattered and crumbled, who can put them back? The sura begins by swearing by the Day itself, and by "the reproaching soul", and then answers with the smallest part of the hand: "Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:4).

The commentators heard two things in it. Ibn Qutayba and al-Zajjaj, followed by al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir: if God can rejoin even the tiny finger-bones and make them even again, the rest is not hard. Ibn ʿAbbas and most early commentators: God could have made the hand a hoof, useless for work. Al-Hasan saw the gift inside the warning: a hand with fingers that open and close.

A reader today may add a third layer, as reflection: the skin of those fingertips carries a pattern set in the womb, spreading in waves, unique to each person — not even an identical twin shares it. The verse does not speak of prints. But someone who looks at their own fingertip can feel the force of its question: the One who made this so finely, can He not make it again?

The sura names the real obstacle a verse later: "But man desires to continue in sin" (75:5). The question is not only whether God can; it is whether we want Him to.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says "Look what Quran says almost 1400 years ago in Surah Qiyamah verses 3-4", concluding that "people's identities are coded at their fingertips" (The Last Dialogue). The verse mentions fingertips (*banān*), not prints, and none of the commentators read it as being about fingerprints; they read it as restoring the finger-bones or as the power to make the hand a hoof. The fingerprint reading is a modern reflection, and should be labelled as one.
- **Uniqueness.** Fingerprint uniqueness is a very strong practical rule; the sources read did not address whether it has been formally proven.
- **Two classical readings.** The page should not present only one: the "hoof" reading was the majority among the early commentators (al-Qurtubi), while al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir themselves preferred the "restoration" reading.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does "proportion his fingertips" mean?** Either that God can restore even the small finger-bones and make them even again (Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjaj; preferred by al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir), or that God could have made the hand a single hoof (Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, ʿIkrima, Qatada; "most commentators", according to al-Qurtubi).

**Does the verse talk about fingerprints?** No. It mentions fingertips, and no classical commentator connected it with prints. Fingerprints are a modern reflection that a reader may bring.

**When do fingerprints form?** Before birth, roughly between weeks 10 and 15 of pregnancy (Langenburg), as ridges spread in waves across the fingertip (Glover et al., 2023).

**Do identical twins have the same fingerprints?** No. They often share the general pattern type, but the detailed features differ (Langenburg).

**Why does the sura say man "desires to continue in sin" (75:5)?** Because doubt about the resurrection is often less about evidence than about not wanting to be answerable.

## S24 — A smoke-like origin; heavens and earth joined then split

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 41:9–12, 21:30, 79:27–30.
- al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi on 21:30 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/ibn-tabary-saadi-qortobi/sura21-aya30.html — the four readings of *ratq* and *fatq*, and al-Tabari's choice.
- al-Tabari on 41:11 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/tabary/sura41-aya11.html — Ibn ʿAbbas's reports on "Come, willingly or by compulsion".
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 41:9–12 and 21:30, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-11-sora-41.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-30-sora-21.html (as on the current page).
- Connelly et al., "The Absolute Chronology and Thermal Processing of Solids in the Solar Protoplanetary Disk", *Science* (2012), doi 10.1126/science.1226919, as reported by the University of Copenhagen (EurekAlert, 2 Nov. 2012) — the age of the first solids.
- Las Cumbres Observatory, SpaceBook, "The Early Universe" (read for run 17) — a hot, dense early universe that expanded and cooled; atoms formed at about 3,000 K.
- NASA, Planetary Fact Sheet (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov) — planet masses.

### In brief
"Then He directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke" (41:11) and "the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them" (21:30): two verses about the beginning, offered as reasons to believe.

### Verse note
"Then He directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke and said to it and to the earth, 'Come [into being], willingly or by compulsion.' They said, 'We have come willingly'" (41:11). Sura Fuṣṣilat is Meccan. Before it: "Say, 'Do you indeed disbelieve in He who created the earth in two days…'" (41:9) and the mountains and sustenance "in four days" (41:10); after it: "And He completed them as seven heavens within two days" (41:12).

"Have those who disbelieved not considered that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them and made from water every living thing? Then will they not believe?" (21:30), Meccan.

- *Dukhān*: smoke.
- *Ratq*: joined, closed, sewn together; *fatq*: split, opened.
- **The order.** 41:9–12 speaks of the earth and then the heaven; 79:27–30 speaks of the heaven and then "after that He spread the earth" (79:30). The commentators discussed this at length (below).

### Classical tafsir
**On 21:30 — four readings** (al-Tabari; al-Qurtubi):
1. **One mass, separated by air.** Ibn ʿAbbas, al-Hasan and Qatada: «كانتا جميعا، ففصل الله بينهما بهذا الهواء» ("they were together, and God separated them with this air"). al-Qurtubi adds ʿAtaʾ and al-Dahhak, and Kaʿb (by a wind).
2. **Each split into seven.** Mujahid, Abu Salih and al-Suddi: «فتقهنّ سبع سماوات، بعضهنّ فوق بعض» ("He split them into seven heavens, one above another"), and the earth likewise.
3. **Closed, then opened by rain and plants.** ʿIkrima, ʿAtiyya, Ibn Zayd and Ibn ʿAbbas: «كانتا رتقا لا يخرج منهما شيء، ففتق السماء بالمطر وفتق الأرض بالنبات» ("they were closed, nothing came out of them; He opened the sky with rain and the earth with plants").
4. **Night before day.** From Ibn ʿAbbas through ʿIkrima: night was created first, then the day split from it.

**al-Tabari's choice.** He prefers the third reading, because the verse goes on: "and made from water every living thing" — it is about rain and growth. al-Qurtubi confirms: «واختار هذا القول الطبري» ("al-Tabari chose this view").

**On 41:11.** Ibn Kathir: the smoke was "steam which arose from it when the earth was created"; al-Jalālayn: "smoke consisting of rising vapours". al-Tabari reports from Ibn ʿAbbas (through Mujahid) that God told the heavens to bring out their sun, moon and stars, and the earth its rivers and fruits; and (through Tawus) that "come" means "give": «أعطيا… أعطينا». The answer "We have come willingly" is creation's obedience.

**On the order of creation.** Ibn Kathir reports Ibn ʿAbbas's reconciliation of 41:9–12 with 79:27–30: "Allah created the earth in two days, then He created the heavens… Then He spread the earth."

**What they did not say.** No classical commentator read "smoke" as a cloud of gas and dust around a young star, or 21:30 as one dense point that exploded.

### How it works
These are modern findings set beside the verses; they are not their classical meaning.

- **A hot beginning.** [Established] The early universe was hot and dense, and expanded and cooled; when it had cooled to about 3,000 K, atoms could form and light could travel freely — the cosmic microwave background we still see (Las Cumbres Observatory, SpaceBook).
- **The solar system from a cloud.** [Established] The Sun and planets formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust. The oldest solids in meteorites date from 4.567 billion years ago, and the small rocky grains called chondrules formed "during the first three million years" (Connelly et al., *Science*, 2012).
- **Where the mass went.** [Established] Of the mass in the eight planets, the four giant planets hold about 99.6% (computed from NASA's Planetary Fact Sheet).

### Points of awe
- The first solid grains of the solar system formed 4.567 billion years ago, a date measured in meteorites (Connelly et al., 2012).
- The light of the early universe is still arriving, as a faint microwave glow from every direction (Las Cumbres Observatory).
- Jupiter and Saturn alone hold over 90% of the planets' mass (NASA Planetary Fact Sheet: 1,898 and 568 of 2,667 × 10²⁴ kg).

### The reflection
"Then He directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke and said to it and to the earth, 'Come [into being], willingly or by compulsion.' They said, 'We have come willingly'" (41:11).

What strikes a reader first may be the answer. Before anything else is said about heaven and earth, they are pictured as obedient. Ibn ʿAbbas, in al-Tabari, heard in the command an order to *give*: the heavens their sun, moon and stars, the earth its rivers and fruits. Creation says yes, and gives.

21:30 asks a question of those who did not believe: have they not considered that heaven and earth were closed, and were opened? Al-Tabari chose the reading closest to daily life: the sky that gave no rain and the earth that grew nothing, until God opened them — and the verse goes on, "and made from water every living thing". Others read it as one mass parted by the air, or as one heaven and one earth each split into seven.

A modern reader brings another picture: a hot beginning, a cloud of gas and dust, a solar system condensing out of it. A reader may notice the resonance with "smoke" and with "joined… then separated". But the verses do not ask us to admire their physics. They end: "Then will they not believe?"

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** "The Quran described the Big Bang." The classical readings of 21:30 are quite different — rain and plants (al-Tabari's choice), separation by air, or seven heavens from one — and the early universe was a hot plasma, not smoke. The modern reading is a reflection, not the verse's meaning (see also the Ask page on "scientific miracles").
- **"Smoke-like" cloud.** Calling a gas-and-dust cloud "smoke-like" is a reasonable modern reflection [Debated], but the classical commentators read the smoke as vapour.
- **The order of creation.** 41:9–12 and 79:27–30 seem to give different orders; Ibn ʿAbbas's reconciliation (earth created, then heavens, then the earth spread out) is reported by Ibn Kathir. It is an old discussion in tafsir, not something to hide.
- **"Just under 99%".** The current page's "just under 99% of all the mass orbiting the Sun" has no primary source; the NASA figures give 99.6% of the planets' mass.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does 21:30 describe the Big Bang?** The verse speaks of heaven and earth "joined" and "separated". Al-Tabari, the earliest great commentator, read it as a sky without rain and an earth without plants, opened by God; others read it as one mass separated by air. Reading it as the Big Bang is a modern reflection, not a classical meaning.

**What was the "smoke" in 41:11?** Ibn Kathir and al-Jalālayn read it as vapour or steam rising as the earth was made. No classical commentator connected it with a cloud of gas and dust.

**Was the earth created before the heavens?** 41:9–12 mentions the earth first; 79:27–30 says the earth was spread "after that". Ibn ʿAbbas, as reported by Ibn Kathir, said the earth was created, then the heavens, and then the earth was spread out.

**What does "willingly or by compulsion" mean?** God commanded heaven and earth to bring forth what He placed in them, and they answered "We have come willingly" (41:11). Ibn ʿAbbas read "come" as "give" (al-Tabari).

**How old is the solar system?** Its first solid grains formed 4.567 billion years ago, measured in meteorites (Connelly et al., 2012).

## S21 — Night and day; the sun as a lamp, the moon as a light

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 10:5–6, 25:61–62, 71:16, 78:13.
- al-Qurtubi on 10:5 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura10-aya5.html — *ḍiyāʾ* and *nūr*; Ibn ʿAbbas on why there are two lights.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) and *al-Jalālayn* on 10:5 and 78:13, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-5-sora-10.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-13-sora-78.html (as on the current page).
- NASA, "Sun Fact Sheet" (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov) — luminosity and central temperature.
- NASA, "Moon Fact Sheet" (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov) — albedo; revolution and synodic periods.
- D. O'Brien, "Derived Light and Eclipses in the Fifth Century", *Journal of Hellenic Studies* 88 (1968) 114–127 (read for run 17) — Greek thinkers who knew the moon's light comes from the sun.

### In brief
"It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light and determined for it phases - that you may know the number of years and account [of time]" (10:5): two lights, described differently, given to us as clocks.

### Verse note
"It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light and determined for it phases - that you may know the number of years and account [of time]. Allah has not created this except in truth. He details the signs for a people who know" (10:5), Meccan. The next verse: "Indeed, in the alternation of the night and the day… are signs for a people who fear Allah" (10:6).

"Blessed is He who has placed in the sky great stars and placed therein a [burning] lamp and luminous moon" (25:61), Meccan, followed by "And it is He who has made the night and the day in succession for whoever desires to remember or desires gratitude" (25:62).

"And made [therein] a burning lamp" (78:13), Meccan; compare "And made the moon therein a [reflected] light and made the sun a burning lamp" (71:16).

- *Ḍiyāʾ* (10:5, "shining light") and *nūr* ("derived light" in Saheeh's rendering; literally "light").
- *Sirāj* / *sirāj wahhāj*: "lamp", "burning lamp". *Qamaran munīran*: "luminous moon".
- *Manāzil*: "phases", literally stations.

### Classical tafsir
**al-Qurtubi on 10:5.** He defines the two words: «الضياء ما يضيء الأشياء، والنور ما يبين فيخفى» ("*ḍiyāʾ* is what lights things up; *nūr* is what makes visible and then fades"), and notes they share one root. He reports Ibn ʿAbbas: if God had made two suns, one for the day and one for the night, with no darkness, people could not know the number of years or the reckoning of months. On the moon's stations, in his comment on Sura Yā Sīn, he gives «ثمانية وعشرون منزلا» ("twenty-eight stations"), with two more nights when the moon is hidden.

**Ibn Kathir on 10:5.** "He made the rays that come forth from the bright sun as the source of light, and made the beams that come forth from the moon as light. He made them of two different natures so they would not be confused with one another." And: "He ordained phases for the moon".

**al-Jalālayn on 10:5.** God "made the sun a radiance that is emitting light and the moon a light"; the moon moves "in stations", "28 stations in 28 nights every month". **On 78:13:** Ibn Kathir: "the radiant sun that gives light to all of the world"; al-Jalālayn: "a radiant lamp? namely the sun".

**Where they differ, and what they did not say.** All notice that the sun and moon are described with different words. al-Qurtubi distinguishes them by strength and constancy; Ibn Kathir by "two different natures". Neither says, in the passages read, that the moon's light comes *from* the sun; Saheeh's English "derived" and "[reflected]" (71:16) are the translator's choice.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "shining light", "light" and "phases".

- **A lamp that makes its own light.** [Established] The Sun's luminosity is 382.8 × 10²⁴ joules per second, and its central temperature about 15.7 million K (NASA Sun Fact Sheet); it shines by nuclear fusion in its core.
- **A light that is returned.** [Established] The Moon makes no light; it reflects sunlight. Its geometric albedo is 0.12 and its Bond albedo 0.11 (NASA Moon Fact Sheet): it returns only about a tenth of the light that falls on it.
- **Known long before.** [Established] Greek thinkers knew this too: "several thinkers before Anaxagoras are said to have derived the moon's light from the sun", including Parmenides (O'Brien, *Journal of Hellenic Studies*, 1968).
- **Phases for counting time.** [Established] New moon to new moon is 29.53 days; one orbit against the stars, 27.32 days (NASA Moon Fact Sheet). Twelve lunar months make about 354 days, about eleven days shorter than the solar year, so the Islamic months move through the seasons.

### Points of awe
- The Sun gives out about 3.8 × 10²⁶ watts, continuously (NASA).
- The full Moon reflects only about a tenth of the sunlight that reaches it; its surface is dark rock (NASA).
- Because the lunar year is about eleven days shorter than the solar year, Ramadan passes through every season in about 33 years (worked out here from NASA's 29.53-day month).

### The reflection
"It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light" (10:5). Two lights, described with two different words. Al-Qurtubi heard the difference as one of strength: *ḍiyāʾ* lights things up; *nūr* shows them, gently, and fades. Ibn Kathir heard it as "two different natures so they would not be confused".

Then the verse gives the reason that touches every life: "that you may know the number of years and account [of time]". Ibn ʿAbbas, in al-Qurtubi, imagined the alternative: two suns and no night, and no way to count the months. The prayers follow the sun; the months, the fast and the pilgrimage follow the moon. Two clocks, set in the sky, free for everyone, everywhere.

"Allah has not created this except in truth." The order of the lights is meant to be read — "for whoever desires to remember or desires gratitude" (25:62).

Today we know the moon gives back only a tenth of the light that falls on it; Greek thinkers knew its light came from the sun long before. There is a picture here for a believer, offered as reflection, not as the verse's meaning: we too have no light of our own. Whatever light we give, we give back.

### Honest notes
- **A harmony, not a discovery.** The different words fit the science well, and the commentators already noticed the difference; Greek astronomers knew the moon's light was borrowed (O'Brien, 1968).
- **"Derived" is the translator's word.** *Nūr* means "light"; Saheeh renders it "derived light" (10:5) and "[reflected] light" (71:16). al-Qurtubi distinguishes *ḍiyāʾ* and *nūr* by strength, not by source. The page should not present "reflected" as the Arabic word's dictionary meaning.
- ***Nūr* elsewhere.** The same word is used of God, "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth" (24:35), and of revelation, so it cannot simply mean "reflected light".
- **The popular claim.** That 10:5 "revealed that the moon reflects light" is common; no named source for it was read.

### Questions readers ask next
**Why does the Quran use different words for the sun and the moon?** al-Qurtubi: *ḍiyāʾ* lights things up, while *nūr* shows them more softly; Ibn Kathir: God gave them "two different natures so they would not be confused". Both noticed the difference.

**Does "derived light" mean the Quran says the moon reflects sunlight?** "Derived" is Saheeh's translation of *nūr*, which means "light". The classical commentators read here did not draw that conclusion from 10:5. The moon does reflect sunlight — and Greek thinkers knew it centuries earlier.

**How long is a lunar month?** On average 29.53 days from new moon to new moon (NASA); Islamic months have 29 or 30 days.

**Why does Ramadan move through the seasons?** Twelve lunar months make about 354 days, about eleven days fewer than the solar year, so each year Ramadan comes earlier by about eleven days.

**What are the moon's "stations"?** The places where the moon is seen night by night against the stars; al-Qurtubi and al-Jalālayn count twenty-eight.

## S18 — Fire from the green tree

### Sources read
- Saheeh International (Tanzil): 36:78–81, 56:71–73.
- al-Qurtubi on 36:80 (Arabic), https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura36-aya80.html — the fire-sticks *markh* and *ʿafār*, and the argument from opposites.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged English) on 36:77–80 and 56:71–73, and *al-Jalālayn* on 56:71, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-78-sora-36.html and https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-71-sora-56.html (as on the current page).

### In brief
"[It is] He who made for you from the green tree, fire, and then from it you ignite" (36:80): fire from moist, living wood, offered as a sign that life can come from dry bones.

### Verse note
"And he presents for Us an example and forgets his [own] creation. He says, 'Who will give life to bones while they are disintegrated?' Say, 'He will give them life who produced them the first time; and He is, of all creation, Knowing.' [It is] He who made for you from the green tree, fire, and then from it you ignite" (36:78–80). Sura Yā Sīn is Meccan. Then: "Is not He who created the heavens and the earth Able to create the likes of them? Yes, [it is so]; and He is the Knowing Creator" (36:81).

"And have you seen the fire that you ignite? Is it you who produced its tree, or are We the producer? We have made it a reminder and provision for the travelers" (56:71–73), Meccan — the last of four questions in Sura al-Wāqiʿa (the sperm-drop, the crops, the water, the fire).

- *Al-shajar al-akhḍar*: "the green tree". *Tūqidūn*: "you ignite".

### Classical tafsir
**al-Qurtubi on 36:80.** The verse refers to «ما في المرخ والعفار» — the two Arabian trees *markh* and *ʿafār*, used as fire-sticks: the *ʿafār* is the lower stick, the *markh* the upper; two green twigs, "dripping water", are rubbed together until fire comes. He cites the Arab proverb «في كل شجر نار، واستمجد المرخ والعفار» ("in every tree there is fire, and *markh* and *ʿafār* have the most"). The argument: the denier objected that bones, cold and dry, cannot receive life; God answers that He brings fire out of what is cold, wet and green — «فهو القادر على إخراج الضد من الضد» ("He is able to bring an opposite out of its opposite").

**Ibn Kathir on 36:78–80.** The verses answer a denier of resurrection: Mujahid, ʿIkrima and others name Ubayy ibn Khalaf, who came "with a dry bone in his hand"; Ibn ʿAbbas names al-ʿAs ibn Waʾil, who "crumbled it in his hand". On 36:80: God is "the One Who initiated the creation of this tree from water, when it has become green and beautiful"; Qatada: "the One Who brought forth this fire from this tree is able to resurrect him."

**Ibn Kathir on 56:71–73.** "The Arabs had two kinds of trees called Al-Markh and Al-'Afar"; the fire is "a reminder" — "of the Hellfire, according to Mujahid and Qatadah" — and provision for *al-muqwīn*, "travelers" (Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Dahhak, al-Nadr ibn ʿArabi; the meaning al-Tabari chose). **al-Jalālayn on 56:71:** "the fire which you produce out of the oil of green trees".

**Where they differ.** al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir explain the "green tree" through the two fire-sticks; al-Jalālayn through "the oil of green trees". All share the argument: fire from what is green and wet is a sign that life can come from what is dry and dead.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "from the green tree, fire". These are modern facts set beside the verse.

- **Stored sunlight.** [Established] Plants use sunlight to build sugars and wood from carbon dioxide and water (photosynthesis). Burning wood reverses this: the stored carbon combines with oxygen and releases the energy as heat and light. (Standard biology and chemistry; no named source was read in this run.)
- **Friction fire.** [Established] Rubbing two pieces of wood together heats the fibres until they smoulder; this is the method al-Qurtubi describes. (No named source on the physics of friction fire was read.)

### Points of awe
- The Arabs made fire by rubbing two green branches, the *ʿafār* below and the *markh* above (al-Qurtubi).
- "In every tree there is fire" was an Arab proverb (al-Qurtubi).
- A flame from wood releases energy a living tree once captured from sunlight (standard science).

### The reflection
The verse begins with a man holding a dry bone. Ibn Kathir names him — Ubayy ibn Khalaf, or al-ʿAs ibn Waʾil — crumbling it in his hand and asking, "Who will give life to bones while they are disintegrated?" (36:78). The answer is quiet: the One who made them the first time.

Then comes a picture every Arab knew. Green wood is the opposite of fire: wet, cool, alive. Yet two green branches, rubbed together, brought out a flame. Al-Qurtubi names the logic: God "is able to bring an opposite out of its opposite". If fire can come from what is wet and green, why should life not come from what is dry and dead? Qatada drew the conclusion: the One who brought fire from the tree "is able to resurrect him".

Sura al-Wāqiʿa asks the same in another way: "And have you seen the fire that you ignite? Is it you who produced its tree, or are We the producer?" (56:71–72). Every cooking fire, every lamp, began with a tree we did not grow. And the same fire is "a reminder" — of the Fire to come, said Mujahid and Qatada — and "provision for the travelers".

Today we can add, as reflection, that the energy in that flame is sunlight caught by leaves and stored in wood. The verse does not teach photosynthesis. But the reflection deepens the sign: opposites come out of each other in this world all the time, by God's command.

### Honest notes
- **No photosynthesis claimed.** The verse refers to a known Arabian practice, fire-sticks of *markh* and *ʿafār* (al-Qurtubi; Ibn Kathir). Using photosynthesis as a reflection is fine; saying "the Quran revealed photosynthesis" is not.
- **Two readings of "green tree".** Fire-sticks (al-Qurtubi; Ibn Kathir) or "the oil of green trees" (al-Jalālayn); the page should give both.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about 36:80 was read.
- **Science sources.** The photosynthesis and friction points are standard but were not tied to a named source in this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What is the "green tree"?** al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir: the Arabian trees *markh* and *ʿafār*, whose green branches were rubbed together to make fire. al-Jalālayn: the oil of green trees.

**What is the argument?** The denier said dry bones cannot live again. God answers that He brings fire out of wet green wood: "He is able to bring an opposite out of its opposite" (al-Qurtubi). Qatada: the One who brought fire from the tree "is able to resurrect him".

**Who was the man with the bone?** Ibn Kathir reports that Mujahid, ʿIkrima and others named Ubayy ibn Khalaf, and Ibn ʿAbbas named al-ʿAs ibn Waʾil.

**Why is fire "a reminder" in 56:73?** Mujahid and Qatada said it reminds of the Fire of the Hereafter (Ibn Kathir). It is also "provision for the travelers".

**Does the verse describe photosynthesis?** No. It describes making fire from green wood. That the flame's energy came from sunlight is a modern reflection, not the verse's meaning.
