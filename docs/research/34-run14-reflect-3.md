## S17 — Clouds and winds

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via the site's verified Tanzil data (`src/data/quran.json`) — 24:43, 30:48, 15:22.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 24:43, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-43-sora-24.html, and on 15:22, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-22-sora-15.html.
- USGS Water Science School, "How much does a cloud weigh?", https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/how-much-does-a-cloud-weigh.

### In brief
24:43 asks us to watch a storm being built: God "drives clouds", brings them together, piles them into a mass, and rain comes "from within it".

### Verse note
"Do you not see that Allah drives clouds? Then He brings them together, then He makes them into a mass, and you see the rain emerge from within it. And He sends down from the sky, mountains [of clouds] within which is hail…" (24:43), Medinan, in Sura al-Nūr, in a passage on signs in the heavens and earth (24:41–45).

"It is Allah who sends the winds, and they stir the clouds and spread them in the sky however He wills, and He makes them fragments so you see the rain emerge from within them" (30:48), Meccan.

"And We have sent the fertilizing winds and sent down water from the sky and given you drink from it. And you are not its retainers" (15:22), Meccan.

- **Key words.** *Yuzjī* ("drives"): pushes gently along. *Yuʾallifu baynahu* ("brings them together"). *Rukām* ("a mass"): piled up. *Al-wadq* ("the rain"). *Lawāqiḥ* (15:22, "fertilizing"): from *laqqaḥa*, to fertilise or pollinate.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 24:43.** God "drives the clouds from the beginning, when they are formed and are still weak"; "He piles them up on top of one another"; the rain comes "from the gaps between them". On the "mountains" with hail, he gives two views: one "that there are mountains of hail in the sky from which Allah sends down ice", and another that "mountains" is a metaphor for the clouds. On the lightning: "the brightness of its lightning almost takes away their sight if the eyes follow it and try to look at it."

**Al-Jalālayn on 24:43.** God "piles them up some on top of others"; "The brilliance of its lightning its flashing would almost take away the eyes that look at it".

**Ibn Kathir on 15:22.** He gives two readings of "fertilizing". Ibn Masʿud: "The wind is sent bearing water from the sky, then it fertilizes the clouds until rain begins to generously fall". He then quotes ʿUbayd bin ʿUmayr al-Laythi: "Allah sends the fertilizing wind which pollinates the trees." His own gloss joins both: "fertilizing the clouds so that they give rain, and fertilizing the trees so that they open their leaves and blossoms."

**Al-Jalālayn on 15:22.** "And We send the winds as fertilisers to fertilise the clouds".

**Where they differ.** On 15:22, Ibn Kathir keeps both readings, clouds and trees; al-Jalālayn names only clouds. On 24:43, Ibn Kathir leaves open whether the "mountains" are literal mountains of hail or a picture of towering clouds.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "brings them together… a mass… rain… hail".
- **How heavy a cloud is.** [Established] USGS, citing one published estimate, gives the water in a cumulus cloud as "about 0.5 gram per cubic meter". A cloud of 1 km³ — "1 billion cubic meters" — therefore holds about "500,000 kilograms or 1.1 million pounds (about 551 tons)" (US short tons; about 500 metric tonnes).
- **Why it stays up.** [Established] "clouds float on air because the moist air in clouds is less dense than dry air" (USGS).
- **Piling up, rain and hail.** [Established] When clouds merge and grow upward into towering storm clouds, strong rising air carries droplets high enough to freeze; hailstones grow as they are carried up and down. (Standard meteorology; no named source on hail formation was read for this run — see Gaps.)
- **Wind and pollination.** [Established] Many plants, including grasses and cereals, are pollinated by wind. (No named source on wind pollination was read for this run.)

### Points of awe
- In USGS's worked example, a cumulus cloud of 1 km³ holds about 500 tonnes of water, and clouds float because "the moist air in clouds is less dense than dry air" (USGS).
- Ibn Kathir reads "fertilizing winds" both as winds that load the clouds with rain (with Ibn Masʿud) and as winds that make trees blossom (with ʿUbayd bin ʿUmayr).

### The reflection
"Do you not see that Allah drives clouds?" (24:43). The verse is a question. It asks us to look up and watch, as if for the first time, something we see every week. Thin clouds drift; they gather; they pile into a dark mass; and then rain comes "from within it".

Ibn Kathir notices how the verse begins: with clouds "still weak". A storm starts as almost nothing. Then come the lightning flashes that "almost take away" the sight of anyone who stares at them. The commentators did not need a weather satellite to feel the power in this.

15:22 adds a line that should stop us: "And you are not its retainers." We do not store the rain. We cannot keep it in the sky, or call it down when we want. In USGS's example, half a million kilograms of water float above us in one cloud, and none of it is ours to command.

The winds that carry it are called "fertilizing": they load the clouds and carry pollen to the trees. One movement of air feeds both sky and field. The verse turns weather into a lesson in dependence, and invites the response the Quran asks for elsewhere: gratitude for water we did not make.

### Honest notes
- **No meteorology claimed.** The verses describe what anyone can watch. They do not predict the stages of a thunderstorm cloud, and the commentators did not read them that way.
- **Estimates.** The 500-tonne figure is USGS's example for a 1 km³ cumulus at 0.5 g/m³; real clouds vary.
- **"Fertilizing winds."** Ibn Kathir already gives the pollination reading, so it is not a modern addition.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about these verses was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What are the "mountains" of hail?** Ibn Kathir gives two views: literal "mountains of hail in the sky", or a metaphor for towering clouds.

**What does "fertilizing winds" mean?** Winds that load the clouds with rain (Ibn Masʿud, via Ibn Kathir) and that make trees blossom (ʿUbayd bin ʿUmayr, via Ibn Kathir; Ibn Kathir's own gloss holds both).

**How can a cloud weigh 500 tonnes and still float?** USGS's answer: "the moist air in clouds is less dense than dry air." (Why the droplets stay up in more detail was not covered by the source read.)

**What does "you are not its retainers" mean?** That people do not store or control the rain; it is given.

## S18 — Fire from the green tree

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 36:78–80, 56:71–72.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), on 36:77–80, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-78-sora-36.html (as read for run 11), and on 56:71–73, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-71-sora-56.html; *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English) on 56:71, same page.

### In brief
"[It is] He who made for you from the green tree, fire" (36:80): the verse points to fire coming out of moist, living wood, as a sign that life can come out of dry bones.

### Verse note
"And he presents for Us an example and forgets his [own] creation. He says, 'Who will give life to bones while they are disintegrated?' Say, 'He will give them life who produced them the first time; and He is, of all creation, Knowing.' [It is] He who made for you from the green tree, fire, and then from it you ignite" (36:78–80), Meccan, near the end of Sura Yā Sīn.

"And have you seen the fire that you ignite? Is it you who produced its tree, or are We the producer?" (56:71–72), Meccan, the last of four questions in Sura al-Wāqiʿa: about the sperm-drop we emit (56:58), the crops (56:63), the water (56:68) and the fire (56:71) — the first about our own creation, the rest about what sustains us.

- **Key words.** *Al-shajar al-akhḍar*: "the green tree". *Tūqidūn*: "you ignite".

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 36:78–80.** The verses answer a denier of resurrection: Mujahid, ʿIkrima and others name Ubayy ibn Khalaf, who came "with a dry bone in his hand"; Ibn ʿAbbas names al-ʿAs ibn Waʾil, who "crumbled it in his hand" (as read for run 11). On 36:80 Ibn Kathir explains God as "the One Who initiated the creation of this tree from water, when it has become green and beautiful", and quotes Qatada: "the One Who brought forth this fire from this tree is able to resurrect him."

**Ibn Kathir on 56:71–73.** "The Arabs had two kinds of trees called Al-Markh and Al-'Afar ( that they used to ignite a fire )." The fire is also "a reminder" — "of the Hellfire, according to Mujahid and Qatadah" — and a provision for "Al-Muqwin"; he reports that Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Dahhak and al-Nadr bin ʿArabi took it to mean "travelers", and that Ibn Jarir (al-Tabari) chose that meaning.

**Al-Jalālayn on 56:71.** "Have you considered the fire that you kindle? the fire which you produce out of the oil of green trees?"

**Where they differ.** Ibn Kathir explains the "green tree" through the two Arabian fire-sticks, *markh* and *ʿafār*; al-Jalālayn through "the oil of green trees". Both see the same argument: fire from what is green and wet is a sign that life can come from what is dry and dead.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "from the green tree, fire".
- **Stored sunlight.** [Established] Plants use sunlight to build sugars and cellulose from carbon dioxide and water (photosynthesis). Burning wood reverses this: the stored carbon combines with oxygen and releases the energy as heat and light. (Standard biology and chemistry; no named source was read for this run.)
- **Friction fire.** [Established] Rubbing two pieces of wood together heats the fibres until they smoulder (The link to Ibn Kathir's *markh* and *ʿafār* is in Classical tafsir and The reflection.)

### Points of awe
- A flame from wood releases energy a living tree captured from sunlight (standard science).
- The Arabs of the Prophet's (pbuh) time made fire by rubbing green branches of two particular trees (Ibn Kathir).

### The reflection
A man brought a dry bone and crumbled it in his hand: "Who will give life to bones while they are disintegrated?" (36:78). The Quran answers with two arguments. The first: "He will give them life who produced them the first time." The second is in the next verse: "[It is] He who made for you from the green tree, fire".

Green wood is the opposite of fire. It is wet, cool and alive. Yet the Arabs knew that rubbing two green branches of *markh* and *ʿafār* could bring out a flame. If fire can come out of something wet and green, why should life not come out of something dry and dead? Qatada drew the conclusion plainly: the One who brought fire from the tree "is able to resurrect him".

Sura al-Wāqiʿa asks the same in a quieter way: "And have you seen the fire that you ignite? Is it you who produced its tree, or are We the producer?" (56:71–72). Every cooking fire, every lamp, began with a tree we did not grow.

Today we can add that the energy in that flame is sunlight, captured by leaves and stored in wood. The verse does not teach photosynthesis. But the reflection deepens the sign: opposites come out of each other in this world all the time, by God's command.

### Honest notes
- **No photosynthesis claimed.** The verse refers to a known Arabian practice, fire-sticks of *markh* and *ʿafār* (Ibn Kathir). Using photosynthesis as a reflection is fine; saying "the Quran revealed photosynthesis" is not.
- **The popular claim.** No named source making a scientific-miracle claim about 36:80 was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What are *markh* and *ʿafār*?** Two Arabian trees whose green branches were rubbed together to make fire (Ibn Kathir).

**Why does a verse about fire follow a question about bones?** Because both are about opposites: fire from wet wood, life from dry bones (Qatada, via Ibn Kathir).

**What does "a reminder" mean in 56:73?** A reminder of Hellfire, according to Mujahid and Qatada (Ibn Kathir).

**Is this verse about photosynthesis?** No. It is about fire from green wood; photosynthesis is a modern reflection that deepens it.

## S19 — Iron "sent down"

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 57:25.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 57:25, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-25-sora-57.html.
- "Iron-56", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron-56.
- "Why Does the Qur'an Say Iron Was 'Sent Down'? (57:25)", al-islaah.org (updated 20 July 2026), https://al-islaah.org/?p=1624 — a popular modern reading.

### In brief
57:25 places iron next to the messengers, the Book and the balance: "We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people".

### Verse note
"We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people, and so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen…" (57:25), Medinan, in Sura al-Ḥadīd ("Iron").

- **Key words.** *Anzalnā* ("We sent down"): the same verb is used for the Book in this very verse. *Baʾs shadīd*: "great military might". *Manāfiʿ*: "benefits".
- **The structure.** Messengers, Book, balance, iron: guidance, the standard of justice, and the power to defend it.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** The balance is "justice, according to Mujahid, Qatadah and others". Iron is "a deterrent for those who refuse the truth and oppose it after the proof has been established against them". Its benefits include "coins, hammers, axes, saws chisels, shovels and various tools". He does not comment on the word "sent down".

**Al-Jalālayn.** On "We sent down iron": "We caused it to be extracted from mineral ores."

**Where they differ.** Al-Jalālayn explains "sent down" as God's making iron available from ores; Ibn Kathir passes over the word and focuses on iron's role beside justice. Neither says iron came from the sky.

### How it works
- **Where iron is made.** [Established] Iron-56, "comprising about 91.754% of the iron on Earth", forms in massive stars: "Among the heavier elements formed is 56Ni, which subsequently decays to 56Co and then 56Fe" (*Wikipedia*). Because iron's nucleus is so tightly bound, "further nuclear reactions become energetically unfavorable" — fusion beyond iron absorbs energy rather than releasing it.
- **Not only iron.** [Established] Every element heavier than hydrogen and helium, including the oxygen in water and the carbon in our bodies, was made in stars. (Standard astrophysics; no named source was read for this run.)
- **Iron in us.** [Established] Iron is at the centre of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in our blood. (No named source was read for this run.)

### Points of awe
- Most of Earth's iron is iron-56, made in massive stars from nickel-56 decaying through cobalt (*Wikipedia*).
- Iron is the point where fusion in a star stops releasing energy (*Wikipedia*).

### The reflection
The verse sets iron in surprising company. First come the messengers; then the Book; then "the balance", which Ibn Kathir explains as justice. And then: "We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people" (57:25).

Iron is power. It makes ploughs and swords, nails and chains. The verse does not pretend that power is harmless; it calls it "great military might". But it places that power after the balance. Strength is meant to serve justice, not to replace it. Ibn Kathir calls iron "a deterrent" against those who oppose the truth after it has been made clear — and he also lists hammers, axes and saws: tools for ordinary work.

The word "sent down" is the same word the verse uses for the Book. Al-Jalālayn says God "caused it to be extracted from mineral ores". Whatever the physical path, the verse treats iron as a gift, given with a purpose and a test: "so that Allah may make evident those who support Him and His messengers unseen".

The question the verse leaves with us is about our own strength, whatever form it takes: is it serving the balance, or have we put it in the balance's place?

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A popular article notes that "much of the iron found on Earth was not originally formed here" and that the wording "has prompted many readers to reflect on the correspondence" with modern astronomy, while saying the Quran "is not presenting a scientific explanation" (al-islaah.org, 2026). Stronger versions elsewhere say the Quran "revealed that iron came from space"; no named source for that stronger claim was read for this run.
- **Weighing it.** *Anzalnā* is used in the same verse for the Book; al-Jalālayn reads it as extraction from ores; Ibn Kathir does not read it as "from the sky". And iron is not special in having a cosmic origin: so do oxygen and carbon. Stellar nucleosynthesis enriches the reflection; it is not a fulfilled prediction.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does "sent down" mean iron fell from the sky?** Not in the commentaries read. Al-Jalālayn: God "caused it to be extracted from mineral ores". The same verb is used for the Book.

**Why is iron mentioned next to justice?** Because, as Ibn Kathir reads it, iron is power to defend justice — "a deterrent" — as well as the material of everyday tools.

**Is it true that Earth's iron was made in stars?** Yes: iron-56 forms in massive stars (*Wikipedia*). So do most other elements heavier than helium.

## S20 — The ant

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 27:18–19.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 27:18 (the page's al-Jalālayn covers 27:18 only), https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-18-sora-27.html.
- Arabic Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari on 27:18, via quranislam.github.io, https://quranislam.github.io/tafsir/sura27-aya18.html.
- "Alarm Communication", AntWiki (excerpting Hölldobler & Wilson, *The Ants*), https://antwiki.org/wiki/Alarm_Communication.
- Sasaki, Hölldobler, Millar & Pratt, "A context-dependent alarm signal in the ant *Temnothorax rugatulus*", *Journal of Experimental Biology* 217:3229–3236 (2014) — citation only (the abstract was not on the page read), https://pratt.lab.asu.edu/node/68.

### In brief
27:18–19 tells of an ant who warned her community — "enter your dwellings that you not be crushed" — and a prophet who heard her, smiled, and turned to gratitude.

### Verse note
"Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said, 'O ants, enter your dwellings that you not be crushed by Solomon and his soldiers while they perceive not.' So [Solomon] smiled, amused at her speech, and said, 'My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me by Your mercy into [the ranks of] Your righteous servants'" (27:18–19), Meccan, in Sura al-Naml ("The Ants"), after Solomon (pbuh) says he was taught the speech of birds (27:16, paraphrased; not quoted from verified data).

- **Key words.** *Namla*: an ant (grammatically feminine). *Wa-hum lā yashʿurūn*: "while they perceive not".

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "Sulayman, peace be upon him, understood what the ant said." His section then prints the verse in its translator's wording — "smiled, amused at her speech"; "My Lord! Grant me the power and ability that I may be grateful for Your favors" — which is the verse, not his comment. In the Arabic, he cites, through Ibn ʿAsakir and al-Hasan, that the ant's name was "حرس" (Ḥaras), and that she was from a tribe called Banū al-Shayṣān (quranislam.github.io). These details rest on reports whose grades were not given.

**Al-Jalālayn.** The valley was "located either in Tā'if or in Syria and whose ants are either small or large"; the speaker was "the queen ant who had seen Solomon's hosts".

**Al-Tabari.** On "while they perceive not": "وهم لا يعلمون أنهم يحطمونكم" — "they do not know that they are crushing you" (Arabic text, quranislam.github.io).

**Is the talking ant meant literally?** Ibn Kathir treats the ant's words as real speech that God let Solomon (pbuh) understand: "Allah enabled Sulayman to understand what the birds said", among "the blessings that Allah bestowed upon him". Al-Jalālayn's section (on 27:18 only) does not discuss Solomon's hearing, but adds a grammar note: "the ants are likened to rational beings in their use of the latter's speech." Al-Tabari's Arabic section explains the words and does not address whether the speech was real. None of the three reads it as allegory. Al-Qurtubi and al-Razi, whom readers asked about, were not available for this run (see Gaps).

**What they did not say.** None describes how ants communicate.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "enter your dwellings" and "while they perceive not".
- **Alarm by chemistry.** [Established] Ant alarm signals "typically consist of two or more pheromones", which often alert, attract and provoke aggression at once. AntWiki's list of "alarm-defense substances" includes citronellal, dendrolasin, dimethyl sulfate and undecane; formic acid, used by some formicine ants, it calls "probably purely defensive" (AntWiki, from Hölldobler & Wilson).
- **How far a warning reaches.** [Established] In the ant *Lasius claviger*, the alarm comes from the Dufour's gland (discharged with the mandibular gland). For the full contents (about 2.5 µg of undecane), "the diffusion model of Bossert and Wilson (1963) predicts" a signal reaching "a maximum of about 20 cm in still air" — a model prediction, not a measurement. In behaviour, the system "seems designed to bring workers to the aid of a distressed nestmate over distances of up to 10 cm"; ants a few millimetres away react within seconds, those a few centimetres away take a minute or longer. Unless reinforced, the signal "dies out within a few minutes" (AntWiki).
- **Two kinds of alarm.** [Established] "In aggressive alarm some of the colony members… are drawn toward the threatening stimulus"; "In panic alarm the colony as a whole flees from the stimulus" (AntWiki).
- **Sound too.** [Established] Some ants add stridulatory chirps, which "enhance attraction" (AntWiki).
- **Context matters.** Sasaki, Hölldobler, Millar and Pratt (2014) studied "a context-dependent alarm signal" in *Temnothorax rugatulus* (title; the findings were not read for this run).

### Points of awe
- A single ant's alarm is built to reach nestmates up to about 10 cm away (a diffusion model predicts at most about 20 cm), and fades within minutes unless others reinforce it (AntWiki).
- Ant colonies have two alarm responses: attack, or the whole colony fleeing (AntWiki).

### The reflection
The verse gives an ant a voice. She sees an army coming and calls out: "O ants, enter your dwellings that you not be crushed by Solomon and his soldiers while they perceive not" (27:18). She warns her whole community, and in the same breath she excuses the army: they would not mean it, they just would not notice. Al-Tabari explains: "they do not know that they are crushing you."

There is a lesson in that alone. She does not curse the powerful; she protects the weak and assumes no malice.

Then the prophet hears her. Solomon (pbuh) had armies of men, jinn and birds (27:17, paraphrased). He could have ignored an ant. Instead he "smiled, amused at her speech", and turned at once to gratitude: "My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents" (27:19). Power, in this story, makes him listen more carefully, not less.

Real ants do raise the alarm, with chemicals and sometimes with sound, and a whole colony can flee into its nest. The verse does not describe pheromones. It describes a small creature's care for its people, and a great man's humility before it.

### Honest notes
- **A gift to a prophet, not entomology.** The verse tells of Solomon (pbuh) understanding an ant. It does not describe pheromones.
- **The feminine verb.** Some articles say the feminine form proves "the Quran knew worker ants are female". *Namla* is grammatically feminine in any case, so the grammar does not settle biology (the current page's note; no named source for the claim was read for this run).
- **The skeptic's question.** Readers ask whether the story of Solomon and the ant appears in later Jewish tradition, suggesting borrowing. No source on Jewish versions of the story was read for this run (see Gaps), so this page makes no claim either way.
- **The ant's name.** "Ḥaras" comes from a report Ibn Kathir cites from Ibn ʿAsakir, by way of Ishaq ibn Bishr, from Saʿid, from Qatada, from al-Hasan; the same report says she was lame and "as big as a wolf" (بقدر الذيب). Ibn Kathir adds no judgement; Ishaq ibn Bishr's standing as a narrator was not checked. It should not be presented as fact.

### Questions readers ask next
**Did the ant really speak?** The commentators read here (Ibn Kathir, al-Jalālayn, al-Tabari) treat it as real speech God let Solomon (pbuh) understand.

**How do real ants warn each other?** Mainly with alarm pheromones, sometimes with sound; the colony may attack or flee (AntWiki).

**What does "while they perceive not" mean?** That the army would crush them without knowing it (al-Tabari).

**Why did Solomon (pbuh) smile and pray?** He was "amused at her speech", and turned the moment into gratitude for God's favour (27:19; Ibn Kathir).

**Is the story in Jewish tradition?** Not checked for this run.

## S21 — Night and day; the sun as a lamp, the moon as a light

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 10:5, 25:61, 78:13.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 10:5, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-5-sora-10.html, and on 78:12–13, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-13-sora-78.html.
- NASA NSSDC, "Moon Fact Sheet", https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html.

### In brief
10:5 says God "made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light and determined for it phases - that you may know the number of years and account [of time]": two lights, two jobs.

### Verse note
"It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light and determined for it phases - that you may know the number of years and account [of time]. Allah has not created this except in truth…" (10:5), Meccan.

"Blessed is He who has placed in the sky great stars and placed therein a [burning] lamp and luminous moon" (25:61), Meccan.

"And made [therein] a burning lamp" (78:13), Meccan, in a list of signs in Sura al-Nabaʾ.

- **Key words.** *Ḍiyāʾ* ("shining light", 10:5) and *nūr* ("derived light"). *Sirāj wahhāj* (78:13): "a burning lamp". *Qamaran munīran* (25:61): "luminous moon". *Manāzil* ("phases", literally stations).

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 10:5.** "He made the rays that come forth from the bright sun as the source of light, and made the beams that come forth from the moon as light. He made them of two different natures so they would not be confused with one another." And: "He ordained phases for the moon".

**Al-Jalālayn on 10:5.** God "made the sun a radiance that is emitting light and the moon a light"; the moon is "determined it with respect to its movement in stations", "28 stations in 28 nights every month".

**Ibn Kathir on 78:13.** "the radiant sun that gives light to all of the world." **Al-Jalālayn:** "and set a radiant lamp? namely the sun".

**What they share.** Both notice that the sun and moon are described differently — "two different natures" (Ibn Kathir) — and both tie the moon to counting time through its "stations". The current page also cites al-Razi and al-Zamakhshari on *ḍiyāʾ* and *nūr*; they were not read for this run (see Gaps).

### How it works
Each point is tied to "shining light", "derived light" and "phases".
- **A lamp that makes its own light.** [Established] The Sun is a star; it produces its own light by nuclear fusion in its core. (No Sun fact sheet was read for this run; see Gaps.)
- **A light that reflects.** [Established] The Moon makes no light; it reflects sunlight. Its geometric albedo is 0.12 and its Bond albedo 0.11 (NASA NSSDC): it returns only about a tenth of the light that reaches it. Its surface is dark; it looks bright because it is in full sunlight against a black sky.
- **Phases for counting time.** [Established] The Moon's synodic period — new moon to new moon — is 29.53 days; its sidereal period (one orbit against the stars) is 27.3217 days (NASA NSSDC). Twelve synodic months make about 354 days, about eleven days shorter than the solar year, so the Islamic months move through the seasons.

### Points of awe
- The full Moon reflects only about 11–12% of the sunlight that reaches it (NASA NSSDC).
- The Moon's cycle of phases averages 29.53 days — the average length of an Islamic month, whose months run 29 or 30 days (NASA NSSDC).
- Because the lunar year is shorter than the solar year, a Muslim who lives long enough fasts Ramadan in every season.

### The reflection
"It is He who made the sun a shining light and the moon a derived light" (10:5). Two lights hang in the same sky, and the verse names them differently. Ibn Kathir saw it: God "made them of two different natures so they would not be confused with one another". The sun burns; the moon glows.

Then the verse gives the reason that touches every life: "that you may know the number of years and account [of time]". The moon's "stations", al-Jalālayn says, move through twenty-eight nights each month. The prayers follow the sun; the months, the fast and the pilgrimage follow the moon. Two clocks, set in the sky for everyone, free, everywhere.

The verse ends: "Allah has not created this except in truth". The order of the lights is not an accident; it is meant to be read.

Today we know the moon gives back only a tenth of the light that falls on it. Its surface is dark rock. It shines only because it faces the sun. There is a picture there for a believer, offered as reflection, not as the verse's meaning: we too have no light of our own. Whatever light we give, we give back.

### Honest notes
- **A harmony, not a discovery.** The different words fit the science well, and the commentators already noticed the difference. But *nūr* is also used of God (24:35) and of revelation, so it does not strictly mean "reflected light". The current page notes that many ancient writers also knew the Moon is lit by the Sun; no source for that was read for this run.
- **The popular claim.** That 10:5 "revealed that the moon reflects light" is common; no named source for it was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What is the difference between *ḍiyāʾ* and *nūr*?** Ibn Kathir: the sun's rays are "the source of light", the moon's beams "light", of "two different natures". Al-Jalālayn: the sun is "a radiance that is emitting light", the moon "a light".

**Does the moon make its own light?** No. It reflects about 11–12% of the sunlight that reaches it (NASA NSSDC).

**Why are Islamic months 29 or 30 days?** Because the Moon's cycle of phases is 29.53 days (NASA NSSDC).

**What are the moon's "stations"?** The 28 positions through which it passes in a month (al-Jalālayn).

## S22 — Fingertips and resurrection

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 75:3–4.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English) and *Tanwīr al-Miqbās* (attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas), on 75:3–4, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-4-sora-75.html.
- Glenn Langenburg (Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension), answer to "Are one's fingerprints similar to those of his or her parents in any discernable way?", *Scientific American* (no date shown), https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/are-ones-fingerprints-sim/.
- "Quran Miracle: Identity In Fingertips" (page title; its heading reads "Quran Reveals About Uniqueness of Fingertips"), The Last Dialogue (26 September 2019; updated 3 October 2026), https://thelastdialogue.org/article/quran-miracle-identity-in-fingertips — the popular modern claim.

### In brief
75:3–4 answers doubt about the resurrection: "Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips."

### Verse note
"Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:3–4), Meccan, at the start of Sura al-Qiyāma ("The Resurrection"), after oaths by the Day of Resurrection and the self-reproaching soul (75:1–2).

- **Key words.** *Banān*: fingertips (the ends of the fingers). *Nusawwiya*: "proportion", shape, make even.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** On 75:3: "on the Day of Judgement does he think that We are not able to return his bones and gather them from their various places?" On 75:4: "We could make his Banan, which are the tips of his fingers, all equal ( in length )."

**Tanwīr al-Miqbās** (attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas): "We are able to gather his fingers, such that his palm becomes like the hoof of a camel or beasts."

**Al-Jalālayn.** "We are able in addition to assembling them to reshape even his fingers".

**What they share.** All three read the verse as God's power to rebuild. Al-Jalālayn: God can reshape even the fingers. Ibn Kathir: "This means Our power is suitable to gather ( and recreate ) them, and if We wished We could surely resurrect him with more than what he originally had"; the fingers made "all equal" is his illustration of that power. *Tanwīr al-Miqbās* uses the hoof image the same way: if God can do that to the hand, He can restore the bones. None of them speaks of fine work, or of fingerprints.

### How it works
Each point is tied to *banān*, "fingertips".
- **When fingerprints form.** [Established] Before birth, small pads (volar pads) form on the fingertips, stop growing around the 10th week and then shrink back; the events that set the ridges "collide during weeks 10 through 15". A high pad yields a whorl, an intermediate pad a loop, a low pad an arch (Langenburg, *Scientific American*).
- **Inherited, but not copied.** [Established] The pattern type reflects inherited timing, but the exact ridge arrangement is "random and not genetically linked". Identical twins often share pattern types, but "The identifying characteristics are different, however" (Langenburg).

### Points of awe
- Fingertip ridge patterns are set before birth, in weeks 10–15 of pregnancy (Langenburg).
- Identical twins share DNA but not fingerprint details (Langenburg).

### The reflection
The doubter's question is old: when bones have scattered, who can put them back? The verse answers with the smallest part of the hand: "Yes. [We are] Able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:4). If God can shape the tips of the fingers, the rest is not hard.

Ibn Kathir and the commentary attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas press the point with an image: God could have made the fingers "all equal", or joined like a hoof. For them this shows the power that can rebuild. A reader today may add a thought of their own, labelled as such: fingers that are not all equal are what let us write, sew, plant and pray with open hands — an everyday gift we rarely notice.

Today we can add a third layer, as reflection: the skin of the fingertips carries a pattern set in the womb, between the tenth and fifteenth weeks, that no one else shares — not even an identical twin. The verse does not speak of prints. But a reader who looks at their own fingertip can feel the force of the question: the One who made this, so finely, can He not make it again?

The sura's name is "The Resurrection". Its first argument is placed in our hands.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** A widely shared article says: "Look what Quran says almost 1400 years ago in Surah Qiyamah verses 3-4", concluding that "people's identities are coded at their fingertips", and that "It is only in the late 19th century that fingerprinting became the scientific method of identification" (The Last Dialogue).
- **Weighing it.** The verse mentions fingertips (*banān*), not prints, and none of the three commentaries read here reads it as being about fingerprints; they read it as power to rebuild. The fingerprint reading is a modern reflection, and should be labelled as one.
- **Uniqueness.** Fingerprint uniqueness is a very strong practical rule; the source read did not address whether it has been formally proven.

### Questions readers ask next
**Did the Quran reveal fingerprints?** No. The verse speaks of fingertips; the classical readings are about God's power to rebuild the body, down to the fingers. Fingerprints are a modern reflection.

**What did classical scholars think the verse meant?** That God can rebuild even the fingers (al-Jalālayn, Ibn Kathir, *Tanwīr al-Miqbās*); Ibn Kathir and *Tanwīr* illustrate the power with fingers made "all equal" or hoof-like.

**When do fingerprints form?** Between about weeks 10 and 15 of pregnancy (Langenburg).

**Do identical twins have the same fingerprints?** No; they may share pattern types, but not identifying details (Langenburg).

## S23 — Expansion of the heavens

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 51:47.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), on 51:47, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-47-sora-51.html (as read for run 9).
- Caroline Harper, "Hubble tension explained", *BBC Sky at Night Magazine* (26 July 2024), https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/hubble-tension.

### In brief
"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander" (51:47): the verse speaks of a sky built with power and made wide.

### Verse note
"And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expander" (51:47), Meccan, followed by the spreading of the earth (51:48) and the creation of pairs (51:49).

- **Key words.** *Bi-ayd*: "with strength", power (not "hands"). *Mūsiʿūn*: "expander" — from *wasiʿa*, to be wide; it can mean "We make wide", "We are making wide", or "We have vast power/capacity".

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "With strength" is explained by Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatada and al-Thawri; *la-mūsiʿūn* means: "We made it vast and We brought its roof higher without pillars to support it" (as read for run 9).

**Other readings.** The current page reports, from al-Tabari, readings of *mūsiʿūn* as "We are able, with vast capacity" and "We widen provision for creatures". Al-Tabari was not read for this run (see Gaps).

**What they did not say.** In Ibn Kathir's reading, the sky was *made* vast; he does not describe it as growing over time.

### How it works
Each point is tied to *mūsiʿūn*.
- **The universe is expanding.** [Established] In 1929 Edwin Hubble presented the first observational evidence that the universe is expanding in all directions (Harper, *Sky at Night*). (The current page also credits Georges Lemaître in 1927; no source on Lemaître was read for this run.)
- **How fast.** [Established, with an open problem] The expansion rate, the Hubble constant, comes out at 67–68 km/s per megaparsec from the early universe (the Planck mission's measurements of the cosmic microwave background) but 73–74 from the nearby universe (Hubble Space Telescope and Gaia). This disagreement is the "Hubble tension" (Harper). A megaparsec is almost 3.3 million light-years.

### Points of awe
- Hubble found that "the Universe is expanding in all directions" (Harper).
- Two careful ways of measuring the expansion disagree by about 8–10% (computed here from Harper's ranges, 67–68 and 73–74), and "We can't explain this with our best theoretical models" (Harper).

### The reflection
"And the heaven We constructed with strength" (51:47). The verse begins with power. The sky, so vast and high, was built — and built with strength. Then: "and indeed, We are [its] expander".

Ibn Kathir read this as God having made the sky wide and raised it "without pillars to support it". For him, the point was the size and order of what God had made, and His power over it.

Modern astronomy has found something the first listeners could not see: the universe is not only vast, it is growing. The distances between galaxies are increasing, and we are still arguing about exactly how fast. The verse's word *mūsiʿūn* can be read in harmony with that, and a reader may notice it.

But the verse's own direction is clear: it points to the One who built, and who widens. The verses that follow move from the sky to the earth spread out, and then to the pairs in all things, "perhaps you will remember" (51:49). The sky is a sign that should send us back to God, not only forward to a measurement.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** "The Quran predicted the expanding universe" overstates. The word allows an "expanding" reading, but Ibn Kathir read it as having made the sky vast (see also "Are 'scientific miracles' in the Quran real?" in run 9).
- **Harmony, not prediction.** The modern reading is allowed by the word; it stands beside the classical readings, and is not the verse's only meaning.
- **Numbers are unsettled.** Cosmology is still debating the exact expansion rate, so "the Quran matches the number" claims cannot hold.

### Questions readers ask next
**Does 51:47 say the universe is expanding?** The word can be read that way, but Ibn Kathir read it as God having made the sky vast.

**What is the Hubble tension?** The disagreement between the expansion rate measured from the early universe (about 67–68 km/s/Mpc) and from nearby stars (about 73–74) (Harper).

**What does *bi-ayd* mean?** "With strength", according to Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatada and al-Thawri (Ibn Kathir) — not "hands".

## S24 — A smoke-like origin; heavens and earth joined then split

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 41:11, 21:30.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), and *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English), on 41:9–12, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-11-sora-41.html, and on 21:30, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-30-sora-21.html (as read for runs 9 and 10).
- "Formation and evolution of the Solar System", *Wikipedia*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_and_evolution_of_the_Solar_System.

### In brief
41:11 says God "directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke", and 21:30 that "the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them".

### Verse note
"Then He directed Himself to the heaven while it was smoke and said to it and to the earth, 'Come [into being], willingly or by compulsion.' They said, 'We have come willingly'" (41:11), Meccan, in Sura Fuṣṣilat, after the creation of the earth in 41:9–10.

"Have those who disbelieved not considered that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them and made from water every living thing? Then will they not believe?" (21:30), Meccan.

- **Key words.** *Dukhān*: smoke. *Ratq*: joined, sewn together. *Fatq*: split apart.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir on 41:11.** The smoke was "steam which arose from it when the earth was created". On the order of creation: "Allah says that He created the earth first, because it is the foundation." Reconciling this with 79:27–30 (where the earth's spreading comes after the heaven), Ibn Kathir himself says "the earth itself was created before the heavens according to some texts", and then reports Ibn ʿAbbas: "Allah created the earth in two days, then He created the heavens… Then He spread the earth."

**Al-Jalālayn on 41:11.** "Then He turned to the heaven when it was smoke consisting of rising vapours"; heaven and earth answer, "We come together with all those inhabiting us willingly!"

**Ibn Kathir on 21:30.** He reports Ibn ʿAbbas: "the heavens were joined together and it did not rain, and the earth was joined together and nothing grew", until rain and vegetation came forth; al-Hasan and Qatada: "They were joined together, then they were separated by this air"; and his own gloss: "in the beginning they were all one piece… piled up on top of one another" (as read for run 9).

**Where they differ.** On 21:30 there are three classical readings: rain and plants (Ibn ʿAbbas), separation by the air (al-Hasan, Qatada), and one original piece (Ibn Kathir's gloss). On 41:11, both commentaries read the "smoke" as vapour.

### How it works
- **Where our solar system came from.** [Established] The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from the "gravitational collapse of a small part of a giant molecular cloud", mostly hydrogen and helium (about 98% of its mass), with about 2% heavier elements; the rest of the collapsing material "flattened into a protoplanetary disk", from which the planets formed (*Wikipedia*). The solar nebula was "the disc-shaped cloud of gas and dust left over from the Sun's formation".
- **The early universe.** [Established] The early universe was a hot, dense plasma that expanded and cooled (the current page; no named source on early-universe cosmology was read for this run).

### Points of awe
- Our Sun and planets formed from a cloud of gas and dust about 4.6 billion years ago (*Wikipedia*).
- The four giant planets hold "just under 99% of all the mass orbiting the Sun" (*Wikipedia*).

### The reflection
Two verses describe a beginning in very few words. In one, the heaven "was smoke", and God said to it and to the earth, "Come [into being], willingly or by compulsion." They answered: "We have come willingly" (41:11). In the other, heaven and earth "were a joined entity, and We separated them" (21:30).

The commentators read these in more than one way. The smoke was vapour rising as the earth was made. The joining was a sky that did not rain and an earth that did not grow, until God opened both; or heaven and earth pressed together until the air came between them; or one piece, later divided.

What strikes a reader first may be the answer of heaven and earth: "We have come willingly." Before anything else is said, creation is pictured as obedient. Al-Jalālayn adds that they answered "with all those inhabiting us". The whole cosmos says yes to its Maker.

Modern science describes our solar system forming from a vast cloud of gas and dust. A reader may notice a resonance with "smoke". But the verse's own question is not about physics. It ends: "Then will they not believe?" The beginning of everything is offered as a reason to trust the One who began it.

### Honest notes
- **The popular claim.** "The Quran described the Big Bang" overstates. The classical readings of 21:30 are quite different (rain and plants; separation by air), and the early universe was a hot plasma, not smoke (see run 9, Q12).
- **A modern reflection.** Calling a gas-and-dust cloud "smoke-like" is a reasonable modern reflection [Debated], but it is a reflection, and is labelled as one.
- **The order of creation.** 41:9–12 gives a sequence (earth, then heaven) that commentators discussed at length alongside 79:27–30; Ibn Kathir reports Ibn ʿAbbas's reconciliation. It is an old discussion in tafsir, not something to hide.

### Questions readers ask next
**What was the "smoke"?** Ibn Kathir: "steam which arose from it when the earth was created"; al-Jalālayn: "rising vapours".

**Does 21:30 describe the Big Bang?** The classical readings do not; it can be read in harmony with modern cosmology, but that is a modern reflection (run 9, Q12).

**Which came first, earth or heaven?** Ibn Kathir reports Ibn ʿAbbas: the earth was created first, and spread out after the heavens.

## S25 — The protected roof

### Sources read
- Saheeh International, via `src/data/quran.json` — 21:32, 2:22.
- Ibn Kathir, *Tafsir* (abridged English), *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (English) and *Tanwīr al-Miqbās*, on 21:32, https://surahquran.com/tafsir-english-aya-32-sora-21.html.
- Fraser Cain, "How Does the Earth Protect Us From Space?", *Universe Today* (13 February 2009), https://universetoday.com/25370/how-does-the-earth-protect-us-from-space.

### In brief
"And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32): the verse calls the sky a roof that is kept safe, and complains that people do not look.

### Verse note
"And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32), Meccan, in a run of signs after 21:30–31.

"[He] who made for you the earth a bed [spread out] and the sky a ceiling and sent down from the sky, rain and brought forth thereby fruits as provision for you. So do not attribute to Allah equals while you know [that there is nothing similar to Him]" (2:22), Medinan, among the first commands of Sura al-Baqara.

- **Key words.** *Saqf*: ceiling, roof. *Maḥfūẓ*: "protected", guarded, preserved.

### Classical tafsir
**Ibn Kathir.** "Protected" means "high and protected from anything reaching it"; Mujahid: "Raised up." On the end of the verse: "Yet they turn away from its signs."

**Al-Jalālayn.** "a roof for the earth functioning like the roof of a house preserved from collapsing".

**Tanwīr al-Miqbās.** It reads the protection as protection from devils (as reported on the surahquran page; the wording was not quoted).

**Where they differ.** Three meanings of "protected": out of reach (Ibn Kathir), kept from falling (al-Jalālayn), and guarded from devils (*Tanwīr al-Miqbās*). None mentions radiation or the atmosphere's layers.

### How it works
Each point is tied to "protected ceiling".
- **The ozone layer.** [Established] "the ozone layer is a special region of the atmosphere that absorbs much of this radiation" — the Sun's ultraviolet light (Cain, *Universe Today*).
- **The magnetosphere.** [Established] Earth's magnetic field protects us from solar plasma, "keeping it safely away from the surface of the planet" (Cain).
- **Meteors.** [Established] Most small meteoroids burn up high in the atmosphere as "shooting stars" (the current page; no named source with figures was read for this run).

### Points of awe
- The ozone layer absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet light (Cain).
- Earth's magnetic field keeps solar plasma away from the surface (Cain).

### The reflection
A roof is something you only think about when it leaks. The verse asks us to think about the sky that way: "And We made the sky a protected ceiling, but they, from its signs, are turning away" (21:32).

The commentators each saw a different protection. Ibn Kathir: high, beyond anything's reach. Al-Jalālayn: a roof "preserved from collapsing", like the roof of a house. Another early reading: guarded from devils who would listen in. In every reading, the sky is kept for us.

The complaint at the end is the real point. The roof is there, every day, and people "are turning away" from its signs. Ibn Kathir: they do not think about how it was made, "so vast and high".

Today we can add, as reflection, protections the first listeners could not see: a thin layer of ozone that absorbs much of the Sun's ultraviolet light, and a magnetic field that holds solar plasma away from the ground. They are invisible until we learn about them — and then, like a good roof, they are easy to forget again. The verse's call is simply to look up, and not to turn away.

### Honest notes
- **No ozone in the verse.** The verse speaks of a protected roof; the classical commentators understood the protection as height, being held up, and being guarded from devils. Ozone and the magnetosphere are modern reflections that fit the image.
- **The popular claim.** That the verse "described the ozone layer" or the magnetosphere is common; no named source for it was read for this run.

### Questions readers ask next
**What does "protected ceiling" mean?** High and out of reach (Ibn Kathir), kept from falling (al-Jalālayn), guarded from devils (*Tanwīr al-Miqbās*).

**Does the verse describe the ozone layer?** No; that is a modern reflection on the image.

**Why does the verse say people "turn away" from its signs?** Because they do not reflect on how the sky was made (Ibn Kathir).
