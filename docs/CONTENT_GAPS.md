# Content gaps and open questions

Things the site needs that the research docs do not (yet) provide, and places where a human check is needed. Nothing listed here is filled in by invention.

## Added beyond the research (for transparency)
- **S1:** the DOI link for Sallon et al. 2008 (`https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1153600`), checked against Crossref. The research cites the paper but gives no link.
- **Summaries:** each item has a one-line summary for index pages. Where possible it is a sentence taken from the item's own research text.

## Needs a reviewer
- **Clinician review.** The research asks that sensitive pages be reviewed by a theologian *and* a clinician (01-batch-1, Phase 5 and Recommendations), including a clinician experienced in scrupulosity. Q8 (vesvese) and the help card need that review before launch.
- **Q8 clinical figures.** "Scrupulosity appears in about 5–33% of OCD patients" and "religious-themed OCD averages around 52% in Middle Eastern samples" are both verbatim from the research, but read as contradictory without an explanation of the different samples and definitions. A clinician should clarify; the site does not invent the explanation.
- **Transliteration.** Verse transliterations come from Quran.com's word-by-word data. They are consistent and sourced, but follow that dataset's own conventions (e.g. `l-laha`, `yukh'riju`, unmarked shadda) rather than a strict academic scheme. A reviewer should decide whether this is acceptable or whether a different source is needed.
- **Turkish wording.** All Turkish prose (except verse translations) is my translation of the English research. A Turkish-speaking theologian should read it for terminology (e.g. evidence labels: *Yerleşik bilgi* / *Ana akım yorum* / *Tartışmalı*; whether an evidence tag such as [Scholarly] reads well after a clinical statistic).

## Missing from the research
- **Tafsir sources.** The research names commentators (e.g. al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr on 6:95) but not the works, volumes or links. Pages list them as plain citations. A scholar reviewer should supply precise references.
- **Hadith links.** Hadith are cited by collection and number, as in the research, without links. The numbering should be confirmed by a hadith specialist (doc 04 says so too) before links are added.
- **Help-card resources outside Türkiye.** The research names only Turkish resources (112, AFAD psychosocial support, university clinics). The English card says "your local emergency number, or a doctor" rather than inventing hotlines. Umut should choose international resources.
- **"Scrupulosity and Islam: a perspective"** (Q8 source) has no link in the research, and could not be found by that exact title.
- **M2 (mercy)** in the research has only verses, hadith, a gap and a visual idea — no scholarly explanation, maqāṣid, social-science evidence or stories, which the brief's Phase 4 template asks for. The page shows only what exists.
- **Bukhārī 3467** ("the woman who gave water to a thirsty dog") appears as a fragment, as in the research. A hadith reviewer could supply the fuller account.
- **S1 source titles.** The research links the 2020 *Science Advances* follow-up and the National Geographic article without their titles; the site describes them instead of quoting titles.

### Needs a reviewer
- **Q13** says in its short answer that Ibn Taymiyya "argued the punishment of unbelievers will end", while its religious layer (citing Hoover) says he "never states categorically that it ends". Both are verbatim research; a scholar should reconcile them. The order of positions in the range of views is editorial.
- **S19** "Iron has the most tightly bound nucleus": by binding energy per nucleon, nickel-62 is marginally higher. A science reviewer should check.
- **S16:** the source label gives Tibetan crust as 75–85 km; the body says 70–75 km (up to 80).
- **S3 and S12:** confirm the feminine-verb remarks belong to the classical commentators named.
- **Author-facing sentences.** Some research notes are written to the site's authors ("Do not say…", "Present this fairly", "Avoid…"). They are kept verbatim (Turkish: "…gerekir"), except where they made no sense to a reader (Q6's "Avoid a lecturing tone" is left out; M9, M11–M15 and Q18 have commented rewrites). Umut to decide whether all of them should be rewritten as plain statements.
